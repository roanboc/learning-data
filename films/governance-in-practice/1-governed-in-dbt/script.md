# Governance you can use · Governed in dbt: script

*The script of *Governed in dbt*, the opening film of *Governance you can use*, the third series of data governance (film 1 of 6), in nine chapters, in English, painted in watercolour. Drafted on 28 October 2026 and made the same day, when the author asked for the films of the three series to be made and merged one after another. It follows the plan the author approved on 23 October 2026 (film 14 of that plan: the tools films open on a scene of the university's own, on a line from the film they expand), and the rule of 28 October 2026 for every opening (scenario, problem, solution and benefits, each said outright; *Opening story* quotes them). Every file on screen is the example project's own, as it is on main at `00161b92`, or a worked example's, added beside it (*What the example project has*); every check's words are the project's output, captured on 28 October 2026. As the author asked for these films, it isn't rendered here: the release workflow renders it.*

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never opened a dbt project can follow it, and an analytics engineer who keeps one finds nothing wrong in it. *Governance principles* found what's worth keeping, and *Governance on the map* drew where each piece lives. This film shows the first home on Noor's map: the data team's dbt project, where owners, classes, contracts, tests and decisions are written beside the code, and checks hold every change to them before it merges. dbt decides who may build on what; it doesn't decide who may read, which the next film shows. **The project carries governance with the code.**

## The story in one paragraph

Today, in the data team's room, Jun Park builds the way the team's poster shows, in ten steps round a loop, and opens a pull request: a new table for Planning, the awards conferred this year, by faculty. When Yusuf first asked what slowed him down, Jun said the rules arrive after he's built it. One rule says every table other teams use has an owner; for four months, the council carried "name the data owners" from one meeting's minutes to the next, and on census day nobody answered for Planning's count. Now the rules live in the project, and a check runs them on every pull request. It answers Jun in a minute: no owner, and how to add one. He adds two lines, it turns green, and the change goes to Planning for review by itself. The film then pins each decision where it enters the team's loop, as *Off the shelf* first did, and opens the project to show each pin as a line beside the code: owners, terms and classes on models and columns; groups that decide who may build on what; contracts and versions; exposures that name who uses each table; the impact of Tuesday's change written into its pull request; a reviewers file made from the owners; decisions kept as files; check messages that teach, and a rule that warns before it starts; and an agent that reviews every pull request against the same rules while people decide. It ends where it began: the rules don't arrive after Jun has built it; they arrive with every change.

## The look

*Watercolour*, the series' look, as in *Off the shelf*, with this series' files and screens.

- **The university's own places,** from *Governance principles*' shared set: the data team's room with Jun at his desk and the board across the room, its board and the team's poster close up, Yusuf's desk and his board, Noor's map close up, and the Faculty of Health's office for Anil. Nothing is redrawn: the film adds its things on top. Two places are this series' own: a big screen on a desk, in a room's colour, for pages too full for the series' screen close-up, and the data team's desk from above, for the files on it.
- **The team's poster,** *How we build*, is the series' own, with its steps named and marked, since this series shows technical words (`{words:true}`, decided 27 October 2026); the gold pins and Yusuf's words on them are drawn on top, as *Off the shelf* first drew them.
- **Files look like files.** Each file is a sheet of paper-white with its path at the top, monospaced text and a gutter of line numbers, few lines and large (28 px at the screen's own size, so more after a zoom), copied from the project with its keys as the project writes them (PLAYBOOK §3, *Copy code on screen from the project*). Lines a pull request adds have a "+" and a green edge, lines it removes a "−". A governance line lights in a gold wash as it's named, and a tag beside it names the film it comes from, by its title ("Who decides"). Lines the film doesn't need are folded, with a pencil "…".
- **Tools look like their pages, drawn for the film.** The pull request, its checks, its reviewers and the agent's comment are drawn in the series' paint as forms (labels in a column, values in the typeface; PLAYBOOK §4, *Make each way of keeping a thing look like itself*), in no product's colours, with the product's name as a small label in the screen's bar ("GitHub · pull request"). The checks are a strip of lights, each a pill with its name; the strip's summary says "running", "1 failed · 4 passed" or "all checks passed". A report written in Markdown is shown as the page renders it. Logos only where a card names the tools, on the board's six cards and the map's dbt project, unaltered and credited in `NOTICE.md`.
- **Colour carries meaning, as in the series.** A check is red when it fails, amber when it warns, and green when it passes; gold is governance's decisions and the lit governance lines; cyan is the technical side and the agent. The classes keep *Who may see it*'s chips (direct, pseudonymous, none). The glossary's meaning, `counts_as_award`, lights in its violet.
- **This series' shared pieces,** painted once for its six films, in [`../shared/src/tools.js`](../shared/src/tools.js), beside *Governance principles*' shared pictures, which stay as they are: the file sheet, the strip of checks, a tool's page as a form, a report in Markdown, the board and its six cards, the poster's pins, the agent's star, name cards and tags. This film added them; the films after it use them unchanged.
- **Words on screen** are what the files and checks say, shortened only with "…", and every message the film shows is the project's own output (*Rigour*).
