## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never given anyone access to a table can follow it, and a platform lead or a security architect finds nothing wrong in it. The idea: **design the grants once, by area and role.** Who may read what is decided before a single grant, from who reads what, and written down once: a shelf for each set of readers, a grant only to a group and only on a catalog or a shelf, so every new table is read by the right people from its first minute and nobody grants by hand. Owners are groups, not people. A catalog can be read only from the workspaces it's bound to, and from a study's workspace only read. And the whole plan is kept as code, its changes reviewed by the data team.

The film builds on what the module has shown: *Governed in Unity Catalog* (one catalog, read by groups), *People and groups* (groups from the university's directory), *Power you switch on* (an admin account, switched on when it's needed), *Network, storage and keys* (each catalog bound to its own workspace), *From a branch to production* (the path to production, and `registrar-loader`, the identity that deploys) and *Who did what* (the record). It hands on to *Tags that travel*, *One policy for many tables*, whose policies decide what each reader sees, *Copies that keep the rules* and *Proof it held*.

## The story in one paragraph

The Galápagos, in the late 1960s: volcanic islands about a thousand kilometres west of Ecuador, whose animals don't fear people, and boats that bring more visitors each year. Scientists and guides fear what all those feet will do: visitors walk anywhere, a step from nesting birds, iguanas and sea lions; nests could be trampled and the lava worn down, coral is already being carried off, and many of the animals live nowhere else. So the national park, on the Charles Darwin Research Station's advice, plans the visits once, for the whole park: most of it gets no visitors at all; a few sites near the coast are open, each along its own marked trail; every group walks with a trained guide; and the rules are the same for everyone. Each new boat, guide and visitor fits the plan the day they arrive. In 1982, 25,000 people visit and the sites they walk are near pristine; flamingos nest again beside a visited lagoon; in 2025, 290,404 people came, still on the trails, still with a guide. A university's data has visitors too. Its tables were opened one at a time, so a new table waited six days for its first reader, and nine tables were owned by the person who made them. Now the registrar's tables move into a catalog of their own, and Mei Tanaka, who owns them, asks for a plan first: three rights to read a table, shelves by who reads them, groups as owners, catalogs bound to where they may be read from, and the plan kept as code, with her yes on every change.

## The reference, and practice

The module's references are its own: DAMA-DMBOK's data security area as the map, and zero trust (NIST SP 800-207: least privilege, every request checked); the module's *Decided* table records why. For this film, three bodies of knowledge, each named here once and none on screen:

| Body of knowledge | What it adds | Where the film uses it |
|---|---|---|
| Role-based access control (NIST's model, standardised as ANSI/INCITS 359) | Rights belong to roles, and people get them by belonging to a role, never one by one | A grant always goes to a group, never to a person (*Shelves by who reads them*); a new person reads what their group reads at once (*Back to the islands*) |
| Least privilege (NIST SP 800-53, control AC-6) | Each person and each process gets only the access its job needs | Shelves nobody reads (`staging`, `intermediate`); the exam support table on its own shelf; the data team with nothing of its own in production; a study that may read and never write |
| Databricks' Unity Catalog best practices (read 10 November 2026) | Grants to groups; catalogs by area and environment; groups, not people, as the owners of what runs in production; `BROWSE` so people can find what exists and ask for it | The whole design, and the page's *Read the guidance yourself* |

**Where the film adopts, extends or differs.** It adopts role-based access control's core: roles, here the university directory's groups, hold the rights. It extends it with one rule practitioners use and the model doesn't state: the grant's place carries as much meaning as its holder, so a grant goes only on a catalog or a shelf, and a table's readers are those of its shelf. Arranging shelves by who reads them, not only by how the data is built, is the film's own advice: a team that starts from dbt's layers alone (staging, intermediate, marts) ends up granting table by table, because one layer holds tables for different readers. It differs from a plain reading of least privilege on discovery: everyone may find every table and see what it holds (`BROWSE`), because a table nobody can find gets copied instead of asked for. It never reads data without a grant.

**Practice, as the module's plan gives it (5 November 2026):** grants made one table at a time, when someone asks, are how most catalogs grow; they work until nobody can say who reads what without reading every grant, a new table waits days for its first reader, and tables are owned by whoever made them, including people who have left. The fix practitioners describe is the one the film shows: design once, by area and role, write the design as code beside the pipeline's, and let the data's owner approve every change to it. A practitioner will recognise the opening's data story: a list of grants nobody can read, and a table nobody could open after its owner left.

**What the film leaves to others.** Groups and the directory are *People and groups*'; admin accounts switched on are *Power you switch on*'s; the network, storage and binding of each workspace are *Network, storage and keys*'; the path to production is *From a branch to production*'s; the record of who did what is *Who did what*'s. Which columns are personal is *Know what you hold*'s, how each is hidden *Show the least*'s, and the policies that decide what each reader of core sees are *One policy for many tables*'. The study's agreement is *An agreement first*'s, and the proof that the design held is *Proof it held*'s.

## The look

*Night glass*, the module's look (`films/locks-before-the-data/shared/src`): a dark hall of frosted panes, frosted where what's behind may not be read and clear where it may, with people drawn to the agent films' rules and lit for the dark. Nothing is watercolour, and nothing has film grain.

- **Two places.** The islands are one, painted once in the module's evening light (`gdShore`, in `src/gd_islands.js`): a shore of the 1970s at dusk, seen from the sea's edge, with what moves drawn live over it: boats, visitors and their guides, small and far off, in the clothes of the time, the animals, the trail and its stakes, and the frosted zones. The university's hall is the other (`gdHall`, in `src/gd_room.js`): the registrar's catalog as a room of clear glass on the right and the groups on the left. From *One table at a time* to *Written once, kept as code*, every chapter glides on from where the last one left the camera, what stays stays put and only what changes fades; the end comes back to the islands by night.
- **The tools' pages are drawn for the film** (`src/gd_pages.js`), with the kit's pages: GitHub's file tree, the plan's file in the code font, a review, and Catalog Explorer's permissions, each with its official logo, in the module's colours, never a product's own look, and no release status anywhere. No screen is captured.
- **Colours carry meaning,** as in every film of the module: cyan an identity (a group's or an identity's name), green allowed (a grant, a door that opens), coral denied or closed (a shut door, a struck-out grant, a write stopped), amber power switched on (the admin account), violet hidden personal data, grey what needs no protection.
- **Its own pieces,** each name starting with `gd_`: the shore and what moves on it; the room, its shelves, tables, doors and plates; the groups and their lines; the owners' chips; the workspaces and their bindings; the tools' pages. The module's kit is never changed for one film.

### 3 · Three doors to a table · 3:10–3:58

**Narration.** In Unity Catalog, a table sits in a schema, and a schema in a catalog. Think of the catalog as a room, and each schema as a shelf in it. To read a table, a group needs three rights: into the room, onto the shelf, and into the table. USE CATALOG, USE SCHEMA and SELECT. Like a landing, a trail and a site: all three, or none. SELECT on a table, without the first two, opens nothing. One more right lets everyone look without reading: BROWSE. With it, anyone can find a table, see what it holds and who owns it, and ask for it.
