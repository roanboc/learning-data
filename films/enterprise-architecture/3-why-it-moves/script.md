## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has watched their organisation be pulled in three directions at once can follow it, and an experienced enterprise architect finds nothing wrong in it. Before anyone asks what the organisation must be able to do, find out why it has to change: who cares, what pushes on it, what that means here, what must become true, and how anyone will know. The motivation layer holds it, as a chain: stakeholders, drivers, assessments, goals and outcomes. Goals pull against each other, and principles are how the pull is settled, out loud and the same way every time. A principle that can't be tested isn't one. **Know why it must change, and what it will be checked against.**

## The story in one paragraph

On 9 November 1965, one relay near Niagara Falls tripped, and thirty million people lost power: no utility could see past its own wires, and the answer was a council that wrote rules every one of them had to keep. Today, Tomás's note asks why the utility must change, and three letters answer at once: the minister wants lower bills, a dividend and net zero; the regulator has cut what the network may earn; a valley community objects to a new line for a wind farm. Ama, the regulatory lead, sorts them: who cares, what pushes on them, what it means here, with evidence. Goals follow, each with an outcome someone can check. When the goals pull apart over the new line, principles settle it, and only principles that can be tested count. The chain is drawn, each outcome is a number someone will one day have to measure, and Ama confirms the drivers; the goals go to the board.

### 8 · Principles that can be tested · 3:37–4:13

**Narration.** A principle is a rule every choice is checked against. Ama has three. Use what we have before we build. Every option is costed for the customers who pay for it. Checked against these three, the new line isn't the first option. Upgrading the old line, with batteries in the valley, is.

## Rigour to check before recording

- **The 1965 blackout.** On 9 November 1965 a protective relay at the Sir Adam Beck station, near Niagara Falls in Ontario, set below the line's actual load, tripped a transmission line; the cascade left about 30 million people in the north-east of the United States and in Ontario without power, for up to 13 hours. It led to the founding of the North American Electric Reliability Council in 1968, whose standards were voluntary until the 2000s. "Rules that all of them had to keep" simplifies that: say "rules for all of them" if challenged. Check against a primary or archive source on the day.
- **Motivation elements** follow ArchiMate 3.2 (stakeholder, driver, assessment, goal, outcome, principle); requirements and constraints, which also belong there, are left to later films, where they meet the information layer.
- **A statement of expectations** from a minister to a government-owned business, and a regulator's five-year revenue decision, exist in several jurisdictions under different names. Keep both generic.
- **"No higher in real terms"** means after inflation; keep it, since it's how such targets are usually set.
- **The three principles** are invented for the fictional utility. The third, the wall between network and retail, sets up the films on domains and on rules with a home. The first is a widely used kind of network-planning principle (non-network options before new build), stated simply.
- **The figures** (a third of poles over fifty, bills up 18%, ten working days, net zero by 2045) are fictional and plausible; they're not drawn from any one utility.
