| # | Question for Rodrigo | Assumption taken | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which framework is the reference for designing grants? | The module's own (DAMA-DMBOK's data security area, and zero trust, NIST SP 800-207: least privilege, each request checked) stays the reference. For this film: role-based access control (NIST's RBAC model, ANSI/INCITS 359) for "rights by role, through groups", and least privilege (NIST SP 800-53, AC-6); Databricks' Unity Catalog best practices on the day for the tools (grants to groups, groups own production objects, catalogs by area and environment, `BROWSE` for everyone). Named once in the script, never on screen | The script's *Reference*; the page's *Elsewhere* |
| 2 | Which opening story? (the module left it to this thread) | The Galápagos, 1969 to 1982: the national park and the Charles Darwin Research Station plan visits once for the whole park (zones, set sites, marked trails, a trained guide with every group, nothing touched, fed or taken), so visits that began on a regular basis in the late 1960s could grow to 25,000 people in 1982, and 290,404 in 2025, and the visited sites stayed near pristine. Chosen because each part of the plan is a part of the film's design: zones as shelves, a trail's landing, path and site as the three doors, every group with a guide as grants only to groups, "look, don't touch" as the read-only binding, one plan for every boat as one design for every table. Checked against R. W. Tindle's report in *Oryx* (1983), the Charles Darwin Foundation and the park's 2025 visitor report. *Copies that keep the rules* opens on Lascaux, also a place kept from harm by its visitors; there the copy is visited, here the original is visited by design, so the two don't teach the same thing. The other candidate was Bologna's *Authentica Habita* (1155 to 1158), the privileges given to scholars by their standing, not one by one | Chapter 1, the *Opening story* table, and the end |
| 3 | One catalog with a schema per layer (as *Governed in Unity Catalog* shows), or a catalog per area? | A catalog per area, as *From a branch to production* names them (`registrar`, `learning`, `planning`, `hr`, `finance`; `_test` and `_dev` in the other workspaces), each with the same shelves (schemas), one per set of readers. *Governed in Unity Catalog*'s `university` with `prod_core` and `prod_marts` is named in a line as where the university started; the example project in this repository keeps dbt's default schema names, and the film's platform repository is the fictional `uni-example/data-platform` | Chapters 2 and 4; `names.md` |
| 4 | Which shelves, and who reads each? | `staging`, `intermediate` (no reader), `core` (the area's records, `confidential`), `support` (the registrar's only: exam support, `restricted`), `reference`, `published` (totals, `public`, everyone), `policies` (the masks' functions), `rooms` (for *Copies that keep the rules*, no reader). Readers by group: `registrar_office` (`core`, `reference`), `registrar_exam_support` (`support`), `help_desk` (`core`, its policy masking in part; added at *One policy for many tables*' ask), `health_progress_study` (`core`, and its own space), `planning_learner_readers` (`core`, until 15 December 2026), `account users` (`BROWSE` on the catalog, and `published`) | Chapters 3 to 5; `names.md` |
| 5 | Where does a study work: a shelf in the registrar's catalog, or a place of its own? | A place of its own: a workspace `studies` (downloads, exports and the clipboard off) and a catalog `studies`, one schema per agreement (`studies.health_progress`), and `registrar` bound to that workspace read only, so the study reads the registrar's table where it is and can never write to it. A shelf inside `registrar` would be simpler, but would give a study's group the right to make tables in a production catalog of records; Databricks' practice keeps direct changes to production for service principals | Chapter 7; `names.md` |
| 6 | Who owns the catalog and its shelves? | A group, `registrar_owners`, with no person in it, only `registrar-loader`, which deploys and builds; in test, `registrar_test_owners` with `registrar-loader-test`. The bundle's deploying identity owns a schema it creates, and the bundle can't set another ("The owner of a schema resource is always the deployment user, and cannot be changed", *Bundle resources*, updated 5 November 2026, read 10 November), so the registrar's job hands the catalog, each schema and each table it builds to the group as it makes them (`ALTER … OWNER TO`); as the group's only member, `registrar-loader` still deploys them. The film shows the owner, not that step. "Whoever makes it is its first owner" (the narration) is how the platform behaves; the docs read on 10 November say every object has an owner and that a schema's is its deployer, but not that rule in one sentence | Chapter 6; rigour sheet |
| 7 | Who decides who may see the registrar's data, if a group owns it? | Two kinds of owner: Mei Tanaka, the registrar's data owner (*Who decides*), decides, by approving every change to the grants' files as their code owner (`@uni-example/registrar-data-owners`); the owner in Unity Catalog, the group, is who may change the object | Chapters 6 and 8 |
| 8 | Does the data team keep its read of the learner table (*Governed in Unity Catalog*'s second group)? | No: it builds in development on made-up records (*Walls, doors and keys*, *Copies that keep the rules*), so in production it has nothing of its own, and finds tables like everyone. An emergency is an admin account switched on (*Power you switch on*) | Chapter 4 |
| 9 | Should everyone be able to find every table? | Yes: `account users` get `BROWSE` on each catalog (see a table's name and what it holds, and ask for it, never read it), as Databricks recommends; only `published` is read by everyone | Chapters 3 and 4 |
| 10 | How are grants kept as code? | In the registrar's bundle in `uni-example/data-platform` (`registrar/resources/catalog.yml`, `registrar/resources/schemas.yml`), privileges written as the bundle writes them (`USE_CATALOG`, `USE_SCHEMA`, `SELECT`, `BROWSE`, `CREATE_TABLE`, `MODIFY`, the names of the platform's API, checked in the Databricks SDK for Python on 5 November 2026), deployed by `registrar-loader` through *From a branch to production*'s path: test first, then production after a release approver | Chapter 8 |
| 11 | What stops a grant made by hand? | Not a claim that a deploy removes it (today's docs don't say what a deploy does with a grant it didn't make): nobody can make one. Only the owner group can grant on `registrar`, and its only member deploys from `main`; anyone else would need an admin account switched on, approved and recorded (*Power you switch on*, *Who did what*) | Chapter 8 |
| 12 | The film's numbers for the old catalog | Invented, blameless: in the one catalog, 58 tables and 163 grants, each made by hand on one table; 9 tables owned by the person who made them, 2 of whom had moved on; a new table waited 6 days, on average, for its first grant | Chapter 2 |
