## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has had to explain who their organisation is for can follow it, and an experienced enterprise architect finds nothing wrong in it. Before any capability or process, find out who the organisation serves, what they're trying to get done, what hurts and what would help, what it offers, and how each offering pays. Two canvases hold it: a value proposition canvas per customer segment, and a business model canvas per offering. A canvas only means something if it fits: every pain relieved, every gain created, and an unmatched pain named as a missing capability or a customer not served. The canvases aren't the architecture; the architecture is derived from them. **Know who it serves, and how it pays, before anything else.**

## Rigour to check before recording

- **Pearl Street.** The Pearl Street Station opened on 4 September 1882 in Lower Manhattan. Edison set out to price electric light to compete with gas lighting, and used his chemical (electrolytic) meter, whose zinc plates were removed and weighed to work out what was used. Check the wording against a museum or archive source on the day.
- **The canvases** are Strategyzer's (Value Proposition Canvas, Business Model Canvas); credit them. The sections of the value proposition canvas are drawn in a simplified layout.
- **Revenue and costs have no ArchiMate element of their own**: ArchiMate's Value is the nearest, and the series keeps them as tables. Say "in the model" rather than "in ArchiMate" if challenged.
- **A regulated network's revenue** is set by the regulator in many markets (a revenue cap or allowed revenue), recovered through network charges on retail bills. Keep it generic.
- **Hardship support funded by government** (in some places called community service obligations) varies by jurisdiction. Keep it generic.
