## The brief

The first two series are about the skills of each person. This one is about leading the team they make, through four ideas, one per film, each taught from its own sources and never as anyone's philosophy (the module's *Decided* table). Simpler is better, and it doesn't just happen, so *Less, on purpose* stops, with the team, what nobody uses. Improvement is everyone's job, so *One small change at a time* shows the cycle of plan, do, check and act on one person's own work. A person is a person through other people, so *The team comes first* puts the team's collaboration and well-being first, then each member, then the manager. And a leader can't make every decision, so *Rules that let people decide* gives people a few clear shared rules, good tools and trust, and lets decisions be made where the knowledge is. Minimalism balances the last: few rules, each with its reason.

The series follows the team from May to October 2027 (the module's shared numbers): the 17 of 38 old reports not opened in twelve months; standing meetings from Monday 10 May 2027 (a 15-minute stand-up each morning, planning on Monday, a review every second Thursday); the alerts, from about sixty a week to twelve by Monday 7 June; Ivy's Monday refresh, two hours by hand in July, ten minutes, checked, by October; the night rota of four from Monday 5 July, after pairing on the platform in June; Sam's two weeks away in August, when nobody calls him; and the faculties' timetable officers, from October, reading *Teaching space use* to plan 2028's classes.

## The films

| 1 | `1-less-on-purpose` | Less, on purpose | The team lists what it keeps running and who uses it: 17 of 38 old reports not opened in a year, about eight hours of meetings a week each, about sixty alerts a week, most needing nothing. It stops them together, openly and reversibly, and says where the time goes: to the work that matters. *Kanso* and lean's waste are named once | Keep what people use; stop the rest, together. |
| 2 | `2-one-small-change` | One small change at a time | Ivy's Monday refresh takes two hours by hand in July. One small change a week, each checked, kept or dropped: measure it, simplify it, standardise it, then automate it; ten minutes, checked, by October. Helena's part: ask what's in the way, give the time, show the small wins. Kaizen's Japanese roots and the cycle's history are named once | Change one small thing, check it, keep what works. |
| 3 | `3-the-team-first` | The team comes first | Until June Sam alone answers the platform's night alerts. The team pairs on the platform in June and shares the nights in a rota of four; when Sam has to leave at a day's notice in August, the rota and the runbooks hold, and nobody calls him. A mistake said out loud is thanked; one-to-ones are about the person. Ubuntu is taught from Southern African writers | Nobody alone, nobody the only one who knows. |
| 4 | `4-rules-to-decide` | Rules that let people decide | The four faculties' timetable officers plan 2028's classes themselves, from *Teaching space use*, with a few shared rules, each with its reason, and a template; the team doesn't decide for them. Engineering's officer finds its labs empty on Monday mornings, which nobody had asked about | A few clear rules, good tools, and trust. |

Each opens with a story from outside data, in the four beats said outright: GOV.UK's "Do less"; 5S and kaizen in a Colombo hospital; stokvels in South Africa; and Bali's subak and water temples (the module's `openings.md`, in the project's shared files).
