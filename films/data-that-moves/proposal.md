## The brief

The author, on 5 November 2026, asked which topics the site is missing:

> data integration: that's the most known challenge in data and the reason I didn't want to touch much on this, we now look for the simplest integration mechanisms like Lakeflow, mirrors, API data as a service so we don't actually need to spend a lot of time there. It's still important for interface data contracts and how data flows between applications via events or data batches.

> running the platform: again don't want to focus on this but the principles are around bringing software engineering practices to data, something already know but lacked for decades until tools like dbt and vendors realized scripts had to be better maintained as software.

> from tables to decisions: same here, old way of doing things was serving data as reports but now there are more channels and consumers like agents and systems. Semantic models is the hype of just defining the basics of data which is known for decades but now finally coming to better standards and not isolated tool definitions

> document and unstructured is an interesting one as context and data itself, but they are generally very specific in use cases so probably good to mention and all the new functions in Databricks to process them more easily but not much more from there.

> As remarks maybe comment for all of these there are already some standards so don't try to reinvent the wheel, investigate, adapt, implement, iterate. This is for me now the very basics of modern data stacks. If you are struggling with those, you probably need to improve either your governance or your tooling, already described in other sections.

Later the same morning he added the temperature of data and one platform for both kinds of work: "Add to data that moves the data temperature and the coming goal of having one single engine to process analytical and transactional data, Databricks working on that in ltap. That means you can consolidate your data integration services in a single unified platform instead of having multiple isolated platforms." And the way data is served now: "Reports can now be generated on demand, so switch the focus to deliver good data models and context as part of the semantic layer and let the users explore the data with the agents. Repetitive queries are the ones deserving dashboards". Databricks describes LTAP as one copy of the data and one governance for both kinds of work, with separate engines, not one engine (*One copy, one platform*, below), and the films say it that way.

On the module's place and weight: "After data modeling, we need to be explicit that we deliberately don't touch much this in deep as that's where other learning materials for technical people go deep and official documentation might be better to stay updated with particular features, so we provide the architectural relevant advice. The other topics will touch on some of the specifics as part of their content." On the examples: "maybe more generic examples, HR or finance that everyone knows". On mirrors: "Federation yes, but also mirror like Databricks mirrors in fabric, which we use today. We can include that as an example because are federation inbound and mirrors outbound". On documents, from his own work: "official documents with metadata, scraping and standard json or yaml structures but that's more on the application side rather than business documents". **The module's promise.** Moving data, running the platform, serving it to people and agents, and reading documents used to take whole teams. Databricks and dbt now do much of it. These films show what got easier, what still needs agreeing, and where the official docs take you next.

**Light on purpose, and said outright** on the module's page and in its opening film: we deliberately don't go deep here. Technical courses go deep on these features, and the official docs stay current as they change, so these films give the architectural advice: what matters, what to choose, and why. The depth stays in the modules that own it: contracts in *When things go wrong*, meaning in data governance, privacy in data protection.

## Practical advice the films carry

- Move data the simplest way the tools offer (a managed connector, a query in place, a mirror, a share) before writing a pipeline of your own.
- Spend the time saved on the contract at each interface: what each field means, who owns it, what a deletion means, and what is personal.
- Say which way data flows: query a system in place when you only read it (inbound), and mirror your catalog out to the tools other teams use (outbound), rather than copying it by hand.
- Set each kind of data's temperature from how fresh it must be and how often it's read: keep hot data small and close, warm data refreshed on a schedule, and cold data cheap, until the date it may be deleted.
- Keep one copy where you can, governed once, and move data only when a reader truly needs it somewhere else.
- Keep data work as software: in Git, reviewed, tested before it reaches production, and deployed the same way every time.
- Let the platform watch its jobs, pipelines and tables and propose a fix; a person reviews it, and nothing reaches production without them.
- Let people ask for the answer they need, on demand; build a dashboard only for the questions asked again and again.
- Define each measure once, near the data, in a format other tools can read, with its owner and its words.
- Serve the same definitions to every reader: people, agents, BI tools, apps and other systems.
- Read documents with the platform's functions only where a use case needs them; keep each file and its metadata, and write the documents your applications make in standard structures, such as JSON and YAML.
- End every film the same way: investigate, adapt, implement, iterate, starting from the official docs.

## The sound

Each film has a palette of its own, made in its `tools/score.py`, with a lead instrument the site doesn't have, reserved by the module's setup: steel reeds sung by electromagnets, as in the harmonic telegraphs of the 1870s; a pyrophone; a log drum; an ophicleide; a terpsitone; repeater gongs; an organette; a horn violin; and an optophone's tones. Each film's thread chooses its partner. Music plays under the opening story, then only at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen or in a pause without words, at most about once every 10 seconds, and no figure comes back again and again. Nothing trembles, and nothing is held under the voice. Effects are soft, low, one at a time and tied to something on screen; held notes sit at even levels. An urgent pulse plays only where a story turns to an error or a rush. `quiet.py` checks it all, and the mix is at -16 LUFS.

## Rules from the plan

- Light on purpose: each film names the idea, shows it once in the tools, gives the architectural advice and links the official docs on its page. It says outright, on the module's page and in its opening film, that the depth is left to technical courses and to the docs, which stay current.
- No film shows or says a feature's release status (preview, beta, generally available), so none needs updating when it changes. Each film checks its products' names and what they do against their docs on the day, and keeps the status it read, with its date, in its script's sources only.
- Tools show, they don't lead: the concept first, then Databricks and dbt (and Microsoft Fabric for mirroring out), named as their docs name them on the day.
- An agent proposes and a person approves, as the agent films set it: nothing reaches production without a person.
- The fictional university, people and numbers. Nothing from the author's own talks is named; they are input only.
- Spanish is captions and pages only. Site text follows the short style. Each film has Pause and think questions; labs come later, as one set for the module.
