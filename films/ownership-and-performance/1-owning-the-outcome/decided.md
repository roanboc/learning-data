| 10 November 2026 | *Owning the outcome* opens in Kenya, in the four beats: in the 1970s, in the hills of the central highlands, rural women tell Wangari Maathai, a biologist, that their streams are drying up, their food is less sure and each year they walk further for firewood; the forests had been cleared for farms, for tea and coffee to sell and for timber, each choice sensible to the people who made it, but without trees the soil washed off the hills and the springs ran dry, and the harm fell first on the women, who fetched the water, gathered the firewood and grew the food, and on their children; in 1977 Maathai starts the Green Belt Movement with the National Council of Women of Kenya, and groups of women grow seedlings in their own nurseries from the seeds of local trees and plant them out, paid a small sum for each tree still alive months later, not for each seedling put in the ground; more than fifty million trees since, which give fuel, food and shelter, an income for thousands of women, and in 2004 the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize. Then the same for data: a job that ran and a report that was sent are data work's seedlings. It comes back in *A Monday that looked fine* ("a seedling in the ground earned nothing until it lived") and at the close (the women answered for trees still standing; Ivy answers for true counts). Nobody in the story is to blame. |
