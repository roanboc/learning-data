| 12 November 2026 | *A road people choose* takes its four beats from the pilgrimage to Ise in the 1700s: ordinary people might not travel as they pleased, but a pilgrimage was hard to refuse, and people said "once in a lifetime, to Ise"; from Edo, the Tōkaidō most of the way, then the road south to Ise; a farmer who had never left his village didn't know the road, where to sleep, how far to the next station, what it all cost, and the journey cost more than one household could spare; so villages formed pilgrimage groups (*Ise-kō*), every household paying in each year and two or three a year, chosen by lot, going for everyone; priests from Ise, the *oshi*, visited those villages every year with charms and calendars, lodged the pilgrims in their own houses at Ise and guided them to the shrines; guidebooks listed every station, how far apart, the inns and the sights; nobody was made to go, and ordinary people went, hundreds of thousands in an ordinary year; in 1771 more than two million crossed the river into Ise in a few months, counted at the ferry; later a comic tale of two travellers on their way to Ise became a craze like none before, and prints of the road's stations followed. It comes back to the story in *Teams, as customers* (as the oshi visited the villages), *A guide, and someone to ask* (as the guidebooks listed every station), *Counted at the ferry* and at the end. The film says "hundreds of thousands in an ordinary year", since the sources give from 300,000 to 700,000; it doesn't name the comic tale, its author or the printmaker, nor the years 1705 and 1830. |
| 12 November 2026 | The film's data story: a year ago network's team wanted its first data product on the platform, planned outages for the call centre; it took eleven tickets (a workspace, storage, an account for its job and each right it needed), each waiting its turn in the platform team's queue, then a pipeline written from nothing and a first pull request sent back for the catalogue's rules: six weeks. Two other teams gave up: field services runs a database on its own server, and metering works from spreadsheets. Nobody did anything wrong: the platform was the safe way, but not yet the easy one. Ravi Menon could order every team onto it, but an order makes teams use it, not want it. So he treats the platform as a product and the teams as its customers; his team sits with each domain team, and three things come up again and again. The path: the template every new job starts from, from *Security all the way through*, grows into one for a whole data product; one form (name, owner, what it holds), no tickets, and minutes later a repository with a pipeline that runs, its tests, a contract for its readers, its entry in the catalogue, rights by role and its log; the team writes only its logic, in a pull request the data team reviews, and may leave the path where it needs something else, that part then its own to run. The rules built in (personal data classed before anything is published, the tests on every pull request, nothing live without an owner); a guide written from teams' questions, and an answer in the platform's channel the same day. In March metering's team goes live in two days and retires its spreadsheets; by December eight of the eleven domain teams have chosen the path, none told to, a first data product takes two to four days, nine in ten teams would tell another to use it, field services' server is switched off, and metering's check goes into the template for every team. The numbers are invented. |
| 12 November 2026 | The film's positions, from the plan: the platform is a product, and the teams that build on it are its customers; the platform team goes to them to see what they need and what gets in their way, and makes the right way the easiest one, rather than ordering teams onto it. A path already laid (a template and self-service: no tickets), the rules built into it as guardrails, not gates, a guide and someone to ask; a team may leave the path, and what it builds off it is its own to run. The platform team counts what tells it the platform works as a product: how many teams choose it, how long a first data product takes, and what teams say. The film teaches *Team Topologies*' platform as a product, the CNCF's platform white paper and maturity model, and the golden path, without naming them; it names no product. It hands over to *Services with customers*, which shows how to write down who each service is for and what they need. |
