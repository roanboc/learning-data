| 3 November 2026 | *More than a junior* opens in Berlin in 1904 with Clever Hans, in the four beats, each said outright. Scenario: von Osten, a retired maths teacher, shows his horse every noon, taught for four years the way children are taught; Hans taps sums, fractions and the date. Problem: how could a horse do sums? Some papers called von Osten a fraud, and an old man's good name was at stake; thirteen experts found no trick, many took that to mean the horse could think, but in every test someone knew the answer. Solution: Pfungst tested Hans as a horse: with no one knowing the answer he failed, and with large blinkers he got 6 in every 100 right, against 89 in sight; he was watching the one who asked. Benefits: von Osten was no cheat, Hans had a real gift nobody had looked for, and careful tests have kept the answer from the one who asks ever since; then the same for Jun's team and its agent. Facts from Pfungst's own account (1907; English, 1911) and Stumpf's reports in it, not from popular retellings, several of which get the numbers and the commission wrong. |
