What each look costs to draw

The site draws every film live, in the visitor's browser. A look that is heavy to draw is a film that stutters on a phone. So every film was measured the same way: sixteen moments of each, drawn as the site's player draws them. Pick a device in the bar and every number on this page follows.

How it was measured

The same six steps for every film

  1. Build the film and open it as the site's player does. The browser has no graphics card, as where the repository's checks run.
  2. Take sixteen moments, evenly spaced from the first second to the last.
  3. Draw each moment at 1920 × 1080 with the processor slowed four times. The first draw is the first picture. Then the moment is drawn three more times, each followed by reading one pixel back, and the middle time is kept.
  4. Split the time in two. The film's script is done when its drawing calls return. The browser paints when the pixel is read.
  5. Time every person. The picture is brought up to date before and after each person is drawn, so the person's painting is counted and nobody else's.
  6. Do it again at 960 × 540, and at full speed. Then add up the pictures the film still holds, and weigh what the site sends.
  • Slower than a phonePainting without a graphics chip is the slow way. The numbers are for comparing looks, and for seeing a look get lighter or heavier.
  • Sixteen moments are a sampleA film's heaviest second can fall between two of them, and the same film measured twice differs by up to a tenth: two looks closer than that are a tie.
  • The player protects itselfWhen frames take more than 40 ms it draws at half size. That is the second device in the bar.
