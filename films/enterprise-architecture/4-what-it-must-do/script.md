## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has been handed an org chart and asked "so what does this place do?" can follow it, and an experienced enterprise architect finds nothing wrong in it. A team is who does the work; a capability is what the work makes possible. Name capabilities for what the organisation must be able to do, whoever does it, and they outlast the restructures that move the teams. Levels show how deep to go, owners show who answers for each one, and a heat map shows where to look first. **Name what it must be able to do, and make sure someone answers for each one.**

## The story in one paragraph

Across the Andes, in the 1400s, runners carried messages from post to post along a road system thousands of kilometres long. At every post the runner changed; the road and the ability to carry a message along it stayed. Today Tomás's note asks what the utility must be able to do, and his first answer is the org chart, copied onto the wall one box per team. Grace, who owns network operations, says a team is who does the work, not what the work is. Ask what her team does, and the list is three capabilities: manage outages, maintain assets, connect customers. The map is built in levels, each capability gets an owner, and the heat map shows connecting customers running hot. A reorganisation moves two teams; the capabilities stay where they are. The map stops at the depth where decisions stop, and goes to the board as a draft.

## Rigour to check before recording

- **The Inca road system and the chasqui.** The road system ran for thousands of kilometres across the Andes, and it is inscribed by UNESCO as the Andean road system (Qhapaq Ñan, 2014). The relay runners, the chasqui, carried messages between posts; the narration gives one estimate of about 240 km in a day, and calls it an estimate. Check against a primary source on the day; a standard reference is John Hyslop, *The Inka Road System* (1984). The film draws the road in the series' own sketch style, not in Inca art, as the proposal asks.
- **Capabilities in ArchiMate.** ArchiMate 3.2 puts a capability in the strategy layer, which is the amber layer in the stack. The series names them as abilities ("manage outages"); many frameworks use noun phrases ("outage management"). The series uses one form throughout.
- **Levels.** ArchiMate doesn't fix how many levels a capability map has; practice in BIZBOK and APQC goes to three or four. The series uses three, and says why: level 3 only where order matters.
- **Heat maps** use the same colours for the same meaning across the film: cool is working, hot is hurting. The evidence behind each colour must be on the same card.
- **The figures** (thirty-five working days to connect, ten as the outcome, a reorganisation that moves two teams, five level 1 capabilities) are fictional and plausible. The ten-day outcome is the one from *Why it moves*, and keeps its figure.
- **Names of teams** are invented for the fictional utility. "Grid operations" and "network planning" are generic.
