Who is it, really? Every request proves who it is

Every night at two, a job loads the registrar's records onto the university's data platform. It signs in with Jun Park's personal token, from a workspace open to the internet, so to the platform, the job is Jun. What should every request prove, and where should its rights come from?

Read the guidance yourself

The film names no product, and one framework, once. These are the public guides it follows, from the bodies that write them.

  • NIST SP 800-207: Zero Trust ArchitectureThe United States' guide to zero trust: no request is trusted for where it comes from, each is checked, and each gets the least it needs.
  • NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0The framework the film names once: under Protect, identities for users, services and devices, checked, and given the least they need.
  • Threat Modeling ManifestoWhere the four questions come from: what are we working on, what can go wrong, what are we going to do about it, and did we do a good enough job?
  • OWASP: Non-Human Identities Top 10The ten commonest risks of the identities that jobs and services sign in with, from secrets that never end to one identity shared by many.
