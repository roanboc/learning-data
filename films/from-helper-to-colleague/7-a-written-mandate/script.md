## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has only heard of AI agents can follow it, and a platform lead, a data engineer or a risk specialist finds nothing wrong in it. The series ends at its top stage, a contributor with a mandate, and shows what that word holds. A mandate is one page, saying the one kind of task an agent may do alone: its task and where, its folders and its checks, limits on rounds, time and money, a switch that stops it and a way to undo each change. It is a requirement on the university's map, beside the task it covers, signed by the agent's owner and approved by a second person. A page is only words, so each line is held by a tool, written from the map like every right: Unity Catalog's grants, GitHub's push rules and the run's own timeout, the plain code that counts its rounds, a rate limit in Unity Gateway, and a plain check as the run's first step; and the project's check fails when any of them allows more than the page. Even at stage five, it only drafts in production; what a word means stays its owner's; and the mandate can be switched off, undone, narrowed or withdrawn by the person who signed it.

**Takeaway:** "Autonomy is a written, narrow, revocable mandate."

## The story in one paragraph

Today, at the university: every week Anil Kurian's workload agent tells Health's heads of school how many teach in each school, and how many are casual, and each night every change is built in test, on a copy of the data, before it can reach production. Last night, Wednesday 4 November 2026, at 23:14, that build failed in test, on the agent's own numbers: Nursing showed 67 teachers, 41 of them casual, against HR's own 64 and 38. Nobody was at work, and finding out why is the agent's job. But an agent that works alone can do harm alone: one night in October, to make a failed build pass, it also changed HR's model of contracts, which other teams' numbers come from; had that reached production, their numbers would have changed overnight with nobody knowing why; and what it might do alone was written nowhere. So Anil wrote its mandate, one page, a requirement on the map beside the night task, signed on 30 October and approved by Noor Rahman as a second person, and each line was written into the tools that hold it. Last night a plain check saw that tonight's copy had arrived, and the agent started; in three rounds it found that the day's change read every version HR keeps of a contract, so three tutors who had moved to another unit this term counted twice; it put back one line of its own dbt model, checked it in test, touched nothing else, and twenty minutes after the failure its report was waiting for Anil. It also found a question no rule answers, whether a tutor who moved counts in both schools this term, and asked it of HR, who decides what a word about staff means, changing nothing. Production never saw the bug. This morning Jun Park reviewed and merged the fix, it passed in test again, and Noor said yes for production: Nursing counts 64 teachers, 38 of them casual, and the agent decided nothing a person owns. The series closes on its five stages and its seven lessons, and names the next series, *A colleague that isn't a person*.

## The reference, and practice

The module's reference for oversight is Europe's AI law, read for what it asks of the people around a system; the film names it once, and teaches in plain words what a mandate gives those people. The map's notation says where the mandate lives, and the tools' own docs say what holds each line.

| Body of knowledge | What it adds | Where the film uses it |
|---|---|---|
| Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the AI Act, article 14 (human oversight of high-risk AI systems) | High-risk systems "can be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period in which they are in use" (14(1)); the people given oversight are able "to properly understand the relevant capacities and limitations" of the system and "duly monitor its operation" (14(4)(a)), "to disregard, override or reverse the output" (14(4)(d)), and to "interrupt the system through a 'stop' button or a similar procedure" (14(4)(e)) | *One page, on the map*: "Europe's AI law asks this of what it counts as high-risk: people who can understand it, watch it, reverse it and stop it." |

**Where the film adopts, extends or differs.** It adopts the law's four abilities for the people around an agent, in plain words (understand it, watch it, reverse it, stop it), and gives each a line of the page: what it may do and its limits (understand), its report and its measures (watch), the undo (reverse) and the switch (stop). It extends the law from oversight to delegation: the law asks that people can oversee a system while it's in use; a mandate also writes down, beforehand, the one kind of task the agent may do without asking, and where, signed by the person who answers for it and approved by a second person, and it is held by the tools, not by anyone's watching. It differs in scope: the law speaks of what it counts as high-risk, and the film doesn't say the workload agent is high-risk, nor give the law's dates, which have moved. ArchiMate has no element for a mandate, so the film writes it as the series writes every rule with a home: a requirement, in a folder of its own, named on the assignment it bounds, while the stage stays on that assignment, as *A place on the map* writes it. The products are named where the narration names them (Databricks' Unity Catalog and Unity Gateway, GitHub, dbt), each checked against its docs on the day; none is the subject, and the film never says a feature's release status.

**Practice, from the author (the plan's insights):** an agent works alone only after a track record, and only under a mandate its owner signs; in test, an agent may do agreed tasks alone and report; in production, it finds the cause and drafts the fix, and a person approves; a plain job reruns a build, never an agent; an agent never weakens a check to make it pass, and never approves; what a word means is its owner's to decide. A practitioner will recognise the night job that failed with nobody on call, the report waiting in the morning that says what was found and what was changed, the fix that never needed anyone's approval because it never left test, and the question that was a matter of definition, not of code, sent to the people who own the word.

## Sound

**The palette, its own** (reserved for this film by the series' thread on 5 November 2026, after checking every `score.py` on main and on every branch; the series' `palettes.md`): **drawn sound**, after the Oramics machine of the late 1950s and 1960s, whose player drew shapes by hand on strips of film, one for the wave and others for each note's pitch and loudness, and light read them: the machine played exactly what was drawn, nothing more, and stopped when the strip stopped, which is the film's idea of a mandate. Made in the film's own `tools/score.py`, since the shared instruments have none: each strip's wave is one period drawn by hand, its first six harmonics with uneven weights and phases, never quite a sine and never quite the same twice; each note's loudness is a line drawn by hand, up, along with a slight unevenness, and down. Nothing trembles or slides: a drawn line holds its pitch. **Its partner, a bass trumpet in E flat,** played softly and low, with no vibrato, made there too (its harmonics on a soft hump near 500 Hz, the upper ones growing as it swells, with the lips' soft start): it sounds only where a person signs, approves, decides or holds the switch, never for the agent, so a person's word is the one warm, breathing voice beside the drawn lines.

- **The film's mark is a line drawn, then played as drawn:** E flat, G, F and B flat, its last note cut when the strip ends, not faded. It sounds three times: as Anil writes the mandate, into the title, and on the end card. Once, as the series closes, 3.1's five stages' notes, drawn and played in E flat (E flat, F, G, A flat and B flat), sound as each gate passes.
- **Keys, all tuned pure** (just intonation from E flat): E flat major by day and wherever a person's word is heard; C minor, its relative, for the night in test. F is never held with C or A flat, which aren't pure together in this tuning, so no two notes beat.
- **Under the opening:** E flat major under the lab by day, the answer card and the way to production; C minor for the night, the failed build, nobody at work and the October night, with the stamp on HR's model and the teams' numbers; silence from "written nowhere" through "Something had to change", but for a soft tap as each of the three missing places comes; E flat again, and the mark, as Anil writes the page, a tap for each line, the mandate's line joining the map, a pen and the bass trumpet as he signs, and the bass trumpet again as Noor approves; the three tools; C minor again for the night in test, a gear for the plain check, one drawn note for each round, the fix, the report and a person's yes; and the mark into the title.
- **After the opening,** music only at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen, or in a pause without words, one drawn note or one person's note at a time: a low E flat as the agent is assigned to its role, the gate passed on the bass trumpet, each tool's line held, the written rule and the tutor who moved, HR's decision and Noor's approval on the bass trumpet, Sam and Anil at the switch, the night task's stage turned down from B flat to A flat as 3.1 turned it, Jun's merge and Noor's yes, the five stages' notes, the film's own lesson on B flat, the next series on an open D, and the mark on the end card, with silence when the strip ends.
