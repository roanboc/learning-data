# Who creates it, who owns it: questions and assumptions

*Enterprise architecture* · *Business architecture for data*, film 3 of 5 · PR #346 · [pull request](https://github.com/roanboc/learning-data/pull/346)

[Watch it on the site](https://data.learn-all.org/business-architecture-for-data/who-creates-it-who-owns-it/)

> Written by the film's thread on 12 November 2026, from the series' proposal the author confirmed and merged on 11 November 2026 (PR #318). Every question below would have gone to the author, and was answered instead with the best-reasoned option, for him to review when he watches the film.

| # | Question | Assumption taken | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which part of the qanat story does this film tell? | Who owns a qanat's water and who looks after it, as the proposal's row has it: the Vakilabad, dug at Mahan in the 1860s, its water owned in six shares by three men, one share given to the keeper of the town's shrine; shares sold, inherited and split until many qanats had two or three hundred owners; the owners deciding the repairs each year, voting by their shares; the mirab and his helpers looking after the water; and the Vakilabad's record of every owner, to the keeper's twenty descendants and some seventy families. All from Paul Ward English's account of qanat villages (1998, citing his 1989 work) | Chapter 1; `script.md`, *Opening story* and *Rigour* |
| 2 | How long after the 1860s did the keeper's share become a third? | English says the share "is now owned by twenty of his descendants" without a date, and his book on the Kerman basin, *City and Village in Iran*, came out in 1966, about a century after the qanat was dug. The film says "Generations later", true whichever date his "now" is, where an earlier draft said "More than a century later" | Chapter 1, the dial and its tag |
| 3 | Which shrine? | English names it: the shrine of Shah Ni'matullah Vali, in Mahan. The film says "the keeper of the town's shrine", so a newcomer needn't learn a name the story doesn't use again | Chapter 1 |
| 4 | Could a family really lose its crop if the water missed its turn? | English says the mirab guides the water "to the right field at the right time"; that a missed turn can cost a crop is the film's plain reading of irrigation in a desert, as *What the business owns* already says ("each family's crop lives or dies by when the water reaches it"). The village shows the third field's turn missed and its crop drying, as an example | Chapter 1, the village |
| 5 | Who paid the mirab? | English: the owners' meetings, votes cast by shares, decide the mirab's and his helpers' pay, in cash or in kind. The film says "They paid a water master" and shows "paid by the owners". English adds that in some small villages the water users do the mirab's work themselves; the film follows the mirab | Chapter 1 |
| 6 | Did every share have a named owner? | English says a complete record of changes in ownership exists for the Vakilabad. The film reads that as every share having a named owner, the story's benefit, and marks each share on the dial | Chapter 1, the dial |
| 7 | The proposal says two capabilities both create "the customer". Why the account? | The account is the concept on the series' map since *What the business owns* (an account belongs to the head of retail: someone who pays a bill), and it is what a new home's first bill goes to, so two accounts mean two first bills a family can see. A customer is a person, who may hold several accounts; the film doesn't go into it. The sound's figure follows the account too | Chapters 4 and 5 |
| 8 | Which concepts does the matrix hold, and what's in its squares? | Five of the map's concepts, the network's three (outage, connection point, meter) and retail's two (account, bill), against the eight capabilities of *The map before the data*. The squares are invented, as the people who run each capability would say them: operating the network creates and updates outages, connecting customers creates the connection point and the meter, maintaining assets updates and deletes meters, serving customers creates, reads and updates accounts and bills, and others read; generating power touches none of them. Connecting customers creates the account too, until the business decides | Chapter 3 |
| 9 | Who decides when two capabilities create one concept? | The owners of both capabilities, together: Grace and the head of retail. Not Tomás, not the data team, and not data governance, which steps in only where code and the glossary or an enterprise contract differ (AGENTS.md). Tomás brings the matrix. The decision goes on the map with its date (12 May, invented), as the proposal's practice says, so nobody settles it again | Chapters 4 and 5 |
| 10 | Is a concept's owner always the owner of the capability that creates it? | Yes, as the series' fourth insight says (TOGAF's matrices, Information Engineering). A word several domains share, like location, sits in a shared domain with its own owner (*What the business needs to know*); the film doesn't go into it | Chapter 3 |
| 11 | The utility's numbers? | Invented, as the series' world is: twelve hundred new homes with two accounts last year, three hundred and forty families with two first bills, 310,000 accounts, the outage table built in 2019 | Chapters 4, 6 and 7 |
| 12 | Who are the stewards? | Roles, not new people, so the series' cast stays as it is: retail's billing lead for accounts and bills, and Grace's connections lead, metering lead and outage desk lead for the connection point, the meter and outages. The film names each by role only | Chapters 6 to 9 |
| 13 | What does a steward do, and what doesn't a steward do? | Keeps the definition, answers questions about it and follows up when something's wrong (DAMA-DMBOK2's data steward; Seiner's stewards, who already work with the data). A steward doesn't own the concept and never reviews pull requests; nor does an owner (AGENTS.md). The film shows no pull request | Chapter 6 |
| 14 | An owner nobody wrote down: which concept? | Outages, from a table built in 2019 that Ama's yearly report to the regulator counts from, whose builder has left. Asking which capability creates outages finds the owner: operating the network does, so outages are Grace's, and her outage desk lead becomes their steward. The film's practice, from the proposal: the owner is always the business, hard to find where metadata is scarce | Chapter 7 |
| 15 | What does this film add to the data practice on the map? | The data owner and the data steward as roles on the business layer, held by each domain's owner (Grace for the network, the head of retail for retail) and the stewards each names. The processes where each concept is born wait for *Processes, as deep as data needs* | Chapter 8 |
| 16 | Which sound? | Nothing new fitted (the topic's `palettes.md` lists what the thread checked), so, as *What the business needs to know* does, this film pairs two instruments the site has but no film pairs, and no film of this topic plays: an English concertina (*No secret to lose*, with a prepared piano) and a clarinet (many films, never with a concertina). Kept apart from *No secret to lose*: no ladder of pressed chords and no sealed notes, and the concertina plays under a clarinet line, in D. Whether the four architecture series may do this when nothing new fits is already a question on the author's checklist | The soundtrack; `script.md`, *Sound* |
