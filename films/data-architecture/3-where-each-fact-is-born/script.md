## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has found the same thing recorded twice, and the two records disagreeing, can follow it, and an experienced data or enterprise architect finds nothing wrong in it. Every fact has a place where it's born: the one system where it's created, and corrected, which DAMA calls the system of record. Every other system that holds the fact holds a copy. A matrix of what each system does with each thing, creates it or copies it, finds the facts with no home and the facts born twice. Each fact then gets one home and one owner: the other systems ask for it and copy it, and a copy is read anywhere but corrected only at home. Each copy keeps a line back to where it was born (which system, which record, and when it was copied), its lineage, so a correction made once reaches every copy. Records that look like duplicates may be different facts, each with its own home, and the facts that have to be born in two places are master data's work. **Give each fact one home and one owner, and every copy a line back to it.**

## The story in one paragraph

By 1972, Cybersyn had a network: Chile's telex lines, being extended to every factory the state ran, along a country more than four thousand kilometres long, and some managers were won over by the offer of a free telex. But the software could only warn about a factory if it had that factory's own numbers, from that day. A figure from anywhere else would mislead it, last month's or a guess made in an office in Santiago: a bad day could look normal, and a mill short of cotton could wait for help that never came. Each number had to come from where it was made. So each factory sent its own numbers by telex, once a day, from the factory itself, though the team had to keep reminding managers to send them. Now each number had one place where it was born, the factory that made what it counted, and what Santiago read was a copy of what that factory had sent, the same day, where the statistics before had taken six months. At the utility, Inés finds a fact that's born in two places: when a family signs up for a new home, billing creates a premise for it, and when the home is connected, the network map creates a service point; last year, 214 of 1,160 new homes were created twice. At 14 Quarry Lane, billing created its premise in March, from the family's form, and the network map its service point in April, at Lot 14, the builder's name for the plot; each was copied, and when the power went out in June, the outage system saw a cut with no account, customer service found no cut at the family's address and told them to call an electrician, and Grace's measure counted no customer there. Every fact has a place where it's born, which DAMA calls the system of record; Inés's matrix of what each system creates or copies finds two facts born twice, the connection point and the customer. Grace agrees the connection point's home is the network map, which creates it with its ID as soon as a connection is planned; billing asks for one and copies it, the same day; the account's home is billing, owned by the head of retail. One fact, one home, one owner: a copy is read anywhere, but corrected only at home. Each copy keeps a line back to where it was born, its lineage, so when the network team corrects the address in July, every copy says the same by the next morning. One cut's three records, the meter's event, the family's call and the interruption, aren't three copies of one fact but three facts, each with its own home; and a customer, who can start in billing or in customer service, has to be born in both places, which is master data's work. In Chile, each number was born in its factory and Santiago read copies, knowing where each came from. At the utility, each fact now has one home and one owner: in October, when the power went out on Quarry Lane again, the family's call found the cut and Grace's measure counted them, and since July no new home has been created twice. But a fact doesn't stay at home: a meter reading replaced in the network has to reach billing. How does it travel?

## The reference, and practice

The series follows DAMA-DMBOK2's Data Architecture, and this film takes its words from three of its neighbours: the system of record from Reference and Master Data (chapter 10: "an authoritative system where data is created/captured, and/or maintained through a defined set of rules and expectations"), lineage from Metadata Management (chapter 12), and master data, where two systems have to create the same thing, from chapter 10 again, with identity resolution ("the same person?") and survivorship ("which value wins?") left to *The same one, everywhere*. Inés's matrix is the CRUD matrix of Information Engineering and TOGAF's application/data matrix (Phase C), cut to the two words a newcomer needs, creates or copies; the data practice on the map draws the result in ArchiMate, as the series does since *Between the business and the systems*: a business object, its owner, and the data objects that realise it. The industry's words follow the IEC Common Information Model, where a network's usage point and a service location are different things from a customer's account.

What practitioners would tell a newcomer, and the film shows: name each fact's home before anyone copies it, and ask "who creates it, and who corrects it?" rather than "where is it stored?"; draw the create/copy matrix across the systems that hold a thing, because two "creates" in a row is the bug report you haven't had yet; give the home an owner who agrees to it, and make the other systems ask for the fact and copy it, with its ID, instead of making their own; correct a copy only by correcting its home; record each copy's line back when it's made (which system, which record, when), not afterwards; before calling two records duplicates, ask what each one says and who saw it, since one cut recorded three ways can be three facts; and where two places really have to create the same thing, call it master data and match the records, rather than fight over the home.

## Sound

The film's palette (12 November 2026, its row in the four new architecture series' palettes register) pairs two instruments the site has, which no film pairs: **glass bars** (a crystallophone) and a **monochord**. The register allows a pairing where nothing new fits, and this one is an assumption in the film's assumptions file, with the question on the author's checklist. The glass bars are struck softly with yarn mallets, in their middle and upper notes (C 4 to G 5), with no resonator: a clear, cool note with the faint overtones of an uncut bar of glass, gone in a moment, and the small touch of the yarn. They spread each chord from the bass up under the opening story and at a chapter's turn, and give one note as a copy is made or kept, since the series draws every record as a card of glass. The monochord is one long gut string over a long box, its bridge set for each note, plucked softly with the finger at the string's middle, so its note is hollow and round: a string plucked at its middle has no even partials. It plays low (C 2 to G 3), one note at a time, where a fact is born, and under the series' mark. Kept apart from the two films that have them (*Context engineering*'s monochord, with a twelve-string guitar, and *Context and memory, governed*'s glass bars, with a baritone sarrusophone): the monochord here is plucked with the finger at its middle, low, and only where a fact is born, never quill-plucked or touched for a harmonic; the glass bars sit higher, uncut and dry. Everything is made in code, in [`source/tools/score.py`](source/tools/score.py): nothing is sampled.

Keys, tuned pure from C: C major, and A minor for what goes wrong (a bad day that looks normal; a fact born twice; the power out and the cut nobody matched); G major where something is needed or named; F major where the story opens a door (Cybersyn; master data, the next series). The series' mark, at the title, at the rule in *One home, one owner* and at the end card: C and its fifth, G, apart (the business and the systems), then the third between them, E, which makes the chord, with the monochord's low C under it. The film's own figure, *born once*: the monochord gives a note where a fact is born, and the glass bars ring its own harmonics above it while it still sounds (C 4, G 4 and C 5 over C 3), as copies born of it; once where the network map creates the connection point and billing copies it, and once at the end, where every copy has a line back to its home. Elsewhere a copy's glass note is a harmonic of the note its fact was born on: a copy of billing's premise (born on C 3) rings G 4, a copy of the network map's service point (born on A 2) rings E 4. Nothing from Chile stands for Cybersyn (no Andean flutes, no guitar, no dance rhythm), and no machine rhythm for the telex.

Music under the opening story; after it, only at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen or in a pause without words. Every effect is tied to something on screen, one at a time, soft and below about 2.4 kHz: the telex as one soft, muffled texture, a knock as a card arrives, paper as a strip comes in or the calendar's page turns, a pane of glass set down for a tag, a pencil on paper for the form, the matrix and a correction, a soft click for a cross or a cell turned to "copy", a low thud as Grace agrees, and a breath of air as things move together or a light runs along a line. `source/tools/quiet.py` (12 November 2026): after the opening the music sounds 32% of the time and comes in 24 times, once every 12.6 s, at most 4 times in any 30 s; its loud moments sit at -29.8 dB and its loudest notes at -25.8 dB; the effects' loud moments at -47.2 dB, at most 2 beginning in one second; nothing beats, nothing pulses, and no room is laid over the notes. ## Pause and think

The film stops at the end of three chapters, with one question each ([`site/assets/where-each-fact-is-born/think.en.js`](../../../site/assets/where-each-fact-is-born/think.en.js), Spanish in `think.es.js`):

## Labs

Candidates for the series' labs and scenarios pack, to build with the series:

- **Where is it born?** For a thing the learner's team uses (a customer, an order, a site), draw the create/copy matrix across the systems that hold it, and mark every row with no "creates" or two.
- **One home, one owner:** take one row with two "creates"; propose its home and its owner, say what the other system does instead (asks, then copies, with the ID), and who has to agree.
- **Follow a line back:** take a figure from a report and trace the records it counts to the system and record each was copied from, and when; find the copy nobody can trace.
- **Duplicates or facts?** Given three records of one event from three systems, say what each one says and who saw it, and whether any is a copy.
