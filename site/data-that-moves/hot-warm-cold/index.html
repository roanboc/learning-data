Until September, nobody at the university had asked how fresh each kind of data must be. Contract changes came in once a night, and 23 tutors were paid two weeks late; old pay records sat in the costliest storage, never deleted. Now each kind of data has its own temperature.

Go further in the official docs

The film shows the idea once. The docs say how to keep each temperature on the platform today: a stream for what's hot, a view on a schedule for what's warm, and the archive tier for what's cold.

  • Zerobus IngestA system pushes each change as it's made, straight into a table, with no message bus to run.
  • Streaming tablesTake in each row as it arrives, and keep what's hot fresh to the minute.
  • The AUTO CDC APIsApply each change a system sends, in order, to a table of how things stand.
  • Materialized viewsWork out an answer on a schedule, and keep it ready to read.
  • Databricks configurations in dbtA materialized view as a dbt model, with its schedule in its config.
  • Archival support in Azure DatabricksTell a table which files have gone to the archive tier, and find the ones a query needs back.
  • Access tiers for blob dataThe cloud's tiers, from hot to archive: what each costs to keep and to read, and how long it takes to bring back.
