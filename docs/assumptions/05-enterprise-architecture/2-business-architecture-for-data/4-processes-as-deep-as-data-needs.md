# Processes, as deep as data needs: questions and assumptions

*Enterprise architecture* · *Business architecture for data*, film 4 of 5 · PR #PRNUM · [pull request](https://github.com/roanboc/learning-data/pull/PRNUM)

[Watch it on the site](https://data.learn-all.org/business-architecture-for-data/processes-as-deep-as-data-needs/)

> Written by the film's thread on 12 November 2026, from the series' proposal the author confirmed and merged on 11 November 2026 (PR #318). Every question below would have gone to the author, and was answered instead with the best-reasoned option, for him to review when he watches the film.

| # | Question | Assumption taken | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which part of the qanat story does this film tell? | How a qanat's turns were kept, and how deep, as the proposal's row has it ("a cycle of days, each owner's turn within it, timed with a water clock, mapped as deep as it takes to settle disputes"): Mahan's cycle of twelve days, each day fifteen shares; another qanat's twelve dividers; a turn missed leaving a field dry, and lots drawn for a place in the cycle where turns were disputed; the cycle kept in days, shares and sixteen-minute parts, down to single minutes where a share was split; and the mirab's water clock. All from Paul Ward English's account of qanat villages (1998, citing his 1989 study, "The Qanats of Mahan") | Chapter 1; `script.md`, *Opening story* and *Rigour* |
| 2 | Is "a cycle of twelve days, and each day was fifteen shares of time" right? | English: one of Mahan's four qanats "is divided into fifteen sahms ... on a twelve day rotation", each sahm "ninety-six minutes of water once every twelve days". Fifteen times ninety-six minutes is a day, so the film says each day was fifteen shares, as *What the business needs to know* drew Mahan's day of water | Chapter 1, the ring and the dial |
| 3 | English says each share is six dangs, "each composed of six habbeh", yet a dang is sixteen minutes and a habbeh a single minute. Which does the film follow? | The minutes, which English states twice: each share in six parts of sixteen minutes, and single minutes where a share was split. The film names no units, so a newcomer needn't learn words it doesn't use again, and the levels show a part as sixteen single minutes | Chapter 1, the levels |
| 4 | Did Mahan's mirab use a water clock? | English describes the water clock (a tas, a metal cup with a hole in its base that sinks in a container of water) for the plateau's villages in the past, not for Mahan by name, and UNESCO's nomination of the Persian qanat says the mirab traditionally distributed the water with one. The film shows it with Mahan's mirab, as the way turns were timed | Chapter 1, the water clock |
| 5 | Where were lots drawn? | In "other very small settlements" where water rights were disputed, on the autumnal equinox (English). The film says "some small villages drew lots each autumn", tags the panel "some small villages · each autumn", and doesn't place it at Mahan | Chapter 1, the lots |
| 6 | When is the story? | "In the twentieth century": English's fieldwork in the Kerman basin (*City and Village in Iran*, 1966; "The Qanats of Mahan", 1989). He adds, in 1998, that clocks had by then replaced the water clocks; the film doesn't say when | Chapter 1 |
| 7 | APQC has five levels; the author's method has four. Which does the film draw? | The author's four (the proposal, *How processes are drawn*), "drawn from APQC's process framework, which has a version for utilities", not presented as APQC's own: level one, the macro process, covers APQC's category and process group. *How value reaches people* used levels loosely, with restore supply as one process at level two under the map; here restore supply is a macro process at level one, under the author's method. Whether that earlier film should change is left to the author | Chapter 4; `script.md`, *Rigour* |
| 8 | The four bands and the thirteen macro processes? | The bands are the author's (strategic, operational, support, evaluation), a common convention often used with ISO 9001's process approach, which doesn't prescribe them. The thirteen are the utility's own, invented to fill every band (three, three, four and three): set direction, plan the network, manage risk; connect a home, restore supply, meter to cash; people, finance, technology, fleet and stores; measure reliability, audit, improve. The operational band's three follow the value streams of *How value reaches people*, as the author's method fills that band from the value stream | Chapters 3 and 4 |
| 9 | Meter to cash's five processes, and their SIPOC? | Invented, in the industry's usual shape: read meters, check readings, make bills, collect payments, answer billing questions. The SIPOC drawn is check readings' (supplied by read meters, readings in and out, to make bills), with the author's trigger (each billing day) and owner (the metering lead) | Chapter 5 |
| 10 | Who owns what: the process or the concept? | Two roles. The metering lead owns check readings, the process, as its one person accountable; Grace owns readings, the concept, as the meter is hers (*Who creates it, who owns it*, where the metering lead is the meter's steward). The film says "Grace, who owns readings" and shows "owner: metering lead" only on the SIPOC | Chapters 5 and 7 |
| 11 | The payment, not on the map: who owns it? | The film says only that it wasn't on the map, and where it's born. By the series' fourth insight its owner is the owner of the capability that creates it, likely the head of retail, as collecting payments is retail's work; the film doesn't say, and leaves it for the author | Chapter 5 |
| 12 | How are readings checked and estimated? | The utility's own rule, simplified: each reading checked for range and that it's no lower than the last; one that fails, or never came, replaced by an estimate from the same month last year. In line with utilities' validation, editing and estimation of meter readings (the CPUC working group's monthly rules; Oncor's tutorial for ERCOT, 2013), named in *Rigour*, not on screen | Chapters 6 and 7 |
| 13 | What do the owners decide about the estimate, and who asks the data team? | Grace, who owns readings, decides a reading is what the check sends on, read from the meter or estimated; the head of retail, who owns bills, decides each bill says if it used an estimate. The glossary's entry for meter reading gets Grace's line first, and a ticket from Grace and the head of retail asks the data team to take its readings from the check (AGENTS.md: definitions start in the glossary; business users ask in tickets). The film shows no pull request, and data governance doesn't step in, since code and glossary don't differ | Chapter 7 |
| 14 | The utility's numbers? | Invented, as the series' world is: complaints about bills doubled last winter, two in three about estimated bills, 4,120 households with estimates this winter, 380 of them for four weeks or more; the meters, days and readings in the table | Chapters 6 and 7 |
| 15 | What does this film add to the data practice on the map? | The data team's own processes, answering a data request, building a data product and taking on a source, at level two, each with its trigger, output, owner and what it reads; the first one's card names a ticket, an answer and the data team lead. None goes deeper until something hurts | Chapter 8 |
| 16 | Which sound? | Nothing new fitted (the topic's `palettes.md` lists what the thread checked), so, as *What the business needs to know* and *Who creates it, who owns it* do, this film pairs two instruments the site has but no film pairs, and no film of this topic plays: a cylinder music box (*Off the shelf*, under a brass choir) and a cello (*Who decides*, with a vibraphone, and many master data and governance films). Kept apart from *Off the shelf*: no brass, and the music box gives single notes only at the finest level. Whether the four architecture series may do this when nothing new fits is already a question on the author's checklist | The soundtrack; `script.md`, *Sound* |
| 17 | *Four bands* runs at 117 words a minute, under the 120 to 135 the films aim for. Keep it? | Yes: the chapter gives Grace's map time to be drawn band by band, each band lit as it's named and two emptied to show a finding; the film runs at 131 words a minute overall, and no chapter goes over 140 | Chapter 3; `script.md`, *Pacing report* |
| 18 | Labs for the series? | None yet, as in the series' other films: the series' lab comes with the platform series' last film, as the proposal plans, so the Pause and think questions link to no lab | `site/assets/processes-as-deep-as-data-needs/think.en.js` |
| 19 | Spanish captions: which words? | Latin American Spanish, as the series' other films: la empresa, comercialización and quien dirige comercialización (never given a gender), equipo de datos, dueño, ticket; una parte (a share) and una porción (its sixteen-minute part), el mirab, partidores (the dividers); the process words of *The map before the data*: mapa de procesos, estratégicos, operativos, de apoyo, de evaluación, restablecer el suministro, del medidor al cobro; franjas (bands), macroproceso, disparador and resultado (trigger and output), and la persona que rinde cuentas (the one person accountable), never responsable, which is the steward's word. Two very short lines run outside the usual length: "Those near the end of it usually got none" (a little longer in Spanish) and "If it passes, it goes to billing" (a little shorter), each still a few words | `source/src/i18n/es/captions.js` |
| 20 | *Rules with a home* already marks every reading actual or estimated (RUL-020), and *A map people and agents can read* shows its column, read_type. What was missing, then? | Where the data team took its readings from. The estimate is born in the check, after the meter, so tables loaded from the meters held no estimate at all, and the rows of a meter that stopped sending were simply missing. The film's table names the column read_type, every row "actual", until the data team takes its readings from the check and the estimates fill the empty rows. The column is there before the fix, as those two films have it; only where the rows come from changes | Chapter 7; `script.md`, *Rigour* |
