| 1 | Which part of the story does this film take? | Alternate attendance on the Tōkaidō. From 1635 the shogunate made the lords live in Edo every other year, so every year each lord made the journey, with about a hundred people or, for the greatest, two thousand or more. Each Tōkaidō station kept a hundred porters and a hundred horses, and an inn for lords; a procession arriving unannounced would find too few. So the lord's officials planned the journey end to end: who went, within the limits for his rank, and a notice sent ahead to every station (*sakibure*) with the porters and horses, the day, and where the lord would rest and sleep; a board with his name (*sekifuda*) went up at the inn a day or two before; each station called on nearby villages (*sukegō*) in time. It stands for one solution designed end to end on the platform everyone shares, with every team told ahead. The plan's line was "a domain lord's journey to Edo, planned station by station on the shared road", and the film keeps to it. Checked against Kotobank (*Sekai daihyakka jiten*, *Nihon daihyakka zensho*, *Seisenban Nihon kokugo daijiten*, Yamakawa, Britannica, *Mypedia*). | *A notice sent ahead*, *Three architects*, *Most of it is already there*, *Told ahead*, *Each journey, planned* |
| 2 | What does the film leave unsaid? | The exceptions to "every other year" (Tsushima every third year, the Kantō lords every half year, Mito always in Edo); the shogunate's numbers for each rank (1721), said only as "the limits the shogunate sets for his rank"; that the stations' porters and horses served official travel first, at set rates, and others for hire, said as "for everyone on the road", meaning shared rather than any one lord's; and the heavy burden the helper villages came to carry, which the series' last film can name among what the story didn't achieve. "A procession of three hundred, arriving unannounced" is the film's own example of what would happen, not a recorded event: no source gives how often it did. | *A notice sent ahead* |
| 3 | Is the data story believable? | Yes, and it is *When strategy moves*' own: the government's direction in November, the discount from the first of July with no forms, the social services agency that knows who is eligible, the three rules already agreed, the tenants whose account is in a landlord's name left for a second route, and about nine in ten eligible households on the first bills. This film designs it on the platform. Three teams starting three pieces at once (billing's vendor, the data team's own table, IT's server) is common in practice; the film says it blamelessly, as each team doing what it could see from where it stood. | *Three pieces*, *Everything it touches*, *The first of July* |

| 7 | Why does the voice say only "a standard"? | Plain words: the voice says "A standard lists nine", and the screen names it once, *ISO/IEC 25010*, over its nine qualities as the 2023 edition lists them (functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, interaction capability, reliability, security, maintainability, flexibility and safety). | 8 | Is the decision record right? | Yes, as practitioners keep one: a short record kept with the solution's code, with the decision, the option considered, why, and the consequence, and its status, accepted (Michael Nygard's record, with MADR's options considered). | 9 | Does the second option hold together? | Yes: the agency's answers stay on the data platform as a data product of retail's, the business that holds the customers, so the principle holds; billing asks the integration platform's gateway before each run for only what a bill needs, on the tariff and from which date, which matches *When strategy moves*' "only billing reads it". The catalogue checks and logs every read. Its risk, billing depending on the platform, is handled by asking the day before each run and keeping what it got, and that is what saves the September bills. | *Two options*, *The first of July* |
| 10 | Is the practice map right in ArchiMate? | The new parts are in the application layer beside billing: the exchange (a component), the answers (a data object), the discount step (a function of billing) and the letter; the data platform's technology services serve them, reused, with one new one, sending a file out, and the integration platform's gateway serves billing. A work package builds the new parts and triggers an event, ready on the first of July: ArchiMate 4 has one event element for every layer, in place of version 3.2's implementation event (checked in two guides to version 4; the licensed specification wasn't read). Each quality is a requirement, realised by the parts that meet it. | *The data practice on the map* |
