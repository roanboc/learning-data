## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has found data moving between teams by a path nobody remembers making can follow it, and an experienced data or enterprise architect finds nothing wrong in it. When a fact crosses from one domain to another, three things are chosen: what crosses, in which shape, and under which contract. What crosses is an agreed list: what the reader needs and may see, and nothing else. The shape is one of the three DAMA names: point to point, hub and spoke, or publish and subscribe; which one fits depends on three questions: who reads it, when they act on it, and what each may see. The contract is agreed by the teams at the flow's two ends: what crosses and what each part means, how soon, what happens when one is late or wrong, and how it changes. A path nobody drew has no owner at either end, so every path across a boundary goes on the map, with its shape, its contract and an owner at each end, or it's closed. **Let every fact cross on purpose: drawn, with its shape and its contract.**

## The story in one paragraph

In October 1972, Cybersyn's telex lines ran from the north of Chile to the south, and every afternoon each factory sent its numbers along them, one way, to Santiago. That month, truck owners went on strike against a plan for a state trucking company, and shopkeepers, bank clerks and others joined them. With most trucks standing still, shops and factories across the country could run out of essential goods. A report once a day, one way, couldn't keep goods moving: the government needed to hear, as it happened, where goods were short and which trucks could still run. So Fernando Flores, who had started Cybersyn at CORFO, set up an emergency operations centre in Santiago, where the Cybersyn team and officials read about two thousand telexes a day, from all over the country, and answered them. The same lines now carried new messages, many times a day, in both directions, and about two hundred trucks still running were sent where they were needed most, against the effects of forty thousand on strike. At the utility, Inés lists every path that crosses the wall between the network and retail: eleven, and only four are on the map. One is a link into the meter system's database, made years ago so the complaints team could check a reading during a call; it shows every connection point's readings, the 230,000 served by other retailers among them, which is what the wall is there to stop. A path nobody drew has no owner at either end: in March, a nightly script wrote for months to a queue nothing read, and about 1,900 bills went uncorrected. In November, the Mendozas' meter at 14 Quarry Lane stopped sending readings for nine days while a radio mast was mended; the network estimated those days, and billing used the estimate; when the meter came back, it sent what it had stored, and the network replaced the estimate. The replacement has to cross the wall, and the agreed list says what may cross: five things, to each retailer only for the connection points it serves, the group's own retailer no differently, and nothing else from the meter. How it travels is a choice too: DAMA names three shapes, and which one fits depends on who reads it, when they act on it, and what each may see. The replaced reading has many readers, billing acting at the next bill and customer service during a call, and each retailer may see only its own customers'; so the network publishes each one once, when it's replaced, each retailer receives only its own, within the hour, and the complaints team's link is closed. The flow gets a contract, agreed by the two teams at its ends, Grace's and billing's, with nobody else's sign-off, since it keeps to the agreed list. On the data practice on the map, the flow is drawn across the wall, with its shape, its contract and an owner at each end; five of the eleven paths become flows, each with its contract, and the other six are closed. In Chile, the lines didn't change; what crossed them did. At the utility, every fact that crosses the wall now crosses on purpose: on 29 November the Mendozas' estimate was replaced at 10:14, and billing had it a minute later; their December bill charged for what they really used, and nobody had to call; that month, 2,140 estimates were replaced, all but nine within the hour, and those nine the same day. But who owns which data, and who answers for what's made from it?

The series follows Cybersyn in all six films (the author, 11 November 2026): this film tells the October strike and the emergency operations centre; how little of the design was built, and its end, come in the last film.

## The reference, and practice

The series follows DAMA-DMBOK2's Data Architecture, and this film takes its words from its neighbour, Data Integration and Interoperability (chapter 8): its three interaction models, point to point, hub and spoke, and publish and subscribe, and its question of latency, how soon a reader needs a change, from batch to near real time. DMBOK2 asks for data sharing agreements that say what is exchanged and how it may be used; today's practice writes them as data contracts (A. Jones, *Driving Data Quality with Data Contracts*, 2023; the Open Data Contract Standard, Bitol, Linux Foundation), and the film's contract keeps the four terms practitioners would expect first: what crosses and what each part means, how soon, what happens when it's late or wrong, and how it changes, additively first and a new version last, as the example project's contracts do (decided 20 October). Under the site's rules for contracts (the author, 12 November 2026), a contract between two teams is agreed team to team, with no agreement from data governance; only a canonical table's enterprise contract is written by data governance's information architect, and this flow isn't one. The data practice on the map draws the flow in ArchiMate, as the series does since *Between the business and the systems*: one business object realised by two data objects, its home and its copy, and a flow between them, named with its shape and its contract. The wall is the utility's, from *The map before the data*: in many markets, rules keep a regulated network business apart from its retailer, limit what customer information it may share, and require it to treat every retailer alike.

What practitioners would tell a newcomer, and the film shows: before designing a new flow, list every path that already crosses the boundary, including the database links, scripts, copied tables and spreadsheets nobody drew, because each one is a flow with no owner and no contract; agree what crosses as a list, field by field, and send nothing else, above all across a legal or a privacy boundary; choose the shape from who reads it, when they act on it, and what each may see, not from what's quickest to build, since a point-to-point link is quick and each new reader adds another; ask how soon each reader really acts, because near real time costs more than within the hour, and nobody needs it sooner than they act; let the two teams at a flow's ends agree its contract, with what happens when a message is late or wrong written down before it happens, and correct at home, never in the copy; and close a path when its flow replaces it, or it will still be there in five years.

## Sound

The film's palette (12 November 2026, its row in the four new architecture series' palettes register) pairs two instruments the site has, which no film pairs: an **autoharp** and a **euphonium**. The register allows a pairing where nothing new fits, and this one is an assumption in the film's assumptions file, with the question on the author's checklist. The autoharp has fifteen strings, the F major scale from F 3 to F 5, strummed slowly across with a felt pick, from the bass up, one chord bar held down: the pick crosses every string, but only the strings of the pressed chord ring, and the bar's felts damp the rest to a faint touch. That is the film's idea in one instrument: the home sends each change once, and each reader receives only what it may. It strums at a chapter's turn and under the opening story, and picks one string, alone, under a moment on screen; in the opening, it strums up and back down, as the same lines carried messages both ways. The euphonium plays alone, soft and low (F 2 to C 4), plain held notes at one level, no vibrato, where a message starts: Santiago answering in the opening, and the network's meter data at the utility. Kept apart from the films that have them (*Skills and workflows*' autoharp, keeping a cornett's phrase; the euphoniums of *How long, and then what*, with drawers, and of *Governed in Unity Catalog*, in a low brass choir): the autoharp is strummed slowly, only where something is sent, and never under a cornett; the euphonium plays alone and low, never in a choir. Both play dry, with no room round them. Everything is made in code, in [`source/tools/score.py`](source/tools/score.py): nothing is sampled.

Keys, tuned pure from F: F major; D minor and A minor for what goes wrong (the strike, goods running out, what the wall is there to stop, the queue nothing read); C major where something reaches those who need it, or a door opens (the trucks sent where they're needed most, every retailer alike, *Data that moves*); B flat major at a chapter's turn. G and D never sound together, and there's no G minor chord, which isn't pure in this tuning. The series' mark, at the title and at the end card: F and its fifth, C, apart (the business and the systems), then the third between them, A, which makes the chord, each one string of the autoharp, with the euphonium's low F under it. The film's own figure, *published once*: the euphonium gives one note at the home, and while it holds, one slow strum crosses every string and only the chord's strings ring; where the network publishes each replaced reading once, under the light that runs along the flow on the map, and at the end, as the Mendozas' replaced reading reaches billing a minute later. In *Three shapes*, the two come apart: the euphonium as the home lights, the strum as its change goes. Nothing from Chile stands for Cybersyn (no Andean flutes, no charango, no rhythm of a dance), no truck or engine sounds as music, and no machine rhythm for the telex.

Music under the opening story; after it, only at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen or in a pause without words. Every effect is tied to something on screen, one at a time, soft and below about 2.4 kHz: the telex as one soft, muffled texture, a knock as a card arrives, a pane of glass set down for a tag, paper as a strip comes in or the calendar's page turns, a pencil for the list and the contract's terms, a soft click for a cross or a tick, and a breath of air as the camera moves or a light runs along a line. `source/tools/quiet.py` (12 November 2026): after the opening the music sounds 28% of the time and comes in 19 times, once every 17.8 s, at most 4 times in any 30 s; its loud moments sit at -28.9 dB and its loudest notes at -27.1 dB; the effects' loud moments at -48.8 dB, at most 3 beginning in one second; nothing beats, nothing pulses, and no room is laid over the notes. The mix is at -16 LUFS.

## Pause and think

The film stops at the end of three chapters, with one question each ([`site/assets/how-it-travels/think.en.js`](../../../site/assets/how-it-travels/think.en.js), Spanish in `think.es.js`):

- **After *Paths nobody drew*:** why nobody noticed the nightly script writing for months to a queue nothing read. Right: it wasn't on the map, so it had no owner at either end to see that one end had changed. Near miss: it was on the map, but the owner at the network's end forgot to tell billing (it wasn't on the map: only four of the eleven were).
- **After *Published once*:** why the network publishes each replaced reading once, instead of wiring each retailer's systems to meter data. Right: it has many readers, who act on it at different times, and each retailer may see only its own. Near miss: billing needs each one within seconds (within the hour is soon enough: nobody acts on it sooner).
- **After *Under a contract*:** what the flow's contract says happens when a replaced reading reaches billing late, and wrong. Right: the network's team is told that day, and corrects it at home, never in billing's copy. Near miss: billing corrects its own copy and tells the network's team afterwards (a copy is corrected only at home).

## Labs

Candidates for the series' labs and scenarios pack, to build with the series:

- **Every path across the wall:** for a boundary the learner's team works across (two domains, two companies, a team and its suppliers), list every path data takes across it: links, scripts, files, exports, spreadsheets; mark the ones on the map, and the ones with no owner at either end.
- **What crosses:** for one flow, write its agreed list, part by part, from what the reader needs and may see; name what stays behind.
- **Pick the shape:** given a fact's readers, when each acts on it and what each may see, choose point to point, hub and spoke, or publish and subscribe, and say how soon is soon enough.
- **Write the contract:** for one flow, write what crosses and what each part means, how soon, what happens when one is late or wrong, and how it changes; and say who agrees it, and who doesn't need to.
