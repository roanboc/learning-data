One thread: a tutor's contract, from HR to Finance

Linh Nguyen tutors HI101 this semester, on a contract born in the HR system, which sends each change as it's made and its whole file again each teaching period. Payroll pays her every second Thursday, and Finance asks what casual teaching costs. Film by film, the team moves that data the simplest way the tools offer, agrees the contract at each interface, sets how fresh each part must be, keeps one copy on one platform, governed once, and keeps each part where it's read, for as long as the law asks.

Every film opens somewhere else, at another time

FilmOpens withStill true today
Moving is easy, agreeing is the workAlbury, New South Wales, 1883 to 1962: two gauges at one stationThe trains always ran; what took 79 years was agreeing the gauge. Once it was agreed, freight and people passed straight through.
Hot, warm, coldAachen and Brussels, 1850: Reuter's pigeons carry stock prices across a gap in the telegraphA price is fresh enough when it arrives before someone has to act on it. Data is the same: cold, warm or hot, as the action it serves needs.
One copy, one platformAmsterdam, 1609: the Wisselbank's one ledgerMerchants stopped carrying coin: a payment became a line moved in one ledger, and the same ledger showed what each one held. One copy of the data can serve everyone the same way.
Read often, kept closeSvalbard, 2008 to 2015: a seed vault, and a gene bank that asked for its copies backSeeds in use are kept close and ready; their copies sleep, frozen far away, until the day they're needed. Data can be kept the same way: close when it's read often, cheaply when it's rarely read.
