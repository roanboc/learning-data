# A plan in ninety days · Every area, one job: script

*The script of *Every area, one job*, film 1 of 4 of *A plan in ninety days*, the first series of the module *Data management*, in six chapters, in English, drawn in the module's sunlit plaster. Written on 10 November 2026 from the series' plan (`../proposal.md`), the module's numbers and this film's brief in the module's working folder. Made on assumptions while the author was away; this film's questions and assumptions are in the module's working folder (`/mnt/project-files/data-management/1-every-area-one-job/assumptions.md`), for the author's final checklist.*

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone new to data work can follow it, and a data manager, an enterprise architect or a practitioner of any area on DAMA-DMBOK's wheel finds nothing wrong in it. As the module's opening film, it says once what the module is: one data manager's first year, every area of data at once, and the management work around it. Its own idea: a data manager answers for every area at once, and nobody is expert in all of them; the job is to see the areas as one system, know who is deep in each, and decide where to start. The way in is a plan for the first ninety days, on one page, in three lanes run side by side, so today's work keeps going while she learns: don't change everything on day one, and don't wait ninety days to help.

**Takeaway:** "See every area as one job; plan ninety days in three lanes."

## The story in one paragraph

In August 2010 the rock gives way in the San José mine, in Chile's Atacama desert, and 33 miners are trapped about 700 metres down with food for two or three days, while their families wait at the gate not knowing if they are alive. The rescue needs drilling, medicine, food, engineering and the families' trust, all at once, and any one plan could fail: so each part has someone who knows it, underground the shift foreman holds the men together, and three big drills work at once, each a different way. On day 17 a narrow drill comes back with a note, "We are well in the shelter, the 33"; on 13 October, after 69 days, all 33 come up. A data manager's work is calmer, but it's shaped the same way. On Monday 1 February 2027, Helena Novak starts as the university's data manager with 46 open requests. She sorts them on DAMA-DMBOK's wheel and sees that most touch three or four areas, so none is someone else's; she finds who on her team is deep in each area, and writes down where no one is; and by Friday her first ninety days are on one page, in three lanes (people, strategy, operations), with dates she can be held to. Everyone waiting hears where their request stands.

## The reference, and practice

The module's reference for the job's areas is DAMA-DMBOK (the module's *Decided* table, 5 November 2026); the film names it once, with its wheel, and borrows one shape for the first ninety days.

| Body of knowledge | What the film takes | Where |
|---|---|---|
| DAMA-DMBOK, second edition (revised 2024), DAMA International | The wheel: data governance at the centre, ten areas around it, from architecture and modelling to quality and security; a data manager answers for all of them | *One wheel*: "the wheel in DAMA-DMBOK, the Data Management Association's body of knowledge" |
| Michael Watkins, *The First 90 Days* (2003; updated 2013) | A plan for a new leader's first months: learn fast, and secure early wins | *Ninety days, three lanes*: "she borrows a shape from Michael Watkins's book" |
| The module's own loop (its *Decided* table): discover, define, plan | The strategy lane's three steps, each ending on a date | *Ninety days, three lanes* |

Where it adopts, extends or differs: it adopts DAMA-DMBOK's wheel as the map of the areas, as the module does, and counts requests on it, which the body of knowledge doesn't do; it takes from Watkins only the shape (learn first, early wins), not the book's transition types or its talks with the boss; the three lanes run side by side, people, strategy and operations, as practitioners plan a new data manager's first months, so delivery doesn't stop while the strategy is made.

**From practice** (the module's brief, in the author's emphasis): keep today's work running while you learn; put the plan on one page, in three lanes; set milestones you can be held to; promise no fix before you've listened; learn each area's words, and who on the team is deep in each; write down where no one is.
