| 1 | Which part of the story does this film take? | The checkpoint at Hakone, on the Tōkaidō, by Lake Ashi. After a century of war the shogunate kept the lords' wives and children in Edo as hostages, and on the roads it feared a family slipping out and guns going in. Around 1619 it set the checkpoint, whose gate every traveller passed through by day; a woman leaving Edo showed a pass from Edo Castle that described her, a woman examiner checked her against it, and nobody could go round by a side road. Most men needed no pass, so the road stayed open; it stood until 1869. It stands for an identity of one's own, checked every time, with nobody going round, while everyone else goes on. The plan also offered permits checked at each station before horses were given; the film keeps to the checkpoint, the stronger image, and leaves the stations to the films before it. Checked against the Hakone Checkpoint's own pages, Kotobank (Yamakawa, *Nihon daihyakka zensho*, *Seisenban Nihon kokugo daijiten*) and Japanese Wikipedia. | *A pass that matches*, *Who is asking*, *Only what it needs*, *Checked where the data is*, *Nobody goes round* |
| 2 | What does the film leave unsaid? | The founding date: the checkpoint's own site gives about 1619, "it is said", and Wikipedia about 1618, so the film says "Around 1619". The punishment for going round: the sources give crucifixion for the offender, and the film says "punished severely". Guns: another checkpoint, Arai, mainly watched for them, so the film never says Hakone searched for guns, only that "the checkpoint looks hardest at what the shogunate fears". Not found, so not said: a file of seals compared at the gate, a register of everyone who passed, and how many travellers passed. | *A pass that matches* |
| 3 | Is the data story's account believable? | data_loader is invented, but common in practice: one account made in a platform's first month, run by 47 jobs and four people's laptops, its key in a script with no end date, able to read and change every table. The film says it blamelessly: a new account took a ticket and two weeks, and data_loader worked at once. | *One account, every door* |
| 4 | Who asks, and who checks? | The regulator asks the utility; Ama Owusu, the regulatory lead, asks the data team in a ticket, and checks the answer before she closes it. No business user reviews a pull request: every pull request is checked for keys by the data team's tools, and reviewed by the data team. | *One account, every door*, *A name on every line* |
| 5 | Are the numbers real? | No, invented: the questions in March and June, 47 jobs, four people, six weeks to switch data_loader off, ten minutes for the second answer, two billing jobs and one analyst, and twelve new jobs since June. | Throughout |
| 6 | Does "a pass that lasts an hour" hold? | Yes, as a plain word for a short-lived token: in Databricks, a service principal's OAuth access token is valid for one hour, and a job that runs on the platform as its service principal, or outside it with token federation, keeps no secret of its own. The film names no product and says "pass", the story's word, for both. | *Who is asking* |
| 7 | Is "checked where the data is" right? | Yes: the catalogue checks every request, every time, before a row is read (in Databricks, Unity Catalog), and nobody reads the files under the tables without going through it, which means few people may reach the cloud storage directly, as Databricks' own best practices ask. The film says it as a rule; how it's built is *Locks before the data*'s. | *Checked where the data is* |
| 8 | Is encryption "set once by the platform"? | Yes, as the platform team's job: data at rest and traffic to the platform are encrypted, and what isn't by default, such as traffic between a cluster's workers, or keys the organisation holds itself, the platform team turns on once, for every domain. | *Checked where the data is* |
| 9 | Why never say "zero trust"? | The phrase has come to name products; the film says what it asks instead: being on the network proves nothing, and every request is checked. NIST SP 800-207 is named in the script as the reference, with Saltzer and Schroeder's principles, TOGAF, ArchiMate, DAMA-DMBOK and CWE-798. | *Checked where the data is*; the script |
| 10 | Is the practice map right in ArchiMate? | The data platform's security is technology services, in the technology layer: sign-in on the identity provider, a node of its own, and the access check by the catalogue, the secret store and the audit log on the data platform; billing's and network's data products and a job from the template use them; the rule is a principle in the motivation layer. | *The data practice on the map* |
