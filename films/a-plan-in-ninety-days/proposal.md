## The brief

The author, on 5 November 2026, at 11:51, asking for this module and the films of two others: "Remember data management should be oriented to data managers that need to cover all these different areas, including also standard management skills, presenting business cases for data initiatives, risks, benefits, and all the standard elements for defining strategy, implementing it, measure it and iterate while keeping the team engaged and stakeholders happy. It's a job requiring multiple skills and consideration of all the different domains we have established in the modules so far. But I think the key for me is enterprise architecture or general design of business, solutions, and data, among the leadership style and agile focus (in the sense of prototype and iterate fast)." In the same message he moved the new skills, soft skills, leadership, ownership and performance to a module of their own, *Leading data people*, and asked for "all the content labeled as draft and then I will be revisiting and fixing elements". At 11:53, away for the night: "Work on the films with assumptions and merge them all. Give me a final checklist with the questions you had for each topic and the assumption and I will later review the entire report and will fix when I see the assumption was incorrect." At 12:02: "Merge everything with a draft label, that's the release per film as it's get built".

So this plan was made without the usual questions. Each question he would have been asked (the reference, his own practice, the world, the look, the films) is answered with the best-reasoned option, and recorded with the assumption taken in the module's `assumptions.md`, in the project's shared files, for his review. Where he has said something before that applies, the plan follows it: people's engagement first (27 October 2026); "If you are struggling with those, you probably need to improve either your governance or your tooling" (5 November 2026); agents never approve, and nothing reaches production without a person and a second approver (3 November 2026). He shared a presentation of his and his public profile for perspective: they shaped the emphasis (a first ninety days in three lanes; discover, define, plan; the generalist; prototype and iterate), and nothing in them is shown or named.

**The author's advice for technical managers (10 November 2026).** He sees a technical manager as both a product owner and a people leader, and "product owners benefit from architectural background": run the services you manage as a business with suppliers and customers, and design every layer (strategy, business, information, application, technology) for them; "visualization everywhere"; one git repository for the architecture, a single source of truth that everything else comes from, built on standards first; architecture updated every time, as a shared capability of the team; and a preferred platform architecture with decision trees for the providers that can't reach it, because "delivering value to the business is what matters". His words are in the new series' proposal, [`../architecture-that-keeps-up/proposal.md`](../architecture-that-keeps-up/proposal.md); his people-leader advice went to the leading data people module. So the module has a fourth series, *Architecture that keeps up*, three films, third on the site; *Listen first* and *Design it simple* use his five layers, and every film draws a picture beside each page.

**The module's promise.** A data manager answers for every area of data at once, and for the management work around it: strategy, money, risk, people and delivery. The job is done well by designing the business, the solutions and the data together, by planning in stages and proving each one with something real in front of people, and by keeping the people who use data and the people who make it at the table. Each film gives one practice a newcomer can use on Monday, shown in a new data manager's first year.

**What the site already has, and what's new here.** Each of the site's modules goes deep in one area of the job: modelling, analytics engineering, quality, architecture, master and reference data, governance, protection, platforms, agents. This module is the job that joins them: where to start, how to decide what comes first, how to pay for it, how to bring people along and how to deliver and improve. It names the earlier films by title where the work touches them, and doesn't teach them again. The leading data people module, made at the same time, follows the same data manager and team day to day, from the person's side; the two never teach the same thing.

## Practical advice

Practitioners' advice, for the author to confirm or correct (each film's own is in its brief):

- Keep today's work running while you learn the place; don't promise fixes before you've listened.
- Put every plan on one page: the ninety days, the strategy, the case, the measures.
- Start from the decisions people make, not from the systems or the tools.
- Design for the business, the solutions and the data together; draw the target in one picture.
- Plan in stages that each give people something they use; date the near ones firmly and the far ones loosely.
- Always show the option of carrying on as now; count running costs; give each benefit an owner.
- Say risks early, with an owner and the sign you'll watch.
- Tell everyone the same story every month; change the plan out loud when someone shows a real constraint.
- One door, one list, ordered in the open; say "not now" with a reason and a date.
- Put something real in front of people within two weeks.
- Measure what people get; take a baseline first.
- Protect a short time every week to improve how the team works.
- Review the strategy every quarter, and say what moved and why.

## Rules from the plan

- Each film is marked draft on the site, and its merge is its release; the release workflow isn't run.
- No film shows or says a feature's release status, on screen, in the voice, in the captions, on its page or in Pause and think.
- Tools show, they don't lead: the practice first, then the tool where the work happens in it, as its docs name it on the day.
- An agent proposes and a person approves; nothing reaches production without a person, and every change is approved by someone other than its author.
- The fictional university, people and numbers; every story blameless. No census day and no student counts.
- Films are made in English; Spanish comes as captions and as the site's pages.
- Labs and real-life situations come later, once the author has reviewed the films; each film has its Pause and think questions.
