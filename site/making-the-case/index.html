Making the case

The second series of Data management. The old warehouse costs A$310,000 a year, and 17 of its 38 reports are never opened. Helena makes the case to retire it, names its risks out loud, brings fourteen stakeholders to the table, and puts every request on one list, ordered in the open.

One thread: retiring the old warehouse, with everyone on board

The old warehouse has run on the university's own servers since 2012; it costs A$310,000 a year, and its licence ends on 31 December 2027. On 25 May Helena, with Finance, asks the data council for A$380,000 to move the 21 reports people use onto the platform and retire the rest. Then she keeps the case on track: eight risks with owners, fourteen stakeholders with a line each on one monthly page, and, from 7 June, one front door and one list for every request.

A case worth fundingNew York State, from 1816 to 1825: the case for the Erie CanalA case that put the route, the cost and the repayment plainly won a yes. Moving a ton fell from about a hundred dollars to under nine, and the tolls paid back the cost within ten years.
Risks, said out loudAntarctica, 1911: flags across the routeAmundsen's team left food in depots for the way back from the Pole. In fog a depot could be missed, so they marked each one across the route, with numbered flags a party off course would still cross. A risk needs its early sign set the same way.
Everyone at the tableLondon, 1850: three elms under glassEveryone with a stake in the Great Exhibition's building had a worry of their own: the park's old elms, any building in Hyde Park, millions of bricks for a hall meant to come down. Paxton answered each worry, and showed everyone the same picture. A plan for data needs the same: each one heard, and one story for all.
One list, not twentyThe Rhine, 1792: help where they fallThe army's field hospital stayed a league behind the fighting, and the wounded waited a day or more for help. Larrey built a light carriage that followed the front line, and the wounded were bandaged where they fell. A data team's requests need the same: one way in, and the most needed first.
