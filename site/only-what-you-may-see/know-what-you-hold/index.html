Know what you hold: a class for every column

Anil's agreement lets his study have each learner's course, results, year of birth and a code, and nothing that names a learner or touches their health. But the platform reads columns, and Mei's register asks only whether a column identifies someone. What does each column tell about a person?

Read the guidance yourself

The film names no product and no standard. These are the public guides its classes and levels follow, from the bodies that write them.

  • NIST SP 800-188: De-identifying government data setsDirect identifiers, and the quasi-identifiers that name no one alone but single a person out together.
  • ICO: What is special category data?The UK regulator's guide to data that needs more care, such as health or beliefs, including what other data can reveal.
  • NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0The framework's first function starts with knowing what you hold: an inventory of data and what it is (ID.AM-07).
  • ONS: Disclosure controlHow a statistics office publishes totals without pointing to anyone, small counts included.
