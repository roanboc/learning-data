| 10 November 2026 | *The team comes first* opens in Johannesburg in the 1930s, in the four beats: in crowded yards of rooms near the city, women who have come from the countryside earn what they can, washing, sewing and selling, for low and irregular pay, and eight or ten who trust each other form a stokvel, each paying the same sum into one pot and one taking it all, in turn; few Black South Africans had a bank account then, or for decades after, under apartheid, banks wouldn't lend to them, and with no safe place to save and no one to borrow from, one funeral, a term's school fees or a lost wage could ruin a family; they built their own way, out of trust (rules agreed together, every member answering to the others, the burial society, and the group carrying a member through hard times), with Ubuntu behind it, *umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu*; for decades these groups were many families' savings, credit and safety net, and today, by the National Stokvel Association's count, more than 11 million South Africans belong to one, putting in over 50 billion rand a year. It comes back in *The rota holds* ("Like a stokvel carrying a member through hard times, the team carried Sam, because everyone had paid in") and at the close. The cause is said as the law and the banks of the time, with no villain. Its sources are the module's: Verhoef (2001), and NASASA's figures as reported in 2022 and 2023. |
