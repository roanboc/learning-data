## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has ever shared a file can follow it, and a cloud security engineer finds nothing wrong in it. The catalog is the one place where every rule about the data is checked: who may read a table, which columns are masked, which rows are filtered. Every other way to the files skips all of it. In the year 97, Rome's water commissioner found the city's water drawn off along the way by holes in the channels, and held the city to one rule: water is drawn only at a distribution tank, through a stamped nozzle of the size granted. The university does the same for its data in Azure Databricks: private doors in, a short list out, one identity at the files, each catalog in its own workspace, a key of its own, and one way around the catalog that stays shut until an admin account is switched on for a task, and recorded as it's used. **No path to the data skips the catalog, except the one you switch on and watch.**

## The story in one paragraph

Rome, in the year 97. Nine aqueducts bring the city its water from the hills, most of the way in channels under the ground, to distribution tanks, and pipes carry it on to the public basins and fountains, the baths, and houses with a grant from the emperor. The new water commissioner, Sextus Julius Frontinus, measures the water where it enters the channels and finds about ten thousand measures more than the records give out: water-men divert it and sell it, landowners tap the channels for their gardens, secret pipes under the pavements carry it to shops, and only a little reaches the public basins and fountains, which should run day and night for the people. Frontinus holds the city to its rules: no one draws from the channels; whoever holds a grant draws it only at a tank, through a bronze nozzle stamped with the size granted, and every grant is in the records; the secret branch pipes come out; and the gangs who keep the channels write down the day before what each will do, and the records keep what each did. He wrote that the supply almost doubled. At the university, production's files sit in an Azure storage account under Azure Databricks, and Unity Catalog is meant to be the only way to them; Sam Okafor counts nine ways around it (two account keys, a link signed with one, six people with a role on the storage), and every door is open to the internet. Sam gives each workspace a private door from the university's network, lets in only the university's addresses for two weeks, then switches public access off; serverless compute may reach only the catalog's storage and a short list; the storage's public access and account keys go off, the six roles go, and one identity keeps a role on the files, the catalog's own access connector; each catalog is bound to its own workspace; the one door left, a group that may only read the storage, is empty until an admin account is switched on for a task, and the storage's log keeps every read; and the key is the university's own, kept by its security team. Like Rome's water, the data is drawn only at the tank.

## The reference, and practice

The module's reference holds (its proposal, *The reference*): DAMA-DMBOK's data security area for what is protected and why, and zero trust (NIST SP 800-207) for how. Zero trust says identity is the boundary, not the network; this film shows why a data team still closes its network doors: every door that bypasses the catalog bypasses its checks, so doors are kept few, private and watched, and the catalog checks each request behind them. For the film's own question, which paths reach the data and who holds the keys, the thread took these, named once here; the film teaches what they agree on and names none of them on screen.

| Body of knowledge | What it adds | Where the film uses it |
|---|---|---|
| NIST SP 800-207, *Zero Trust Architecture* (2020) | No request is trusted for where it comes from; access is decided per request, as close to the resource as it can be; the network is still segmented, so a stolen credential reaches as little as possible | The whole film: the catalog decides, the doors only narrow who can ask |
| Microsoft cloud security benchmark v1, *Network security* (NS-2, secure cloud services with network controls: private endpoints, public access disabled) and *Data protection* (DP-5, use the customer-managed key option where required; DP-6, a secure key management process) | Private endpoints and no public access for platform services; keys in a vault, with rotation, soft delete and separation of who manages keys from who uses them | *Doors in, a short list out*; *One identity at the files*; *The university's own key* |
| Databricks' *Security best practices* and *Security Reference Architecture* (read 5 November 2026) | Private Link front and back; IP access lists or context-based ingress where a public door remains; serverless egress control; Unity Catalog's storage credentials on managed identities; workspace-catalog binding; customer-managed keys | Every tool chapter |
| CIS Critical Security Controls v8.1, Control 3 (*Data protection*) and Control 12 (*Network infrastructure management*) | Data access control lists; encryption at rest; segment by sensitivity; manage the network's doors on purpose | *Nine ways around*, *Each catalog in its own workspace* |

**Where the film differs, on purpose.** The benchmarks list network controls, key controls and access controls as separate families; the film follows one question through all of them, which paths reach the files, and keeps only the controls that close a path or watch the one left open. It doesn't teach virtual network design, DNS for private endpoints or firewall appliances: the platform team's network is shown, not taught.

**Practice.** What practitioners recognise: storage accounts left on their defaults, open to all networks with account keys on; a signed link made "for a quick file share" years ago and never expired; data roles on the storage given to unblock a task and never taken back; one workspace's notebooks reading another environment's catalogs because nothing said they couldn't; serverless compute that can reach the whole internet; and customer-managed keys switched on for an audit, with the same team able to delete the key and the data. And what works: private by default, with a dated, short-lived exception list while teams move; one identity per storage, the catalog's; the admin path empty until switched on, and logged; egress rules run in dry run before they're enforced; and keys kept by a team that doesn't run the platform.

**The author's practice** (3 November 2026): "include considerations on storage access where there is no masking or policy at all, we usually have special admin accounts (each of the team members) to perform more delicate activities". The film says it in its own words: "Some work needs the files themselves", and the one door for it "stays shut" until an admin account is switched on.

## Rigour to check before recording

Checked on 5 November 2026; the sources are listed below. Product names and maturity are as their docs give them that day.

- **The aqueducts.** "But there now run into the City: the Appian aqueduct, Old Anio, Marcia, Tepula, Julia, Virgo, Alsietina, which is also called Augusta, Claudia, New Anio" (Frontinus, *The Aqueducts of Rome*, I.4, Bennett's Loeb translation, 1925): nine. "Most of the way in channels under the ground": the Appia's channel is "11,190 paces, of which 11,130 paces run underground" (I.5); the Marcia's "61,710½ paces; 54,247½ paces of underground conduit" (I.7); the Claudia's "46,606 paces, of which 36,230 are in a subterranean channel" (I.14). "From the hills": the springs and rivers east of the city; the film doesn't name them.
- **Tanks, pipes and grants.** Water went to "247 reservoirs" in the city and from them to Caesar's, private and public uses, among them "thirty-nine ornamental fountains" and "five hundred and ninety-one water-basins" (II.78; the editor warns the manuscript's figures here are confused, so the film shows none of them). "Whoever wishes to draw water for private use must seek for a grant and bring to the commissioner a writing from the sovereign" (II.105): "a grant from the emperor, for so much water". Bennett's "reservoir" (*castellum*) is the film's "distribution tank".
- **Frontinus and his office.** "Nerva Augustus ... has laid upon me the duties of water commissioner, an office which concerns not merely the convenience but also the health and even the safety of the City" (I.1); "the office was transferred to me" under Nerva's third consulship (II.102), which is 97. He was a former consul and had governed Britain; the film calls him only the water commissioner. Born about 40, he was about 57; Roman men of the time were clean-shaven, as he's drawn.
- **What he measured.** "I first of all undertook measurements of the intakes of the conduits and discovered a total supply far greater — that is, by about 10,000 quinariae — than I found in the records" (II.64); "10,000 quinariae were intercepted" (II.74). A quinaria is a pipe's size used as a measure of flow; the film says "measures".
- **The holes.** "The dishonesty of the water-men, whom we have detected diverting water from the public conduits for private use. But a large number of landed proprietors also, past whose fields the aqueducts run, tap the conduits; whence it comes that the public water-courses are actually brought to a standstill by private citizens, just to water their gardens" (II.75): "some stop altogether". Water-men selling what they draw: "draw from it water, which they sell" (II.114) and "this mode of gaining money ... 'puncturing'" (II.115). "Secret pipes run under the pavements all over the City ... furnishing water by special branches to all those engaged in business in those localities ... being bored for that purpose here and there by the so-called 'puncturers'; whence it came to pass that only a small quantity of water reached the places of public supply" (II.115). "Which should run day and night for the people": the Senate's decree, that the commissioners "shall take pains that the public fountains may deliver water as continuously as possible for the use of the people day and night" (II.104). The film doesn't show II.76's list of who drew water unlawfully.
- **The rules, and the fix.** "No one shall draw water without an authorisation from Caesar ... and no one shall draw more than has been granted" (II.103); "it shall not be permitted to any private party to draw water from the public conduits; and all those to whom the right to draw water has been granted shall draw it from the reservoirs" (II.106), so that "the conduits or the public pipes may not be frequently cut into" (II.106). "The calix, now, is a bronze ajutage, inserted into a conduit or reservoir, and to it the service pipes are attached" (I.36); the deputy "must call in the levellers and provide that the calix is stamped as conforming to the deeded quantity" (II.105). "Every grant is in the records": the records he compared (II.64) and "As soon as any water-rights are vacated, this is announced, and entered in the records" (II.109). The rules were older (the Senate's decrees of 11 BC, the Quinctian law of 9 BC, II.129); "holds the city to its rules" is that. "A considerable quantity of lead has been brought in by the removal of that kind of branch pipes", by which he estimated how much was stolen (II.115). The gangs: "Both of these large gangs, which regularly were diverted by exercise of favouritism, or by negligence of their foremen, to employment on private work, I resolved to bring back to some discipline and to the service of the State, by writing down the day before what each gang was going to do, and by putting in the records what it had done each day" (II.117). The gangs were enslaved workmen (II.116, "gangs of slaves"); the film calls them gangs and draws them as workmen at work, and says no more of them.
- **The benefits, as Frontinus wrote them.** "Whatever was unlawfully drawn by the water-men, or was wasted as the result of negligence, has been added to our supply: just as though new sources had been discovered. And in fact the supply has been almost doubled" (II.87); "the increase in the number of the works, reservoirs, fountains, and water-basins"; "those also who with fear drew water unlawfully, now free from care, draw their supply by grant from the sovereign"; "the appearance of the City is clean and altered; the air is purer" (II.88). These are his own words, in praise of his emperor, with no measurement after him; the film says "Frontinus wrote".
- **Where the opening's parallel holds, and where it doesn't.** The tank is the catalog: the one place to draw, where the grant is checked (the stamped nozzle) and the rules applied. The holes are the ways around it. The gangs' written-down work is the admin account's switch-on: a task, a reason, and a record of what was done. Rome measured water; the platform checks each request, which Rome couldn't; the film doesn't say Rome stopped every theft.
- **Production's files, every table's data.** Unity Catalog's managed tables and volumes keep their data as files in the cloud storage the catalog's managed locations point to; here, the university's own Azure storage account, one container per area. "Unity Catalog is meant to be the only way to them" is the module's rule (*Walls, doors and keys*: "The files' only door is the catalog").
- **Account keys and signed links.** "With this permission, a user can use the account access keys to access all data in a storage account" (*Prevent authorization with Shared Key*): "each of which opens every file" (a storage account has two). A link signed with a key is a service or account shared access signature; "When you disallow Shared Key authorization for a storage account, Azure Storage rejects all subsequent requests to that account that are authorized with the account access keys"; service and account SAS signed with Shared Key: "Request is denied for all Azure Storage services"; "fails with error code 403 (Forbidden)". Owner and Contributor can list the keys ("they include the Microsoft.Storage/storageAccounts/listkeys/action"), which is why the keys are switched off rather than only kept secret.
- **Roles on the storage.** "Only roles explicitly defined for data access permit a security principal to access blob data" (*Authorize blob access with Microsoft Entra ID*). Storage Blob Data Contributor: "Read, write, and delete Azure Storage containers and blobs"; Storage Blob Data Reader: "Read and list Azure Storage containers and blobs" (*Built-in roles for Storage*). "No grant, mask or filter applies": Unity Catalog's grants, row filters and column masks are enforced by Databricks when it reads a table; a file read straight from the storage passes none of them (the module's decision of 3 November 2026).
- **"A door on the internet, open to any address."** "By default, storage accounts allow connections from any network" (*Firewall rules and network access*); for workspaces, "The default allows connections from any IP address" (*Manage IP access lists*).
- **Private endpoints for the workspaces.** Azure Private Link for Azure Databricks: inbound (front-end) "Secures connections from users to workspaces and account-level resources"; classic (back-end) "Secures connections from classic compute to the control plane" (*Private Link concepts*). "The machines that run the jobs": the nightly job runs on classic job compute in the university's own virtual network (VNet injection, secure cluster connectivity, which back-end Private Link needs: "Premium plan, VNet injection, SCC"). Web sign-in over a private connection needs its own browser authentication endpoint, which the film doesn't show. "So does the build service": GitHub Actions' runners work inside the university's network (*From a branch to production* says how).
- **IP access lists, then public access off.** "If you use Private Link, IP access lists apply only to requests over the internet (public IP addresses)" (*Manage IP access lists*); "When **Allow Public Network Access** is **Enabled**, workspace IP access lists are evaluated. Otherwise, all public ingress is blocked and workspace public ingress policies are not evaluated" (*Context-based ingress control*). Workspace IP access lists have no settings page; they're set with the CLI or the REST API, each with a label, a type (`ALLOW` or `BLOCK`) and addresses, so the film shows the list as a card in its own words, not a page. Databricks now recommends its context-based ingress policies "as your primary method for managing access" where a public door remains (*Configure IP access lists for workspaces*); the film shows the list only for the two weeks before the door shuts, so that choice doesn't arise. Status read for the record on 5 November 2026: context-based ingress generally available, its account-level policies in Beta; IP access lists for the account console in Public Preview. The film uses neither and says no status.
- **Serverless egress.** Serverless egress control has "**Full Access**" and "**Restricted Access**"; restricted access allows "Unity Catalog external locations" and the domains and storage accounts "listed in the network policy"; "a configuration object applied at the account level... each workspace can only be linked to one policy at a time"; "Full Access: Serverless workloads have unrestricted outbound access to the internet and other network resources", which is where production starts ("could reach anywhere"); "Mitigate risk by evaluating the effects of any new policy first in dry-run mode before full enforcement", which the film says as "it blocks nothing yet, and Sam sees what it would have blocked"; its purpose is "reducing the risk of data exfiltration" (*What is serverless egress control?*). "You can always access the Azure storage account associated with your workspace" too, which the film doesn't need to say. Serverless reaches the university's storage by a private endpoint from a network connectivity configuration, and "The endpoints do not take effect until an admin approves them on the resource side" (*Configure private connectivity to Azure resources*). `api.uni.example` is the film's own.
- **The storage locked down.** *Networking* › "Disable": "To block traffic from all networks" (*Change the default network access rule*); "when a private endpoint is configured, traffic from the associated virtual network is always allowed, even if public network access is disabled on the storage account" (*Use private endpoints for Azure Storage*), with endpoints for `dfs` and `blob` ("If you create a private endpoint for the Data Lake Storage storage resource, then you should also create one for the Blob Storage resource"). *Configuration* › "Allow storage account key access" › "Disabled".
- **The access connector.** "The Access Connector for Azure Databricks is a first-party Azure resource that lets you connect managed identities to an Azure Databricks account"; "Grant read and write access to the entire storage account using the `Storage Blob Data Contributor` role"; "Using a managed identity has the benefit of allowing Unity Catalog to access storage accounts protected by network rules, which isn't possible using service principals, and it removes the need to manage and rotate secrets"; "As an option, you can instead set Public Network Access to Disabled. The managed identity can be used to bypass the check on public network access"; "Storage credentials store the managed identity, and external locations define a path to storage along with a reference to the storage credential" (*Use Azure managed identities in Unity Catalog to access storage*). "An identity Azure gives it, with no secret anyone holds" is *No secret to lose*'s top rung, which *Workloads without secrets* builds. The screens' labels: "Connect > Credentials", "Credential Type" "Azure Managed Identity", "Access Connector ID", "Connect > External Locations", "URL" (Microsoft Learn, read 5 November 2026); the film's page is at "Connect › Credentials", and shortens the credential's type to "Managed identity". Test and development storage follow the same pattern, each with a connector of its own; the film shows production's.
- **Workspace-catalog binding.** "In Unity Catalog, all catalogs are accessible by default from any workspace attached to the same metastore. Workspace-catalog binding lets you override this default to restrict a catalog to one or more specific workspaces. Access from an unbound workspace is denied, even for users with explicit privilege grants on the catalog" (*Workspace-catalog binding*). The labels: "On the **Workspaces** tab, clear the **All workspaces have access** checkbox", "**Assign to workspaces**"; the film's page shows that switch, and production's access as "Read & Write" (the docs' "Change access to read & write"). Storage credentials and external locations bind the same way, on their own Workspaces tabs. "registrar isn't there" and "registrar isn't here" are the film's own words, not a message the product shows. The catalogs' names are *From a branch to production*'s (`numbers.md`). How a change moves between workspaces is that film's; read-only bindings are *Grants by design*'s: in mid-October it binds `registrar` to a fourth workspace, `studies`, read only, on purpose, so the narration says "a workspace it isn't bound to", true before and after.
- **The admin path.** `admin_storage_readers`, one of *People and groups*' admin groups (`numbers.md`), holds Storage Blob Data Reader on `unidataprod`, assigned to the group; no one is in the group until a switch-on through Microsoft Entra privileged identity management (PIM for Groups): eligible, a reason, another specialist's approval, at most 4 hours, an email to the platform team. *Power you switch on*'s example, as agreed with its thread: one morning the 02:00 load stops on a row it can't read; Jun switches on "read the storage" for 1 hour to open last night's file, where no mask or filter applies; Sam approves; Jun reads it and switches off before the hour is up. "Read and list, nothing more" is the role's description. Jun reads the file on the storage account's *Containers* page, which the film doesn't draw ("In the storage account's left menu, expand **Data storage**, and then select **Containers**"), signed in as the admin account: "you see **Microsoft Entra user Account** specified as the authentication method in the portal" (*Choose how to authorize access to blob data in the Azure portal*, 2026-08-18); with account keys off, the portal can't use a key. The storage's log: "Resource logs aren't collected and stored until you create a diagnostic setting"; table `StorageBlobLogs` (*Monitor Azure Blob Storage*); the reader's name is in it (`RequesterUpn`), which *Who did what* reads.
- **Keys.** "Azure Storage uses that key to protect and control access to the key that encrypts your data" (*Customer-managed keys for account encryption*); by default "key management is set to **Microsoft-Managed Keys**", which is "a key the cloud provider kept for the university". The storage reaches the key with a managed identity holding *Key Vault Crypto Service Encryption User* ("Read metadata of keys and perform wrap/unwrap operations"): it may use the key, never take it out. "The people who run the platform can't delete it or turn it off": they hold no role on the vault but that one, through the storage's identity; the vault is the security team's (*Walls, doors and keys*, agreed with its thread). "Every use is recorded": the vault's `AuditEvent` log lists `KeyWrap` and `KeyUnwrap`. "A new version every year": the vault's rotation policy, with which "you can set up Key Vault to automatically create a new key version at a chosen frequency" (*Configure cryptographic key auto-rotation*, 2026-04-10; it advises rotating "at least every two years"); "Azure Storage checks the key vault daily for a new version of the key", with automatic updating of the key version. "Kept ninety days, and nobody can cut that short": soft delete set to 90 days, the default ("You can set it anywhere from 7 to 90 days"), and purge protection ("a vault or an object in the deleted state can't be purged until the retention period passes"); both are required for a storage account's customer-managed key. "Notebooks, queries and disks": Azure Databricks' customer-managed keys for managed services (notebook source, queries and query history, secrets, dashboards, Git credentials) and managed disks (compute's data disks, not its OS disks) (*Customer-managed keys for encryption*); Unity Catalog's own key for default storage isn't used, since the catalogs live in the university's storage. "Take the key back: then nobody can read the files": "After the key is disabled, clients can't call operations that read from or write to a resource or its metadata. Attempts to call these operations fail with error code 403 (Forbidden) for all users." "The cloud provider included" is *Walls, doors and keys*' line; the film shows it as a what-if, never done.
