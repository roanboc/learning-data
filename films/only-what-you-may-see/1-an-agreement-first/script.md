## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has ever asked for data can follow it, and a privacy officer or a research data manager finds nothing wrong in it. Access to people's data is the last step, not the first. Two papers come before it: each person's confidentiality agreement, signed once before any access and kept current, and, for each use, a data sharing agreement that says what the data is for, what the use may have and must never have, who may use it, where, what may leave, and when it ends, signed by the data's owner. Then the access is made from the agreement, and ends with it. **Access is the end of an agreement, not the start.**

## The story in one paragraph

England, 1939. At Bletchley Park, Britain's codebreakers set to work on the enemy's coded radio messages, and by 1945 almost nine thousand people work there, three in four of them women. Each would learn the war's best-kept secret, that the enemy's messages could be read; ships crossing the Atlantic were steered away from submarines whose orders had been read, and if the enemy found out, it would change its codes and the ships would sail into them. So the people who ran Bletchley put a promise first: before anyone was told what the work was, they signed the Official Secrets Act; each was told only what their own part needed; and what they read went only to a few named commanders, carried by an officer who waited while it was read, took it back and destroyed it, and nothing done with it could give the secret away. It held: the enemy never learned, and the secret was kept for almost thirty years, until 1974. At the university, on Monday 5 October 2026, Anil Kurian, in the Faculty of Health, asks for the records of the faculty's 2,614 learners, to study how they progress and where they need support. Mei Tanaka owns them, for the registrar's office. A purpose and an end date, which *Who may see it* gave every access, don't say what the study must never have, where it may work or what may leave it. So two papers come first. Each person's confidentiality agreement, six lines in the signer's voice, signed before any access, renewed every year and kept by Yusuf Demir in data governance; a person stays in a group only while theirs is current, and an agent's owner signs for it. And the study's data sharing agreement, 2026-14, written line by line (purpose, from, may have, never, who, where, may leave, ends), the questions statistics offices call the Five Safes, signed by Mei and Anil on Monday 12 October and kept in Yusuf's register. Only then comes access: the agreement's terms are written again where the platform reads them, its contract, and the platform team makes the grant from it, never by hand; every grant names its agreement. When the faculty wants the names of learners who need support, that's a new purpose and a new agreement: the study's never stretches. On Wednesday 30 June 2027 the grant ends by itself, the study's copy is deleted, the totals stay, and Anil's promise holds after he leaves.

## The reference, and practice

The module's reference holds (its proposal, *The reference*): DAMA-DMBOK's data security area for what is protected and why, and zero trust (NIST SP 800-207) for how. For this film's question, what has to be agreed before anyone may use people's data, the thread took these, named once here; the film teaches what they agree on and names only the Five Safes, once, as the questions statistics offices ask.

| Body of knowledge | What it adds | Where the film uses it |
|---|---|---|
| NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, PS-6 *Access agreements* (and PS-6(3), *Post-employment requirements*) | Each person signs before access is granted, re-signs when it changes or at a set interval, and some duties outlast the job | *A promise from each person*: signed before any access, renewed every year, "it holds after I leave" |
| ISO/IEC 27001:2022, Annex A 6.6 *Confidentiality or non-disclosure agreements* | Confidentiality agreements identified, documented, reviewed regularly and signed | The same chapter: one agreement for everyone, kept by data governance |
| The ICO's *Data sharing code of practice* (UK, 2021), *Data sharing agreements* | What an agreement for sharing personal data sets out: purpose, parties, the data, what happens at each stage, retention and deletion, security, review | *An agreement for each use*: the agreement's lines |
| The Five Safes (Desai, Ritchie and Welpton, 2016; the UK's secure research services; Australia's data sharing principles, 2022) | Five questions for any access to people's data for research: safe people, projects, settings, data and outputs | Named once in *An agreement for each use*: the agreement's lines answer them |
| Data contracts (Open Data Contract Standard, v3) | A dataset's terms written where machines read them, roles and access among them | *Access, made from the agreement*: the agreement's contract, and the grant made from it |

**Where the film differs, on purpose.** The standards keep the person's agreement (personnel security) and the agreement for a use (data sharing) in different places; the film puts them side by side as the two papers that come before access, because a practitioner needs both and they fail in the same way: unsigned, out of date, or stretched to a use nobody agreed. It doesn't teach lawful bases (*A reason to keep it* does), classes (*Know what you hold*), the ways to hide a value (*Show the least*) or how a grant is built (*Grants by design*).

**Practice.** What practitioners recognise: a researcher given a whole table "for now", with names, and a copy on a laptop two years later; an agreement signed once and stretched to every new question; a confidentiality form signed at induction and never seen again, and a contractor who never signed one; access granted by hand, so nobody can say which agreement a grant came from, or whether it ended. And what works: one short promise for everyone, renewed yearly and checked before any group is joined; one agreement per use, with an end date and what happens then; the data's owner signing; a register anyone can read; and access made from the agreement, never by hand.

**The author's practice** (3 November 2026): every contributor signs a confidentiality agreement and takes responsibility for the data they access, as part of governance, and an agent's owner signs for it. The film says it in its own words: "An agent signs nothing: its owner signs, and answers for it."

Winterbotham, *The Ultra Secret*, 1974, and Ronald Lewin, *Ultra Goes to War*, 1978): "The standard procedure was for the liaison officer to present the intelligence summary to the recipient, stay with him while he studied it, perhaps answer questions and then take it back and destroy it"; recipients "were not allowed to transmit, repeat or refer Ultra signals", so that "they did nothing to give away the secret that Enigma was being read".

GCHQ, *What happened next for the codebreakers*: many lived "their whole lives without revealing their contribution".
