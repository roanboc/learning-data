# What the business needs to know: questions and assumptions

*Enterprise architecture* · *Business architecture for data*, film 2 of 5 · PR #PRNUM · [pull request](https://github.com/roanboc/learning-data/pull/PRNUM)

[Watch it on the site](https://data.learn-all.org/business-architecture-for-data/what-the-business-needs-to-know/)

> Written by the film's thread on 12 November 2026, from the series' proposal the author confirmed and merged on 11 November 2026 (PR #318). Every question below would have gone to the author, and was answered instead with the best-reasoned option, for him to review when he watches the film.

| # | Question | Assumption taken | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which part of the qanat story does this film tell? | What a share means, as film 1's assumptions planned: a share didn't mean the same on every qanat (its water came round every six days on one, every twenty-two on another), the deeds of the 1960s that added up to more than a day's water in some villages, and Mahan, where one qanat's day is fifteen shares of ninety-six minutes, so the shares add up to the day | Chapter 1; `script.md`, *Opening story* |
| 2 | Who set Mahan's fifteen shares? | Paul Ward English gives the division, not who made it. The narration says "one qanat's owners measured every share against the day itself", taking the division as the shareholders' own, as film 1 has them own the water and its rules | Chapter 1; `script.md`, *Rigour* |
| 3 | Where do the three men and the seventy families come from? | English's Vakilabad qanat, built in Mahan in the 1860s: its water first divided among three men in six shares, then sold off bit by bit until some seventy families own shares. The film doesn't name it | Chapter 1, the second dial |
| 4 | Where does "as fine as thirty seconds" belong? | English gives it for a third qanat in the town, whose smallest share is thirty seconds of water every twelve days. The narration says it of a share in general ("it could be sold, inherited, split as fine as thirty seconds"), and the screen keeps it in the list of what a share can do, not on either drawn qanat's day | Chapter 1 |
| 5 | The deeds of the 1960s: whose hours? | English says only that some villages' deeds made up more than twenty-four hours of water a day. The seven slips on screen (6, 5, 4, 4, 3, 2 and 2 hours, 26 in all) are an example, not a record, and the village isn't named | Chapter 1 |
| 6 | Which reference models does the film teach? | For electricity, four: the IEC's Common Information Model (IEC 61970 and 61968), IEC 61968-1's interface reference model (drawn in ArchiMate since its 2020 edition), the Smart Grid Architecture Model and the ENTSO-E, ebIX and EFET Harmonised Electricity Market Role Model (2023-01). Others named once on screen: BIAN, TM Forum, ACORD, HL7 FHIR and HERM. APQC, also in the series' plan, waits for film 4, where processes are levelled | Chapter 3 |
| 7 | Are a usage point, a metering point and a connection point the same thing? | For the utility's homes, yes: one meter where power is delivered, so the film says CIM calls a connection point a usage point and the market role model a metering point. In some markets a connection point can have several metering points; the film doesn't go into it, and keeps both names beside the business's word for exchanges | Chapter 5 |
| 8 | Which domains, and who owns each? | Three: the network (Grace), retail (the head of retail, text only, as the series' cast has it) and a shared domain for words both use, such as location. Grace owns the shared domain, since the network's records keep where every connection is; the author's rule (11 November 2026) puts definitions several business domains share in a data domain of their own | Chapters 5 and 8 |
| 9 | Which concept does nobody hold? | Hosting capacity, the solar a street can take before its voltage or its cables pass their limits. It follows EPRI's definition (the generation a feeder can take without passing voltage, thermal or protection limits), with protection left out for a newcomer. The thirty-four working days new solar waits are invented, like every number at the utility | Chapter 6 |
| 10 | Who names and defines it? | Grace's engineers, in their words, with Grace as its owner and no data yet; the data team builds it later, from that definition (AGENTS.md, *Definitions come from the glossary*) | Chapter 6 |
| 11 | Does the glossary need a tool? | No: a table is enough to start, as long as every term has an owner. One term for each business object, with its definition, domain and owner, grouped by domain; later, the data team's tables and reports point to the terms they hold. *One language, kept*, in *Data architecture*, takes it further | Chapter 7 |
| 12 | What does this film add to the data practice on the map? | The glossary's domains on the business layer, the business objects in them (meter reading, hosting capacity, bill, location, with film 1's connection point and account) and each domain's owner. Owners and stewards as roles wait for film 3 | Chapter 8 |
| 13 | Which sound, now that the consort of viols is on the site already? | The consort stops at film 1, which needs no rework. Nothing new fitted (the topic's `palettes.md` lists what the thread checked), so this film pairs two instruments the site has but no film pairs, and no film of this topic plays: a clavichord (*Where each piece lives*, with a harpsichord) and an oboe (*Written once*, *Teachers of data*, *A reason to keep it*, *More than a junior*). Whether the four architecture series may do this when nothing new fits is already a question on the author's checklist | The soundtrack; `script.md`, *Sound* |
| 14 | Labs for the series? | None yet, as in film 1: the series' lab comes with the platform series' last film, as the proposal plans, so the Pause and think questions link to no lab | `site/assets/what-the-business-needs-to-know/think.en.js` |
| 15 | Spanish captions: which words? | Latin American Spanish, as film 1: la empresa (the utility), comercialización (retail), punto de conexión, objeto de negocio, dueño and dueña (owner), capa de información, una parte (a share), hoja en blanco, modelo de referencia, dominio, glosario, días hábiles, and capacidad de alojamiento for hosting capacity, as Latin American grid studies call it. The reference models' own names for a connection point (usage point, metering point) stay in English, as they are on screen | `source/src/i18n/es/captions.js` |
