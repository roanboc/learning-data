Who did what: a record nobody in it can change

Sam has locked the platform's sign-ins, doors, admin power and changes. Now the security review asks him to show they held over the last 90 days, but the answers sit in four records, and one keeps only a month. Where should the record live, and what should ring a bell at once?

The film shows Databricks, GitHub and Azure as their own documentation describes them. These are the pages its record, its alarms, its emergency accounts and its locked copies follow.

  • Databricks: the audit log system tableWhat the platform records of each sign-in, change and read, how long it keeps it, and the columns to search it by.
  • GitHub: streaming the audit logHow GitHub's record of who did what goes, as it's written, to storage you keep, for longer than GitHub keeps it.
  • Microsoft Entra: emergency access accountsTwo accounts for the day every lock jams: how to keep them, sign in with them, watch them and test them.
  • Microsoft Entra: revoke access in an emergencyThe steps to end a person's access at once, and why each app's own sessions must be ended too.
  • Azure Storage: immutable storageCopies locked for a set time, which no one can change or delete before then, not even an admin.
  • NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3: incident responseHow to detect, respond to and recover from an incident, as part of everyday risk management.
