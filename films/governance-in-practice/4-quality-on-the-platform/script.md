# Governance you can use · Quality on the platform: script

*The script of *Quality on the platform*, film 4 of 6 of *Governance you can use*, the third series of data governance, in nine chapters, in English, painted in watercolour. Drafted on 28 October 2026 and made the same day, when the author asked for the films of the three series to be made and merged one after another. It follows the plan the author approved on 23 October 2026 (film 17 of that plan: the tools films open on a scene of the university's own, on a line from the film they expand), and the rule of 28 October 2026 for every opening (scenario, problem, solution and benefits, each said outright; *Opening story* quotes them). Every file on screen is the example project's own, or one of the worked examples kept beside it (*What the example project has*): the red night's log, scorecard and issue are the project's own output for that run; last night's scorecard and the governance check's measures are main's; and the platform's results, the scorecard that reads them and the issue from the night two rows were kept out are the worked example this film adds. The platform's pages are drawn from Databricks' documentation as it stood on 28 October 2026, and each feature's status is said where it isn't generally available. As the author asked for these films, it isn't rendered here: the release workflow renders it.*

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never written a data test can follow it, and a data quality lead or an analytics engineer finds nothing wrong in it. *Rules that run* gave every check an owner, a dimension and its owner's level, and wrote a scorecard from the night's checks; *Fixed where it starts* fixed a problem where it was typed, kept the rule as a check that should never fail, and let monitoring catch what no rule names; *Teachers of data* measured governance from the work. This film shows how each is built: in dbt, the owner by folder, the dimension by the kind of test, the level in the test as its owner chose it, and a governance check that stops a test without them; the scorecard and an issue for each owner, written from every run; four places to check along the data's way, where it's typed, where it's loaded, on the data team's models, and on the platform's tables, as monitoring; a test on the source that should never fail; the loading pipeline's expectations, which keep out a row that can't be loaded, and count it; monitoring on the platform, which learns each table's usual shape and tells the owner on record; and one scorecard for every result, measured, not surveyed. **Rules you write, and monitoring for the rest.**

## The story in one paragraph

In Planning's room, Clara Jensen checks the numbers before they leave: the census numbers go to the government, and the lists to the faculties. When Yusuf asked what slowed her down, she said: which number do I trust? Checks ran every night, but a failed one said only its name, not whose data it was; a warning that emails were typed with spaces and capitals had run for a year, and nobody owned it; and data quality was a survey, 3.9 out of 5. So a broken night could change a number on its way to the government, and nobody knew whose it was to fix. Now every check names its owner, the kind of quality it checks and the level its owner chose; monitoring watches for what no rule names; and each morning, every result goes into one scorecard, with an issue for each owner. On Monday 19 October 2026, five more short-course enrolments arrived with no email, which made six with the walk-in from February, more than the five the learning team allows: everything built from them stopped, so nothing wrong went out, and Planning kept the day before's numbers; by noon the learning team had added the six emails, and that night all 160 checks passed. The film then opens each piece: in the dbt project, the owner that comes from the folder, the dimension that comes from the kind of test, and the level in the test, as the learning team chose it, with the decision that says why, and the governance check that stops a test with no owner until a line gives it one; the two scripts that write the scorecard and each owner's issue from the run, where a test that didn't run counts as not run, not as failed; four places to check along the data's way, each telling the data's owner; the sign-up form that checks each email from 2 November, and the test on the source that should never fail; the platform team's loading pipeline, whose expectations keep out a row with no ID, and count it; and monitoring, which learns each table's usual shape and, one night when half the new badges arrived with no credit, told the learning team, so the surprise became a test. The platform's results land in the same scorecard, by table, each to its owner. This morning, Clara opens it before the numbers leave: one amber cell, an enrolment with no email that the learning team is following up, and everything else passed.
