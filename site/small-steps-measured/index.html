One thread: from a dashboard request to a habit of improving

Graham Ellis, the new Director of Facilities, asks for a dashboard of room use by Easter. His real question is whether the University Council should fund a new teaching building. The team shows him a prototype within two weeks of taking it up, first used eleven working days after they started. From July the team's measures go on one page; from September a Friday half-hour improves one thing a week; and every quarter the data council reviews the strategy, and moves it when the university's plans change.

Every film opens somewhere else, at another time

FilmOpens withStill true today
Prototype firstCuritiba, 1972: a street in a weekendThe shopkeepers feared that a street closed to cars would empty their shops, and no plan on paper could show them otherwise. The city built the street in a weekend, and within days their sales were up. Something real settles what a plan on paper can't.
Measures that matterNew York, 1952: the first minuteAn anaesthetist gave every newborn a score of five signs, taken one minute after birth, the same way every time. A baby in trouble showed at once, and doctors could compare what they did. A data team's measures work the same way.
Better every weekThe United States, 1940: a better way, on a cardFactories had to make far more, with millions of new workers. A government course taught supervisors to improve the jobs they knew best, in four steps on a pocket card: use it until a better way is developed. A data team's way of working gets better the same way.
Strategy is a habitMontreal, 1987: the treaty that looked againCountries signed a modest first cut in the gases thinning the ozone layer, and built in a habit: look at the science again, at least every four years. In London and Copenhagen they went further, out loud, and the layer is healing. A data strategy lives the same way.
