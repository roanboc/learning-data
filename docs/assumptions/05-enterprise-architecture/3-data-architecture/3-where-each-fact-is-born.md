Film 3 of *Data architecture*, `films/data-architecture/3-where-each-fact-is-born`, made on 12 November 2026 from the plan the author confirmed on 11 November (pull request #318) and the series' setup (#320). Every question below would have gone to the author, and was answered instead with the best-reasoned option, for him to confirm or correct when he watches the film.

| # | Question | Assumption taken | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which sound, when no instrument the site lacks fits? | Glass bars (a crystallophone) with a monochord: two instruments the site has, which no film pairs, and which no film of this topic plays (*Context engineering* has the monochord, with a twelve-string guitar; *Context and memory, governed* has glass bars, with a baritone sarrusophone). The palettes register allows a pairing where nothing new fits (12 November 2026). Kept apart from those films: the monochord is plucked with the finger at its middle, low, and only where a fact is born; the glass bars sit higher and ring its harmonics, as copies born of it. Reserved in `/mnt/project-files/enterprise-architecture/new-architecture-series/palettes.md`. The question whether pairings are fine is already on the author's checklist. | The soundtrack |
| 2 | Was the telex network finished by 1972? | No, and the film says it was "being extended to every factory the state ran": Medina (2006, p. 587) writes that Cybernet "expanded the existing telex network to include every firm in nationalised sector". The series' last film says how little of the design was built. | Chapter 1 |
| 3 | Is "Some managers were won over by the offer of a free telex" fair? | The film's plain word for Medina's: the team "occasionally used the promise of free telex installation to cajole factory managers into lending their support to the project" (2006, p. 587); fn 58 adds private owners asked to join "in return for free access to telex technology". "Some" keeps it to what he records. | Chapter 1 |
| 4 | "Once a day", and "the same day"? | Medina (2006, p. 587): "each firm could only transmit data once per day"; p. 592: the office "received information from the individual enterprises every afternoon". A telex arrives as a copy of what was typed at the factory, so "a copy of what that factory had sent, the same day" is the film's reading. "Santiago" stands for CORFO's telex room and the project's offices there. | Chapters 1 and 8 |
| 5 | Did the team really have to remind managers? | Yes: "Reminding factory managers to send data on a daily basis proved to be a great source of frustration for the project team" (Medina 2006, p. 587, fn 59, from his interview with Isaquino Benadof, 2002). The film says "It wasn't easy" and draws three reminders. | Chapter 1 |
| 6 | "The statistics before had taken six months"? | Medina (2006, p. 594): the daily report "represented a considerable advance over the previous six-month lag required to collect statistics on the Chilean economy". The film keeps the comparison and leaves out that the daily report's data could be a few days old. | Chapter 1 |
| 7 | How long is Chile? | "More than four thousand kilometres": Chile runs about 4,300 km from north to south; Medina writes "three-thousand-mile-long territory" (p. 587). The site writes kilometres. | Chapter 1 |
| 8 | Are the mill, its cotton and the reminders real? | No: drawn for the film. Cybersyn's first models included textile firms, and absent workers were one of its measures (Medina 2006, p. 588); the strips (2, 9 and 8 days of cotton, 310 t of cement, 12 workers absent) and the reminders' wording are examples. That a figure from elsewhere, last month's or an office's guess, would mislead the software is the film's own reasoning, from how Cyberstride compared each factory with its own history (film 2's row). | Chapters 1 and 8 |
| 9 | Is "the one system where it's created, and corrected" what DAMA says? | The film's plain words for DAMA-DMBOK2's system of record (chapter 10): "an authoritative system where data is created/captured, and/or maintained through a defined set of rules and expectations". Every other system that holds the fact holds a copy. | Chapter 3 |
| 10 | Which matrix is it? | A CRUD matrix (Information Engineering; TOGAF's application/data matrix) cut to two words: "creates" (C) and "copy", a system that reads the thing and keeps its own copy. Update and delete are left out: the film needs only where each fact is created. | Chapter 3 |
| 11 | Why is the network map the connection point's home, and not billing? | Chosen for the film's utility: the network team knows of a connection first, when it's planned, and owns what's built, so the network map creates each one with its ID, and billing asks for one and copies it, ID and all, the same day. Grace, its owner, agrees. Some utilities make the customer system the home of a premise instead; the film shows the pattern (one home, one owner, an ID carried with every copy), not the only answer. | Chapter 4 |
| 12 | Who owns the account? | The head of retail, with billing as its home: in *What the business owns*, "an account belongs to the head of retail: someone who pays a bill". | Chapter 4 |
| 13 | What does a line back say? | Which system, which record, and when it was copied: the least a copy's lineage needs (DAMA-DMBOK2, chapter 12, Metadata Management). The chips' times (06:00, 06:05, 06:10) are examples of a nightly copy. | Chapter 5 |
| 14 | Are the meter's event, the call and the interruption one fact? | No: three facts, each with its own home. Meter data records what a meter reported, customer service what a customer said, and the outage system the interruption as Grace's team defines it (IEEE 1366, film 2's row), from its start to its end, reading the other two as evidence. Grace's measure counts the outage system's record. | Chapter 6 |
| 15 | Where does the customer go? | To master data: a customer can start in billing or in customer service, so both create one, and the work is to know when two records are the same person and which value wins (DMBOK2, chapter 10: matching and survivorship). The site's master data series, *The same one, everywhere*, takes it from there; this film doesn't teach it. | Chapter 7 |
| 16 | The utility's numbers? | Invented: 214 of 1,160 new homes created twice last year; 14 Quarry Lane and Lot 14; P-88213, CP-0061420, account 4471-209 and meter M-2207114; the times 19:40, 19:52, 20:15 and 21:15; A. Mendoza and Ana Mendoza; the months March to October; and "Since July, no new home has been created twice", the result of only the network map creating connection points. | Chapters 1 to 8 |
