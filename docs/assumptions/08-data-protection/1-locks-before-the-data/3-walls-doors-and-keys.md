| # | Question for Rodrigo | Assumption taken | Where it shows |

| 1 | Keep the proposed opening, Japan's Hakone checkpoint (1619)? | No. Hakone's own museum says the checkpoint mainly stopped lords' wives and families leaving Edo, where they were kept as hostages, and searched women leaving far more than guns coming in; so its "solution" gave the people it checked nothing, and the opening's benefits beat wouldn't be honest. The site also has three openings from Japan already. Replaced by Ragusa (today's Dubrovnik), 27 July 1377: the first known quarantine law, a month apart on the islet of Mrkan or in Cavtat for anyone coming from a place with plague, so the port could stay open; and, for centuries, the gates' keys taken to the rector each evening. Nobody on the site has used it (no Dubrovnik, Ragusa or lazaretto in any file on main or any branch), and it carries all three of the film's ideas: few gates, a place apart, and keys someone answers for. Claimed in the module's openings.md | *Few gates*, *A month apart*, and the returns in *Few doors*, *Rooms apart*, *Keys you can see*, *The walk again* |
| 2 | The opening's benefit needs a number where the sources give one: is there one? | None found: no source gives a count of lives the law saved, so the film says what the law made possible (the port stayed open, the sickness was held on an island) and gives the one number the sources do, the wait that grew to forty days and gave the word quarantine | *A month apart* |
| 3 | Which areas are the walls drawn from? | The module's five catalogs by area (numbers.md: registrar, learning, planning, hr, finance), shown in plain words as the university's map's areas, each with the owner *Who decides* names. Worth one decision across the module | *Who secures what*, *If someone got in* |
| 4 | Who secures what: how is it split, in a film that names no cloud? | Three layers, as the brief says: the cloud provider (buildings, machines, the network between them); the platform's makers (its software, and how it checks every request); the university (who gets in, what each may do, its settings, its keys, its copies). | 5 | Do walls still matter under zero trust? | Yes, said carefully: a wall doesn't make anyone inside it trusted, every request is still checked (*Who is it, really?*); walls keep the ways in few and the rooms small, so a mistake or an intruder stays where it started (NIST SP 800-207's smallest trust zones and limits on lateral movement) | *Who secures what*, *The walk again* |
| 6 | What does "a private path" cover? | People and jobs reach the platform only from the university's own network (staff away from campus join it first); the platform reaches the storage by a private path too; data may leave only for places on a short list, three examples shown (the government's returns, the university's reports, the backups' place apart). *Network, storage and keys* builds it, agreed with its thread | *Few doors* |
| 7 | Who holds the key? | The university's own key, kept by its security team in a vault of its own; the platform may use it, never take it out; the people who run the platform can't delete it or turn it off; the security team can take it back; every use recorded; a deleted key kept 90 days, which nobody can cut short; a new version every year (not said in the film). Before this film, the cloud provider kept the key. Agreed with *Network, storage and keys* and *Who did what*; in numbers.md, *Doors, keys and copies* | *Keys you can see* |
| 8 | How are the backups kept, and for how long? | Every night after the 02:00 load, a copy of production's data (each table as it stands that night) goes to a place apart, with admins of its own; each copy is locked for 30 days, so nobody can change or delete it before then, not even an admin; then it's removed by itself; once a month the platform team restores one, checks it and removes it. Agreed with *Who did what* (which builds it) and *Deleted means deleted* (a person's deleted rows leave the last locked copy 30 days later: for Linh, on 24 December 2026) | *Copies nobody can delete*; numbers.md |
| 9 | Separate environments: how much does this film say? | The idea only: one workspace becomes three, development, test and production, each reaching only its own data; made-up or masked data outside production, never a copy of production's; one reviewed path between them, which *From a branch to production* builds and starts from this film by title (agreed with its thread) | *Rooms apart* |
