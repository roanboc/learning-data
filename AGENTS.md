# AGENTS.md

Guidance for any AI agent (Claude, Codex, Copilot, Gemini or others) working in this repository. [`PLAYBOOK.md`](PLAYBOOK.md) holds the detailed lessons behind the films; this file is the order to work in and the rules that always apply. The README's *What's here* explains the layout.

## The process for every new film, series or topic change

Go through the stages in order. The concept is settled before anything is drawn.

### 1. Conceptual foundation (always first)

1. **Ask for the reference framework.** Before drafting, ask the author which framework, standard or body of knowledge is the reference for the topic. Then suggest the others worth considering, with one line each on what they add (for data modelling: Kimball, Inmon, Data Vault, entity-centric; for enterprise architecture: TOGAF, Zachman, ArchiMate, BIZBOK, domain-driven design, DAMA-DMBOK). Say which one the films follow and why, and record where they adopt, extend or differ from it.
2. **Ground it in practice.** Ask the author for their real experience: what works, what goes wrong, what they would tell a newcomer. Check that every concept and process is something practitioners actually do, not only what a framework prescribes. Write that advice into the proposal's brief.
3. **Keep the essential.** Frameworks are inputs, not the syllabus. As *From words to data* did with modelling techniques and *The map before the data* with EA frameworks, name the bodies of knowledge once, then teach the insights they share. Cut what a newcomer doesn't need for the decision the film is about.
4. **Tools show, they don't lead.** Databricks and dbt are the main tools to demonstrate the ideas (dbt writes and tests the recipes; Databricks runs, stores and governs). Choose the concept first, then show it in the tools. A topic never exists because a product feature does.
5. **Write it down and confirm.** A `proposal.md` with the brief in the author's words, the reference and its alternatives, an insights table with where each comes from, the practical advice, the thread to follow, and a *Decided* table with dates. The author confirms it before any script is written.

*Ask: would an expert in each framework agree, and would a practitioner recognise it from real work?*

### 2. Story and world

Follow PLAYBOOK §1, §2, §8 and §9: the two-audience promise, one thing followed from where it's born to a human outcome, a metaphor tested against the hardest mechanism, openings varied across cultures and eras, people on every side of the problem, blameless and believable numbers, wrong drafts corrected by the people who know, and a rigour sheet for every scene.

**Every opening story has four beats, each said outright in the narration, in this order:**

1. **Scenario:** who, where, when, and what was happening.
2. **Problem:** what went wrong or what people feared, for whom, and why it mattered to them, as a harm a viewer can picture. Explain every word the story leans on (if a law lets town halls "check up on" residents: check what, and what could happen to them?), and say why things couldn't go on as they were, in the story's own words: what had to stop, or what people now needed (*Sailors needed a chart they could steer by, not just look at*; *A key has to stop working when the stay ends*). Never the stock line *Something had to change*, which every film had come to say; `site-tools/check_site.py` fails it, in English and Spanish.
3. **Solution:** what was done, and by whom.
4. **Benefits:** what got better, and for whom, with a number where the sources give one; then the same problem and benefit in the film's own data story.

The film comes back to the story along the way, and at the end to close on its benefit for data. Test it before the script goes to the author: someone who has never heard of the event can answer, from the narration alone, "why was that a problem?" and "what did the fix give people?". Each script has an *Opening story* table, a row for each beat and one for where the film comes back to the story, quoting the line that says it; `site-tools/check_site.py` fails a film whose table misses a row or quotes words its narration doesn't say. Films made before 28 October 2026 add theirs when their opening changes. A series may follow one true story across all its films instead of one per film, as the four architecture series after *The map before the data* do (the author, 11 November 2026): its opening film sets the scenario, each film's four beats come from its own part of the story, and its last film says what the story didn't achieve.

### 3. Video craft, at the highest effort

Run film and visual style work at the highest reasoning or effort setting your tool offers. Draft style frames, compare options, review full-size stills, and iterate until the result is the best you can make, not the first that passes. Apply PLAYBOOK §4 to §6 and its checklists, in particular:

- **Look:** one design language per series; colour carries meaning; living things drawn with tapering curves, weight and light; people dressed for their time; reusable components, not one-off drawings.
- **People:** an adult's proportions (for a half-figure in the 1920 px frame, a head about 225 px from crown to chin, eyes halfway down it, short hair that follows the skull, a real jaw over a neck about half as wide as the head is tall, shoulders a third of a head below the chin); one turn for the whole head, its eyes, nose, mouth, ear, hair and hat placed from one table of the face's points (`AG_FACE`), and the nose one small curve; arms down, painted with the body, and a hand only on the thing the story is about (`hold`); a prop in one place at a time; a hat inside the head's picture (figure x 160 to 440, y 250 to 560), and a cloth that falls further, such as a mantle or a shawl, painted as its own picture over the person or split between the head's picture and the body's at y 560; a person who fades drawn whole first, then faded as one picture. Look at every new person's head at full size, facing both ways, before a film uses it.
- **Phone legibility:** text at least 28 px in the 1920 px frame after zoom, or marked as decoration on purpose; lay out from the drawn size; every piece of text inside its box: make a card, a pill or a row from its words as they are drawn, after the raise to 28 px and with their capitals and letter spacing, or wrap or shorten the words (`site-tools/fits.py`); take turns when room runs out; nothing in the caption band (below y ≈ 890).
- **Motion:** spring and settle, a slow camera (at least about 1.4 s, sine ease), things carried across cuts, motion blur, no film grain. In one room, never cut: the next shot's camera glides on from where the last one is, what stays stays put, and only what changes fades; whoever moves walks or slides there, and a later moment in the room (`later:true`) lets what was there leave before what comes later comes in. Between rooms, cut where the two shots or chapters show the same people or words (`cut:true` in the shots; `gv_cutInto` or `ag_cutInto` for a chapter, which also glide a chapter on in one room); dissolve only where the whole picture changes. The older films, which fade every chapter into the next, name each join that would show something twice in `CHAPTER_JOINS` (From words to data's `shared/src/joins.js`): `glide` on, `leave` first, or `cut`. Take up a new pose from the one before over 0.3 to 0.5 s; never switch to it.
- **Narration and pace:** one idea per line; 120 to 135 words a minute (at most 140 in a chapter); voice at most 82% of any chapter; about 0.8 s after each sentence.
- **Sound:** a new palette for each film; music under the opening story, then only from time to time (at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen or in a wordless pause, about once every 10 seconds at most), never a bed under the voice, and no figure played again and again; every effect tied to something on screen, one at a time, never a burst of tiny sounds; effects soft and below about 2.5 kHz; music and effects no louder than the governance films'; held notes at even levels, a room's echo included; an urgent pulse only where the story turns to an error or a rush; mix to -16 LUFS.
- **Page speed:** a film's page does nothing heavy until the visitor reaches for the player. Make textures and places when the player asks for them (`FILM.prepare`, on a pointer, focus or Play), never as the page opens; keep each place under 2 s to paint (`tools/check.py` times them); with the processor slowed four times, as on a phone, nothing should hold the page for more than about 0.3 s. A lab never waits for the player (`FILM.ready`): it draws on its own, and `site-tools/smoke.py`'s `labs-drawn` fails a lab picture left blank before anyone touches a player. The player never paints a place while the film plays: in the watercolour films, `tools/publish.py` draws each place in `WC_PLACES` as a picture (`site/assets/<key>/places/`), and anything else the film keeps (`wc_kept`) at the frame's size or larger, such as a place at twice the size for close-ups, or that takes 50 ms or more to paint; the player shows those, keeps only what it uses within a budget (`WC_KEEP`), reuses its spare canvases and washes people in fewer layers. The video draws the full watercolour, as before.
- **Pause and think:** a question tests the idea, not the reader's eye for the longest answer. Spread the right answers across the options, never always in one place; write every option with the same length and detail, so the right one isn't the one that explains everything; and make one wrong option a near miss, almost right but wrong for a clear reason the film gives (`near:true`), with the right one `ok:true`. Keep each option clear. The Spanish pack keeps the English places. `site-tools/check_site.py` checks all of it except whether the near miss is truly near: read each question as a newcomer would.
- **Scenarios and labs:** a page shows a scenario's options, a lab's rows of options and a sorting list's cards in the order their pack writes them, so the place can give the answer away there too. In each pack, spread the right answers across the options: at most half of its scenarios' in one place, the same for its labs' rows, and never every row of one lab in the same place. Mix a sorting list's cards, so they aren't written box by box, nor turn and turn about between two boxes, and never let an order question start in order, in reverse or nearly so: the engines mix its steps another way for each scenario, with one line they all share (`mixed`), unless its pack gives a `start`. The Spanish pack keeps the English places. When options move, a saved answer may point at another option: the engines ask again where a saved pick no longer gives its saved result. Write a scenario's options with the same length and detail, as in Pause and think: each one an action or a claim with a reason of its own, the right one never the one that explains everything, and what explains the lesson in the scenario's `why`, which a reader sees after choosing. Options that only name something stay names. Write a lab's rows the same way: every phrase in a row as full as the others, and what explains the right one in its own `why`. `site-tools/check_site.py` reads every pack.
- **Known faults:** long labels spilling out of small boxes, a pointing person whose hand leaves the frame near the right edge (mirror them), labels under the captions, a pose or a held thing that jumps in one frame (blend it in from the one before), the same people or words shown twice in a dissolve (cut instead), a camera that jumps a little in, out or aside where the shot changes in one room, with what only the shot before showed vanishing in that frame (glide on, and fade what changes), two pages or sheets cross-faded in one room so their words show twice (let the first leave, `later:true`, or turn the page), a line pushed down by a new one in one frame (open its gap, then type it), the same words shown twice where one chapter fades into the next in the older films (name the join in `CHAPTER_JOINS`: glide on, let the first leave, or cut), the agent shown in two places as shots or chapters cross, or two agents, which look alike (in one room, let it move there once they have crossed, or let the first moment leave, `later:true`; between rooms, cut), a card that comes in while the camera still glides in from far off, its words under 28 px until the camera arrives (let the camera arrive, then bring the card in), a person faded layer by layer, who shows through themselves as a ghost (fade them as one picture), a face whose eyes, nose and ear are turned different ways (one turn for the whole head), a nose drawn as two curls (it reads as a squiggle; one small curve for the nostril), a hat or prop drawn in two places at once, a hat or cloth that reaches past the head's picture and is cut off there in a straight line (keep it inside, or give it its own picture), gesturing arms (keep arms down; a hand only on the thing the story is about), a hand with no arm, or a sleeve from the frame's edge longer than an arm, about three and a half hand lengths (draw the hand larger, as nearer the camera, bring the arm in from the nearer edge, or draw the person reaching; declare the hand with `__ARM__`), two thumbs drawn at the hold pose's crossed hands, floating in the middle of a card or tray (draw them at its edges), a small head on a long thin neck, a burst of tiny sounds that crackles (a few sounds, or one soft texture), a room's echo scaled by all its energy, which rings about 10 dB louder than its level, and a held note made louder or quieter by it (set the room by what it rings where the notes are, and each held note back to its own level), a poster left showing an older drawing after a fix (draw it again), a key named twice in one object, such as a chapter's moments (the second silently replaces the first), a Pause and think pack with quoted keys (its page counts no questions), Pause and think questions whose right answer always sits in the middle and is the longest option, the one that explains everything (spread them, give every option the same detail, and add a near miss), scenarios whose right answer is always the second option, a lab whose right option is the first in every row, or sorting cards written box by box, in packs no check read (spread them in the pack, in English and Spanish alike, since a page shows them in the order written; `check_site.py` now reads every pack of labs and scenarios, and *The Inner Life of Data*'s Pause and think), an order scenario that first shows its steps in order, in reverse or as two halves swapped, the same for every scenario of its length, so one solved order gives away the next (mix each scenario's steps its own way, with the engines' shared `mixed`; `check_site.py` holds every engine to that line), a scenario whose right option is the longest by far, an action with its whole reason beside wrong ones of a few bare words (give every option the same detail and a reason of its own, and keep what explains the lesson for the scenario's `why`; `check_site.py` reads the lengths of every scenario a page shows), a lab whose right phrase is the longest in nearly every row, which a count over every pack hid among the labs no page shows (make each row's phrases as full as each other, and count what a page shows; `check_site.py` reads the rows of the labs a page shows), a feature's release status said or shown, such as "in preview" in the voice or a "beta" pill on screen (it dates the film when it changes: say what the feature does, and keep its status in the script's sources), a place painted while the film plays, which freezes the picture for a moment each time on a laptop (list it in `WC_PLACES`, or keep it with `wc_kept`, and `tools/publish.py` draws it as a picture when it is the frame's size or larger or slow to paint), a canvas made, or a picture kept, at every size the camera shows it, which fills the memory past a gigabyte in one viewing (reuse one, `wc_buf` or `wc_bufAt`, and keep it within `WC_KEEP`), a film page whose title and intro retell the opening story and every step over ten lines (a title of two lines and an intro of four at most, as on *Day one*), a platform-kept copy called zero copy, such as "the lakehouse reads the same rows in place" or "not one claim was copied" for Lakebase, whose rows the platform keeps in a second form for analysis (say what copies and who keeps it; only a query sent to the source stores no copy), a thin line along the right and bottom of a person's picture in the site's player, left in a reused canvas by an earlier, bigger person (clear past what is copied out of it, as `wc_bufAt` does; `smooth.py` redraws moments with the canvases emptied), the same stock line in every film's opening, such as *Something had to change* after the problem (say what had to stop, or what people needed, in the story's own words; `check_site.py` fails the stock line), labs numbered within each film, or restarting at 1 on each film's tabs, or listed out of film order (one count for the whole pack, 1 to N in film order, on the tab and in the heading; `check_site.py` and `smoke.py` check it), Spanish chapter names written as a list in a film's `site.json` (`chapters_es`), which leaves the Spanish page's chapter buttons in English (name each chapter by its id in `narration.js`; `site-tools/build_series.py` stops on anything else), words running past the edge of their card, pill or row, as a card laid out for a width wraps its title and its lines and draws the line above the title as one, 80 px out of the card in *The question behind the request*, or a pill measures its words at the 20 px asked for and draws them at 28 (make the box from its words as they are drawn: measure at the drawn size, `phSz` in the measuring call, with the capitals and letter spacing, or wrap or shorten them; `site-tools/fits.py` holds every piece of text to its box), a line that just fits its card where the film is made and ends 3 px past it in the video, whose letters sit on whole pixels (never lay a line out to fill its box to the last pixel: give it room, or make the box from its words; `fits.py` draws the film both ways), a real person's name read by its spelling, as the voice said W. Edwards Deming's, "deeming", in three films (it is DEM-ing, with a short e: spell it "Demming" for the voice only, in the line's `say` or in `film.json`'s `say`; `check_site.py` fails the name left as written, and its `VOICE_NAMES` takes the next name found), and one place said two ways in a series whose films are made side by side, as three films of *People first* spelled Quehue and the Apurímac differently for the voice (settle how the voice says each name of a shared story once, in the series' shared words, before any film is voiced, and add it to `VOICE_NAMES` with the spellings set aside).

### 4. Check, publish, learn

1. Per film, from its `source/`: `tools/build.py` (it stops on a name declared in two files, a key named twice in one object, a cue word that isn't whole in its line, and a caption over 140 characters), `tools/check.py`, `tools/legible.py --overlaps`, `site-tools/fits.py` (every piece of text inside its box), `tools/pace.py`, `tools/quiet.py` (the sound, where the film has it), full-size stills (`tools/stills.py --size 1920`), then `tools/publish.py` (`--keep-sound` when only the pictures changed). Then `site-tools/build_series.py`, and `.github/scripts/affected.py --run`: it runs `site-tools/check_site.py`, and `site-tools/smoke.py` only on the pages the change touched, never the whole site again. `dist/` is gitignored and can hold a stale soundtrack.
   - `tools/check.py` draws every frame, 30 a second. In the governance and agent films it also stops on text smaller than 28 px, and on words on screen that name a feature's release status (From words to data's `legible.py`), times every place, finds anything shown twice where shots or chapters change, the agent included (`twice.py`), any arm that jumps in one frame (`steady.py`), a hand whose sleeve runs more than 3.5 hand lengths to the frame's edge and two thumbs floating in the middle of what they hold (`hands.py`), and, in one room, a camera, person or line of words that jumps, appears or vanishes in one frame where the shot or chapter changes (`jump.py`; all four in `films/data-governance/shared/tools/`), and the agent films stop on a gesture (any named pose but `hold`) and on paint that reaches past the edge of a person's picture (`edges.py`, in `films/what-an-agent-is/shared/tools/`). Every watercolour film (governance and master data) also draws itself as the site's player does, with its places as pictures, and stops on a moment that takes too long, anything slow painted while it plays, memory past the player's budget and a canvas made every moment, also with the processor slowed four times (`smooth.py`, in `films/master-and-reference-data/shared/tools/`). In every other film, drawn with *The Inner Life of Data*'s engine, it finds the same words shown twice where one chapter fades into the next (`twice.py` too).
   - `site-tools/fits.py`, run from a film's `source/` after its build, draws the film every half second and holds every piece of text to its box: the smallest box a viewer can see under it, whichever helper drew either. It fails on text that ends more than 2 px, and more than a tenth of its own size, past its box for a second and a half or more. It draws the film twice, on every machine: as the site's player places its letters, and as the video is drawn, with each letter moved to a whole pixel, where a line can come out about 1% wider (3 px in 420); text that leaves its box either way fails. A moment in passing isn't a fault (a card springing open, a camera on its way), the tails of letters may hang below a highlighter's band, and text marked as decoration isn't read. It also draws each caption as the smallest phone shows it, in every language the film has captions for, and holds it to its own box and the frame. A film names text that is meant to leave a box in its `film.json` (`spills`: chapter, text, why). The films made before 12 November 2026 were fixed from a list, `site-tools/fits_later.json`, which each left with its fix: the list is empty, so every film is held to its boxes. Should a stricter check find old faults, `--later` lists them there again, to fix film by film.
   - `tools/quiet.py` plays the score on its own and fails music that comes in too often or too loud, a figure repeated, a burst of effects, notes that beat, a room that rings louder than its level where the notes are, a held note the room makes more than 2 dB louder or quieter, and a pulse outside the film's urgent chapters.
   - `check_site.py` also reads every Pause and think pack: one right answer and one near miss per question, the right answer at most a quarter longer than the longest wrong one and clearly the longest in at most half a pack's questions, no option under half the longest, right answers not all in one place in a pack nor more than 40% in one place across the site, and the Spanish pack's in the English places. *The Inner Life of Data*'s questions, a block of its pack of labs and scenarios, are read too.
   - `check_site.py` also reads every pack of labs and scenarios: one right option in each choice; at most half of a pack's right answers in one place, among its scenarios and again among its labs' rows (an even share where the options are too few for half, as with three rows of two options), and no more than 40% in one place across the site's scenarios; a lab's own rows not all in one place; a sorting list's cards not written box by box, nor four or more turn and turn about between two boxes; an order's `start`, where a pack gives one, mixed (not in order, in reverse, in either from some step on round, nor with most steps where they belong), and every engine that shows order scenarios mixing their steps with the one line they share (`MIXED`); and the Spanish pack's options and cards in the English places. It reads only the labs a page shows (a series' pack can point to one lab of each film's own pack), and not the packs an engine mixes itself as it shows them (`SPREAD_SHUFFLED`: *The map before the data*).
   - `check_site.py` also reads the lengths of the options of every scenario a page shows, in English and Spanish, with Pause and think's limits: the right option at most a quarter longer than the longest wrong one, no option under half the longest, and the right one clearly the longest in at most half of a page's scenarios. It doesn't compare options that only name something (every one under 25 characters), nor the scenarios no page shows. It holds a lab's rows of options to the same limits: in each pack, the rows of the labs a page shows.
   - `tools/publish.py` stamps the poster with the player it was drawn from, and `check_site.py` fails a poster drawn from any other player. In the watercolour films it also draws each place, and anything else kept at the frame's size or larger or that takes 50 ms or more to paint, as a picture, listed in `places/places.json` with the same stamp and when the film shows it, and `check_site.py` fails pictures drawn from any other player.
   - `check_site.py` also fails any film whose voice, captions, page text, Pause and think, labs or scenarios say a feature is in preview, in beta or generally available, in English or Spanish.
   - `check_site.py` also fails a film that names Lakebase, LTAP, synced tables or a change data feed and says nothing is copied ("zero copy", "in place", "not one claim was copied", in English or Spanish): the platform keeps a copy there, even when no team runs it.
   - CI (`.github/workflows/checks.yml`) runs the build, the check that the site plays that build, `tools/check.py`, `site-tools/fits.py` and `tools/quiet.py` for every film a change touches, and `check_site.py`.
   - Checks sample what they measure. Where people or props move, also look at the frames one by one, at full size, not only at the stills.
   - `check_site.py` also fails a film whose narration or Spanish captions say the stock *Something had to change* (or *has to*, *Algo tenía que cambiar*); the reviewed films that still say it are listed in `STOCK_BEFORE`.
   - `check_site.py` also fails a film page whose intro is too long: in its `site.json`, in English and Spanish, the topic label (`kicker`) at most 32 characters, the title (`h1`) at most 75 and the intro (`lead`) at most 300, which is four lines on a laptop. Write it as *Day one* does: who, what goes wrong in the film's own world, and what is done, with the opening story left to the film (PLAYBOOK §7).
   - `check_site.py` also fails a film whose voice would misread a name as it's written. It takes each line as the voice reads it (its `say`, or its text, after the film's own `say` in `film.json`) and looks for the names in `VOICE_NAMES`: W. Edwards Deming's, said DEM-ing, which the voice reads as "deeming" unless it is spelled "Demming"; and the names the films of *People first* share from the rope bridge at Quehue, each spelled one way for the voice in every film (Quehue "Kehweh", Apurímac "Ahpooreemahk", Quechua "Ketchwa", minka "meenka"), where the spellings set aside fail too (PLAYBOOK §5).
2. Spanish captions, pages, Pause and think questions, labs and scenarios match the film's names, numbers and colours.
3. **Share the video for review.** Whenever a new or changed video is made, render it and share it with the author on a review page, with the pull request link. `python site-tools/review_page.py <film folder> --pr <number> --note "<what changed>"` makes the page from the film's own files: a 720p copy under 15 MB (made by `site-tools/review_copy.py`), a button for each chapter, and the captions in each language. Publish it as a private artifact, one page per film (a new version goes to the same link), and share the link in the conversation. Don't attach video files: the chat may only offer them as a download, and a full render can't be downloaded from it. The author watches it and either asks for corrections or approves the pull request. Never call a film done before that.
4. Turn every review finding into a check, in the series' shared tools so every film of the series runs it, and in CI where it can run. Write the lesson into `PLAYBOOK.md`, and the fault into *Known faults* above, so the next film starts from it.

## Working here

- **For parallel videos, use an isolated checkout.** Reuse the task's worktree if it already has one; otherwise `python site-tools/work.py start <release-key>` fetches main and creates a separate video branch/worktree without switching the current checkout. `work.py list` shows registered local ownership; duplicate claims made through this helper are rejected. Still check open PRs and other task worktrees for overlapping work. Use `--base HEAD` for unpublished tooling or `--no-fetch` offline. The owning worktree keeps each film's generated files separate.
- **Execute the handoff, not a duplicate checklist:** `python site-tools/work.py check` regenerates shared pages/indexes from their owners, then runs the affected plan. Voice generation invalidates edited lines automatically; don't manually delete cached WAVs. For requested audio work, `media.py index --film <key>` chooses a content-based tag and `media.py publish --film <key>` resumes/uploads and verifies that film's audio. The video-render release workflow remains author-started.
- **Check a change with `python .github/scripts/affected.py --run`** (CI runs the same task list): only the pages a change touched and the films whose source changed. Planner/workflow changes run the tooling regression tests, not every film; `--all` is the explicit full run. Published assets draw no frames; nested assets and changed audio check their owning pages. Initial pointers to unchanged tracked audio and URL-only edits validate manifests without retesting playback. `--json` explains the plan; `--film <release-key> --tier player --run` builds and compares one player.
- **A film's questions and assumptions go in its own file** under [docs/assumptions/](docs/assumptions/README.md), named as its film folder is, in the film's own pull request. `python site-tools/assumptions.py --out all.md` joins them into one file in the films' order.
- **The learning route is in [`modules/route.json`](modules/route.json).** The Topics page draws the topics as one route, with goals, beside a list with a search of what the films say; the home page only leads to the route and the Making of ([docs/navigation.md](docs/navigation.md)). A new topic goes in one of its groups; a new film or series needs nothing. The header's "?" opens a guided tour of whatever the page shows; a new control gets a step in `site/assets/learn/tour.en.js` and `tour.es.js`. `site/assets/learn/search.*.json` is never committed: the site's publish and the browser checks write it, and `python site-tools/build_series.py --search` does for a local look.
- **Find content through [modules/README.md](modules/README.md).** Module membership lives in `modules/<id>/module.json`; regenerate the catalog with `python site-tools/catalog.py --write`. Keep existing film folders, public routes and progress keys stable.
- **Published audio has per-film `audio.json` pointers.** The affected runner restores needed audio automatically; manual browser runs can use `python site-tools/media.py fetch --pages '<JSON page list>'`. Index only the film changed by the task, and commit its new MP3 with `git add -f` beside the pointer: *Store audio outside Git* publishes and verifies the release when the change reaches main, and once a day catches anything a run missed, so nobody runs it by hand. Never untrack audio before its release copies verify. See [docs/maintenance.md](docs/maintenance.md).
- **On Windows, run Python tools with `PYTHONUTF8=1`**, so they read the UTF-8 scripts, captions and pages.
- **`smoke.py` on Windows can't start `npx` from Python**: serve the site yourself (`npx http-server site -p 8777 -c-1 -s`) and run it with `--base http://127.0.0.1:8777/`; `python -m http.server` refuses the checks' parallel requests. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).
- **A film's Spanish narration** is an alternate `soundtrack.es.mp3` built from its published Spanish captions by the film's `tools/audio_es.py` (es-MX-DaliaNeural, each line fitted to its cue, mixed under the original score and effects; it needs network for the voice and caches lines in `build/es_voice/`).

## Rules that always apply

- **Look before you start.** Check open pull requests and unmerged branches for the same film or series, so nothing is made twice or overwritten.
- **Stay in the film's lane.** A series' shared source (such as `shared/src/*.js`) is bundled by every film in it; never change it for one film. Put the helper in that film's own source.
- **A shared drawing/timeline fix is a new version of every consumer.** Rebuild every film whose `release.json` `paths` name those inputs, copy its player and redraw its poster (`tools/publish.py --keep-sound`). Do the same for films merged into main since checks ran: two PRs can merge without a conflict and leave a stale player. Audio-production tool changes run synthesis/cache contract tests; any regenerated timings, pictures or published audio independently trigger their normal checks.
- **Each film keeps its own rows.** A film's entry in the release, its *Decided* rows, its legibility row and its *past* row live in its own folder (`release.json`, `decided.md`, `site.json`); a series finds its films by their numbered folders. The shared lists are made from them (README, *Each film keeps its own rows*), so threads never edit the same lines. On a clash in a generated list or page, run `site-tools/build_series.py` again.
- **Generated files are generated.** Pages made by `site-tools/build_series.py` and the *Decided* tables in `proposal.md` are never edited by hand, and `check_site.py` fails if they differ. The legibility report's table isn't committed, since every film adds a row to it: `build_series.py --legibility` prints it. Nor are the series' counts of films on the home and Scenarios pages' topic tiles: the committed pages say "Series", and the site's publish fills in the count (`build_series.py --publish`). Don't rename numbered film folders or `assets/film3/`: the release workflow and links use them.
- **Keep cited lines in place.** Rigour sheets cite files by line number (for example in the dbt project), so append or replace in place rather than shifting lines.
- **Definitions come from the glossary.** In the example dbt project, a term the business glossary holds is copied from it, with its `source:` (decision DEC-PRJ-11).
- **Owners and stewards never review pull requests.** In every film, page, lab, scenario and the example project, a definition starts in the glossary or the data contract catalogue and the code is built from it; the data team reviews the code; owners are told what a change touches; data governance steps in only where code and the glossary or an enterprise contract differ; and only canonical tables have an enterprise contract, written first by data governance's information architect and built by the data team (the author, 10 November 2026; PLAYBOOK §3). The data team's other contracts, with the teams that send it data or read its data, are agreed team to team and need no agreement from data governance (the author, 12 November 2026).
- **Business users ask in tickets; technical people review pull requests.** Finance, HR and other users raise and approve the work in a ticket (Jira or GitHub issues), linked to the branch and pull request that do it; the data team reviews the pull request; the people who asked check the result, in test or after release, before the ticket closes. A film, page, lab or example never shows a business user reviewing or approving a pull request, nor a pull request "asking" an owner to decide: the ask goes in a linked ticket (the author, 11 November 2026; PLAYBOOK §3).
- **Agents never change production.** In every film, page, lab, scenario and the example project, an agent in production only reads, finds the cause and drafts: a person approves, and the release, a plain job, makes the change. It reads there with limited access: read-only, only what its role's steps need, and generally with all personal data, and anything classified sensitive or above, masked. A film that says what an agent may read in production says that too (*personal and sensitive data masked*, as *A place on the map*, *Rights follow the role* and *The map, enforced* do), and never shows production closed to an agent's reading, nor an agent reading unmasked personal data there; the two films that say *names masked* of an agent's samples (*Tools are hands* and *Borrowed rights*) take the fuller words when those lines next change. What only needs to be started or run again, such as a pipeline that failed on a lost connection, is a plain, scheduled job's, with no model in it, even hours later, never an agent's. An agent works alone only in test, under a signed mandate (the author, 12 November 2026; PLAYBOOK §3).
- **A law is the least to do.** A film about AI or agents that names a law or a standard for how much care a system needs also says, once and in plain words, that regulation usually moves more slowly than technology, so a team takes more care than the law asks, most of all in critical systems, as *A mandate, written and enforced* does. It doesn't say which systems are critical unless it shows why. The two films that named Europe's AI law before 12 November 2026 without it (*Ethics we can't read, accountability it can't hold* and *Five stages, not a switch*) add it when that line next changes (the author, 12 November 2026; PLAYBOOK §3).
- **Spanish is captions, pages and optional audio.** Films are made in English. Spanish comes as captions and as the site's pages (page text, Pause and think, labs and scenarios); the visuals and on-screen words are not translated. A film may also have a `soundtrack.es.mp3` beside its English soundtrack: its Spanish page uses that audio, while English playback and downloadable English videos stay unchanged. Fit each Spanish line to the existing cue, review a sample before the full soundtrack, and keep each Spanish caption about as long as the English line.
- **A fictional world.** Organisations, people and numbers are invented composites. Never name a real company as the subject, or the author's private repositories used as reference.
- **Rights and respect.** Only original art; official logos unaltered and credited in `NOTICE.md`; code under MIT and content under CC BY 4.0. No visual styles or sounds borrowed from other cultures; check a story from another culture against its own sources, and label imagined futures as imagined.
- **Sources and names on the day.** List sources with the date checked. Check product names and maturity when publishing, and write the maturity, with its date, in the script's sources. A film never says a feature's release status (preview, beta, generally available): not in its voice, on screen, in its captions, on its page or in its labs and scenarios, so it needn't change when that status does (the author, 5 November 2026).
- **Write decisions down.** Every decision goes in the series' *Decided* table with its date, so the next session starts from it.
- **Heavy jobs.** Run one render at a time, in resumable chunks. A render to share for review is part of making a film; the release workflow (`release.yml`) runs only when the author starts it.
- **For every viewer.** Every interaction works by keyboard, every picture has a line of text saying what happened, pages work in dark mode and on a phone, and progress stays in the visitor's browser.
- **Plain words.** Short sentences in plain English, on the site and in docs. Internal names never appear as text on the site.
