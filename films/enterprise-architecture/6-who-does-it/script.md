## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has worked in two departments that use the same word differently can follow it, and an experienced enterprise architect finds nothing wrong in it. Work is done by roles, not names; whoever fills a role, a person, a team, a partner under a contract or an AI agent with its rights written down, is an actor, and actors change while roles stay. A domain is a part of the organisation modelled as an organisation in its own right, with its own customers, capabilities and words, and meaning changes at its edge. One definition for everyone fits no one, and here it would break a rule too; instead, each domain keeps its meaning, and the edge gets a translation: a short, agreed list of what crosses, and how. That translation is where data rules will live, and where things go wrong. **Know who does the work. Translate at the edges.**

## The story in one paragraph

In the 1890s, Tokyo bought German generators that made power at fifty cycles a second, and Osaka American ones at sixty. The two grids grew until they met in the middle of the country, and they still can't simply be joined. In March 2011, after the earthquake and tsunami, the east lost a large share of its power stations while the west had power to spare, but only about one gigawatt could cross, through a few converter stations, and around Tokyo the power was cut in turns. Making one side match the other was judged far too costly, so Japan builds more converters. Today, Tomás writes a name on each step of the night on Hill Street; Grace crosses them out, because the steps are done by roles, and the people who fill them change. The crew was a contractor's, bound by a contract; one actor that night was an AI agent, with its rights written down. Then a simple question: how many customers? Retail says 310,000 accounts; the network, 540,000 connection points. Both are right, and Ama explains the rule that keeps the two apart. Tomás draws the edge: two domains, each with its own customers, capabilities and words. One definition of customer would fit neither side and carry what the network knows across the wall; instead, the edge gets a short, agreed list of what crosses. When someone on life support moves into a home, retail hears first, and if that message is late, the rule for who goes first works from the wrong list. The owners confirm the roles, the edge and the agent's rights.

## Rigour to check before recording

- **Japan's two frequencies.** In the 1890s, Tokyo Electric Light bought generators from AEG in Germany, built for 50 Hz, and Osaka Electric Lamp bought General Electric generators from the United States, built for 60 Hz; sources commonly date them to 1895 and 1896 (some give 1897 for Osaka), so the narration says "the 1890s". Eastern Japan (Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kanto) runs at 50 Hz and western Japan at 60 Hz, divided roughly from the Itoigawa area in Niigata to the Fuji River in Shizuoka; a few areas near the line are mixed. The two networks are linked only through frequency converter stations. In March 2011, after the Great East Japan Earthquake and tsunami, many power stations in the east were shut down, and the converters between east and west could carry about 1 GW (1,000 MW, at the Shin-Shinano, Sakuma and Higashi-Shimizu stations). TEPCO cut power in turns ("planned outages") across its service area in the second half of March 2011, mostly outside central Tokyo, so the narration says "around Tokyo". Converting the whole country to one frequency has been studied several times and judged impractical, with cost estimates in the trillions of yen; Japan has instead added converter capacity, among it the 900 MW Hida–Shinano link, in operation since March 2021 (Hitachi, April 2021), with more planned. Check the dates, the capacities and the plans against a Japanese source, such as the Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO) or the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy, on the day. The disaster itself isn't shown; only the power stations going dark. Japan is drawn in the series' own style, not in the style of Japanese art, and the music has no borrowed Japanese instruments (PLAYBOOK §2).
- **The map** is a sketch of Japan's four main islands and of the frequency line, not a survey or to scale; the grids are drawn as a few lines from Tokyo and Osaka, not the real network; the converter stations and power stations are placed approximately; the smaller islands, Okinawa and national borders are left out.
- **"Fifty and sixty cycles are in the ratio five to six, a minor third"** (in the score's notes, not the narration): a just minor third is 6:5.
- **Actors and roles** follow ArchiMate 3.2: a business actor is an organisational entity, a person or an organisational unit, capable of performing behaviour; a business role is the responsibility for performing specific behaviour, to which an actor can be assigned; the assignment relationship is drawn with a dot at its source and an arrowhead at its target. A team is modelled as a business actor; ArchiMate's actor glyph is a stick figure, and the role's a cylinder on its side. A partner is modelled as a business actor outside the organisation's grouping, and a contract as a contract, a specialisation of a business object. A domain drawn as a dashed frame with a tab is ArchiMate's grouping element; ArchiMate has no "domain" element of its own.
- **The AI agent** is fictional. Modelling it as an actor with an autonomy level, decision rights and an escalation path follows the series' insight 11 (human-in-the-loop practice); whether an AI system is modelled as a business actor, an application component or both is debated, and later films come back to it. Outage management systems really do group reports and meter alarms into likely faults ("outage prediction"); the agent's rights are invented.
- **Domains** follow domain-driven design (Evans): a bounded context is where a model and its language hold, and a context map shows how contexts relate; the agreed list at the edge is close to a published language, and translating at the edge to an anticorruption layer. "A part of the organisation modelled as an organisation in its own right" is the series' insight 7, from the proposal; data mesh's domain ownership follows the same idea. The film doesn't use the terms.
- **Two kinds of customer.** In markets with retail competition, a distribution network serves every connection point in its area, whichever retailer supplies it, and usually bills its network charges to retailers, not to households; a connection point has its own identifier (for example the NMI in Australia, the ICP in New Zealand or the MPAN in Great Britain), which stays when the people living there change. A retailer counts accounts or customers, people and businesses, some of them with several connection points. The narration doesn't say the network bills retailers, to keep to one difference.
- **Ring-fencing.** In many markets, rules keep a regulated network business apart from an affiliated retailer, including limits on sharing confidential customer information (for example, the Australian Energy Regulator's ring-fencing guideline for distributors, and the unbundling and confidentiality rules for distribution system operators in the EU's electricity directive). The rules differ by jurisdiction, so the narration says "in many markets", and the example (solar connection applications as sales leads) is illustrative.
- **Life support.** In many markets, a customer who depends on power for medical equipment can be registered, and the retailer and the network must tell each other (Australia's life support rules, Great Britain's Priority Services Register). Registers are called different things in different places; the film says "life support", as in *How value reaches people*.
- **The figures** (310,000 accounts and 540,000 connection points, account 20417 and 31882, connection point 7001 2345, on site within two hours, the reports and ranks) are fictional and plausible. The roles (call-taker, duty controller, crew leader, sales adviser, billing officer) are common names; real utilities use many others.
