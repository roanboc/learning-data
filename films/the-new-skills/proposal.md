## Practical advice the films carry

The module was planned on assumptions overnight, without the author's advice from practice. This is what practitioners do, from the sources above and from the earlier films' teams, for the author to correct; the author's own advice for technical managers, sent on 10 November 2026, follows the list:

- Keep depth in one area; learn the words of the others; draw the whole route of one real question on one page; spend time with each specialist; ask "who uses this, for which decision?" (1)
- Give an agent the decision, the words and what counts; check its draft against something you know is true; the decision, and the responsibility, stay with a person. (2)
- Pair with someone who knows, an hour a day at the start; protect learning time; teach what you learned within a week. (3)
- Ask what the request is for: what will you decide with it, by when, who else decides, what would change your mind; say back what you heard; agree the question in writing. (4)
- One page: the decision first, then the answer, then the evidence; the peak, not only the average; what you don't know; the same numbers for everyone. (5)
- Never a bare no: ask what the date is for, put the trade-off where everyone can see it, offer what you can do well and when, let the people who decide choose, and keep your word. (6)
- List what the team keeps running and who uses it; stop what nobody uses, openly and reversibly; say where the time goes. (7)
- Change one small thing, check it, keep or drop it; the leader asks what's in the way, gives the time and shows the small wins. (8)
- Pair, rotate and write things down, so nobody is alone on what matters or the only one who knows; thank the person who says "I made a mistake"; protect leave. (9)
- A few rules, each with its reason; a template and good tools; let people decide where the knowledge is, and check the rules still serve them. (10)
- Every product has a named owner with the authority to act; the owner says first when something is wrong, and fixes it or raises it; never blame. (11)
- Read the measures together, as a team, to find what to fix in the system; never rank people by them; pair each with one that balances it. (12)
- Judge the team's shared work before anyone's own; ask before you judge; look for the cause (training, fit, a hard time) and remove the blockers first; measure against the team's change, not your expectations; let the person write the plan and its dates, and check it monthly; keep private matters private; say honestly where it's going. (14)
- Weekly one-to-ones about the person, never a status report; feedback about the work and its effect, both ways; goals the person helps set; credit to the team. (13)

**The author's advice for technical managers** (10 November 2026, 01:09 UTC), whom the author sees as both product owners and people leaders. The people leader's side is this module's:

- "Management and leadership are aspects of the same role. It was so harmful that many people understood leadership as something good and management as outdated and bad, competing with each other." Said once in *Many hats, one picture*, and framed in *Measure the work, not the people*.
- "You need to measure and know what to measure that delivers value, because it's hard to improve sustainably what you can't monitor. And you will always have deadlines and plans to execute. All of that requires management skills but it needs to be updated to the type of work and delivery." *Measure the work, not the people*.
- "Leadership is a bit more important than management every single time, because people in the business you lead are always the most important success factor, so the team wellbeing is the top priority. [...] Analyse the team as a whole and consider individual performance vs shared performance and not your expectations, especially when you are joining a new team." *The team comes first*.
- "You will find under performers from time to time. Many times there are valid reasons and you need to work on identifying the root cause (training, fit for role, personal traits) and removing all blockers you can, sometimes you just need a bit of patience if the person is having a difficult time, which is also a valid reason." And: "Trying to boost someone's performance can take months or even years, especially when you see there are improvements but they are inconsistent or still present a significant gap with the rest of team (as they could have also been improving performance due to new tools and processes implemented)." A film of its own, *When someone falls behind*.
- The product owner's side (services run as a business with customers, a picture beside every page, the architecture kept in a repository as the single source of truth, architecture updated with every change, a preferred platform and a path for every provider) belongs to the module *Data management*; its "picture beside every page" also reaches *Explaining so people can decide*. Where each point went: `manager-advice/where-each-point-went.md`, in the project's shared files.

## The look

**Sunlit plaster**, an assumption for the author to review, shared with the module *Data management*: the data team's rooms in daylight. A warm limewash wall, lit from a tall window on the right of the frame, whose sun falls on the wall as a slanted patch of panes with the soft shadows of leaves outside; warm timber below; timber and graphite things with soft shadows falling down and to the left. Words are on one-page sheets of thick paper pinned to the wall, each with a band of colour only where the colour means something, and every word at least 28 px as seen. The light tells the time: morning, afternoon, late, grey, or evening with a lamp. For the story of the rooms, the university's teaching building is cut open like an architect's section, each room a small stage behind the cut walls, lit when it's in use, so a viewer sees at a glance which rooms are booked, which are used and which stand empty; the camera can glide into any room.

Colour means the same in every film of both modules: **blue** design and plans; **green** done, or value; **amber** risk, cost, or waiting; **orange** data; **violet** only an agent (the agent films' ceramic sphere with its violet band, never a person). The room lights them: the side towards the window a little warm, the side away from it in the room's shade, and their soft shadow on the wall behind.

Why: a module needs one design language of its own, and this one is a room where people work things out, calm and spare, with the light doing the work. ## Rules from the plan

- **Draft, and the merge is the release.** Each series' `series.json` says `"draft": true`; a film merges into main once its checks pass, and that is its release. Nobody runs the release workflow for these films.
- **No release status** in a film, its captions, its page or Pause and think; a film keeps the status it read, with its date, in its script's sources only.
- **Nothing personal about the author** on the site: no employer, role history, personality type or hobbies. The four ideas are taught from their own sources, never as the author's philosophy.
- **Light on tools.** A tool appears only where the story's work happens in it (a pull request, a catalog entry with its owner, a dashboard, the one list of requests), drawn at the level of what people do, with its official logo, unaltered, where a film names it.
- **Agents propose, people approve.** An agent drafts; nothing reaches production without a person, and every change is approved by someone other than its author.
- **Blameless, and fair to everyone.** People on every side of a problem, each right about what they saw. Measures are the team's, never a target for one person; nobody is ranked.
- **The kit as it is.** Every film bundles `films/the-new-skills/shared/` unchanged; a film's own pictures go in its own source, with a prefix of its own; a change every film needs goes in a pull request of its own.
- **The fictional university**, a year ahead and tagged as imagined; Spanish as captions and pages only.
