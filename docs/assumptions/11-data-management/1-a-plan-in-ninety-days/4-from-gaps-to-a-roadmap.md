## The opening: Switzerland's clock-face timetable (1982) and Rail 2000

| # | Question | Assumption made | Where it shows | If it's wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the opening right: Switzerland's clock-face timetable (1982) and Rail 2000? | Yes, as the brief and `../openings.md` suggested, in the four beats. Checked on 10 November 2026: the railways' share of travel from 27.8% (1960) to 16.0% (1970), said "from over a quarter to a sixth"; the 1972 brochure by three young railway engineers ("the timetable is the main ingredient"); the fast new lines "implying billions", dropped in the early 1980s; the clock-face timetable from 23 May 1982; Rail 2000's hubs and its slogan, "as fast as necessary, not as fast as possible"; the vote of 6 December 1987; built in stages after the costs rose, the first stage keeping one of four new lines (Mattstetten to Rothrist, on Bern to Olten); the first stage opened on 12 December 2004, about 130 projects; Bern to Zürich under an hour; about 2,500 km by train per person in 2019, the most in Europe (LITRA, via swissinfo.ch) | *Every hour, at the same minutes*; the script's *Rigour* | The opening chapter (its lines, pictures and music) and the three lines that come back to it |
| 2 | Which claims were left out or softened? | "12% more trains" from 1982 was left out (no source found); "the railways were in debt" became "ran at a loss" (the deficit, over 700 million francs, in Hürlimann 2005); the fast lines "were costly" became "would cost billions" (Hürlimann's words) | The opening | One line each, voiced again |
| 3 | How to show the harm before 1982? | Trains at different minutes every hour, a change meaning a long wait on the platform, and people without a car (students, workers, older people) looking up times and waiting: the general picture that follows from the 1981 interpellation ("more trains, more regular intervals, better connections"), said as such in the script, not as a quote | *Every hour, at the same minutes* | Two lines, and the platform's board and people |
| 4 | How to draw Switzerland without borrowing its look? | The module's sunlit plaster: valleys in soft layers with a town, a railway and a motorway; a platform with a plain canopy and a plain clock (never the railway's own clock design, logo or livery); a schematic map of six cities and the main lines; nobody a likeness. | 5 | How is the opening's harm tied to data? | "Built all at once, it would take years, and if the money stopped half way, nobody would have anything to use" | The end of the opening | One line and its picture |
| 6 | Where does the film come back to the opening? | Three times: "As the timetable decided what Switzerland would build, the target decides what comes first"; "Like Rail 2000's first stage, each works on its own"; and at the end, the hubs connected beside stage one's door, list and measure | *What each unlocks*, *Each stage on its own*, *Approved* | Three lines |
