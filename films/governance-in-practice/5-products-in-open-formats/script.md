# Governance you can use · Products in open formats: script

*The script of *Products in open formats*, film 5 of 6 of *Governance you can use*, the third series of data governance, in nine chapters, in English, painted in watercolour. Drafted on 28 October 2026 and made on 29 October 2026, when the author asked for the films of the three series to be made and merged one after another. It follows the plan the author approved on 23 October 2026 (film 18 of that plan: the tools films open on a scene of the university's own, on a line from the film they expand), and the rule of 28 October 2026 for every opening (scenario, problem, solution and benefits, each said outright; *Opening story* quotes them). Every file on screen is the example project's own, as it is on main, or one of *Governed in dbt*'s worked examples kept beside it (*What the example project has*); the products check's and the impact check's lines are their own, captured in a scratch copy. Ontos's pages are drawn from its user guide, and Databricks' words on its Labs projects from its Labs page, as they stood on 29 October 2026. As the author asked for these films, it isn't rendered here: the release workflow renders it.*

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never read a data contract can follow it, and a data architect or a data product owner finds nothing wrong in it. *A product with an owner* made each table other teams read part of a product, with a promise and an owner who keeps it; *Change without surprises* kept a change from breaking its readers; *Where each piece lives* gave every artifact one home. This film shows the promise as files: the product's file, written by its owner; its contract, written by a script from the model's own code, so the two can't disagree, and checked on every pull request; the check that every table other teams read is in exactly one product; the impact check that stops a breaking change, and the version that goes beside the old one, with a date to go; who reads each product, and what it's built from; the two open standards both files are written in, kept by Bitol at the Linux Foundation; and a catalogue on the platform, Ontos, from Databricks Labs, that imports a copy, while the files stay at home in the project. **A contract in an open format outlives the tool.**

## The story in one paragraph

Today, in Noor's room, Noor and Clara go over Clara's product. Noor designs the university's data and its six data products, each data with a promise and an owner who keeps it; Clara's is the count of learners near an award, ready by seven on census day. But the promises were typed by hand into a catalogue, where people look for data, while the tables changed every week: the catalogue could promise a column the table no longer had, and on census day a faculty's report could break, just when it's needed to plan next semester's places; and each catalogue kept its promises in its own format, so a new one meant typing them all again. Now each promise is a file kept with the code, in two open standards that any tool can read, one for the product and one for its table's contract; the contract is written from the table's own code, and a change that would break it stops. When Jun added a column to credentials, its contract grew by a line by itself, and no report broke; all ten tables other teams read have a promise that's checked on every change; and whichever catalogue shows them, the promises stay the university's. The film then opens each piece: Clara's product file, with what it's for, how to use it, its owner, its table, its service levels and where to ask; the contract, written by a script from the model and the product's file, each line of the model threaded to its line, and the check that fails when someone edits it by hand; the ten shared tables in six products, and the check that stops a new one in none; a rename in Clara's table, stopped by the impact check, the old version kept with a date to go, and credentials' two versions, the first retiring on 31 March 2027; who reads each product, and what it's built from, so Mei knows who to tell; the two standards, Bitol's, and the versions the project keeps; and Ontos, which imports each contract, lays the products out by domain, has a steward review each before it's published, checks every product each night, and links a column to its business term, but is a Labs project, with no formal support, so it holds a copy. On census day next March, Anil, in the Faculty of Health, plans next semester's places from Clara's product by seven, whichever tool shows it.
