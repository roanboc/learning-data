# Agents at work on data · Plan, define the check, do, check: script

*The script of* Plan, define the check, do, check*, Film 5 of 9 of* Agents at work on data*, the second series of the module on AI agents in data work, as built: 8:50, in eight chapters, in English, in the module's marble-and-oak look. Written on 4 November 2026 from the series' briefs (section 2.5), the way the author set for the module ("I don't need to check the scripts as I already provided all my insights and the plan is right, so just ask me any question you might have for each video if needed", 3 November 2026), and made the same day. On 5 November 2026, as the author asked, *Define the check* was changed to start from what people often don't know, the answer itself, and *Do* to show it (*Rigour*, *When nobody knows the answer*). It follows the four-beat rule for every opening (AGENTS.md, *Story and world*). Every claim is checked against its source on the day (*Rigour*, *Sources*). The university and its people are the series' invented ones; Singapore's water study, its numbers and its parade are real, told from the water agency's and the ministry's own accounts.*

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never worked with an AI agent can follow it, and a quality engineer, an analytics engineer who works with agents every day, and a water engineer who knows Singapore's story each find nothing wrong in it. The film shows the work cycle the author teaches: before an agent starts, a person sets the goal, what it reads, the tools and data it may and may not use, and how the result will be checked, which is rarely the answer itself, since nobody knows it yet, but what any right answer must meet: how the parts relate, and what the words mean; what everyone already uses can be wrong, so where the data disagrees the agent says so, with its evidence, and where nobody can say what's right it brings the options for a person to choose; the agent builds, tests and documents, with evidence for every claim; a person checks the work against the plan and owns it, and a second person approves it for production; and each loop leaves something for the next. It tells, in full, the author's own example of what goes wrong without such a plan: an agent that searched the web for what its tool server already gave, because nobody had said what was there. Takeaway: **Decide how you'll check before the agent starts.**

## The story in one paragraph

Singapore, the late 1990s: an island city of four million people, with little land to catch the rain, buying much of its water from Malaysia under two agreements that end in 2011 and 2061. Engineers can clean used water until it's pure enough to drink, but who would drink water that went down a drain? If people don't trust it, the country can't use it. So in 1998 the water agency and the environment ministry begin a study, and before the test plant makes any water they write down how it will be judged: 190 physical, chemical and microbiological measures, from the world's drinking-water standards; a panel of experts from Singapore and abroad helps set the rules and checks the results for two years. In July 2002 the panel finds the water well within the standards; that August, at the parade for the country's birthday, about 60,000 people raise a bottle of it and drink; since 2003 it has flowed into the reservoirs, and today it is tested for about 300 measures and audited every year by experts from outside. At the university, Jun's team once gave an agent a goal and nothing else, and it spent 40 minutes and 38 web pages on the census rules that Docs gives in one call, bringing back another university's. Now Clara asks for the census-day count by level of study, a number nobody knows yet. Jun writes one page first: the goal, what to read, the tools it may use, where it may write, what it must never do, a limit, and two checks on what any right answer must meet, how the parts relate and what the words mean, which the agent writes as tests before any model and only a person can change. What everyone already uses can be wrong, sometimes for years: if the data disagrees, the agent says so, with its evidence, and where nobody can say what's right, it brings the options. The agent's first run is 28 short: exchange students with no level, and none has had one since 2021, so every split by level since then was short. It doesn't drop them or touch the test; it asks Mei, who owns the meaning, with its evidence and two options that both add up. She decides they count as non-award, a level of its own, and the years before go to Planning, for people to look into; the tests pass; the agent documents the model from the glossary and opens a pull request with its evidence. Jun checks it against the plan and answers for it; Noor approves it for production; the agent never approves. The loop leaves a decision, metadata, an updated skill and a plan the next one starts from. In Singapore the measures came before the first drop; at the university the checks came before the agent's first step.

### 6 · Check · 7:00–7:46

**Narration.** Now a person checks the work, against the plan, not against the agent's word. ## Rigour to check before recording

Checked on 4 November 2026. The research notes are in `notes.md` beside this script in the project's files.

- **The opening, Singapore.** About four million people in the late 1990s (3.93 million in 1998, 4.03 million in 2000, the World Bank's series). Little land to catch and store rain, and water bought from Malaysia under the 1961 and 1962 agreements, the first expiring in 2011 and the second in 2061 (Singapore's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, *Water agreements*). Used water cleaned by membranes and ultraviolet light (PUB: microfiltration or ultrafiltration, reverse osmosis, ultraviolet). The fear: the ministry and PUB's own account speaks of "the psychological barrier towards reclaimed water" (25 September 2002). The study: "A 2-year NEWater Study was initiated in 1998", with a demonstration plant of 10,000 cubic metres a day (PUB, 2010); the plant's design began in February 1999 and it was commissioned in April 2000, while the sampling and monitoring programme, with its 190 physical, chemical and microbiological parameters drawn from the WHO's and the US EPA's drinking-water guidance, was set up in 1999 (search extracts of PUB and the press, 4 November 2026): so "before the test plant makes any water". The panel: "Comprises both local and foreign experts", "Involved in audit and set up of the study protocol" (PUB, 2010); the film's "helps set the rules, and checks the results". "For two years": "comprehensive physical, chemical and microbiological analysis conducted over a two-year period" (MSE and PUB, 25 September 2002). The report: received on 11 July 2002; NEWater "well within the drinking water standards specified by the US Environmental Protection Agency and World Health Organisation" (the same). The parade: "60,000 Singaporeans toasted to NEWater at 2002 National Day Parade" (PUB, 2010; PUB's *Our Water Story*: "60,000 people toasting the nation's birthday with bottles of NEWater"); the parade was on 9 August 2002. Into the reservoirs: "2 mgd of NEWater (less than 1% of total daily water consumption) into our raw water reservoirs in Feb 2003" (MSE and PUB, 2002); the film says only that it has flowed into the reservoirs since 2003. Today: "tested for about 300 parameters" and "subject to rigorous audit processes annually, conducted by local and international multidisciplinary experts" (US EPA, *Water reuse in Singapore*, citing PUB, November 2023). Not said: the "up to 40%" of demand (a figure from 2017, before a factory closed in 2024); the panel's members' names; the 98% who accepted it in a survey at the end of 2002 (true, PUB 2010, cut for length).
- **The author's example.** As *A model, reasoning, hands and a loop* tells it briefly ("one of the team's agents once searched the web for the census rules, though Docs had them, because its plan didn't name its tools. Now each plan names its tools, and gives the agent only those the job needs"), told here in full: the goal and nothing else, the web, 38 pages in 40 minutes where Docs answers in one call, another university's rule, caught in Jun's review. The numbers are the series' (`series-2/numbers.md`), set by this film and named by *How it goes wrong*. His practice: most waste comes from a plan that didn't say what the agent may use (intent, execution, validation); the university and the team are the series' invented ones.
- **The work cycle.** Plan, do, check, act: the cycle of quality improvement that Walter Shewhart drew as specification, production and inspection (1939) and W. Edwards Deming taught in Japan (1950); here the check is decided before the doing, as in test-driven development (Kent Beck, 2002) and in the example project's own step 5, "Tests, before any model". The film names neither: it shows the idea in the author's words.
- **When nobody knows the answer** (added 5 November 2026, as the author asked). His practice: often nobody knows the value an agent will find, or even the measure, so the expectation isn't a number but how the concepts relate, or how a measure is defined; data a university has used for years can turn out to be wrong, and an agent that reads every system can be the one to find the mismatch, which may go back years; where an expectation can't be set, the agent gathers options. In software testing this is the oracle problem: Elaine Weyuker, "On testing non-testable programs" (*The Computer Journal*, 1982), names among the programs that can't be checked against a known answer those "written to determine the answer", since "if the correct answer were known, there would have been no need to write the program"; the way out is to test what any right answer must satisfy. The film's two checks are of that kind: a relation (the levels add up to the count already reconciled with the census report) and a definition (every student has exactly one level, as the glossary defines it). The film names neither the term nor the paper. The new numbers, exchange students with no level since 2021 and the second option's 19 and 9, are the series' (`series-2/numbers.md`); 19 and 9 add to 28, and 13,925 and 4,487 to 18,412.
- **The data story.** Census day, 31 March 2026, and 18,412 students, never "enrolments", reconciled with the census report (*A model, reasoning, hands and a loop*). The split by level is this film's (`series-2/numbers.md`): undergraduate 13,906, postgraduate 4,478 and non-award 28, which add up to 18,412; the first run 18,384, 28 short. Non-award is a level in the government's statistics for students not enrolled in a degree, among them study abroad and exchange students (Australian Government, *Research snapshot: non-award*, read in a search extract). The tools, Catalog, Query and Docs, read only, and the limit of 10 rounds, are *A model, reasoning, hands and a loop*'s. Who approves what, the agent's own branch and development schema, evidence with its query, and never weakening a test are the example project's `AGENTS.md`; the agent writes the tests first and Jun approves them (`docs/process.md`, step 5). The module's rules: agents never approve, their review only for reference; a change to production needs a second person, for agents too; read-only tools, drafts only in a sandbox, no sensitive data.
- **Not shown.** The agent's own loop step by step (*A model, reasoning, hands and a loop* showed it); how a test is written in dbt; the government's other levels (enabling, sub-bachelor); any product.
