# People first · No two alike: script

*The script of *No two alike*, film 3 of 4 of *People first*, the series that opens the module *Leading data people*, in six chapters, in English, drawn in the module's sunlit plaster. Written on 12 November 2026 from the series' proposal (`films/people-first/proposal.md`), which the author confirmed that day, and from his decision of the same day on personality: the five broad traits as a reference for understanding that a team is different people with different traits, all of them valuable for working together, and never a tool for assessment, which rests on outcomes and team culture. It was made as the module's other films were, on stated assumptions and with no script review; this film's questions and assumptions are in `docs/assumptions/10-leading-data-people/1-people-first/3-no-two-alike.md`. ## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone new to leading people can follow it, and an experienced data manager finds nothing wrong in it. The film's idea is that the people in a team differ by degree, not by type; that a team has a use for every mix of traits; and that a leader finds out how each person works by asking, never by sorting them by a type, a test or a first impression. It shows nobody's place on a scale, ranks no trait above another, names no test and draws no letters, and never uses a trait to judge anyone.

**Takeaway:** "Ask each person how they work. Never sort them."

## The story in one paragraph

Lu, in what is now eastern China, about 2,500 years ago. A teacher there, Kong Qiu, whom the West calls Confucius, turns no student away: whoever brings the smallest gift, a bundle of dried meat, is taught, so his students are of every kind. Zilu is bold, and the teacher tells him he wouldn't take to war a man who fights tigers with his bare hands. Ran You holds back, and the teacher tells him it isn't strength he lacks: he sets his own limit. One rule for both would fail one of them. One day Zilu asks whether he should act at once on what he hears, and the teacher says his father and elder brothers are alive: ask them first. Ran You asks the very same question, and the teacher says: act at once. A third student, Gongxi Hua, has heard both answers, says he is confused, and asks why. "Ran You holds back, so I urged him on. Zilu has the energy of two, so I held him back." Each gets the answer he needs; asked if the two could serve in government, the teacher says yes to both, one decisive, the other skilled; and a historian later counts both among the seventy-seven who mastered what he taught. At the university, in February 2027, Helena Novak is in her third week as the data manager. On Monday 15 February Erin Walsh, the platform engineer, says nothing in an hour of planning, and Helena reads it as lack of interest. It's her first guess, and it's wrong: on Tuesday morning Erin's note names two things the plan missed. On Wednesday Helena asks instead of guessing, and Erin says she thinks after a meeting, not in it. The film then gives its reference: five broad traits, each a scale; a person moves along them with the situation and the years; no place on a scale is the right one; and traits are for understanding people, never for assessing them. On Thursday Yusuf Demir proposes a test that sorts people into types. A type is a scale cut in two, and most people are near the cut, so Helena says yes as a way to start talking, with four rules. What the team keeps is a page each person writes in their own words, *How I work*: Helena writes hers first, and by Friday 26 February all seven have shared one. From Monday 22 February her meetings are built for the whole range, and that Monday Erin speaks in her turn. The film closes where the story stops being a guide: the teacher decided what each one needed, and Helena asks.

**Practice.** A practitioner will recognise the planning meeting where the same few people talk and one says nothing; the note that arrives the next morning with what the meeting missed; the colleague who proposes a personality test in good faith, because it once gave a team words for their differences; the manager who writes her own page first; a heading left blank, and nobody asking why; and a question sent the day before, which costs a minute and changes who speaks. The author's own experience with such tests wasn't asked before the film was made (the series' assumption 3): it is on his checklist.

### 3 · By degree · 3:48–5:14

**Narration.** How do people differ? Most research describes it with five broad traits, often called the Big Five. How open to new things. How organised. How outgoing. How agreeable. - **The exchange.** *Analects* 11.21 as the Chinese text read numbers it (chapter XXI in James Legge's translation, 22 in editions that count differently): Zilu's question, "your father and elder brothers are alive", Ran You's same question, "act on it at once", Gongxi Hua's "I am confused. May I ask?", and the teacher's reason. The film's words are translated from the Chinese and checked against Legge ("Ch'iu is retiring and slow; therefore, I urged him forward. Yu has more than his own share of energy; therefore I kept him back"). "He means: ask them first" is the film's gloss, said as one: Legge has "to be consulted", and Zhu Xi's commentary has that with a father and elder brothers alive, some things aren't one's own to decide alone. In the text the teacher calls the two by their given names, Qiu and You; the film keeps to "Ran You" and "Zilu", and says "Kong Qiu, whom the West calls Confucius" once.
- **The teacher, the place and the time.** Kong Qiu of Lu, 551 to 479 BC as Sima Qian dates him: "about 2,500 years ago". Lu lay in what is now Shandong, in eastern China. "Turns no student away": *Analects* 7.7, "From anyone who brought as little as a bundle of dried meat, I have never withheld teaching".
- **What he tells each of them.** The tiger: 7.10 on the page read (7.11 in other editions).
