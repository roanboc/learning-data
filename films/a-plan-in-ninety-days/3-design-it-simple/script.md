## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never designed an organisation's data can follow it, and an experienced data manager or enterprise architect finds nothing wrong in it. It is the module's define film: after listening, design the target state for the business, the solutions and the data together, from a few principles anyone can use to decide, each with what it rules out. Draw the target in the same five layers as the current state, each on a page with its picture. Keep the centre small and helpful, with partners in the business who build their own area's products with its tools; write how the work flows as one page per procedure, with its roles, and one front door. Try the design with one partner before everyone, and fix what doesn't work. A design simple enough to explain is simple enough to run, and a good design still has to be argued for. **Start from the decision; design the fewest parts that serve it.**

## The story in one paragraph

Korea, in the 1440s: the kingdom of Joseon, under King Sejong. Its people wrote in Chinese characters, a sign for each word, thousands of them, made for another language; learning them took years, so most people couldn't write down what they wanted to say. In a court, a person's words were written down in characters they couldn't read, and one wrong character could punish someone innocent, with no way for them to check. King Sejong started from what people needed, to write the way they speak: in 1443 he made 28 letters, the consonants drawn from the mouth and throat as they make each sound, from five basic shapes, with a stroke added for a stronger sound, and the vowels from three marks, a dot for heaven, a flat line for the earth and a standing line for a person; put together a syllable at a time, they write any word. One of his own scholars, Choe Manri, objected that they would turn people away from learning in characters; the king argued for them, and in 1446 published the letters, with a book eight of his scholars wrote to explain them. Its closing words say a quick learner can know them before the morning is over, and anyone within ten days. Today they're called Hangul, both Koreas write with them, and in the South about 98 adults in 100 can read and write. A year from now, at a university, there are eleven ways to ask for data, three copies of the staff list and 41 working days to wait. In March 2027 Helena Novak, its data manager, and the team write five principles, each with what it rules out, and settle two choices by them; with Noor, the data architect, she draws the target on Noor's map in five layers, each a page with its picture. In place of today's worst of two designs, one queue at the centre and private copies at the edges, she designs the way between: a small team in the middle that keeps the platform, the standards, the shared products and the guardrails, and helps, and seven data partners in four faculties and three offices, about a day a week each, given by their dean or director. A head of school writes back that partners take people away from teaching, and the design answers it: about 40 people already do that work, in spreadsheets. The work flows by six procedures, ask, choose, build, release, fix and retire, a page each with its picture, and starts at one front door, one form with five questions, opening on Monday 7 June. Anil, in the Faculty of Health, tries the build page on Health's report of casual teaching hours; his change waits four working days at step four, "the team checks it", so step four now names one reviewer, from a rota, within two working days, never the person who built it. On Monday 5 April, the define milestone: every part fits on a page. The king's letters could be learned in a morning, so people used them; a design anyone can learn in a morning is one people use.

**Where the film adopts, extends or differs.** It adopts TOGAF's order (the target after the baseline, both before the gaps) and its kind of principle, with a reason and what follows from it; it makes what follows sharper, as one thing each principle rules out. It adopts data mesh's domain ownership and federated governance as the shape of the operating model, and differs on scale: a university of this size doesn't run a full mesh, so it gets the way between, a small team in the middle and seven partners who give about a day a week, as Strengholt's shapes in between allow. The words stay plain: *products*, *partners*, *the platform*; no topology is named. It extends both with the practice the module's plan asks for: business, solutions and data designed together, on one map; one page per procedure, each naming its four roles, with its picture; one front door; and the design tried with one partner before everyone.

**Practice (the module's plan, the author's deck as input, and practitioners).** Principles fail when nothing could fail them ("data is an asset"); written with what each rules out, anyone can settle a choice without asking the data manager. A target drawn as one picture, across business, solutions and data, shows a part with no decision behind it. A central team that builds everything becomes a queue, and a faculty that can't wait keeps its own copies; the way between is a small, helpful centre and partners in the business, given time by their dean or director, not asked for it on top of their work. A procedure that fits on a page, with who asks, who decides, who builds and who checks, is one people follow; "the team checks it" is a step nobody owns. Try the design with one partner first: the gap is found by one person, not by everyone. The author's words (10 November 2026): design every layer, strategy to technology, and a picture beside every page, drawn with it, never after.

**What the film leaves to others.** How a leader lets people decide is the leading data people module's (*Rules that let people decide*, named only once it's on main; the film says "The leading data people module tells how a leader lets people decide."); turning the gaps between today and the target into stages is *From gaps to a roadmap*'s, the next film; moving the map's source into the team's repository is *One map, in the repo*'s; the front door's first list is *One list, not twenty*'s.
