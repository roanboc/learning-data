## What changed for pack 1

The opening, the story, the people, the numbers and the take-away stay, and so does most of the narration. Every picture changes. What changed:

- **No files or tool screens.** Anil's pull request becomes a change request: a plain form listing the list's columns, each with its class. The three checks and their thirteen messages become the sign-off sheet the series shares with *Who decides*, *Who may see it*, *Rules that run* and *Teachers of data*: the change comes back stamped "sent back", with four gaps in red (no owner; ten columns that don't say whether they identify a person; a faculty that isn't in the register; no product). Columns have plain names: learner code, name, email, award code, award, and so on.
- **The skill becomes a checklist.** The skill's file becomes one printed page, "How we review a change for governance", with seven steps and four don'ts in plain words. The narration says "checklist", never "skill" or "file".
- **The agent's note is three cards.** Each finding has three dashed cyan cards, "where", "our rule" and "who decides", and its draft sits where the answer goes, for a person to accept. The agent's comment at night is the same kind of note.
- **Governed, on the university's own papers.** The page for agents becomes "Rules for AI helpers", beside binder 26. Sam's access screen becomes the agent's access request, approved in words. The reviewers file becomes a page of the register of owners: "Who signs off what".
- **Plain words.** A check, not a test; a change, not a pull request; "how it's built" for Jun's part, not "the code"; long codes, not hashes; "Health", not HLT. The agent is "an AI agent" at first, then "the data team's AI agent", then "the agent".
- **Two lines say more.** The symptom is said outright: Jun is the one who knows the rules, and every change nobody else owns waits for him. And the third finding says what it shows: the faculty wrote a rule, nothing checks it, and no check could have found it, because it takes reading.
- **What's worth keeping, named at the end.** The last chapter names it: a checklist in plain words, rules for AI helpers, a register of who signs off what, and people who decide.
- **Policy 12's page** is the one *Off the shelf* leaves after its own pack 1 update: 12.3 and 12.4 swap places, and Yusuf's notes read "a field no one can leave empty", "checked every night" (twice) and "a label".
- **Moved to pack 3,** to *Governed in dbt* (film 14): the skill as a file, the agent reviewing pull requests, the checks and their messages, the agent's own access, and the drafted classes as the files hold them. The rigour sheet keeps what each plain word stands for.

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has waited days for someone to review their change can follow it, and an experienced data governance lead finds nothing wrong in it. The film takes binder 26, *AI and automated tools*, and its line "AI use follows the organisation's data policies", and shows both halves of it. **AI for governance:** the rules that can be checked are written plainly, as a checklist an agent follows on every change. A review that waited for one person comes at once, with a draft and the evidence for each finding, and the name of the person who decides it. **Governance for AI:** the agent is held to the same policies as everyone, by the university's rules for AI helpers: its own identity, only the access Sam grants it, counts instead of personal data, a glossary it only reads, and no power to approve, because only owners sign off. The agent finds, drafts and flags; people decide. Where it isn't sure, it says so, and leans to protection. And an agent can only follow what's written plainly enough to check, so its checklist is open for anyone to read, question and change, through the same review as everything else. What's worth keeping: rules written plainly enough to check, rules for AI helpers, and a register of who signs off what. **An agent checks what's written. People decide.**

## The story in one paragraph

Imagined, and labelled so: a few years from now, before going home, Anil Kurian, an analyst in the Faculty of Health, adds a column to a list: each learner's date of birth. At three in the morning, an AI agent reads the change against governance's written rules, and finds that nothing says the new column is personal data, so nothing would protect it. It doesn't change the change or approve it: it leaves a note, with where, the rule, who decides, and a fix to consider. At eight, Anil agrees, and marks the column. Back to today, and Anil's first change: the list itself, the faculty's call list for its student advisers, with seven learners, how far each is from finishing an award, and a name and an email to call. The checks send it back with four gaps. None says what's right for this list, or who decides, so Anil asks Jun, who knows the rules, and waits: every change that nobody else owns waits for Jun. Yusuf and Noor write governance's rules as a checklist: seven steps, and four things the agent never does. The data team's AI agent reviews Anil's change. It passes two things and makes three findings: nobody answers for the list; ten columns don't say whether they identify a person; and the faculty wrote a rule that nothing checks, which no check could find. Where it isn't sure, it says so: one long code has two values for seven learners, and its description says it's made from the award, so it may not be about a person. The checklist says to draft the stricter class and ask, and Anil confirms it's a course. The faculty, Yusuf and Jun approve, each for their part; the agent decided nothing. The agent is governed too, by the rules for AI helpers: Sam grants what it may read, and only owners sign off, so binder 26's line goes green. And policy 12's last line, "Staff take care to use terms correctly", is the kind no agent can check. The night a few years from now is made of what's worth keeping.

## The look

*Watercolour*, the look of the series (*Off the shelf*, *The look*): a pencil line and paint on paper, only the university's own places, and colour that carries meaning: gold is governance's decisions and a person's decision, cyan the technical side, green what runs in the work, grey what can go, and red what nobody owns yet.

- **This film's places.** The campus at dusk and at night (a night wash over the painted campus, its lamps lit, the clock at three); the Faculty of Health's office in the old building, close at Anil's desk, at dusk, in the morning and by day, and its screen, close, at night and in the morning; the data team's room and its board; Yusuf's office, its board, and his desk from above; the platform team's desk, close; and papers and screens, close. No homes: "before going home" is Anil leaving the office.
- **Only the university's own papers.** Every word on screen is on a form, a sheet, a page, a register, a card or a board, in plain words. There are no files, no code, no column names as code, and no tool screens. A screen shows the same plain form a person would print. The sign-off sheet and the register are the series' shared pictures, drawn as *Who decides*, *Who may see it*, *Rules that run* and *Teachers of data* draw them.
- **The agent is not a person.** It has no face and no body. It's a small four-pointed star, turning slowly, at the top of a dashed cyan card, the sign the master data films gave an agent; a cyan line shows what it's reading. What it writes is on cyan cards; what a person decides is gold.
- **Classes are the series' chips.** Each column's class of personal data is a chip beside it, in *Who may see it*'s colours: plum for direct, a paler plum for pseudonymous, plain paper for none. A class the agent drafts sits in its dashed cyan pill; a class a person decides is ringed or washed in gold. The film draws no mask: the only values it shows are the new list's, whose columns have no class yet, so nothing masks them; the agent counts them, and shows none.
- **Counts, never values.** Names and emails on Anil's list are soft grey bars, never words. The agent's evidence is counts and tally marks.
- **The future is labelled.** The imagined night has its corner tag, and the campus is the same campus: the film doesn't guess at how the future looks.
