# From words to data: the series' own questions and assumptions

*Data modelling* · *From words to data*, films 2 to 6

[Watch the series on the site](https://data.learn-all.org/from-words-to-data/)

> Kept by the thread "Check that text stays inside its box", 12 November 2026.

The series was made with the author from 28 September 2026, so it had no list here. On 12 November 2026 he saw words run past a card on a phone in another series, and asked: "Can we check this via code to fix those glitches?" The check (`site-tools/fits.py`) found 11 pieces of text past their boxes in five films of this series. They were fixed while he was away. Each line: the question I would have asked, what I did instead, and where it shows. The series' *Decided* table has the same change in one row.

| # | Film | Question | Assumption | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | All five | Where a box is too small for its words on a phone: shorten the words, or change the box? | Keep every word. A box is made from its words at the size they are drawn (28 px on a phone). Where the box can't grow, the words take two lines. A small picture of a phone keeps its screen's words small, as part of the picture (row 6). | The 11 places below; *What's in a word* and *Keeping it true* had none |
| 2 | 2. *Older than the systems* | Each program's status, *ran · no errors*, ran past its card. Widen the two cards, or wrap? | Wrap: *ran*, with its green light, then *no errors*, both against the card's right edge. The cards keep their size and place. | 2:39 to 2:45 |
| 3 | 2. *Older than the systems* | On the yardstick, the last mark, *counts towards*, ran past the ruler's end. | A mark at either end moves in until its words are on the ruler: this one, 12 px to the left. The cross under it stays where it was. | 4:09 to 4:17 |
| 4 | 2. *Older than the systems* | *student system team* was wider than the card of the table *awards*. | That card is as wide as its team's name, 19 px wider, and the gaps between the three cards are a little smaller, so the row still fits in its band. | 4:31 to 4:48 |
| 5 | 3. *Built to write, built to read* | In the small star beside the seven joins, the fact's second line, *Σ count · credit points*, was wider than the fact. | The fact is as wide as that line. Its dimensions and the two joins stay where they were. | 3:59 to 4:12 |

| 10 | 3. *Built to write, built to read* | In the large star, the fact's grain, *one row per credential awarded*, filled its card to the last pixel. In the video, where every letter sits on a whole pixel, it ran 3 px past the card: CI's check found it, and the same check had passed on the machine the film was fixed on. Wrap the line, or widen the card? | Widen: the fact's card is as wide as that line with 24 px on each side, 466 px where it was 440. The joins to its four dimensions meet its top and bottom edges as before. | 2:35 to 3:08 |
| 11 | Every film | The check gave one answer where the films are fixed and another in CI. CI's browser, which also draws the videos, moves every letter of a film's own fonts to a whole pixel, so a line comes out up to about 1% wider or narrower there (3 px in 420); the site's player, in a visitor's browser, doesn't. Hold the films to one of the two, or to both? And should the videos place their letters as the player does? | To both: `site-tools/fits.py` now draws each film both ways, on every machine, and text that leaves its box either way fails, so the check gives the same answer everywhere. The videos are left as they are: placing their letters as the player does would end the difference, and would change every future video a little, so it is the author's to choose. | The pull request's checks; row 10 here, and row 8 of *When things go wrong* |
