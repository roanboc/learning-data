| 1 | Which sound, when no instrument the site lacks fits? | A bowed psaltery with a slate lithophone: two instruments the site has, which no film pairs, and which no film of this topic plays (*It feels like understanding* has the psaltery, with a steel tongue drum; *Ethics we can't read, accountability it can't hold* has the slate, with a melodica). The question whether pairings are fine is already on the author's checklist. | The soundtrack |
| 2 | Did Cybersyn's warnings really go first to the factory's manager? | That was the design, and the film says it as the design: "A warning was to go first to the factory's own manager, with time to put it right. Only if it stayed wrong would it go on to Santiago." Medina (2006, p. 588) describes it; on p. 592 he records that in practice the results went to CORFO's telex room "without notifying the individual interventors or passing through the algedonic process". The series' last film says how little of the design was built. | Chapter 1 |
| 3 | Where did a warning go if it stayed wrong? | To CORFO's sector committee (Medina 2006, p. 588), in Santiago: the film says "on to Santiago", and draws Santiago's card as "the people in charge". | Chapter 1 |
| 4 | Is "the software printed its first results" on 21 March 1972 right, and what did Beer telex? | Yes: "The Cyberstride suite generated its first printout on 21 March 1972, at which time Beer sent an enthusiastic telex to Anderton: 'Cyberstride suite really works'" (Medina 2006, p. 592). The film shows the telex's first words on a strip and says "telexed that it really worked"; Ron Anderton isn't named. | Chapter 1 |
| 5 | Did Cyberstride work out "what was normal" for each factory? | The film's plain words for Medina's description: statistical filtration that discarded data within "the acceptable system parameters", with trends "based on historical data", and a warning when a variable fell outside "the range specified by Cyberstride" (2006, p. 588). | Chapters 1 and 8 |
| 6 | Are the mill's numbers, the wall and the three warnings real? | No: drawn for the film. The textile mill follows film 1's; "CLOTH WOVEN 4,180 M", the usual days of 3,900 m and 5,200 m, the chart's days, the wall of thirty-six strips, and the cement works and foundry are examples. "Workers absent" follows Cybersyn's measure of social unease (Medina 2006, p. 588). | Chapters 1 and 8 |
| 7 | Is "interruption" defined as the industry does? | As IEEE 1366 does: power lost for more than five minutes, with shorter cuts counted as momentary, in an index of their own. Other regulators set other thresholds; the film's definition is the utility's, written by Grace's team. The measure, interruptions per customer, per year (SAIFI), is the one *Now the questions appear* gave Grace. | Chapters 1, 4 and 7 |
| 8 | Who invented the pyramid? | Nobody alone, as the film says: T. S. Eliot's line (1934), writers on management in the 1980s (Cleveland 1982, Zeleny 1987), and Ackoff (1989), whose version also has understanding between knowledge and wisdom; the film draws the four levels most people know (Rowley 2007). | Chapter 2 |
| 9 | Is it fair to say the pyramid gets two things right? | Yes, as the film's own reading of the critiques: Rowley (2007) and DMBOK2 keep that data needs context to mean anything, and every version puts people's judgement at the top. "People decide" is the film's plain word for it, and matches the decision chain from *Now the questions appear*. | Chapter 3 |
| 10 | Is "a one-way factory" the critics' word? | The film's picture of Frické's critique (2009): the pyramid is read as data processed upward, when concepts and questions come first and decide what's observed and recorded. | Chapter 4 |
| 11 | The utility's numbers? | Invented: 41 interruptions; the bars for lines 14, 9, 21, 3 and 27; the dial's 1.4; the decision to renew lines 14 and 9 first; three cuts in the outage system (CP-0048213 from film 1, CP-0051177 and CP-0039402) with their times; a meter's readings every half hour. | Chapters 3 to 8 |
| 12 | Which other systems record a cut? | Meter data ("power-off event") and customer service ("no-power call"), named only to set up film 3, *Where each fact is born*, which decides which record is the fact. | Chapter 8 |
