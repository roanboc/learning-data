# What each check stops on

Moved from AGENTS.md, *Check, publish, learn* (24 November 2026). AGENTS.md lists the checks to run, in order; this page says what each one stops on. Add a paragraph here when a check learns something new.

- `tools/check.py` draws every frame, 30 a second. In the governance and agent films it also stops on text smaller than 28 px, and on words on screen that name a feature's release status (From words to data's `legible.py`), times every place, finds anything shown twice where shots or chapters change, the agent included, and words shown twice in part (`twice.py`), any arm that jumps in one frame (`steady.py`), a hand whose sleeve runs more than 3.5 hand lengths to the frame's edge and two thumbs floating in the middle of what they hold (`hands.py`), and, in one room, a camera, person or line of words that jumps, appears or vanishes in one frame where the shot or chapter changes (`jump.py`; all four in `films/data-governance/shared/tools/`), and the agent films stop on a gesture (any named pose but `hold`) and on paint that reaches past the edge of a person's picture (`edges.py`, in `films/what-an-agent-is/shared/tools/`). Every watercolour film (governance and master data) also draws itself as the site's player does, with its places as pictures, and stops on a moment that takes too long, anything slow painted while it plays, memory past the player's budget and a canvas made every moment, also with the processor slowed four times (`smooth.py`, in `films/master-and-reference-data/shared/tools/`). In every other film, drawn with *The Inner Life of Data*'s engine, it finds the same words shown twice where one chapter fades into the next (`twice.py` too). In the films drawn with the people kit it runs From words to data's checks, times every place, runs `twice.py` and `jump.py` with the kit's people followed, and then the kit's own `people.py`: nobody a half-figure, out of scale, in a row, waving a hand about, jumping from one pose to the next, under a box of words or frozen; people in view in every chapter, in more than one place, and face to face at least once; the film's key ideas written by hand, at a board or on a page of notes; and nobody in front of their own words at a board, no row written long after the voice says it or at the edge of its board or page, and no hand that jumps over a page; no arm across its own face, no hand at work above the head and none that stops short of what it reaches for; nobody hidden behind someone else, nothing further away drawn over what is nearer, and no two of a film's solid things in one place; and no board or page that is only writing (`films/people-first/shared/tools/`; what the four films drawn before 18 November 2026 still have to put right in arms and depth is in `site-tools/people_later.json`, listed by the check without stopping it, and each film leaves the list with its fix). Last, the kit's `shown.py` draws each chapter on its own and stops on what the film times but never shows: a shot on screen for less than a second, or never, as when a shot listed after it starts no later, and a row of a board or a page still being written as its chapter ends (words less than a second before it, a mark less than 0.3 s).
- `site-tools/fits.py`, run from a film's `source/` after its build, draws the film every half second and holds every piece of text to its box: the smallest box a viewer can see under it, whichever helper drew either. It fails on text that ends more than 2 px, and more than a tenth of its own size, past its box for a second and a half or more. It draws the film twice, on every machine: as the site's player places its letters, and as the video is drawn, with each letter moved to a whole pixel, where a line can come out about 1% wider (3 px in 420); text that leaves its box either way fails. A moment in passing isn't a fault (a card springing open, a camera on its way), the tails of letters may hang below a highlighter's band, and text marked as decoration isn't read. It also draws each caption as the smallest phone shows it, in every language the film has captions for, and holds it to its own box and the frame. A film names text that is meant to leave a box in its `film.json` (`spills`: chapter, text, why). The films made before 12 November 2026 were fixed from a list, `site-tools/fits_later.json`, which each left with its fix: the list is empty, so every film is held to its boxes. Should a stricter check find old faults, `--later` lists them there again, to fix film by film.
- `site-tools/shots.py`, run from a film's `source/` after its build, draws each chapter on its own every half second and reads each list of shots the chapter hands `ag_shots` or `gv_shots`, the two kits' functions, which show the last shot in a list whose time has come. It fails a shot on screen for less than a second, or never, as when a shot listed after it starts no later: list a chapter's shots in the order they start, and give each the time to be seen. A film names a shot that is brief on purpose in its `film.json` (`brief`: chapter, time, why), such as the picture the chapter before ends on, which a chapter opens on and dissolves from. The shots too short when the check came (18 November 2026) are listed in `site-tools/shots_later.json`, which each leaves with its fix, and `--list` prints every list of shots with how long each shot is on screen. A film that picks no shots this way has nothing to read.
- `tools/quiet.py` plays the score on its own and fails music that comes in too often or too loud, a figure repeated, a burst of effects, notes that beat, a room that rings louder than its level where the notes are, a held note the room makes more than 2 dB louder or quieter, and a pulse outside the film's urgent chapters.
- `check_site.py` also reads every Pause and think pack: one right answer and one near miss per question, the right answer at most a quarter longer than the longest wrong one and clearly the longest in at most half a pack's questions, no option under half the longest, right answers not all in one place in a pack nor more than 40% in one place across the site, and the Spanish pack's in the English places. *The Inner Life of Data*'s questions, a block of its pack of labs and scenarios, are read too.
- `check_site.py` also reads every pack of labs and scenarios: one right option in each choice; at most half of a pack's right answers in one place, among its scenarios and again among its labs' rows (an even share where the options are too few for half, as with three rows of two options), and no more than 40% in one place across the site's scenarios; a lab's own rows not all in one place; a sorting list's cards not written box by box, nor four or more turn and turn about between two boxes; an order's `start`, where a pack gives one, mixed (not in order, in reverse, in either from some step on round, nor with most steps where they belong), and every engine that shows order scenarios mixing their steps with the one line they share (`MIXED`); and the Spanish pack's options and cards in the English places. It reads only the labs a page shows (a series' pack can point to one lab of each film's own pack), and not the packs an engine mixes itself as it shows them (`SPREAD_SHUFFLED`: *The map before the data*).
- `check_site.py` also reads the lengths of the options of every scenario a page shows, in English and Spanish, with Pause and think's limits: the right option at most a quarter longer than the longest wrong one, no option under half the longest, and the right one clearly the longest in at most half of a page's scenarios. It doesn't compare options that only name something (every one under 25 characters), nor the scenarios no page shows. It holds a lab's rows of options to the same limits: in each pack, the rows of the labs a page shows.
- `tools/publish.py` stamps the poster with the player it was drawn from, and `check_site.py` fails a poster drawn from any other player. In the watercolour films it also draws each place, and anything else kept at the frame's size or larger or that takes 50 ms or more to paint, as a picture, listed in `places/places.json` with the same stamp and when the film shows it, and `check_site.py` fails pictures drawn from any other player.
- `check_site.py` also fails any film whose voice, captions, page text, Pause and think, labs or scenarios say a feature is in preview, in beta or generally available, in English or Spanish.
- `check_site.py` also fails a film that names Lakebase, LTAP, synced tables or a change data feed and says nothing is copied ("zero copy", "in place", "not one claim was copied", in English or Spanish): the platform keeps a copy there, even when no team runs it.
- CI (`.github/workflows/checks.yml`) runs the build, the check that the site plays that build, `tools/check.py`, `site-tools/fits.py`, `site-tools/shots.py` and `tools/quiet.py` for every film a change touches, and `check_site.py`.
- `check_site.py` also fails a film whose narration or Spanish captions say the stock *Something had to change* (or *has to*, *Algo tenía que cambiar*); the reviewed films that still say it are listed in `STOCK_BEFORE`.
- `check_site.py` also fails a page that links a script, a stylesheet or a picture under `site/assets/` without that file's current version (`?v=`): run `site-tools/build_series.py` after `tools/publish.py` or any change to a file there.
- `check_site.py` also fails a film page whose intro is too long: in its `site.json`, in English and Spanish, the topic label (`kicker`) at most 32 characters, the title (`h1`) at most 75 and the intro (`lead`) at most 300, which is four lines on a laptop. Write it as *Day one* does: who, what goes wrong in the film's own world, and what is done, with the opening story left to the film (PLAYBOOK §7).
- `check_site.py` also fails a film whose voice would misread a name as it's written. It takes each line as the voice reads it (its `say`, or its text, after the voice tool's own respellings and then the film's `say` in `film.json`) and looks for the names in `VOICE_NAMES`: W. Edwards Deming's, said DEM-ing, which the voice reads as "deeming" unless it is spelled "Demming"; the names the films of *People first* share from the rope bridge at Quehue (Quehue "Kehweh", Apurímac "Ahpooreemahk", Quechua "Ketchwa", minka "meenka"); the names many films share, such as Anil "Unnil", Tomás "Toh-mahss" and Nguyen "Win", each spelled one way for the voice in every film; and "A I", which the voice reads "uh eye". The spellings set aside fail too, and so does a line that names one of them more often than it gives the voice its spelling. It also fails a word that two films respell two ways for the voice. The films made before 17 November 2026 that still say a name of `VOICE_NAMES` another way are listed in `site-tools/voice_later.json`: each leaves the list when it is voiced again, and the check fails a film the list still keeps once it says the name one way (PLAYBOOK §5).
- `check_site.py` also holds every film to its cue words, since only some series' builds stop on them: every word a film's pictures or sounds wait for is said, whole, in its line, as the voice reads it (the line's `say`, or its text). It reads them with the builds' own file, `films/data-governance/shared/tools/cues.py`: the helper written in full (`wAt` or `kt_w`), a chapter's short name for it (`w("line", "word")`, wherever `w` still means that helper) and the score's `W(...)`. It can't read a word that isn't written out where the helper is called, such as one taken from a list: look at those moments in the film. What it leaves as it is, with the reason, is in its `CUES_LATER`: two sounds of *Who owns what*, until that film's sound is next mixed.
