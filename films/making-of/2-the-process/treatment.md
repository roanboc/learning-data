# The process (working title)

*Treatment for the second of [two films on the Making of page](../README.md), v0.1: a first proposal. Status: for discussion; see [Decisions to make](#decisions-to-make).*

## The promise

**Who for:** anyone thinking of making learning material with an AI model, and anyone curious how these films came to be. A newcomer understands what each side did, and someone who works with AI every day agrees it's an honest account.

**The theme: the person drives, Claude makes the next version cheap.** The author brought the purpose, the audience, the platform knowledge, the taste and most of the pushback, and made every decision. Claude proposed options, checked facts, wrote the code, rendered and reviewed frames, and built the site. Because a new version costs minutes instead of weeks, the author could afford to say "that's not quite right" many times, and nearly every improvement started there.

**Logline.** Follow *The Inner Life of Data* from the first message to the published site, on two lanes: what the person did, and what Claude did. Watch the film change at each "that's not quite right".

**Why it's worth making.** People either think an AI model makes a film from one prompt, or that it can't make one at all. Both are wrong, and the truth is more useful: a clear split of work that anyone can reuse. The film also shows the mistakes, so it isn't an advert.

## The device: two lanes and one conversation

The screen has two lanes, in the films' design language. The **person's lane** (warm light) holds the brief, the knowledge, the decisions and the pushback. **Claude's lane** (cool light) holds options, checks, code, renders and reviews. Between them runs the conversation, as a thread of short messages. Each stage of the making sends something across: a question, a set of options, a decision, a frame, a correction. Under the lanes, the film itself grows, drawn by the real code at each stage, from four style boards to the published film.

The author appears only as words and decisions, never as a face or a name.

## The spine: from the first message to the site

Each stage is real, and taken from [the making-of story](../../../site/journey/index.md). The quotes on screen are the author's own.

| Chapter | What happens | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **0. One message** | A single message appears: explain a data platform "so a teenager understands and a data engineer agrees". Under it, an empty canvas. | Everything starts with a purpose and an audience, and only the person can give those. |
| **1. Options, not answers** | Claude's lane fans out five analogies: rivers, libraries, cyberpunk, pipes, the human body. Each is tested against zero-copy sharing and fails. The person's lane picks the documentary fly-through. | Claude widens the choice and tests it; the person chooses. |
| **2. Facts** | Claude's lane checks product names against current sources. Three names change on screen: OpenSharing, the dbt platform, Catalog. A rigour sheet grows. | Checking is cheap for Claude and tedious for people, so it gets done every time. The person still judges what matters. |
| **3. "There is no better light than other"** | The pushback, in the author's words, lands on the style boards. The light breaks into pictures, then paintings for audiences. Claude's lane adds one change: style follows the audience's need, not the channel. | The best ideas came from the person's pushback; Claude's job was to make them precise and consistent. |
| **4. Cheap checkpoints** | Five style frames, a 40-second sound sketch, four 5-second voice tests. The person picks one of each. A counter: minutes spent per checkpoint, compared with a full rebuild. | Agree on small things before building big ones. |
| **5. A note becomes a change** | One review note, "start at the system of record", travels to Claude's lane, becomes a code change, and the whole film re-times itself. New stills come back in minutes. A second note, the dbt layers, does the same. | When the film is code, a note costs minutes, so the person can give many notes. Claude looks at every frame; the person listens, because Claude can't hear. |
| **6. What went wrong** | Honest, short: audio dismissed too soon, voice clips said to be attached when they weren't, a relationship diagram drawn backwards, a render that stopped halfway. Each one caught, each one fixed. | Claude makes mistakes, some with confidence. The person's checking is part of the process, not a courtesy. |
| **7. Publishing** | The site assembles: the player, labs and scenarios, Spanish, and a release workflow that renders the videos on a clean machine. More films follow, faster. | The same split carries on beyond the film: the person decides what learning should feel like; Claude builds, checks and keeps it all in step. |
| **8. The split** | The two lanes side by side, filled. The person's lane: purpose, audience, knowledge, taste, pushback, decisions, the final check. Claude's lane: options, facts, code, pictures, sound, pages, checks. | A summary anyone can reuse for their own project. |

## Numbers worth showing

To confirm when scripting, from the repository and the making-of story:

- *The Inner Life of Data* was made in two days, 25 and 26 September 2026, in one long conversation.
- The number of review rounds, style frames, voice tests and cuts.
- How long a stills check and a full render take.
- The number of commits, films, languages, labs and scenarios on the site today.

## Tone

Honest and specific, never a sales pitch. Claude is shown as a capable collaborator that works fast and needs checking. The person is shown as the one who knows what the film is for. The mistakes chapter is as important as any other.

## Look and sound

- The films' design language: dark glass and luminous edges, with warm light for the person and cool light for Claude.
- Real artefacts on screen: the actual style boards, frames from the first cut and the final one, lines from the script and the rigour sheet, and short excerpts of the conversation.
- Music that grows with the site in chapter 7.
- **Length:** 5 to 6 minutes, about 125 words a minute.

## Decisions to make

1. **The title.** Proposals: *Two hands*, *That's not quite right*, *Who drives*. In Spanish, the same idea.
2. **The conversation on screen.** Real excerpts (more honest; they need the author's approval line by line) or rewritten ones (easier to read, but then the film says so).
3. **Which film it follows.** Only *The Inner Life of Data* (a clean story), or also how the later films went faster with the playbook.
4. **The order on the page.** *Data for Films* first, then this one, or the other way around.
5. **The last line.** A proposal: "You bring the why and the no. Claude makes the next version cheap."
