| # | Question | Assumption | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which opening story, and how is it told? | The Erie Canal's case, New York State, 1816 to 1825 (`../openings.md`), checked on 10 November against the New York State Canal Corporation's history, Edwin G. Burrows's *Little Short of Madness* (*American Heritage*, 2010), History.com's *Erie Canal*, the Erie Canalway National Heritage Corridor and NYC's Department of Records. Its four beats, said outright: farms spreading west with grain to sell, and a ton from Buffalo to New York City for about a hundred dollars and weeks by wagon; a canal of 363 miles the federal government wouldn't pay for, "little short of madness" to Jefferson, "Clinton's Ditch" to critics; DeWitt Clinton and the canal commissioners' case to the legislature (where it would run, about seven million dollars, repaid by taxes and tolls) and its yes on 15 April 1817; open on 26 October 1825, two years ahead of plan, a ton for under nine dollars and days, the tolls paying back the seven million within ten years, New York the busiest port within fifteen. | Chapter 1; back to it in chapters 4 and 7 |
| 2 | How does the film say whose land the canal crossed? | Plainly, in one line: "It crossed the homelands of the Haudenosaunee nations, much of it taken from them in the years before." The six nations' names, the Tuscarora included, are drawn in ink on their homelands along the route; nothing else Haudenosaunee is drawn. How each piece was taken (purchases, treaties under pressure, such as Big Tree in 1797) would need more than a line, and isn't told. | Chapter 1, the map |
| 3 | Clinton: governor or commissioner? | A canal commissioner, as the name tag says: Clinton took office as governor on 1 July 1817, after the yes. Clinton, a farm family and two diggers are drawn plainly, dressed for their time, from no picture, never likenesses. | Chapter 1 |
| 4 | How fast did the tolls repay the canal? | "Within ten years", as Burrows writes ("within a decade… enough revenue in tolls to pay off the $7 million"). Wikipedia's "within the first year of operation" disagrees with every other source read and isn't used; the debt's formal redemption by 1836 isn't said. | Chapters 1 and 7 |
| 5 | Who dug it? | "Thousands… many just arrived from Ireland and Wales" (Burrows), never "most": History.com says most were born in the United States. | Chapter 1 |
| 6 | Which case does the data story make? | The module's seed (`../numbers.md`, *The case and its risks*), confirmed by this film: retire the old warehouse, asked of the data council on Tuesday 25 May 2027 by Helena, with Rohan Desai, Finance's management accountant, who checked every figure. | Chapters 2 to 6 |
| 7 | How many options? | Three, not the UK Treasury guidance's shortlist of about five, as a small case may (the guidance asks to be used in proportion): A, renew for three years, A$930,000; B, move all 38 reports as they are, A$520,000 once; C, move the 21 that about 260 people use, rebuilt on the platform from governed products, and retire the 17 nobody has opened in a year, A$380,000 once, recommended. Carrying on as now is always one of them, as the guidance requires; A and B stay on the page, so the council can see why. | Chapter 3 |
| 8 | The Five Case Model: a form, or a checklist? | A checklist: its five questions in plain words (is it needed, is it the best value, can it be bought, can it be afforded, can it be delivered), named once. Helena's page has no section per case. | Chapter 2 |
| 9 | How is the money measured? | By payback (about 17 months) and five years' saving (A$970,000), undiscounted, as a first view a council can check by hand: A$310,000 − A$40,000 = A$270,000 a year from January 2028. Net present value, discounting, optimism bias and sensitivity analysis aren't taught. | Chapter 4 |
| 10 | Which tools does the film show? | Lightly, unnamed: "the platform's own cost records" for the A$40,000 a year (on Databricks, the billable usage system table, kept in the script's sources) and "the warehouse's own logs" for the 17 unopened reports (its product's usage logs). | Chapters 3 and 4 |
| 11 | Which benefits, and who owns them? | Three: the saving, A$270,000 a year from January 2028, owned by the director of digital services, whose budget pays the licence today (never drawn); one staff list for every report, Ingrid Holm in HR, from December 2027 (named, not drawn); an owner for every report in the catalog, Helena, by March 2028.
