| 10 November 2026 | *One small change at a time* opens in Colombo, in the four beats, from JICA's textbook (Hasegawa and Karandagoda, second edition, 2013), its thematic evaluation (July 2013) and Kanamori and colleagues' review (2016): in 2000, at the Castle Street Hospital for Women, a public hospital where mothers come to give birth, too many mothers catch an infection after a Caesarean, and too many newborn babies catch one too; an infection means fever and pain, days more in hospital and real danger to mother and child, and at first the ministry gives no money for a fix. The director, Dr Wimal Karandagoda, brings in 5S, a way of working from Japanese factories already used by companies in Sri Lanka; it costs little, everyone can see the change, and the staff of each ward do it themselves; then kaizen. Not everyone wants it at first; ward by ward it spreads, and in two years infections after a Caesarean fall by half (52%), and in newborn babies by more than half (58%). The way goes on to other hospitals in Sri Lanka and, from 2007, with Japan's aid agency (not named in the voice), to public hospitals in fifteen African countries. Nobody was told to work harder. The module's `openings.md` seeded the problem as crowded wards, lost records and long waits, and the benefits as shorter waits; the film tells the infections instead, the problem the director set out to reduce and the outcome the sources measure, an assumption for the author. No company is named. |
