# Quality as a habit · Report, fix, tell: script

*The script of* Report, fix, tell*, Film 2 of 4 of* Quality as a habit*, the second series of the module Data quality, in English, in the module's look, the quality bench. Written on 5 November 2026 from the module's briefs (section 6), its shared numbers and its openings, and built on 10 November 2026, the way the author set for the module that night ("Work on the films with assumptions and merge them all", 11:53; "Merge everything with a draft label, that's the release per film as it's get built", 12:02): no script review, every question he'd have been asked answered on an assumption and listed in* `/mnt/project-files/data-quality/6-report-fix-tell/assumptions.md` *for his review. It follows the four-beat rule for every opening (AGENTS.md,* Story and world*). Every claim is checked against its source on the day (*Rigour*,* Sources*). The university, its people and its numbers are the module's invented ones; the smallpox campaign in India, its rewards, its searches and its dates are real, told from the World Health Organization's history and India's own accounts.*

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never worked on data quality can follow it, and a data quality lead, a registrar who answers for graduates' names, and a public health historian who knows India's campaign each find nothing wrong in it. The film shows the loop with the people the data is about, as Thomas Redman's practice and the last of Danette McGilvray's ten steps (communicate) describe it: people find errors no check can see, so make it easy to report one where they see the data; send each report to the owner, with a promise of when; fix it where it started, not in the copy; tell the person who reported it, and thank them; tell the people who create the data what their errors cost, so the next one isn't made; and turn each kind of error a person finds into a check, so nobody has to find it again. A rule about what people do, an answer within two working days, is tracked in its own model, never failed as a check. Takeaway: **Answer every report, fix it where it started, and tell them.**

## The story in one paragraph

India, 1974, the last years of the campaign to end smallpox: a disease that passed from person to person, killed many who caught it and scarred most who lived, and that year came back hard, more than 116,000 cases by the end of May. It hid in villages and city streets far from any clinic, and a case nobody reported passed it on to the next family, a chain; vaccinating everyone couldn't reach everyone. So India's health workers, with the World Health Organization, searched and contained: more than a hundred thousand of them went house to house, anyone who reported a case was promised a reward, every report was answered at once, guards stayed at the door of each case day and night while everyone around was vaccinated, and the person who reported it was paid, so people kept reporting. India's last case was found in May 1975, a year on, on a railway platform in Assam, and the search went on, the reward a thousand rupees from July; India was declared free of smallpox in 1977, the world in 1980. At the university, on Monday 12 October 2026, the portal opens for December's graduates to check their name as it will be printed, and Andrés Peñaloza sees "Andres Penaloza": no accent, no tilde. No check had ever held it: his record dates from 2018, from the old online form that took only A to Z, in capitals, and when he came back for his Master's its capitals were typed again by hand as ordinary letters. He presses "This isn't my name" and types it as he writes it, and the portal says the registrar's office will answer by Wednesday. The report goes to the owner of names, Mei's team, with a promise of two working days, kept in a model of its own and an alert each morning, never failed as a check. On Wednesday they check it against the passport seen at his enrolment and correct it in the student system itself, and the night's update carries it to the list, the portal and the printer's file; the portal tells him, and thanks him. In October 64 graduates report their name: 61 are put right within two working days and 3 wait for a document, and every one is told; the admissions team, who made the old forms, get a note each month of what their records cost, and from November ask every student who comes back to check their name against their passport. Read together, the reports show where the errors are born, so Jun adds a check that can't see a missing accent but can see where a name was born: a name the old form made, still in plain letters, not yet confirmed. Its first night it holds 83, the registrar's office writes to each, and within a week seven give back their accents. In India, every report was answered until none was smallpox; at the university, every report is answered, fixed where it started, and told.

### 5 · Tell them · 4:36–5:47

**Narration.** Then the portal tells him: "Your name will be printed as Andrés Peñaloza. Thank you for telling us." A report that disappears teaches people not to bother. In India, every reporter was paid, so the next one reported too. In October, 64 graduates reported their name. 61 were put right within two working days, and three waited for a document. Every one was told. And the people who make the data hear what it cost: a graduate who had to find their own name wrong. The admissions team, who made the old forms, now get a note each month. ## Pause and think

Three stops, one question each: after *To the owner, with a promise*, after *Fixed where it started*, and after *Every case, a check* (`site/assets/report-fix-tell/think.en.js`, and `think.es.js` with the same places). The right answers sit in three places, every option is about as long as the others, and each question has one near miss that takes the right answer's words and changes the one thing the film turns on.

1. **To the owner.** The registrar's office promises an answer within two working days. How does the team know it keeps that promise? A dbt check on the reports, which fails the night's update whenever a report is late (near miss: the promise is about what people do, so it's tracked, never failed as a check). A count of the month's reports, sent to the admissions team with their monthly note. **A model of the reports, each with its owner and due date, which Mei's team reviews every week.**
2. **Fixed where it started.** Mei's team could have corrected Andrés's name in the graduation list, where it will be printed. Why correct it in the student system? **The list is built again from the student system every night, so a fix in the list would be undone.** The list is built again from the student system every night, so a fix in the list would reach the printer a day late (near miss: the list doesn't keep the fix at all). Only the registrar's office may change the list, and the student system is the only place its team can edit.
3. **Every case, a check.** No check can see a missing accent. What does Jun's new check look for instead? Names typed in capitals only, since the old online form typed every name in capitals. **A name the old form made, still in plain letters, and not yet confirmed by its graduate.** A name the old form made, still in plain letters, and already confirmed by its graduate (near miss: it's the names not yet confirmed that need a person).
