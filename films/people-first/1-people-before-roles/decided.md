| 12 November 2026 | *People before roles* opens the series at the rope bridge of Q'eswachaka, in the four beats, with its own part of the series' story: the time the bridge was left, and why its people went back. Scenario: high in the Andes of Peru a bridge made of grass, twisted by hand into cord, hangs across a gorge of the Apurímac river; by their own account, the people there have crossed on rope since the time of the Incas; it was once the only way between the communities around it, and since grass wears out, many hands make it new. Problem: in 1968 a steel bridge is built almost a hundred metres away, cars cross with their loads and few people need the rope; a geographer who visits in 1970 finds it decaying, not raised, he writes, for a few years, and expects the skill of making it to die with that generation; and its people, in a keeper's own words, feel sad about abandoning it. Solution: in October 1972 one of the bridge's keepers tells two visitors what his people have decided, to rebuild it when the new year comes, and they do, with nobody from outside pushing them to. Benefits: where few had come to help, one elder says, everyone now comes; today four communities make it new each June, about a thousand people, each family bringing its own cord, as *minka*; a steel bridge could carry them across, and couldn't bring them together. The same for data: a team can be read as roles on a chart, or as the people in them. It comes back in *What won't change yet* (a keeper said what his people would keep; Helena says it in her first week) and at the close (steel could do the carrying; what people went back for was each other). |
| 12 November 2026 | The story is told as exactly as its sources tell it, from the study Peru's Ministry of Culture published in 2015, read in full in Spanish. The one report of how long the bridge was left is a visitor's, so the voice keeps it his ("not raised, he writes, for a few years", "he expects"), and the film never says nobody made the bridge in those years. The return has no year in the voice ("When the new year came, they did"), since the article that reported it wasn't read. "Four communities" and "each June" are said of today only, and the bridge's age is its people's own account. The man who says "Only now does everyone come" is the eldest of the community of Huinchiri, 95 years old in 2012, and not one of its bridge builders: the voice says "one elder" and "the people there", and the screen "An elder of Huinchiri, asked in 2012". Those words are this film's translation of "recién ahora vienen todos"; the Spanish captions give them as the study prints them. The builder's interview is dated by when it was held, October 2014, not by its publication in 2015. The people of Quehue also tell of storms and hunger in the time the custom was left; the voice leaves that telling out and doesn't judge it. Nobody drawn is a likeness: no figure stands for the keeper, the elder or the builder whose words are on screen. |

| 12 November 2026 | Its sound is its own, reserved in the module's working folder before a note was written: a cor anglais and a bassoon, as a pair, a player to each. Nearly every instrument the site lacks is taken, so the film pairs two the site has, in a pair no film has. Each sounds through two blades of cane bound together, and neither blade sounds alone. The mark is *the names*: seven short figures, each the rhythm of one person's name said aloud and each starting on its own note of the scale, so all seven notes are in it and no tune can read as five-note; the bassoon says the first names of two syllables and the cor anglais those of one, a choice that follows the name and says nothing about the person. The chart gets taps, and people get notes. D-flat major, with B-flat minor for the time the bridge was left, the plan by lunch and the change people can't see, all tuned pure from D flat. Nothing Andean: no flute or pipe of any kind, no harp, guitar or mandolin, no violin, no drum, no brass, and no river, wind or birdsong as music. Three places where a note was written were left without one. No chapter is urgent. |
