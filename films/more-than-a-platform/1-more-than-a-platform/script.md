# More than a platform

**Video script, as built, 12 November 2026.** It follows [the proposal](../proposal.md), which the author confirmed that day, with two corrections said below. The film was made from the first draft as it stood, for the author to watch (he asked that day to continue and be told when it was done); what it assumes is in [its assumptions](../../../docs/assumptions/00-data-platforms/1-more-than-a-platform/1-more-than-a-platform.md).

3 minutes 22 seconds, with 404 words of narration (120 words a minute), in the voice the films have used so far. English narration; Spanish captions, a Spanish page and a Spanish narration over the same pictures. Every name, place and number is fictional.

It comes after *The Inner Life of Data*, on a page of its own. It has no story from the past, no labs, no scenarios and no Pause and think (the author, 12 November 2026).

## What it corrects in the proposal

- **Three systems, not four.** The proposal had the student system, the learning platform, HR and payroll, and finance each counting *enrolled* its own way. HR and payroll doesn't count enrolments, so a practitioner wouldn't recognise it. The script uses the three that do.
- **No 312%.** In *The Inner Life of Data* the class reads 312% full because of a wrong sketch, not because offices define a word differently. Giving that number a second cause would contradict that film. The script uses two plain counts for one class.

## The numbers

One class, Data Science 101 on Tuesdays, with 180 seats. Three systems count *enrolled*:

| System | What it counts | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Student system | Everyone who signed up | 176 |
| Learning platform | Everyone who can open the course | 171 |
| Finance system | Everyone who has paid | 149 |

Each count is right by its own meaning; nobody made a mistake. The decision that waits on it: whether to open a second class. At 176 of 180 the class is full; at 149 it isn't.

## Script

### 1 · It worked · 0:00–0:24

**Narration.** One tap became a decision. A new class opened, and 140 more students got a seat. The platform did its job. So why, a week later, do two offices bring two numbers for the same class to the same meeting?

**Picture.** The last picture of *The Inner Life of Data*: the phone, Enrolment confirmed, and the card with 140 more seats. They leave. A meeting table, seen from above, and two sheets of paper slide onto it: 176 and 149. Then the title card.

**On screen.** Data Science 101 · Enrolment confirmed · Decision: New class added · 140 more seats · It worked · One week later · Data Science 101 · 180 seats · Student office: 176 enrolled · Finance: 149 enrolled · Which one? · More than a platform

### 2 · One word · 0:24–1:19

**Narration.** Start with one word: enrolled. In one office, with one system, it means one thing. Nobody has to ask. Then the university grows. The student system counts everyone who signed up. The learning platform counts everyone who can open the course. Finance counts everyone who has paid. Three systems, three meanings, and each one is right. The platform moves all three, without a fault. Now add more teams. Add an AI agent that answers from the same data. Whatever the word means now is repeated everywhere, at once, and with confidence. The platform was never the problem. What changes the answer is how the organisation works, what was defined, and who decided.

**Picture.** The word *enrolled*, alone, in white, and one system's card under it. Then three systems, each in its colour from *The Inner Life of Data*, each with the word, what it means there, and its count. Lanes of light carry all three into the platform, unchanged. Three teams' cards appear beside it, then an agent's, and each repeats one of the counts. On the last line, three plain phrases settle, one at a time.

**On screen.** enrolled · signed up: 176 · can open the course: 171 · has paid: 149 · the organisation · the definition · who decides

### 3 · The route · 1:19–2:23

**Narration.** So where do you start? Not with the platform. Here is the route. Enterprise architecture: how does this organisation work? Data governance: who decides? Those two are the foundations. Then you build. Data modelling: what does each word mean? Modern data basics: how does data move, run and get served today? Analytics engineering: how is it built, and tested? Data quality: is it right, and in time? Two side routes leave after the foundations. One is about sharing and protecting. Master and reference data: is it the same student everywhere? Data protection and privacy: who may see it, and for how long? The other is about agents: what may an agent do with it? They all meet again at the end. Data management: how does it all fit in one plan? And last, leading data people: who leads the people who do the work?

**Picture.** The learning route draws itself, in the site's own shape and colours: the blue line first, station by station, each station's name appearing as it is said, with its question on a card under the route for as long as it is spoken. The amber loop rises for the two stations of sharing and protecting, and the violet loop drops for the agents. The loops join the line again before the last two stations. When *Data governance* is named, the two sheets from the meeting reappear for a moment, and one definition replaces them.

**On screen.** Foundations · Build and monitor · Share and protect · Agents · Lead · the eleven stations' names · each station's question, one at a time

### 4 · One map among many · 2:23–2:40

**Narration.** People have mapped this ground before. The best known map is DAMA's wheel, with governance at its centre. Any good map will do. This one is a route, because you have to start somewhere.

**Picture.** Three maps of the same ground, as outlines: layers, a wheel, a grid. The wheel is drawn in the film's own hand: a centre and ten parts around it, with no words but *Governance* in the middle. It opens out: its centre goes to *Data governance*, its parts to the other stations, and the route is whole again.

**On screen.** DAMA-DMBOK · Governance

### 5 · What you can do here · 2:40–3:08

**Narration.** At every stop there are short films. Watch one. Let it stop and ask you a question. Take it apart in a lab. Make the call in a real situation. Pick a goal, and the route lights up your stops. Or search for a word, and find the film that says it. What you have done stays with you, in your own browser.

**Picture.** Six simple shapes, one for each line, each drawn as the line is said, in a row under the route: a play triangle, a question mark in a pause, a lab's dial, three options with one picked, a route with a flag, and a magnifier over lines of words. None is a picture of the screen. When a goal is picked, four stations carry its numbers, 1 to 4. On the last line, a ring closes round the first station.

**On screen.** Watch · Pause and think · Take it apart · Make the call · Pick a goal · Search

### 6 · Start · 3:08–3:22

**Narration.** Platforms have matured. The hard part is everything else. Start there.

**Picture.** The route, whole. The introduction's station is done; every other station lights, and then the next one, *Enterprise architecture*, with *Start here* under it. Then the end card.

**On screen.** Start here · The hard part is everything else. · More than a platform

## Sound

Chime bars, made for this film (`source/tools/score.py`): single bars struck with a yarn mallet, as a station's announcement plays them before it names the next stop. Music under the opening, then only now and then: one note for *enrolled*, three for the three right counts, a low one where the platform was never the problem, one for each thing that changes the answer; in the route, a note where the route appears, where the foundations are named, where the side routes leave and meet, and the mark (four notes, the call before an announcement) when the last station is named and under the two cards. Each station arriving is one soft tap. `tools/quiet.py` passes: after the opening the music sounds 29% of the time and comes in once every 12 seconds.
