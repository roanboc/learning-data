## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never used an AI agent can follow it, and a data engineer who runs agents, or a historian of mathematics, finds nothing wrong in it. *Context engineering* showed that an agent can only be as right as what it reads, and named skills as one of the four kinds of context: how the team does a job, step by step. This film shows what a skill is and where it comes from. A skill is a playbook, earned by doing the job once by hand and refined at every new case, so nothing is solved twice. The agent reads only each skill's name and the line that says when to use it, and loads the steps when a job needs them. A router, a skill whose only job is to choose, picks the right one, and says so when none fits. A workflow runs the known steps the same way every time, and calls the agent only where thinking is needed; a person decides. **Solve it once, write it down, reuse it.**

## The story in one paragraph

Baghdad, about twelve hundred years ago. Every day its people need sums worked out: estates shared within families, legacies, fields divided, lawsuits, trade, land measured and canals dug. The law sets each heir's share, and a legacy can leave part of an estate to someone outside the family. But each problem is worked out on its own, with its own tricks, and a new kind starts from nothing; one slip, and a widow or a child could get less than the law gives them, and two reckoners could reach two answers for one estate. Encouraged by the caliph, al-Khwarizmi, a scholar of the House of Wisdom, writes a short book of what people, in his words, constantly require: two moves, completion and balancing, bring any problem to one of six forms; each form has its own steps, worked on an example and proved with a drawn square; then pages of real cases, legacies most of all. Now anyone who follows the steps can solve a problem they've never seen, the same way every time; the book was copied for centuries, put into Latin in 1145, and gave us the words algebra and algorithm. Data teams have the same problem: a job solved in one chat is solved again in the next, from nothing. Today, at the university, Jun's team reconciles the census-day count, 18,412 students, with the census report each time its code or sources change. The same few causes come back (students who withdrew after census day and still count, a new degree that no faculty holds, a student counted twice), each found before and settled by a person, but kept in one chat, one note, one email; so the agent starts from nothing, spends an afternoon finding what the team already knew, and may settle a cause another way. So Jun does the job once more by hand and writes it down: a skill, a file with a name and a line that says when to use it, its steps, and every cause met so far, how it shows, what settled it and who decided. The agent reads only each skill's name and line, and loads the steps when a job needs them. A router picks among the project's seven skills, and when none fits, it says so and asks a person. Some work needs no choosing: every night a workflow runs fixed steps, build, count as on census day, run the tests, compare with the census report, and calls the agent only when the numbers don't agree. One night in October they don't: 18,426 against 18,412. No cause fits; the agent finds fourteen records made the week before, dated back to February, and asks in its report whether they're students. In the morning Mei decides they're test records, which never count; Jun adds the cause to the skill and a fixed step with a test; Noor approves; and the next night no agent is called. As al-Khwarizmi's steps were followed for centuries, a job done once by hand becomes a skill, a router finds it, a workflow runs what's known, and each new case is written down, so it's never solved twice.

## The reference, and practice

The plan names two references for this film: skills as files an agent loads when needed, and workflows versus agents. Both are common practice, and the film names neither, nor any product: it teaches what they share.

| Body of knowledge | What it adds | Where the film uses it |
|---|---|---|
| The open format for agent skills (its specification, read 4 November 2026) | A skill is a folder with a `SKILL.md`: a name, and a description that "should describe both what the skill does and when to use it", then its instructions. Agents load skills progressively: the name and description "are loaded at startup for all skills", and the whole file "when the skill is activated" | *A skill, earned by hand*: "a file in the project, with a name, and a line that says when to use it"; "The agent doesn't read every skill all the time: just each one's name and line. It loads the steps only when a job needs them." Not named |
| Workflows versus agents (Anthropic, *Building effective agents*, 19 December 2024, and the practice since) | "Workflows are systems where LLMs and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths. This might mean not building agentic systems at all"; routing "classifies an input and directs it to a specialized followup task" | *A workflow runs what's known* (fixed steps, the agent only as one more step when the numbers don't agree) and *A router picks the skill*. Not named |
| The plan's insight, from the author's practice (*His practice*): "Agents can overcomplicate things and cost more: automate deterministically, and keep agents for what isn't deterministic" | The rule the film turns on | Said in *A workflow runs what's known*: "Automate what's known with plain steps first; keep the agent for what isn't." | The module's rules (the plan; *What an agent is*) | Agents are never approvers; nothing reaches production without a person, and a change to production needs a second person's approval; tool servers get the fewest rights, read only when reading is enough, drafts only in sandboxes | The agent drafts its report in its sandbox and asks; Mei decides; Noor approves the new step "before it runs"; the agent's query tool reads only |
| *An agent on the team* and *Context engineering* | The example project's skills, one file each, and its `AGENTS.md`; skills as one of the four kinds of context, "how the team does a job, step by step" | Built on, not taught again: this film shows where a skill comes from, how the agent reads it, and how one is picked |

**Where the film adopts, extends or differs.** It adopts the open format's idea of a skill and of progressive loading, in plain words ("a line that says when to use it"), and the common split between a workflow for what's known and an agent for what isn't. It extends both with the author's practice: a skill is *earned*, by doing the job once by hand, and keeps a running list of the causes met, each with who decided, so the skill grows with every case instead of being written once from a guess; and a cause a person has settled, once it's a rule, leaves the agent's hands and becomes a fixed step, so each new case makes the agent needed less. It differs from a reading of the reference that would let an agent route every job: the router here says so when no skill fits and asks a person, who does the job by hand first; the agent never fills the gap with a guess dressed as a playbook.

**Practice, from the author (the plan's insights):** automate deterministically first, and keep agents for what isn't deterministic; agents can overcomplicate things and cost more; skills and context documents are kept by hand today, and learning is lost when it stays in one person's chat. A practitioner will recognise the problem chapter: the reconciliation that someone does "one more time" every release, and the afternoon spent rediscovering a cause someone settled months ago in a thread nobody can find.

**What the film leaves to its siblings.** What the agent reads, and who writes it, is *Context engineering*'s. The hands a skill's steps use, and their rights, are *Tools are hands, and every hand is a permission*'s. Memory the whole team shares, outside one chat, is *Memory: from one chat to the team*'s; a skill here is written down by a person and reviewed, not learned by the agent on its own. Deciding the check before the agent starts is *Plan, define the check, do, check*'s, and the ways an agent goes wrong, among them a job made bigger than it is, are *How it goes wrong*'s. The film names the lab *Write the agent's skill* as its own; it comes with the module's labs, as one set.

## Script

The times are the film's, from its recorded voice. Words on screen are on cards, pills, tags and the corner.

### 1 · Completion and balancing · 0:00–2:07

**Narration.** Baghdad, about twelve hundred years ago. Every day, its people need sums worked out. Estates shared within families, legacies, fields divided, lawsuits, trade, land measured and canals dug. The law sets each heir's share, and a legacy can leave part of an estate to someone outside the family. But each problem is worked out on its own, with its own tricks, and a new kind starts from nothing. One slip, and a widow or a child could get less than the law gives them. And two reckoners could reach two answers for one estate. Something had to change. Encouraged by the caliph, Muhammad al-Khwarizmi, a scholar of the House of Wisdom, writes a short book. It teaches what people, in his words, constantly require. Two moves, completion and balancing, bring any problem to one of six forms. Each form has its own steps, worked through on an example, and proved with a drawn square. A square and ten roots make thirty-nine. Halve the ten: five. Multiply it by itself: twenty-five. Add the thirty-nine: sixty-four. Take its root: eight. Take away the five: three. Then come pages of real cases, legacies most of all. Now anyone who follows the steps can solve a problem they've never seen, the same way every time. The book was copied for centuries, and put into Latin in 1145. ## Labs (candidates, for the module's set)

## Rigour to check before recording

Checked on 4 November 2026; the sources are listed below.

- **The preface's words.** Rosen, *The Algebra of Mohammed ben Musa* (1831), p. 3: "That fondness for science, by which God has distinguished the Imam al Mamun, the Commander of the Faithful … has encouraged me to compose a short work on Calculating by (the rules of) Completion and Reduction, confining it to what is easiest and most useful in arithmetic, such as men constantly require in cases of inheritance, legacies, partition, law-suits, and trade, and in all their dealings with one another, or where the measuring of lands, the digging of canals, geometrical computation, and other objects of various sorts and kinds are concerned". The film's "Encouraged by the caliph", "a short book", "what people, in his words, constantly require", and the sums of the opening (estates, legacies, fields divided, lawsuits, trade, land measured, canals dug) all come from it; the card quotes "such as men constantly require" and lists "inheritance, legacies, partition, lawsuits and trade". MacTutor: he "dedicated two of his texts to the Caliph", al-Mamun, "his treatise on algebra and his treatise on astronomy".
- **The House of Wisdom, and about 820.** MacTutor: al-Mamun "founded an academy called the House of Wisdom" in Baghdad, and al-Khwarizmi "worked under the patronage of Al-Mamun"; Wikipedia's *Al-Jabr* gives the book's date as 820. "About twelve hundred years ago" is 2026 less 820. No portrait survives; he is drawn as a scholar of his time.
- **The name of the two moves.** Rosen writes "Completion and Reduction"; MacTutor translates the title as "Complete book on calculation by completion and balancing", and *Al-Jabr*'s article as "Restoration and Balancing". The film says "completion and balancing", MacTutor's words, and shows each move as the book uses it.
- **The six forms.** MacTutor: "He first reduces an equation (linear or quadratic) to one of six standard forms: 1. Squares equal to roots. 2. Squares equal to numbers. 3. Roots equal to numbers. 4. Squares and roots equal to numbers; e.g. x² + 10x = 39 …". Rosen, p. 35: "the calculating by completion and reduction must always necessarily lead you to one of these cases". The film's "Two moves … bring any problem to one of six forms", and "Form 4 · Squares and roots equal numbers".
- **Completion, on the book's own example.** Rosen, p. 36, the first of the six problems: "This is a square, which is equal to forty things minus four squares. Reduce it now by the four squares, and add them to the one square. Then the equation is: forty things are equal to five squares". The film's tiles say "roots" where Rosen says "things", as the six forms do. **Balancing** ("a square + 14 roots = 39 + 4 roots" becoming "a square + 10 roots = 39") is the film's own, made to lead to the worked example: the same taken from both sides.
- **The worked example.** Rosen, p. 8: "one square, and ten roots of the same, amount to thirty-nine dirhems … you halve the number of the roots, which in the present instance yields five. This you multiply by itself; the product is twenty-five. Add this to thirty-nine; the sum is sixty-four. Now take the root of this, which is eight, and subtract from it half the number of the roots, which is five; the remainder is three." The narration's five steps follow it word for word in meaning; the card's steps ("Halve the roots", "Multiply it by itself", "Add the numbers", "Take its root", "Take away the half") are its rule in short.
- **Proved with a drawn square.** Rosen, p. 13, "Demonstration of the Case: 'a Square and ten Roots are equal to thirty-nine Dirhems'", and pp. 15–16, "another figure": "two quadrangles on two sides of the quadrate A B … the length of each of them being five", "In order to complete the great quadrate, there wants only a square of five multiplied by five, or twenty-five. This we add to thirty-nine … The sum is sixty-four. We extract its root, eight … By subtracting from this the same quantity which we have before added, namely five, we obtain three". The film draws this second figure. The book's pages carry no Arabic words on screen: only the figure, as the book's own pages carry figures.
- **Real cases, legacies most of all.** Rosen, p. 86, the first problem *On Legacies*: "A man dies, leaving two sons behind him, and bequeathing one-third of his capital to a stranger. He leaves ten dirhems of property and a claim of ten dirhems upon one of the sons." The card shortens it ("A man dies, leaving two sons, and leaves a third of his capital to a stranger. How much does each one get?") and leaves the claim out. MacTutor: "The final part of the book deals with the complicated Islamic rules for inheritance". The chapters on legacies run far longer than the rest of the book's cases, so "legacies most of all".
- **The law's shares.** The Qur'an, 4:11–12: to a son, the share of two daughters; to a wife, an eighth of the estate when the deceased leaves children. The film's estate (960 dirhams; the widow 120, an eighth; the son 560 and the daughter 280, two parts and one, of the remaining 840) is its own, small and believable, with no debts and no legacy. "A legacy can leave … up to a third" is the bequest limit of one third, the "one-third of his capital" of Rosen's legacy problems; "someone outside the family" stands for Rosen's "stranger", one who isn't an heir. A dirham was a silver coin; Rosen writes "dirhems", the film "dirhams".
- **The harm, said as a risk.** "One slip, and a widow or a child could get less than the law gives them" and "two reckoners could reach two answers for one estate" are said as what could happen, as `openings.md` asks: no source read gives a recorded case. - **Copied for centuries, and into Latin in 1145.** Rosen, p. xiii: the only copy he could find is in the Bodleian at Oxford (Hunt. 214), dated A.H. 743, A.D. 1342; the copies on the film's line of years stand for the book being copied, not for dated manuscripts.
