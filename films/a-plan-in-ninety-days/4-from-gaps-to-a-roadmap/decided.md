| 10 November 2026 | *From gaps to a roadmap* opens in Switzerland, in the four beats, as the module's setup proposed: in the 1970s more people drove, on new motorways, and the railways' share of travel fell from over a quarter to a sixth (27.8% in 1960, 16.0% in 1970); trains left at different minutes every hour, a change could mean a long wait, and people without a car (students, workers, older people) looked up times and waited; the railways ran at a loss; in 1972 three young railway engineers wrote that the trains no longer fit what people wanted, and that the timetable was the heart of it; the railways' own plan, new fast lines across the country, would have cost billions and was dropped in the early 1980s. From 23 May 1982 the trains ran by the clock; Rail 2000 started from the timetable people wanted, with hubs where trains from every direction arrive just before the hour and leave just after, and built only the stretches too slow between two hubs: "as fast as necessary, not as fast as possible". Voters approved it on 6 December 1987; when the costs rose it was built in stages, and the first kept one new line of four, Bern to Olten. It opened on 12 December 2004 (about 130 projects); Bern to Zürich took under an hour; in 2019 the Swiss travelled further by train than anyone else in Europe, about 2,500 km each. Two claims were left out for want of a source ("12% more trains" from 1982) or softened to what the source says ("ran at a loss", "would cost billions"). It comes back three times: the timetable decided what Switzerland would build, as the target decides what comes first; like Rail 2000's first stage, each stage works on its own; and at the end, the hubs connected beside stage one's door, list and measure. Sources, with the day they were checked, are in the script's *Rigour*. |
