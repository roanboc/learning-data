One thread: a new data manager's first ninety days

In 2027, imagined: on Monday 1 February Helena Novak starts as the university's data manager. 46 requests are waiting, the oldest from August, and they touch every area of data, from reports to security. Over twelve weeks she listens to 31 people and to the systems' own numbers, designs the target with the team and the faculties, and turns the gaps into six work packages in three stages, on a one-page data strategy the data council approves on 27 April.

Every area, one jobThe Atacama, 2010: every part, at onceThirty-three miners were trapped 700 metres down. Each part of the rescue had someone who knew it, from the drills to the doctors to the families, and every part ran at once, until all 33 came up. A data manager's job is shaped the same way.
Listen firstAccra, the early 1930s: the mothers' wordChildren of one to four were dying of a sickness doctors took for pellagra. Cicely Williams listened to the mothers, who had a name for it, kwashiorkor, and knew when it came. The people who live with a problem often know it best.
Design it simpleKorea, 1443 to 1446: letters for everyoneMost people couldn't write in Chinese characters, so in a court their words were set down in signs they couldn't read. King Sejong made 28 letters from a few shapes, argued for them, and in 1446 published them: learned in a morning, so people used them.
From gaps to a roadmapSwitzerland, 1982 to 2004: every hour, at the same minutesFrom 1982 the trains ran by the clock. Then Rail 2000 started from the timetable people wanted, found the slow stretches between its hubs, and built only those, in stages that each worked on their own. A data roadmap works the same way.
