# AGENTS.md

Guidance for any AI agent (Claude, Codex, Copilot, Gemini or others) working in this repository. [`PLAYBOOK.md`](PLAYBOOK.md) holds the detailed lessons behind the films; this file is the order to work in and the rules that always apply. The README's *What's here* explains the layout.

## The process for every new film, series or topic change

Go through the stages in order. The concept is settled before anything is drawn.

### 1. Conceptual foundation (always first)

1. **Ask for the reference framework.** Before drafting, ask the author which framework, standard or body of knowledge is the reference for the topic. Then suggest the others worth considering, with one line each on what they add (for data modelling: Kimball, Inmon, Data Vault, entity-centric; for enterprise architecture: TOGAF, Zachman, ArchiMate, BIZBOK, domain-driven design, DAMA-DMBOK). Say which one the films follow and why, and record where they adopt, extend or differ from it.
2. **Ground it in practice.** Ask the author for their real experience: what works, what goes wrong, what they would tell a newcomer. Check that every concept and process is something practitioners actually do, not only what a framework prescribes. Write that advice into the proposal's brief.
3. **Keep the essential.** Frameworks are inputs, not the syllabus. As *From words to data* did with modelling techniques and *The map before the data* with EA frameworks, name the bodies of knowledge once, then teach the insights they share. Cut what a newcomer doesn't need for the decision the film is about.
4. **Tools show, they don't lead.** Databricks and dbt are the main tools to demonstrate the ideas (dbt writes and tests the recipes; Databricks runs, stores and governs). Choose the concept first, then show it in the tools. A topic never exists because a product feature does.
5. **Write it down and confirm.** A `proposal.md` with the brief in the author's words, the reference and its alternatives, an insights table with where each comes from, the practical advice, the thread to follow, and a *Decided* table with dates. The author confirms it before any script is written.

*Ask: would an expert in each framework agree, and would a practitioner recognise it from real work?*

### 2. Story and world

Follow PLAYBOOK §1, §2, §8 and §9: the two-audience promise, one thing followed from where it's born to a human outcome, a metaphor tested against the hardest mechanism, openings varied across cultures and eras, people on every side of the problem, blameless and believable numbers, wrong drafts corrected by the people who know, and a rigour sheet for every scene.

**Every opening story has four beats, each said outright in the narration, in this order:**

1. **Scenario:** who, where, when, and what was happening.
2. **Problem:** what went wrong or what people feared, for whom, and why it mattered to them, as a harm a viewer can picture. Explain every word the story leans on (if a law lets town halls "check up on" residents: check what, and what could happen to them?), and say that something had to change.
3. **Solution:** what was done, and by whom.
4. **Benefits:** what got better, and for whom, with a number where the sources give one; then the same problem and benefit in the film's own data story.

The film comes back to the story along the way, and at the end to close on its benefit for data. Test it before the script goes to the author: someone who has never heard of the event can answer, from the narration alone, "why was that a problem?" and "what did the fix give people?". Each script has an *Opening story* table, a row for each beat and one for where the film comes back to the story, quoting the line that says it; `site-tools/check_site.py` fails a film whose table misses a row or quotes words its narration doesn't say. Films made before 28 October 2026 add theirs when their opening changes.

### 3. Video craft, at the highest effort

Run film and visual style work at the highest reasoning or effort setting your tool offers. Draft style frames, compare options, review full-size stills, and iterate until the result is the best you can make, not the first that passes. Apply PLAYBOOK §4 to §6 and its checklists, in particular:

- **Look:** one design language per series; colour carries meaning; living things drawn with tapering curves, weight and light; people dressed for their time; reusable components, not one-off drawings.
- **People:** an adult's proportions (for a half-figure in the 1920 px frame, a head about 225 px from crown to chin, eyes halfway down it, short hair that follows the skull, a real jaw over a neck about half as wide as the head is tall, shoulders a third of a head below the chin); one turn for the whole head, its eyes, nose, mouth, ear, hair and hat placed from one table of the face's points (`AG_FACE`), and the nose one small curve; arms down, painted with the body, and a hand only on the thing the story is about (`hold`); a prop in one place at a time; a hat inside the head's picture (figure x 160 to 440, y 250 to 560), and a cloth that falls further, such as a mantle or a shawl, painted as its own picture over the person or split between the head's picture and the body's at y 560; a person who fades drawn whole first, then faded as one picture. Look at every new person's head at full size, facing both ways, before a film uses it.
- **Phone legibility:** text at least 28 px in the 1920 px frame after zoom, or marked as decoration on purpose; lay out from the drawn size; take turns when room runs out; nothing in the caption band (below y ≈ 890).
- **Motion:** spring and settle, a slow camera (at least about 1.4 s, sine ease), things carried across cuts, motion blur, no film grain. In one room, never cut: the next shot's camera glides on from where the last one is, what stays stays put, and only what changes fades; whoever moves walks or slides there, and a later moment in the room (`later:true`) lets what was there leave before what comes later comes in. Between rooms, cut where the two shots or chapters show the same people or words (`cut:true` in the shots; `gv_cutInto` or `ag_cutInto` for a chapter, which also glide a chapter on in one room); dissolve only where the whole picture changes. The older films, which fade every chapter into the next, name each join that would show something twice in `CHAPTER_JOINS` (From words to data's `shared/src/joins.js`): `glide` on, `leave` first, or `cut`. Take up a new pose from the one before over 0.3 to 0.5 s; never switch to it.
- **Narration and pace:** one idea per line; 120 to 135 words a minute (at most 140 in a chapter); voice at most 82% of any chapter; about 0.8 s after each sentence.
- **Sound:** a new palette for each film; music under the opening story, then only from time to time (at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen or in a wordless pause, about once every 10 seconds at most), never a bed under the voice, and no figure played again and again; every effect tied to something on screen, one at a time, never a burst of tiny sounds; effects soft and below about 2.5 kHz; music and effects no louder than the governance films'; held notes at even levels, a room's echo included; an urgent pulse only where the story turns to an error or a rush; mix to -16 LUFS.
- **Page speed:** a film's page does nothing heavy until the visitor reaches for the player. Make textures and places when the player asks for them (`FILM.prepare`, on a pointer, focus or Play), never as the page opens; keep each place under 2 s to paint (`tools/check.py` times them); with the processor slowed four times, as on a phone, nothing should hold the page for more than about 0.3 s. The player never paints a place while the film plays: in the watercolour films, `tools/publish.py` draws each place in `WC_PLACES` as a picture (`site/assets/<key>/places/`), and anything else the film keeps (`wc_kept`) at the frame's size or larger, such as a place at twice the size for close-ups, or that takes 50 ms or more to paint; the player shows those, keeps only what it uses within a budget (`WC_KEEP`), reuses its spare canvases and washes people in fewer layers. The video draws the full watercolour, as before.
- **Pause and think:** a question tests the idea, not the reader's eye for the longest answer. Spread the right answers across the options, never always in one place; write every option with the same length and detail, so the right one isn't the one that explains everything; and make one wrong option a near miss, almost right but wrong for a clear reason the film gives (`near:true`), with the right one `ok:true`. Keep each option clear. The Spanish pack keeps the English places. `site-tools/check_site.py` checks all of it except whether the near miss is truly near: read each question as a newcomer would.
- **Known faults:** long labels spilling out of small boxes, a pointing person whose hand leaves the frame near the right edge (mirror them), labels under the captions, a pose or a held thing that jumps in one frame (blend it in from the one before), the same people or words shown twice in a dissolve (cut instead), a camera that jumps a little in, out or aside where the shot changes in one room, with what only the shot before showed vanishing in that frame (glide on, and fade what changes), two pages or sheets cross-faded in one room so their words show twice (let the first leave, `later:true`, or turn the page), a line pushed down by a new one in one frame (open its gap, then type it), the same words shown twice where one chapter fades into the next in the older films (name the join in `CHAPTER_JOINS`: glide on, let the first leave, or cut), the agent shown in two places as shots or chapters cross, or two agents, which look alike (in one room, let it move there once they have crossed, or let the first moment leave, `later:true`; between rooms, cut), a card that comes in while the camera still glides in from far off, its words under 28 px until the camera arrives (let the camera arrive, then bring the card in), a person faded layer by layer, who shows through themselves as a ghost (fade them as one picture), a face whose eyes, nose and ear are turned different ways (one turn for the whole head), a nose drawn as two curls (it reads as a squiggle; one small curve for the nostril), a hat or prop drawn in two places at once, a hat or cloth that reaches past the head's picture and is cut off there in a straight line (keep it inside, or give it its own picture), gesturing arms (keep arms down; a hand only on the thing the story is about), a small head on a long thin neck, a burst of tiny sounds that crackles (a few sounds, or one soft texture), a room's echo scaled by all its energy, which rings about 10 dB louder than its level, and a held note made louder or quieter by it (set the room by what it rings where the notes are, and each held note back to its own level), a poster left showing an older drawing after a fix (draw it again), a key named twice in one object, such as a chapter's moments (the second silently replaces the first), a Pause and think pack with quoted keys (its page counts no questions), Pause and think questions whose right answer always sits in the middle and is the longest option, the one that explains everything (spread them, give every option the same detail, and add a near miss), a feature's release status said or shown, such as "in preview" in the voice or a "beta" pill on screen (it dates the film when it changes: say what the feature does, and keep its status in the script's sources), a place painted while the film plays, which freezes the picture for a moment each time on a laptop (list it in `WC_PLACES`, or keep it with `wc_kept`, and `tools/publish.py` draws it as a picture when it is the frame's size or larger or slow to paint), and a canvas made, or a picture kept, at every size the camera shows it, which fills the memory past a gigabyte in one viewing (reuse one, `wc_buf` or `wc_bufAt`, and keep it within `WC_KEEP`).

### 4. Check, publish, learn

1. Per film, from its `source/`: `tools/build.py` (it stops on a name declared in two files, a key named twice in one object, a cue word that isn't whole in its line, and a caption over 140 characters), `tools/check.py`, `tools/legible.py --overlaps`, `tools/pace.py`, `tools/quiet.py` (the sound, where the film has it), full-size stills (`tools/stills.py --size 1920`), then `tools/publish.py` (`--keep-sound` when only the pictures changed). Then `site-tools/build_series.py`, and `.github/scripts/affected.py --run`: it runs `site-tools/check_site.py`, and `site-tools/smoke.py` only on the pages the change touched, never the whole site again. `dist/` is gitignored and can hold a stale soundtrack.
   - `tools/check.py` draws every frame, 30 a second. In the governance and agent films it also stops on text smaller than 28 px, and on words on screen that name a feature's release status (From words to data's `legible.py`), times every place, finds anything shown twice where shots or chapters change, the agent included (`twice.py`), any arm that jumps in one frame (`steady.py`), and, in one room, a camera, person or line of words that jumps, appears or vanishes in one frame where the shot or chapter changes (`jump.py`; all three in `films/data-governance/shared/tools/`), and the agent films stop on a gesture (any named pose but `hold`) and on paint that reaches past the edge of a person's picture (`edges.py`, in `films/what-an-agent-is/shared/tools/`). Every watercolour film (governance and master data) also draws itself as the site's player does, with its places as pictures, and stops on a moment that takes too long, anything slow painted while it plays, memory past the player's budget and a canvas made every moment, also with the processor slowed four times (`smooth.py`, in `films/master-and-reference-data/shared/tools/`). In every other film, drawn with *The Inner Life of Data*'s engine, it finds the same words shown twice where one chapter fades into the next (`twice.py` too).
   - `tools/quiet.py` plays the score on its own and fails music that comes in too often or too loud, a figure repeated, a burst of effects, notes that beat, a room that rings louder than its level where the notes are, a held note the room makes more than 2 dB louder or quieter, and a pulse outside the film's urgent chapters.
   - `check_site.py` also reads every Pause and think pack: one right answer and one near miss per question, the right answer at most a quarter longer than the longest wrong one and clearly the longest in at most half a pack's questions, no option under half the longest, right answers not all in one place in a pack nor more than 40% in one place across the site, and the Spanish pack's in the English places.
   - `tools/publish.py` stamps the poster with the player it was drawn from, and `check_site.py` fails a poster drawn from any other player. In the watercolour films it also draws each place, and anything else kept at the frame's size or larger or that takes 50 ms or more to paint, as a picture, listed in `places/places.json` with the same stamp and when the film shows it, and `check_site.py` fails pictures drawn from any other player.
   - `check_site.py` also fails any film whose voice, captions, page text or Pause and think says a feature is in preview, in beta or generally available, in English or Spanish.
   - CI (`.github/workflows/checks.yml`) runs the build, the check that the site plays that build, `tools/check.py` and `tools/quiet.py` for every film a change touches, and `check_site.py`.
   - Checks sample what they measure. Where people or props move, also look at the frames one by one, at full size, not only at the stills.
2. Spanish captions, pages, Pause and think questions, labs and scenarios match the film's names, numbers and colours.
3. **Share the video for review.** Whenever a new or changed video is made, render it and share it with the author on a review page, with the pull request link. `python site-tools/review_page.py <film folder> --pr <number> --note "<what changed>"` makes the page from the film's own files: a 720p copy under 15 MB (made by `site-tools/review_copy.py`), a button for each chapter, and the captions in each language. Publish it as a private artifact, one page per film (a new version goes to the same link), and share the link in the conversation. Don't attach video files: the chat may only offer them as a download, and a full render can't be downloaded from it. The author watches it and either asks for corrections or approves the pull request. Never call a film done before that.
4. Turn every review finding into a check, in the series' shared tools so every film of the series runs it, and in CI where it can run. Write the lesson into `PLAYBOOK.md`, and the fault into *Known faults* above, so the next film starts from it.

## Working here

- **Check a change with `python .github/scripts/affected.py --run`** (CI runs the same choice): only the pages a change touched and the films whose source changed — never the whole site or every film, unless the checks themselves changed. A change to only a film's published files, such as a soundtrack or a poster, draws no frame: the site checks cover it.
- **On Windows, run Python tools with `PYTHONUTF8=1`**, so they read the UTF-8 scripts, captions and pages.
- **`smoke.py` on Windows can't start `npx` from Python**: serve the site yourself (`npx http-server site -p 8777 -c-1 -s`) and run it with `--base http://127.0.0.1:8777/`; `python -m http.server` refuses the checks' parallel requests. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).
- **A film's Spanish narration** is an alternate `soundtrack.es.mp3` built from its published Spanish captions by the film's `tools/audio_es.py` (es-MX-DaliaNeural, each line fitted to its cue, mixed under the original score and effects; it needs network for the voice and caches lines in `build/es_voice/`).

## Rules that always apply

- **Look before you start.** Check open pull requests and unmerged branches for the same film or series, so nothing is made twice or overwritten.
- **Stay in the film's lane.** A series' shared source (such as `shared/src/*.js`) is bundled by every film in it; never change it for one film. Put the helper in that film's own source.
- **A shared fix is a new version of every film.** A change to shared code changes every film whose `release.json` `paths` name it. Rebuild each one, copy its player and draw its poster again (`tools/publish.py --keep-sound`), and do the same for films merged into main since your checks ran: two pull requests can merge minutes apart with no conflict and leave a film's player older than its source.
- **Each film keeps its own rows.** A film's entry in the release, its *Decided* rows, its legibility row and its *past* row live in its own folder (`release.json`, `decided.md`, `site.json`); a series finds its films by their numbered folders. The shared lists are made from them (README, *Each film keeps its own rows*), so threads never edit the same lines. On a clash in a generated list or page, run `site-tools/build_series.py` again.
- **Generated files are generated.** Pages made by `site-tools/build_series.py`, the *Decided* tables in `proposal.md` and the legibility report's table are never edited by hand, and `check_site.py` fails if they differ. Don't rename numbered film folders or `assets/film3/`: the release workflow and links use them.
- **Keep cited lines in place.** Rigour sheets cite files by line number (for example in the dbt project), so append or replace in place rather than shifting lines.
- **Definitions come from the glossary.** In the example dbt project, a term the business glossary holds is copied from it, with its `source:` (decision DEC-PRJ-11).
- **Spanish is captions, pages and optional audio.** Films are made in English. Spanish comes as captions and as the site's pages (page text, Pause and think, labs and scenarios); the visuals and on-screen words are not translated. A film may also have a `soundtrack.es.mp3` beside its English soundtrack: its Spanish page uses that audio, while English playback and downloadable English videos stay unchanged. Fit each Spanish line to the existing cue, review a sample before the full soundtrack, and keep each Spanish caption about as long as the English line.
- **A fictional world.** Organisations, people and numbers are invented composites. Never name a real company as the subject, or the author's private repositories used as reference.
- **Rights and respect.** Only original art; official logos unaltered and credited in `NOTICE.md`; code under MIT and content under CC BY 4.0. No visual styles or sounds borrowed from other cultures; check a story from another culture against its own sources, and label imagined futures as imagined.
- **Sources and names on the day.** List sources with the date checked. Check product names and maturity when publishing, and write the maturity, with its date, in the script's sources. A film never says a feature's release status (preview, beta, generally available): not in its voice, on screen, in its captions or on its page, so it needn't change when that status does (the author, 5 November 2026).
- **Write decisions down.** Every decision goes in the series' *Decided* table with its date, so the next session starts from it.
- **Heavy jobs.** Run one render at a time, in resumable chunks. A render to share for review is part of making a film; the release workflow (`release.yml`) runs only when the author starts it.
- **For every viewer.** Every interaction works by keyboard, every picture has a line of text saying what happened, pages work in dark mode and on a phone, and progress stays in the visitor's browser.
- **Plain words.** Short sentences in plain English, on the site and in docs. Internal names never appear as text on the site.
