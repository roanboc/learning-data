| # | Question | Assumption | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Where and when does the opening take place? | The seed said "South Africa, through the 20th century and today". A scenario needs a place and a time, so it's Johannesburg in the 1930s, where the module's source starts (Verhoef, *Enterprise & Society*, 2001: African women and stokvels in urban South Africa, 1930 to 1998), then "today" for the benefit. | Chapter 1 |
| 2 | In which order are the beats said? | The stokvel is named in the scenario, as what the women were doing; the problem follows (no bank account, no loans, the harm one funeral could do), then "Something had to change. They didn't wait for a bank: they built their own way, out of trust", with the rules, the burial society and the group carrying a member as the solution. | Chapter 1, the script's *Opening story* table |
| 3 | How to name where they lived? | "Crowded yards of rooms near the city", not "slum yards": the plain words for what Ellen Hellmann studied in *Rooiyard* (1948). | Chapter 1, "yard" line |
| 4 | Is "under apartheid" right for the 1930s? | The line says "Few Black South Africans had a bank account then, or for decades after, under apartheid": the 1930s were segregation, apartheid came from 1948, and the line covers both without dating apartheid to the 1930s. The next line says why banks wouldn't lend: low, irregular pay, and laws (the Natives Land Act of 1913, the Urban Areas Act of 1923) that kept them from owning property a bank would take as security. | Chapter 1, "banks" and "lend" lines |
| 5 | Which work to name? | Washing, sewing and selling. Brewing, the commonest, is left out: it was illegal for Black South Africans then, and would need a sentence of its own. | Chapter 1, "earn" line |
| 6 | Today's numbers? | "By the National Stokvel Association's count, more than 11 million South Africans belong to one", "over 50 billion rand a year": NASASA's estimate (about 810,000 stokvels), as FA News (2022) and IOL (10 October 2023) report it. Wikipedia's *Stokvel* gives another estimate (half of adults), so the page doesn't link it. | Chapter 1, "today" and "rand" lines; the page's links |
| 7 | How are the women dressed? | Dresses with aprons, or a cardigan over a blouse, earrings, and a head-tie over all the hair, knotted at the nape, kept inside the head's picture; no glasses. Drawn from general knowledge of the time, never a photograph. A first version with a cap-like cloth read as men's: fixed. | Chapter 1 |
| 8 | Which language for the saying? | isiZulu, *umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu*, the most widely spoken language around Johannesburg; the Xhosa, Sotho and Tswana forms are in the script. | Chapter 1, the Ubuntu sheet |
| 9 | Tutu's words? | Quoted as the widely reproduced passage of *No Future Without Forgiveness* (1999), the clause the film needs: "does not feel threatened that others are able and good". I couldn't open the book itself. | Chapter 4 |
| 10 | Ramose and Metz? | Named together as "philosophers in South Africa"; Metz's reading (2007) said plainly as "two things held together: sharing a life with others, and caring how their lives go" (identity and solidarity). | Chapter 6 |
| 11 | Psychological safety: how much? | Named once, as "the research word for part of it": Edmondson's 1999 definition in plain words, her hospital teams (the 1996 study, not dated on screen) and her 51 work teams (1999). | Chapter 3 |

The report developer is Tomás Ortega, as film 14 set. Recorded in `../numbers.md`. | Chapters 2, 4, 5 and 6 |

| 17 | How to put your point 8? | In its own words, once, early: "Managing and leading are both her job, but for Helena, leading weighs a little more, every time." "The people are the team's first strength, so their wellbeing comes first." "Wellbeing isn't only comfort. Sometimes one person needs more support, a better tool, encouragement or a push." "That way the team keeps working together, and no big gap opens."
