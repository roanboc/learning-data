# Between the business and the systems: questions and assumptions

*Enterprise architecture* · *Data architecture*, film 1 of 6 · PR #323 · [pull request](https://github.com/roanboc/learning-data/pull/323)

[Watch it on the site](https://data.learn-all.org/data-architecture/between-the-business-and-the-systems/)

Film 1 of *Data architecture*, `films/data-architecture/1-between-the-business-and-the-systems`, made on 11 and 12 November 2026 from the plan the author confirmed on 11 November (pull request #318) and the series' setup (#320). Every question below would have gone to the author, and was answered instead with the best-reasoned option, for him to confirm or correct when he watches the film.

| # | Question | Assumption taken | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Did Cybersyn's engineers draw each factory before choosing what to measure, or choose first? | Drew first. Medina (2006, pp. 588–589) says they "visited plants throughout the country, met workers and managers, selected approximately five critical variables of production, created flowchart models of factory operation" in one sentence, which doesn't settle the order; the film follows Beer's method, in which the indicators come from the factory's flowchart model, and the plan the author confirmed ("walked each factory and drew how it worked, then chose what to measure"). | Chapter 1, *Before any computer*; chapter 8 |
| 2 | Whose figures took six months? | The state's statistics on what the factories produced: Medina's "six-month lag required to collect statistics on the Chilean economy" (2006, p. 594), said as "its figures on what they made took about six months to collect". | Chapter 1 |
| 3 | Which factory does the film show? | A textile mill, drawn for the film, its steps cotton, spinning, weaving, finishing and shipping, and five marks: cotton in stock, yarn spun, cloth woven, cloth shipped and workers absent. Cybersyn's sector committees included the textile one (Medina 2006, p. 588); absence was its measure of "social unease" (p. 588). The marks are an example, not a record of one factory's indicators. | Chapter 1; chapter 8 |
| 4 | Who from Cybersyn is named? | CORFO and Stafford Beer only, with CORFO's team as silhouettes; Fernando Flores, who wrote to Beer for CORFO, isn't named, to keep the opening to four beats. The series draws Cybersyn's people as silhouettes lit from behind, never as faces. | Chapter 1 |
| 5 | Are the utility's numbers real? | No, invented and kept believable: six counts of customers, accounts, premises, supply addresses and service points that don't agree (396,054 to 412,118); a January storm that cut power to 3,400 connection points; 180 households, about 5%, whose texts failed an address match; 2,100 households in the next storm, every one reached; the ID CP-0048213; the address written "Unit 3, 12 Mill Road" by the outage system and "3/12 MILL RD" by customer service. | Chapters 1, 5 and 6 |
| 6 | What do the six systems call a connection point? | Service point (network map), metering point (meter data), premise (billing), supply address (customer service), service location (outage system), and a table of its own, `connection_point`, in the data platform. These follow the industry's usual names, in the spirit of the IEC Common Information Model's usage point, without quoting it. | Chapter 5 |
| 7 | Who owns the line between the word and its records? | Data architecture: Inés, as the utility's data architect, owns the join; Grace owns what connection point means, and each system's team owns how its system records it. The film shows a tick on every line once it has an owner, and names no person per line. | Chapters 5 to 7 |
| 8 | Which system issues the ID every system carries? | The network's: the film says "the network's ID" and shows it as CP-0048213. Where a connection point is born, and who issues its ID, is film 3's subject (*Where each fact is born*), so this film doesn't name the issuing system. | Chapter 6 |
| 9 | When did the systems arrive? | The life chart is invented: connection points across the whole span from 1960, the old billing system from 1998 until March 2026, the network map from 2004, the outage system from 2011, meter data from 2016, and the new billing system from March 2026. | Chapter 6 |
| 10 | Is web information architecture belittled? | No: the narration calls it "a real discipline, and not this one", and the stamp says only "not this one". | Chapter 3 |
| 11 | How precise is the film about DAMA, TOGAF and ArchiMate? | As the series' *Decided* row of 11 November says: DAMA knows the distinction and criticises splitting data and information; TOGAF names data architecture as one of four domains and groups it with the applications in Phase C; ArchiMate puts a business object in the business layer and a data object in the application layer, with nothing in between. The map's information layer is said to be drawn "as many architects do", not as a standard's. | Chapters 2 and 4 |
| 12 | Which sound? | An anémocorde (Paris, 1789), strings sounded by wind and chosen by keys, with its own bass strings: no film on the site plays it. The thread first chose Wheatstone's enchanted lyre (1821) and dropped it as too near data protection's round lyre. Reserved in `/mnt/project-files/enterprise-architecture/new-architecture-series/palettes.md`. | The soundtrack |
