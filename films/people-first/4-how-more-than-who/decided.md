| 12 November 2026 | *How, more than who* opens at Quehue, in the high Andes of Peru, on its own part of the series' story, the three days, in the four beats: every June four communities make a new bridge of grass rope across a gorge of the Apurímac river, twenty-eight metres long, built by about a thousand people in three days; no family could raise it alone, there is no drawing of it and no written plan, yet it has to hold for a year while people cross about fifteen metres above the river, and its strength depends on every cord in it, so everyone needs to know their own part and to trust everyone else's; what they have is a way of working that everyone knows (each family's promised cord, forty arm spans long, checked in on both banks; each community's part; cords twisted into ropes and three ropes braided into each cable of the floor, one man braiding until he tires and another taking his place; the cables pulled across by many hands and tied to old stone bases, the older guiding the younger; two builders weaving from the two banks until they meet in the middle); by the last afternoon four communities have a bridge for another year, and the next day they celebrate. It never says that nothing is written down (the communities keep written statutes), gives no total of ropes, and gives the order of the steps, not which day each falls on. It comes back in *Less about who* (the gorge has both from custom; in a data team somebody has to say them out loud), in *One page* (the way is passed on by doing it, parents beside children; a team has no such years, so it writes) and at the close (the bridge is made again each June, so the page is read again each quarter, and whenever someone joins). |
| 12 November 2026 | As the series' last film, it says what the story didn't achieve, in a chapter of its own, *Where the bridge stops*. The way of working still brings four communities together, and hasn't always kept the bridge: let go for a time around 1970, with a steel bridge newly built beside it; fallen in March 2021, after the pandemic stopped the work in 2020; cut down in a night of May 2025; and woven again each time. The weaving is taught only inside a builder's own family, as their tradition asks; fewer of the young speak Quechua, the language of the work; most people cross on the steel bridge now, so visitors help keep this one going; and custom settles who does which part. The film says that is the communities' own way and not ours to judge, and only that it isn't a model for a data team, which has to ask each person and can't leave what it knows with one person or one line of people. The lapse and the fall are *People before roles*'s and *Wellbeing is in the work*'s parts of the story, so each is one line here. |
