## The brief

*The new skills* shows what a data person needs to be good at now. This series is about the people who ask for data, and the three moments where working with them goes right or wrong. A request usually names someone's guess at a solution, so *The question behind the request* listens for the decision behind it and agrees it in writing. An average can hide what a decision turns on, so *Explaining so people can decide* leads with the decision, shows the peak as well as the average, says what isn't known, and lets the people who decide, decide. And there are more requests than people, so *Saying no, and what instead* says no to a date, not to the person: it finds what the date is for, shows the trade-off and offers what the team can do well, and when.

The series follows Graham Ellis's request from February to July 2027 (the module's shared numbers): his email of Thursday 11 February; half an hour with Helena and Noor on Tuesday 16 February; Semester 2 2026's thirteen teaching weeks of bookings and head counts; the rooms booked 38% of the week and 93% from 10:00 to 14:00, Tuesday to Thursday, and 21% of booked hours with nobody there; the team's finding of Friday 23 April 2027 that the rooms are short only at the peak; Graham's one page for the University Council on Tuesday 1 June; the University Council's decision on Thursday 24 June to defer the building for two years and change the timetable from Semester 1 2028; and Clara's request of Monday 12 July, answered with what the team can do, and when.
