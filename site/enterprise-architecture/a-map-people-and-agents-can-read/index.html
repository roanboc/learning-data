Topics › Enterprise architecture › The map before the data

A map people and agents can read: kept as text, reviewed and versioned, with every view generated from it

The Pacific, 1976: Hōkūleʻa sails from Maui to Tahiti with no compass and no charts. Its navigator, Mau Piailug, holds a star compass in his memory, and chooses to teach what few still knew. Then Tomás's map, used every week and going stale in slides and decks, fails the navigator's test: could someone else sail by it? He moves it to text, each element written once, with a stable ID, an owner and its evidence; every change is reviewed and versioned, and every view is generated from it, down to dbt and Databricks. An agent reads it, with its own rights on the map: it drafts, and people confirm. Then a newcomer's first day, this time with a map.
