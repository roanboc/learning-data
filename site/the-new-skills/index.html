The new skills

A series of Leading data people. In 2027 Helena Novak leads the university's data team, Ivy Reyes joins it straight from her degree, and Graham Ellis, the new Director of Facilities, asks for a dashboard of room use. What does a leader need their people to be good at now, and how do they grow it?

One thread: a year with the data team, and Graham's rooms

In 2027, a year from now, Helena Novak leads the university's data team, and Ivy Reyes joins it in March. Graham Ellis asks for a dashboard of room use; the question behind it is whether to ask the University Council for a new teaching building. Film by film, Ivy learns the whole route from his decision back to the rooms' sensors, frames work for an agent, and learns beside the people who know.

Every film opens somewhere else, at another time

FilmOpens withStill true today
Many hats, one pictureKaifeng, 1015: one move, three jobsA burned palace needed earth dug and carried in, timber hauled and rubble carted away: three slow jobs. Ding Wei, put in charge, saw them as one, and a trench in the street did all three. A leader who sees across a team's hats finds what falls between them.
The draft and the decisionLeón, 1998: with the machine, not against itA year after a computer beat the world chess champion, Kasparov played with a computer at his side. In 2005, two amateurs with three computers beat grandmasters and a supercomputer: the machines calculated, and the people chose what to look at, compared and decided.
Learning while doingAngicos, 1963: their own wordsAdults who couldn't read or write learned from their own words, in a culture circle each evening, one small step at a time, with someone beside them. After forty hours, the organisers counted about 300 who could read and write. Skills grow fastest on real work, beside someone.
