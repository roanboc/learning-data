Who may see it: a line a court drew in 1983, a planner's request, and a class written once, at the column

In 1983, West Germany's Constitutional Court stopped a census and drew a line: counting people is one permission, and knowing who they are is another. At the university, Clara in Planning needs each learner's status to plan next term's places. The old path would have given her the whole learner table, names and emails included, by name and with no end. The new one starts at the column: each column's owner writes down whether it identifies a person, the class travels with the column as a label, and one rule shows direct personal data to the registrar's office and stars to everyone else. Clara's access comes for a purpose, through a group, until a date, and Yusuf tells her why the stars are there and how to ask for more.
