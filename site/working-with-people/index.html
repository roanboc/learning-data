Working with people

The second series of Leading data people. Graham Ellis asks for a dashboard of room use by Easter. Behind his request is a decision worth about A$140 million: whether the university needs a new teaching building. The skills that help most here have little to do with tools.

One thread: Graham's dashboard, and the decision behind it

On 11 February 2027 Graham asks for a dashboard of room use by Easter. In half an hour with Helena and Noor, the real question appears: should he ask the University Council for a new teaching building? The team answers it from the bookings and the rooms' sensors, explains it on one page, and says what it can do well, and when, instead of a dashboard nobody would use.

Every film opens somewhere else, at another time

FilmOpens withStill true today
The question behind the requestPisa, 1990 to 2001: first, what saving meansThe Leaning Tower was closed for fear it would fall, and any work on it was a risk. Before choosing how, an international committee agreed what saving it would mean: about half a degree, too little to see. Agree what a request is for before the work starts.
Explaining so people can decideThe United States Air Force, 1950 to 1952: no average pilotDesigners sized what flyers wear and where they sit to the average man. Gilbert Daniels counted 4,063 flyers on ten measurements, and not one was average on all ten. His note said so in its first lines, and showed the range to design for. Show what a decision turns on, not only the average.
Saying no, and what insteadThe Aral Sea, 1960 to 2008: save the part that can be savedIts two rivers were turned to grow cotton and rice in the desert, and the sea shrank and split in two. Bringing it all back would have meant taking the water from the farms. Kazakhstan saved the northern part with a dam, and its water, its fish and its fishing came back.
