A reason to keep it: each piece, a reason you can say

Linh's tutoring contract ends on 20 November. HR holds her contracts, hours, bank details, a yes to its news, even a photo. Ingrid, who owns HR's data, asks of each piece what it's for, and which reason allows it. What has none goes, the form asks less, and a new use gets a check first.

Read the guidance yourself

The film names one law, Europe's GDPR, as the best known, and teaches the principles most privacy laws share. These are the texts its ideas come from. Checked on 10 November 2026.

  • GDPR, Articles 5 and 6: a purpose, as little as needed, six reasonsEurope's data protection law: a purpose said at the start, only what it needs, and the six lawful reasons.
  • Guidelines 05/2020 on consent, European Data Protection BoardWhen a yes is freely given, and why it rarely is between an employer and the people it employs.
  • The OECD Privacy GuidelinesThe oldest shared source of the principles: collect only what's needed, say what it's for, and keep to it.
  • The Oregon trial, The Lancet, 1988The study the opening tells: HIV testing under a number against testing under a name, and who came.
