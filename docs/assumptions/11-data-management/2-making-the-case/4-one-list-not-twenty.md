| 1 | The opening's seed is "Larrey's flying ambulances and the order of the wounded, the 1790s", but his written order for the wounded comes after Jena, in 1806. Tell both in the opening? | No. The opening tells the flying ambulances, in time order: the field hospital kept a league behind (1792), the wounded left until the battle ended and reached in no less than a day or a day and a half, most dying waiting (Speyer, 1792); the flying ambulance from 1793; Aboukir, 1799, 800 wounded, none waiting more than a quarter of an hour, "by his own account". The order comes back later: Jena, where the walking wounded took up the surgeons' first day (chapter 2), and his rule, "without regard to rank or distinction" (chapter 4). All from his memoirs (Hall's translation, 1814). | Chapters 1, 2 and 4 |
| 2 | Say that rank decided who was treated first before Larrey? | No: no source found says it outright. The film quotes only his words, "without regard to rank or distinction". | Chapter 4 |
| 3 | Which "triage today"? | The Australasian Triage Scale (the university is in Australia): five categories, each with a longest wait, from at once to two hours (ACEM's policy P06, November 2023). Said once, as the story's return in *Not now, and when*, and the word triage explained there. Larrey never used the word, and the film doesn't say he did. | Chapter 6 |
| 4 | One list for requests only, or for all the team's work? | All the work, as the brief says, on one board in three lanes: the roadmap (blue), requests (orange) and running and improving (no colour). The six pieces in progress at most are split as the time is: three, two and one (half, a third, the rest), as Kanban teams do with a limit per lane. | Chapter 5, the board |
| 5 | What does "risk" mean among value, urgency, effort and risk? | What could go wrong while a request waits (a private copy of personal data, a date missed). Value, urgency and risk together are what waiting costs, Reinertsen's cost of delay; divided by the effort, they give the order (his "CD3"; SAFe calls the same thing WSJF, not named). | Chapter 4 |
| 6 | How are they scored? | One to three dots each, on every request's card, and the order is (value + urgency + risk) ÷ effort. Three levels are enough for a team of eight and for a newcomer; relative scores such as Fibonacci's are named nowhere. | Chapter 4 |
