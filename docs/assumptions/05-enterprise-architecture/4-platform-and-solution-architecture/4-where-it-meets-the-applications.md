| 1 | Which part of the story does this film take? | The roads that joined the five: Shinano's farmers' horses, the *chūma*. In the 1600s Shinano's rivers couldn't carry boats, so goods crossed the passes on horseback; on the main roads they went by the stations' relay, a station's horses carrying a load only to the next station, where the shipper changed horses and paid again; officials took the horses first. Farmers carried loads on their own horses straight through, cheaper and less damaged; the stations went to court from 1673; in 1764 the shogunate ruled which goods went which way on each road, wrote down 679 villages and more than eighteen thousand horses, and had the farmers help keep the relay going. That year 9,600 loads went from Iida to Matsumoto. It stands for two kinds of traffic, each on its own route, under one rule written down. Checked against Kotobank (*Sekai daihyakka jiten*, *Nihon daihyakka zensho*, Yamakawa, Britannica's Japanese encyclopedia, *Mypedia*) and Japanese Wikipedia. | *A horse the whole way*, *Two kinds of traffic*, *One map, both teams*, *Two kinds of horse, one ruling* |
| 2 | What does the film leave unsaid? | The ruling's day: the sources give 1764 (Meiwa 1), one of them its twelfth month, which may fall in January 1765, so the film says "In 1764" and no day. The horses: the sources give 18,768 and 18,614, so the film says "more than eighteen thousand". The later suits (1693, 1739, 1760) are "again and again", and the 1873 merger of the chūma and the station companies isn't said. | *A horse the whole way* |
| 3 | Who is Yasmin Saleh? | A new person in the series, the utility's integration lead, who joins this year; technical, drawn like the rest of the cast in this film's own source, and added to the series' people. Ravi and Grace come back; Tomás's map is the practice map, as in every film. | *Flows nobody can see* onward; the series page |
| 4 | What is the integration platform? | The utility's platform for joining its applications (billing, the customer system, outages, metering and work orders), with two services: a gateway, through which one application asks another for something, and events, through which news of a change reaches every application that wants it; every message is checked and logged. No product is named. | *Flows nobody can see*, *The data practice on the map* |
| 5 | Are the numbers real? | No, invented: 34 flows found, one nightly read that broke and two days of wrong reports, forty million meter readings a night, and the split, 25 native, six events and three through the gateway. Grace's feeder-risk score is a new flow, added after the review, so it isn't one of the 34. The customer system has eight of the 34 flows, which the map shows at its next upgrade. | *Flows nobody can see*, *Better together* |
| 6 | Why does Ravi agree with only half of Yasmin's rule? | Every flow must be seen and governed, so every flow goes on the map; but bulk loads and history come in natively, through the data platform's own connectors, since forty million readings sent as one message each would queue at the integration platform, slower and costlier. The film draws the queue and says so; it gives no figures for the cost. | *Two kinds of traffic* |
| 7 | Is the gateway the right route for a score served to an application? | By the film's rule, yes: data served back to an application goes through the integration platform's gateway, as an API the outage app already calls, so the flow is seen, checked and logged like any other. Practitioners also serve such data as events, or by copying it into the application's own store; the film keeps one rule for each kind of flow and leaves how flows are built to *Data that moves* and how a team chooses for each source to *A path for every provider*. Worth the author's look. | *The simplest route for each*, *A score for the outage app* |
| 8 | How does the data platform take the integration platform's events? | It subscribes to them, as any application does, instead of reading the customer system a second way; the film doesn't say how it lands them in its tables. Lakeflow Connect, named once as the platform's own connectors in Databricks, has streaming connectors for message buses, but the film names no connector. | *The simplest route for each*, *Better together* |
| 9 | Who does what? | A new flow goes on the map in the pull request that builds it, reviewed by the team whose platform carries it. Grace asks in a ticket; network's data product makes the score; the data team reviews its pull request; the integration team adds its route to the gateway; Grace checks the scores in the outage app, in test, before the ticket closes. No business user reviews a pull request. | *One map, both teams*, *A score for the outage app* |
| 10 | Is the practice map right in ArchiMate? | The integration platform is a node in the technology layer beside the data platform, with a gateway and events as its technology services; the data platform's connectors are its service; each flow is an ArchiMate flow relationship between an application and a data product, labelled with its route, since the relationship has no route of its own; the rule is a principle in the motivation layer. | *The data practice on the map* |
| 11 | May the film pair two instruments the site already has? | Yes, under the rule *An application or a platform?* asked about (its assumptions, row 1): a hurdy-gurdy with a bandora, in F, tuned pure from F. Worth a listen. | The film's music; the four series' palettes list |
