Borrowed from the brain: what models took, and didn't

In 1888, nobody could see how a brain was wired: under the microscope it was a thicket. Santiago Ramón y Cajal made a stain work and drew separate cells passing messages one way. Machines borrowed that picture. Why do they, like brains, fill a gap with what's likely?

Watch nerve cells learn for yourself

Learning Systems, another learn-all.org site, runs small living worlds you can watch and change. In these, nets of nerve cells and the first brains learn from what they meet, with no one to tell them.

  • Nerve netsA net of nerve cells with no centre: one cell tastes richer food and fires, the signal spreads, and the whole body moves toward the food.
  • BrainsThe same coast, about 540 million years ago: creatures with eyes and brains see green and violet plants, and learn which colour is food and which is poison.
  • Learning a colourA guided lab: how one connection, changed by what each bite brings, turns into a creature that knows what to eat.
