The same problem, everywhere: a bank that can't add up its risks, a body of knowledge written by the people who did the work, and why governance is still at its centre

In 2008, a bank's board asks how much it stands to lose if another bank fails, and nobody can add it up: the numbers are all there, but nobody can put them together, or say which to trust. It wasn't a new problem. In 1980, the people who looked after the data companies shared between departments started meeting to share what they'd learned, and in 2009 their association, DAMA, wrote it down as a body of knowledge for data management, the DMBOK, with governance at the centre of its wheel. Regulators, laws and new technology have asked the same question ever since: who decides about this data, and who answers for it? The book has grown with them, with ethics in 2017 and AI in 2024, and a third edition is being written. Governance stays at the centre.
