## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has ever locked a door can follow it, and a security architect finds nothing wrong in it. Identity is the boundary (*Who is it, really?*), but walls still matter: not because anyone inside them is trusted, but because they keep the ways in few and the rooms small, so a mistake or an intruder stays where it started. In 1377, Ragusa, a walled republic that lived by its port, kept trading through the plague years with a law: whoever came from a place with plague waited a month apart, on an islet or in a town down the coast, before coming in through the gates, and each night the gates' keys went to the rector. The university's platform starts with one door on the open internet, one room for everything, a way straight to the files, data free to leave, copies any admin could delete and a key someone else keeps. Sam asks how far an intruder could go, writes down who secures what, draws the walls from the map's areas, and then gives the data few doors, rooms apart, keys the university holds, and copies nobody can delete. **Few doors, each with a lock you can see.**

## The story in one paragraph

Ragusa, today's Dubrovnik, in the 1370s: a small republic of merchants behind high stone walls on the Adriatic, living by its ships, with only a few gates. In 1348 the ships brought the plague, which killed thousands in the city and kept coming back; at the gate nobody could tell a sick traveller from a well one, shutting the port would end the city's living, and leaving it open let the sickness in to every family. On 27 July 1377 its Great Council made a law: anyone coming from a place with plague must first spend a month apart, on the islet of Mrkan or in the town of Cavtat, down the coast, and only then may enter; no one from the city may go to them, or they stay the month too. Later, health officers watch every ship and every gate. The port stays open, the city keeps its living, and the sickness, when it comes, is held on an island instead of loose in the streets; Ragusa is the first city known to have made it law, other ports followed, and the wait grew to forty days and gave the world the word quarantine. At the university, the nightly job no longer carries Jun's token, but Sam Okafor asks what an attacker would: if someone did get in, how far could they go? Today, the platform's door is on the open internet; inside, one workspace holds the work in progress and the real records; every area's data is in reach; a way runs straight to the files in storage, past the catalog; data can leave for anywhere; the backups sit beside the data, where any admin can delete them; and the cloud provider keeps the data's key. Nobody chose it: the platform started small and grew. Sam writes down who secures what: the cloud provider its buildings, machines and network; the platform's makers its software and how it checks each request; the university everything else, and always its data. The walls follow the areas on the university's map, each with its owner. Then the doors: no door on the open internet, a private path from the university's own network, a private path to the storage, a short list of places data may go, and the catalog as the files' only door, with the back door shut unless an admin account is switched on to read the storage. One workspace becomes three, development, test and production, with made-up or masked data outside production and one reviewed path between them, like the islet off Ragusa. The key becomes the university's own, kept by its security team in a vault of its own: the platform may use it, never take it out, and the security team can take it back; every use is recorded, and a deleted key is kept ninety days, which nobody can cut short, as Ragusa's keys went to the rector each evening. Each night a copy of production's data goes to a place apart, locked for thirty days so not even an admin can delete it, and once a month one is restored to prove it works. Sam walks the attacker's path again: no door from the internet, and with a stolen way in, only what that one person may reach, through the catalog's checks, with no back door, nowhere to send it, no key to take and no copy to delete. Like Ragusa, the university's data stays open to the people and jobs that need it: few doors, each with a lock you can see.

## The reference, and practice

The module's reference holds (its proposal, *The reference*): DAMA-DMBOK's data security area for what is protected and why, and zero trust (NIST SP 800-207) for how. For this film's question, where the data's boundaries are and who holds what, the thread took these, named once here; the film teaches what they agree on and names none of them on screen.

| Body of knowledge | What it adds | Where the film uses it |
|---|---|---|
| NIST SP 800-207, *Zero Trust Architecture* (August 2020) | "Network location alone does not imply trust" (tenet 2); a perimeter alone fails because "once attackers breach the perimeter, further lateral movement is unhindered"; "the implicit trust zone must be as small as possible"; its tenets "aim to reduce the exposure of resources to attackers and minimize or prevent lateral movement" | *Who secures what* ("A wall doesn't make anyone inside it trusted"); *If someone got in*; *The walk again* |
| The cloud providers' shared responsibility model (Microsoft Learn, *Shared responsibility in the cloud*, updated 24 August 2026) | "For all cloud deployment types, you own your data and identities"; whatever the deployment, the customer always keeps the data, the devices that reach it, the accounts and access management; the provider keeps the physical buildings, network and hosts | *Who secures what* |
| ISO/IEC 27001:2022, Annex A, controls 8.31 *Separation of development, test and production environments* and 8.33 *Test information* | Three environments kept apart, and test data chosen and protected so production's personal data isn't copied into test | *Rooms apart* |
| NCSC, *Principles for ransomware-resistant cloud backups* (22 November 2024) | Backups "resilient to destructive actions": "Blocking any deletion or alteration requests for a backup once it is created", "Delaying implementation of any deletion or alteration requests", "Forbidding destructive requests from customer accounts", "both user and machine identities, such as administrative users"; restoring from an earlier version; "Robust key management for data-at-rest protection"; alerts on privileged actions | *Copies nobody can delete*; *Keys you can see* |
| Customer-managed keys, as the cloud providers build them (Microsoft Learn, *Customer-managed keys for Azure Storage encryption* and *Azure Key Vault soft-delete overview*, read 5 November 2026; *Network, storage and keys* cites them) | The provider encrypts by default with its own keys; with a customer's key, the data can be read only while the customer allows it, and taking the key back blocks access to all of it; the key vault keeps a deleted key for up to 90 days, and purge protection stops anyone cutting that short | *Keys you can see* |

**Where the film differs, on purpose.** Zero trust says the network is no boundary of trust; the film keeps walls anyway and says what they're for: few ways in and small rooms, never trust. It doesn't teach network design, firewalls or how encryption works: *Network, storage and keys* shows the paths and keys in the tools, and the module's proposal says the network itself is shown, not taught.

**Where the metaphor stops.** Inside Ragusa's walls, once a traveller had waited and come in, they walked the streets freely; on the platform, every request is still checked inside the walls (*Who is it, really?*), and the film says so in *Who secures what*. The islet is a place apart for what isn't trusted yet, as development and test are for changes; Ragusa's newcomers were people, and the film never says the platform treats people as a sickness: what waits apart is a change, and what never comes in is a copy of real records.

**Practice.** What practitioners recognise: a platform that started as one workspace with its first settings, open to the internet because that's how it came, where people try things next to the jobs that load the real records; a storage account any notebook can read with the right role, past every rule the catalog keeps; data that can be sent anywhere because nobody listed where it should go; backups in the same place, under the same admins, which ransomware deletes first; and keys nobody at the university has ever seen. And what works: draw the boundaries from the areas the business already owns, close the door on the open internet before anything else, give the files one door, keep made-up or masked data outside production, hold your own key with a team that doesn't run the platform, and restore a backup on a schedule, not on the worst day.

**The author's practice** (3 November 2026, the module's *Decided* table): direct access to storage is covered, because no mask or policy applies there, and only an admin account switched on for a task reaches it. The film says it in its own words: "Storage keeps files, and knows nothing of who may see which column: no masks, no rules."

## The look

*Night glass*, the module's look, chosen by the author on 5 November 2026 (frosted glass with solid colour), from the series' kit in `films/locks-before-the-data/shared/`.

- **The hall.** The university's scenes are in the module's dark hall of frosted panes, frosted where what's behind may not be read and clear where it may. The platform is a tall block of clear glass, as in *No secret to lose*; this film gives it its rooms, its doors, its vault and its copies. The board where Sam writes and the university's map are corners of the same hall.
- **The past** is painted in the same light: Ragusa at dusk, at night and at dawn under the same petrol sky, its stone walls cool, its windows warm, the sea dark and slow; the gate from the quay, at dusk and at night; a street of tall houses up to the rector's palace; the council's vaulted hall, its nobles on their benches; the table where the law is sealed, in a candle's light; and the coast as a frosted map. A tag, top left, gives each place and year. Nothing is borrowed from the city's own arts or crafts: no motifs, no lettering styles, only the shapes of walls, gates, ships and roofs.
- **Colour carries the module's meanings, and nothing else.** Cyan is an identity (people's and the job's badges; the catalog's ring when it checks), and the plain colour of the opening's cards; green allowed (the reviewed path, a tick, the short list, a restored copy); coral denied or closed (the intruder's path, a shut door, the plague's rim and haze, a key taken back, a copy locked); amber power switched on (once, at the back door); violet hidden data (the masked values in development and test, the frost of encryption, and the cards on keeping and deleting); gold the chip on the sealed card. A key is always a sealed card with a gold chip: no real-looking secret, token or key is ever drawn. The plague's coral in the opening is the module's colour for what must be kept out.
- **People** have the agent films' geometry and one three-quarter head, lit at night with a cool rim: in 1377, porters and travellers at the gate, small, in tunics and cloaks of the time; the council's nobles on their benches; health officers with lanterns; the keys' bearer in a hooded cloak, a hand only on the ring of keys; and Sam, drawn as in the series' other films, arms down, a hand only on the marker while writing who secures what. The sick are never shown: windows going dark, and a coral haze held at the islet. The intruder is never shown: a coral dotted line.
- **Every word** at least 28 px as seen and at least 4.5 to 1 against what's behind it; nothing under y 890; names round the block fade out over the last 44 px before the frame's edge, so the camera never cuts a word in half. **The camera** glides in one room: *If someone got in*, *Few doors* and *The walk again* glide on from where the chapter before ends, in the hall. ## Sound

- **The douçaine leads.** The soft reed instrument of the 1300s (Machaut names it in the 1360s, the decade before the opening's law), as reconstructed from the one found in the *Mary Rose*: a double reed on a narrow cylindrical bore whose far end is closed, so the sound leaves only by its few holes. Its odd harmonics carry it, the even ones faint: hollow, reedy and sweet, nothing above 2.2 kHz. Played softly in its middle range, G3 to D5, one voice, no vibrato. Few doors, each one you can see: each phrase a few notes, the last left open, on the fifth or the second, never home. Its mark, the film's: D, F, A, one note for each gate as it lights, and then E, left open.
- **The timpani are the walls.** Two drums, soft, with felt sticks, tuned to D and A: a note as a gate or a door shuts (the gate in *Few gates*, the platform's door, the back door, the key taken back, the gate at night), under the opening's turns, and the two together as the walls rise. ## Rigour to check before recording

Checked on 5 November 2026; the sources are listed below.

- **Ragusa, a republic of merchants, behind walls, with few gates.** Ragusa, today's Dubrovnik in Croatia, governed itself as a republic from 1358, under the Hungarian-Croatian crown's protection, until 1808, and lived by sea trade and the caravan trade inland. Its walls, built and rebuilt from the 1200s to the 1600s, run about two kilometres round the old city, with two land gates and two harbour gates today; the film says "only a few gates" and draws three, lit in turn, without naming them or their dates (the Pile and Ploče gates as they stand are later than 1377). "Its ships sail the whole Mediterranean" is the republic's trade at large; the film doesn't size its fleet.
- **The plague of 1348.** The Black Death reached Ragusa in early 1348 and "killed a few thousand citizens" (*Lazzarettos of Dubrovnik*, Wikipedia, read 5 November 2026); the film says "thousands" and shows no number. It returned many times in the following decades, which "it kept coming back" says. "A sickness that spread fast, and killed within days" says what people saw; the film doesn't say how it spread (fleas, then from person to person when it reached the lungs), which nobody in 1377 knew.
- **"At the gate, nobody could tell a sick traveller from a well one."** Plague takes days to show after someone is infected; a traveller who looked well at the gate could fall ill inside. That's why the law's answer is a wait, long enough for any sickness to show, rather than a look at each traveller.
- **The law.** "On 27 July 1377, the Great Council of the Republic adopted a decree which introduced a quarantine ... by which all merchants, sailors, and goods arriving from 'suspicious lands' could not enter the city if they haven't spent a month in a quarantines which were on the remote, uninhabited islands of Mrkan, Bobara and Supetar" (*Lazzarettos of Dubrovnik*); "a 30-day separation period (trentino) for visitors from plague-endemic areas" (CDC, *Emerging Infectious Diseases*, "Etymologia: quarantine", February 2013). Its title in the Republic's *Liber viridis*, "Veniens de locis pestiferis non intret Ragusium vel districtum" ("whoever comes from plague-stricken places shall not enter Ragusa or its district"), is quoted the same way by several accounts; the film shows a few lines of Latin as texture only, never as words to read. The places: the islet of Mrkan and the town of Cavtat, down the coast, as most accounts give them (Bobara and Supetar, the islets beside Mrkan, are named by some; the film names the two the narration can say). "The Great Council, the nobles who made the city's laws": the Republic's Great Council was its assembly of the noble men, which made its laws.
- **"No one from the city may go to them: whoever does, stays the month too."** The decree's rule that anyone from the city who went to those in isolation, or brought them food without the Council's leave, had to stay there the month too (as reported by *El Español*, 13 March 2020, and in English in the history of medicine's standard summaries of the decree); the fine for breaking the law isn't shown.
- **"Later, health officers watch every ship and every gate."** Ragusa set up health officers, the *kacamorti*, to see the quarantine kept: three were named by a decree of 1397 (*Lazzarettos of Dubrovnik*), and the city had a permanent health office from 1390 (Blažina Tomić and Blažina, *Expelling the Plague*, 2015, the publisher's description). The film says "later" and gives no date. From 1590 the city built its great lazaretto by its eastern gate; the film doesn't show it.
- **The benefits.** No source gives a count of lives the law saved, so the film gives none: it says what the law made possible, an open port with the sickness held outside the walls, which is how the decree worked. "Ragusa is the first city known to have made it a law": "Quarantine was first introduced in 1377 in Dubrovnik" (Tognotti, *Emerging Infectious Diseases*, February 2013, citing Grmek); the medical historian Mirko Grmek's study of it is titled "the foundation of the first quarantine in Dubrovnik (1377)" (*Rad JAZU*, 1980); McGill-Queen's describes Blažina Tomić and Blažina's book as showing Dubrovnik "the first city in the world to develop and implement quarantine legislation". Earlier measures elsewhere (Reggio Emilia, 1374) sent the sick out of a city; Ragusa's was the first known law to make arrivals wait apart, so "known" stays in the line.
- **Forty days, and the word.** "In the following decades, the practice spread to other cities and was extended from 30 to 40 days"; "From the Italian *quaranta* (forty), 'quarantine' refers to the practice established in European port cities during the Black Death requiring vessels to lie at anchor for 40 days before landing" (CDC, "Etymologia"). The film says "Other ports followed, and the wait grew to forty days", without saying where first.
- **The keys and the rector.** The Republic's rector, its head of state, was chosen for one month at a time and lived in the rector's palace; each evening the city's gates were locked and their keys given to the rector to keep until morning, when the rector handed them back to the city's guards. Guides to the palace (now the Museum of Dubrovnik, which still shows the keys of the city's gates) describe the custom; none we read dates it to 1377, so the film says "for centuries", and shows it as a scene of the Republic, not of the law. "Everyone knew who held them" is the film's point: the custom was public, done in the street, every evening.
- **The job and the platform at the start.** The nightly job, `registrar_nightly_load` at 02:00, runs as `registrar-loader`, owned by Sam Okafor, with no secret since *No secret to lose*; "from the one workspace the university had, open to the internet" is the module's `numbers.md`, and this is the film that closes it. "The backups sit beside the data, where any admin can delete them" and "the cloud provider keeps the data's key" are this film's starting state, set in `numbers.md`'s *Doors, keys and copies*; providers encrypt stored data by default with keys they manage, which protects against a stolen disk, and the customer can't take those keys back.
- **"Nobody chose this."** A blameless start: the platform began as one workspace with its first settings and grew around it, as platforms do.
- **Who secures what.** The cloud provider's part, "its buildings, its machines, and the network between them", is the physical data centre, hosts and network (Microsoft Learn, *Shared responsibility in the cloud*: the provider keeps "physical security, physical network, physical hosts"); the platform's makers' part, "its software, and how it checks every request", is the platform as a service; and the university's part is what every provider's model leaves with the customer, whatever the deployment: "you own your data and identities", and keep "Data", "Endpoints", "Accounts" and "Access management". "Whatever the contract says, the data is the university's to protect" is that line, in plain words. The film names no provider.
- **Walls from the map's areas.** The five areas are the module's catalogs by area (`numbers.md`: registrar, learning, planning, hr, finance), shown in plain words; each has an owner on the university's register (*Who decides*). The example project's areas for the catalog's Discover page (DEC-PRJ-15: student, course, learning, planning, finance, wallet) and *Who decides*' eight areas are finer; the film shows the module's five and lists the difference for the author (`assumptions.md`).
- **"A wall doesn't make anyone inside it trusted."** NIST SP 800-207, tenet 2: "Network location alone does not imply trust"; a perimeter alone fails because "once attackers breach the perimeter, further lateral movement is unhindered"; "the implicit trust zone must be as small as possible". The film's "walls keep the ways in few and the rooms small, so a mistake, or an intruder, stays where it started" is that: fewer exposed resources and less lateral movement, with every request still checked inside.
- **Private paths.** "People and jobs reach it only by a private path, from the university's own network": the platform's front end and its connections are reached only over private network links; staff away from campus join the university's network first. "The platform reaches the storage by a private path too": storage reached over a private link, never its public address. "Data may leave only for places on a short list": outbound traffic allowed only to listed destinations; the film's three are examples (the government's returns; the university's reports; the backups' place apart). *Network, storage and keys* builds them in the tools.
- **The files' one door.** Storage controls who may read a file, but knows nothing of columns, masks or row rules, which the catalog applies; anyone who reads the files directly skips them (the author's practice, 3 November 2026; *Power you switch on*). "The back door ... stays shut: only an admin account, switched on to read the storage, opens it" is *Power you switch on*'s, in its words, agreed with its thread on 5 November 2026.
- **Rooms apart.** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 8.31 (environments separated and secured) and 8.33 (test information selected and protected); "Development and test get made-up or masked data, never a copy of production's" is the module's practical advice. The three workspaces, and each one bound only to its own catalogs, are `numbers.md`'s, set with *From a branch to production*, which builds the "one reviewed path" and starts from this film by title (agreed with its thread on 5 November 2026).
- **Encryption and the key.** "On disk and on the move": encryption at rest and in transit. "Until now, the cloud provider did": the provider's default keys. "Now the key is the university's own, kept by its security team, in a vault of its own": a customer-managed key in a key vault the security team runs; the platform's storage uses it to unwrap the keys that encrypt the data, and never holds the key itself; the people who run the platform may use the key but can't delete or disable it (agreed with *Network, storage and keys* and *Who did what*, 5 November 2026). "The security team can take it back, and then nobody can read the data, not even the cloud provider": revoking a customer-managed key blocks access to the data it protects. "Lose that key, and the data is lost with it": data encrypted under a key that's gone can't be read again. "Every use is recorded": the vault's logs. "A deleted key is kept ninety days, which nobody can cut short": soft delete with a 90-day retention and purge protection, which the providers require for a customer-managed key on storage. The vault makes a new version of the key every year (`numbers.md`); the film doesn't say it.
- **Copies nobody can delete.** "An attacker who gets in often deletes the backups first, then locks the data, and asks for money to unlock it": ransomware, as the NCSC's principles and CISA's #StopRansomware guide describe it (both advise backups an attacker can't reach or destroy). "A place apart, with admins of its own": a separate account and subscription, none of production's admins among its admins. "Locked for thirty days: nobody can change or delete it before then, not even an admin": an immutable, locked retention period, which the NCSC calls "Blocking any deletion or alteration requests" and "Forbidding destructive requests from customer accounts", "such as administrative users". "Once a month, Sam's team restores one": a tested restore, the NCSC's principle 3 and CISA's "regularly test". The numbers, nightly, 30 days and monthly, are this film's, set in `numbers.md` and copied by *Who did what* and *Deleted means deleted* (agreed with their threads on 5 November 2026): each copy holds each table as it stands that night, so a person's deleted rows leave the last copy made before their deletion when it's removed, 30 days later.
- **"A copy nobody has restored is a hope, not a backup."** Practitioners' saying, in the film's words; *Who did what* echoes it.
- **The walk again.** "With a stolen way in, there's only what that one person may reach, in one workspace, through the catalog's checks": rights from roles, never people (*Who is it, really?*); the walls of this film; the catalog's checks. It doesn't claim an intruder can do nothing: they still reach what that person may, which is why *Who did what* watches.
- **Agents.** The film doesn't show an agent; an agent reaches the platform as a workload, through the same private paths and the same catalog (*AI agents in data work*).
- **No release status** is shown or said (the author's rule of 5 November 2026). The film names no product.
