| 1 | Which part of the story does this film take? | The road before the Gokaidō: in the 1400s every owner of a stretch of river or road (the court, the shogunate, temples, shrines and lords) could set a barrier and take a toll; in 1462 the Yodo alone had 380, it's said, and the road to Ise once had sixty in the eighteen kilometres from Kuwana to Hinaga. Lords cleared their own lands, Hideyoshi forbade the tolls from about 1585, the Tokugawa ran the five roads under one set of rules and in 1635 forbade the lords barriers of their own; each domain kept its own lord, laws and taxes, and shared the road. In 1771 more than two million pilgrims went to Ise. It stands for every platform's own rules, and one platform shared by many owners. Checked against Kotobank (*Nihon daihyakka zensho*, *Sekai daihyakka jiten*, Britannica's Japanese encyclopedia), Japanese Wikipedia (武家諸法度) and Kokugakuin University's *Encyclopedia of Shinto*. | *A toll at every barrier*, *When two can't be helped*, *One road, many domains* |
| 2 | What does the film leave unsaid? | Nobunaga's year: the one found (1568) is only in a secondary source, so the film says "Oda Nobunaga among them" with no year. Whether the shogunate's checkpoints took a fee: the sources read don't settle it, so the film says only that they were there to keep order and check who passed. The 380 is said with the source's own "it's said". | *A toll at every barrier*, *When two can't be helped* |
| 3 | Which checkpoints are shown? | Hakone and Arai (Imagiri) on the Tōkaidō and Usui on the Nakasendō, the three the sources name, among about fifty. Hakone's checkpoint isn't the station of *Inside the platform*, though both stood by the lake. | *When two can't be helped* |
| 4 | Who is generation? | A domain new to the series: the utility's hydro and wind farms, with their readings, on a platform bought years ago from another vendor, unnamed. The earlier films drew network, retail, finance and the enterprise domain. | *Two platforms, and a third* onward |
| 5 | What is Ama's measure? | The share of green-tariff customers' use, hour by hour, met by the utility's own farms: one measure that needs both platforms, which is why the analysts propose a third product. The numbers are invented. | *Two platforms, and a third*, *Every rule, once* |
| 6 | Does the move copy any data? | No, and the film says why: generation's tables are already files in an open table format in the utility's own cloud storage, so the data platform reads them where they are. The script says a platform that keeps its tables in its own storage or in a closed format would need a copy first. Until the move is done, the data platform's catalogue federates generation's, so Ama's measure starts at once. | *Generation moves*, `script.md`, *Rigour* |
| 7 | Who does what in the move? | Ama asks in a ticket; a tool converts most of the jobs' code, with no product named; the data team reviews each pull request and compares every number with the old platform's for a month; generation checks its own reports in test; after four months the old platform is switched off; the two people who kept it running now build generation's data products. No business user reviews a pull request. | *Generation moves*, *Every rule, once* |
| 8 | Is "months, not years" a promise? | No: the film says moving "can take months, not years", from the author's brief (moving to one platform is easier than it was); the move it shows takes four months. | *As simple as it can be*, *Generation moves* |
| 9 | How is data mesh put? | As Zhamak Dehghani set it out: each domain owns its data, as products, on a self-serve platform, under rules the domains agree together; that platform is one platform, shared, so a data mesh is often the simplest architecture. Done badly, the old teams with new names and nobody in the business owning anything (Thoughtworks, *The state of data mesh in 2026*). | *Many owners, one platform* |
| 10 | Is the practice map right in ArchiMate? | Generation's platform is a second node with its own services to govern and secure; its data products stay in the application layer, and only the serve line moves to the data platform; Ravi's rule is a principle in the motivation layer; the move is a work package, ending the day the old platform was switched off. | *The data practice on the map* |
