## 12 November 2026: the author's answer

Asked on his final checklist whether admin access switched on for at most four hours, approved by a peer, with an alert each time, is the practice he'd show (rows 2 to 4 above), Rodrigo answered on 12 November 2026:

> yes, having also separate accounts, so platform contributors will have their standard user for standard operations and admin accounts with temporary permissions for more sensitive operations and access. Not sure if organisations need to pay 2 account licenses for that, I guess they do. In that case at least the elevated permissions for the normal account would be good to have, which is more common now by Cloud providers (Azure my reference)

So the practice stays as the film shows it: two accounts for each specialist, the admin account's power off until it's switched on for a task, for four hours at most, with another specialist's yes, an alert and a record. What's new is the case where a second account isn't practical. *A second account* now ends on three lines: "A second account isn't always practical: it can cost a second licence." "In that case, the power stays in the everyday account, but switched off, until a task needs it." "That's one lock, not two: still far safer than power that's always on." The film is 8:51 (it was 8:32). Row 5 (who approves when one of two specialists is away) wasn't part of the answer and is still open.

**The licence question, checked on 12 November 2026.** In Microsoft Entra ID, his reference, the safe reading is yes. Microsoft's licensing page asks for a Microsoft Entra ID P2 or Microsoft Entra ID Governance licence for every user with an eligible or time-bound role assignment in Privileged Identity Management, for members and owners of PIM for Groups, and for everyone who approves a switch-on. An admin account that switches roles on is such a user, so it needs a licence of its own: US$10.00 a user a month for P2 at list price, paid yearly, or US$7.00 for ID Governance. It needs no mailbox and no office licence. The page doesn't say whether the licence a person already holds on their everyday account covers their admin account too, and the answers on Microsoft's own Q&A site disagree, so that part is a question for a Microsoft licensing contact. Switching power on in the everyday account costs nothing more where that account already has P2, which Microsoft 365 E5 includes. Other tools differ: GitHub Enterprise counts a licence for each unique user, and Azure Databricks is priced by what runs, not by user. The pages are listed in the script's sources.
