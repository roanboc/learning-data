| 12 November 2026 | *One platform, or many* takes its four beats from Japan's toll barriers, the road before the Gokaidō: in 1462 boats carried rice, goods and people along the Yodo between Kyoto and the sea; every stretch of river and road had owners (the court, the shogunate, temples, shrines and lords), any of whom could set a barrier and take a toll; that year the Yodo alone had 380 barriers, it's said, and the road to Ise once had sixty in the eighteen kilometres from Kuwana to Hinaga; a load stopped and paid at every one, so goods came late and dearer. Lords cleared their own lands, Oda Nobunaga among them; from about 1585 Toyotomi Hideyoshi forbade the tolls; under the Tokugawa the five roads ran under one set of rules through every domain, and in 1635 the lords were forbidden barriers of their own; each domain kept its own lord, laws and taxes, and shared the road; travellers paid for a horse, a porter or a night at an inn, and in 1771 more than two million pilgrims went to Ise. It comes back to the story in *When two can't be helped* (the shogunate's own checkpoints, about fifty, there to keep order) and at the end (the road from Kuwana to Hinaga, open). Nobunaga's year isn't said, since the one source for it is secondary; nor is whether the checkpoints took a fee, which the sources read don't settle. |
| 12 November 2026 | The film's data story: years ago, generation bought a data platform from another vendor, and every other domain uses the data platform; the enterprise domain needs both for one measure, the share of green-tariff customers' use, hour by hour, met by the utility's own farms, and Ama asks for it in a ticket; its analysts propose a third product to query both where they are. Ravi draws what each brings, its own way to say who's who and who may see what, its own masks on private data, its own lineage and its own log: every rule kept three times. Generation's platform has no reason to stay, so it moves: its tables are already files in an open format in the utility's own cloud storage, so no data is copied; the data platform's catalogue federates generation's until the move is done, so the measure starts at once; a tool converts most of the jobs' code, the data team reviews each pull request and compares every number with the old platform's for a month, generation checks its own reports in test, and after four months the old platform is switched off. The two people who kept it running now build generation's data products. The numbers are invented. |
| 12 November 2026 | The film's positions, from the author's brief: the simplest architecture that does the job, since unmanaged complexity is what makes services fail; few components on one platform for most organisations, many platforms joined only for a few, such as a group spread over many countries; moving to one easier than it was, months, not years. A data mesh is many owners on one self-serve platform (Dehghani), often the simplest architecture; done badly, the old teams with new names. A second platform can't be helped for a company just bought, until it moves, a separate legal entity that must keep its data apart, or data that must stay in its own country; open table formats and a federated catalogue lessen the harm, but each platform keeps its own permissions, policies and logs. |
