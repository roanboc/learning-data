Show the least that still answers the question

Anil Kurian studies how learners in the Faculty of Health progress. The records, as kept, would show him names, birthdays, and who has a disability; hide it all, and he can't follow anyone. So Mei Tanaka picks a way to hide each column, and the study never sees who they are.

Go further

The film names no product. These pages go further into its story and its ideas: a museum's account of the secret ballot, Europe's guidance on codes in place of names, and two public guides to hiding values.

  • What is the secret ballot?The Museum of Australian Democracy on open voting, the ballot of 1856 and how it spread.
  • Guidelines 01/2025 on pseudonymisationThe European Data Protection Board on codes in place of names, and why data that a key can bring back stays personal.
  • Pseudonymisation techniques and best practicesENISA, the EU's cybersecurity agency, on plain and keyed hashes, tokens and encryption, and the attacks each must withstand.
  • De-identifying government datasets (SP 800-188)NIST's guide to removing identifiers, generalising values and publishing totals with small counts hidden.
