| 5 November 2026 | The opening, as the module's `openings.md` proposed it and checked against the treaty's text and the UN Treaty Collection: Vienna, November 1968. More people than ever drive from one country into the next, and the countries at a United Nations conference have spent a month (7 October to 8 November) on the rules of the road. But their signs still differ, and many give their message in words, in the country's own language: a driver from abroad can pass a warning of a sharp bend and never know what it said, and a missed warning can end in a crash. On 8 November, 31 countries sign the Convention on Road Signs and Signals, which defines each sign once, by its shape, its colour and its symbol (a triangle edged in red warns, a circle edged in red forbids or sets a limit, a blue circle says what you must do). Its benefits: 75 countries keep it today, and a driver reads the same signs from one country to the next; and the signs have readers nobody planned for, since every new car in the European Union must read the speed limit off the signs it passes (since July 2024). The same problem and benefit for data: casual teaching cost, defined once, has readers nobody planned for too, and read where it's defined, each as itself, every reader gives the same number and sees only what it may. The film comes back to it in the agent's chapter (a car reading the signs it passes, an agent reading a measure's words) and at the close, with where the analogy stops: unlike a road sign, data isn't for anyone who passes, so every reader shows who it is. The new text of the convention in force on 12 November 2026 is the close's "iterate". |
