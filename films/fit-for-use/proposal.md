## The brief

The author, on 5 November 2026, reviewing what the site is missing: "I agree we need a separate module for data quality" (09:33). Earlier that morning, for *Modern data basics*, he had said what he tells teams who struggle with the basics: "If you are struggling with those, you probably need to improve either your governance or your tooling, already described in other sections." At 11:51 he asked for the films of this module, "all the content labeled as draft and then I will be revisiting and fixing elements", and at 11:53, away for the night: "Work on the films with assumptions and merge them all. At 12:02: "Merge everything with a draft label, that's the release per film as it's get built".

So this plan was made without the usual questions. Each question he would have been asked (the reference, his own practice, the world, the look, the films) is answered with the best-reasoned option, and recorded with the assumption taken in the module's `assumptions.md`, in the project's shared files, for his review. Where he has said something before that applies, the plan follows it: people's engagement first (27 October 2026), checks with owners and levels set in advance (the governance films), checks for the data and operational models for what people do (20 October 2026), and fixing problems where they start.

**The module's promise.** Quality isn't a property of data; it's whether data is fit for a use. These films show how to say what good enough means for each use, measure it, check it against the truth and the clock, find where errors are born, close the loop with people, make data ready for agents, and get better in small steps. Not a feature tour: practice, shown once in the tools.

**What the site already has, and what's new here.** *When things go wrong* shows changes, contracts and levels of warning; the governance films show quality rules as checks with owners, the scorecard, monitoring on the platform and fixing where problems start. This module goes deeper on what those films only touched: what quality means for a use, how to measure it, accuracy, timeliness, root causes and the hidden work, the loop with people, quality for agents, and improving it as a habit. It names those films by title where it builds on them, and doesn't teach them again.

## Practical advice the films carry

- Ask what data is for before you ask if it's good: write down who uses it, for what, and what goes wrong for whom if it's wrong.
- For each use, name the few data elements it can't do without, and ask the dimensions as questions: complete, unique, valid, consistent, accurate, on time.
- Agree a level for each use with the people who use the data, owned by the person who answers for it, and write it beside the data, in its contract.
- Look at a hundred recent records with the people who know them, and count the perfect ones; then let the platform profile the whole table; then write checks, starting with what you saw.
- Say which reference each check uses. A check without one can only say a value is allowed. The closest reference is the person the data is about.
- Agree how fresh each use needs the data; show its age wherever it's shown; check freshness at the source; hold the send, not the data.
- Count the hours spent fixing data by hand. Then trace each kind of error to where it's born and fix it there, with its owner.
- Make it easy to report an error where people see the data. Answer every report, fix it at the source, tell the person who reported it, and tell the people who create the data what it cost.
- Turn each kind of error a person finds into a check.
- Describe data for a reader who can't ask: what it means, what empty means, its unit, its owner. Read an agent's answer before acting on it.
- Improve in small steps: one change a week, measured before and after; the last step of each change is a check that keeps it.
- If quality keeps failing, look at your governance and your tooling, which the other modules show.

## The thread, the world and the people

The fictional university of the earlier films, and one list everyone understands: **the graduation list**, which says who graduates, at which ceremony, with which award, and the name printed on their degree certificate. By December 2026, 3 certificates are printed again. *Fit for use* runs from December 2025 to June 2026; *Quality as a habit* from August to December 2026.

The people: Malia Fonoti, the graduations officer, who runs the ceremonies and sends the list to the printer, the list's main user; Mei Tanaka, in the registrar's office, who owns students' names and awards and decides what's right for a name; Jun Park, who builds the list's models and checks in the data project; Sam Okafor, who runs the platform; Noor Rahman, the data architect, who traces where errors are born; and Andrés Peñaloza, a graduate of the Master of Data Analytics, the person the data is about, whose name the list holds without its accents. ## The look

The quality bench: an assumption made on 5 November 2026, for the author to review (the *Decided* table above). A module of its own needs a design language of its own, and this one is built on what quality work is: measuring against a reference. Its main room is the data team's workroom at the university: a wall of warm limestone lit from a window on the right, and a long bench of dark speckled granite, the flat stone inspectors measure on, under a pendant lamp in graphite enamel (not brass, which means the requirement). Records are cream index cards, each field in the mono face; checks are drawn as an inspector's tools, a gauge and a tolerance band; results are tags in the verdict's colour, tied on with string. The Great Hall, warm and wooden, is where the ceremonies happen.

The colours mean the same in every film: green passes, amber holds for a person, red stops, grey wasn't checked; brass is the requirement, what good means for a use; steel blue is a measure taken. They're used for nothing else.

People, the camera, words and timing are the agent films' kit, `films/what-an-agent-is/shared/`, unchanged, so every check of 4 and 5 November 2026 holds: every word at least 28 px in the 1920-pixel frame and none under the captions; people with an adult's proportions and one three-quarter head, arms down and a hand only on what the story is about; a camera that glides in one room and cuts only between rooms; people faded whole; a page's work held back until the visitor reaches for the player. The module's setup builds its own kit on top, in `films/fit-for-use/shared/` (the bench and the hall, the colours, the record cards, tags and gauges, the series' title and end cards, Malia and Andrés, and the tools), and merges it before any film's pictures. Both series bundle it as it is.

## The sound

Each film has a palette of its own, made in its `tools/score.py`, with a lead instrument the site doesn't have, reserved by the module's setup: cloud-chamber bowls, the tops of large glass laboratory bottles struck softly with felt; a Savart wheel, whose clicks, close enough, become one note; a singing arc; ice bells; tuned anvils, struck softly and damped short; a string telephone made into an instrument; an early computer's pulse tones; and a hydraulophone. Each film's thread chooses its partner. Music plays under the opening story, then only at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen or in a pause without words, at most about once every 10 seconds, and no figure comes back again and again. Nothing trembles, and nothing is held under the voice. Effects are soft, low, one at a time and tied to something on screen; held notes sit at even levels. An urgent pulse plays only where a story turns to an error or a rush. `quiet.py` checks it all, and the mix is at -16 LUFS. No sound is borrowed from an opening's culture.
