| 10 | Does customer really mean three things at a utility? | Yes, as a composite: the one who pays the bill (account holder), the connection a utility counts as a customer (connection point), and someone who called (contact). The fourth, whoever signs in to the app, is the film's. | Chapters 1, 2 and 7 |
| 11 | Why build names from parts? | ISO/IEC 11179-5's naming principles (an object class term, a property term and a representation term) in plain words: the thing, in the glossary's words; what about it; and the kind of value, from a short list. The utility's short list (date, amount, count, identifier, name) is invented, after 11179's representation terms. | Chapter 3 |
| 12 | Are the Common Information Model's classes fairly described? | Yes, in plain words: UsagePoint as "where power is delivered", ServiceLocation as "the premises", Customer as "receives the service" and CustomerAccount as "for billing". The definitions were checked against the CIM17 schema documentation Netbeheer Nederland publishes, a secondary copy of the IEC model; the IEC's own text wasn't open to read. "None of the three" means the utility's own words on screen (and customer's three meanings): the film doesn't say the model has no class for an app's users. | Chapter 4 |
| 13 | Keep the utility's words, or take the model's? | Keep its own and map each to the model once, as practitioners use a reference model: so a new system or a partner can be matched through words they already know. The Pause and think question tests it. | Chapter 4 |
| 14 | Which principles? | Four, each from an earlier film of the series, in TOGAF's form (a rule, why it holds, what follows): one concept, one name, from the glossary; each fact has one home; data crosses a domain's edge only by a published flow, with its contract; every table belongs to a data product. | Chapter 5 |
| 15 | What goes in a decision record? | What was decided and why, what else was considered, who decided and when, as architecture decision records (Nygard 2011) with the fields MADR adds.
