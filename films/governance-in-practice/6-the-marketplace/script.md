# Governance you can use · The marketplace: script

*The script of *The marketplace*, film 6 of 6 of *Governance you can use*, the third series of data governance, in nine chapters, in English, painted in watercolour. Drafted on 28 October 2026 and made on 29 October 2026, when the author asked for the films of the three series to be made and merged one after another. It follows the plan the author approved on 23 October 2026 (film 19 of that plan: the tools films open on a scene of the university's own, on a line from the film they expand), and the rule of 28 October 2026 for every opening (scenario, problem, solution and benefits, each said outright; *Opening story* quotes them). Every file on screen is the example project's own, with the two additions this film makes to it (*What the example project has*); the impact check's report is its own, captured in a scratch copy. The catalogue's pages are drawn from Ontos's user guide, workflows and code, and the platform's from Databricks' documentation, as they stood on 28 October 2026. As the author asked for these films, it isn't rendered here: the release workflow renders it.*

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never asked for data can follow it, and a data product owner, a platform engineer or a privacy officer finds nothing wrong in it. *A product with an owner* gave each table other teams read an owner and a promise; *Who may see it* gave access a purpose and an end date; *A capability, not a project* promised an answer to "Let me see this data" within five working days. This film shows the whole flow in the tools: a catalogue on the platform where the products are found, by domain and by idea; each product's promise, read before asking; a request with a reason and a number of days; the owner's yes, and the request kept as the record; access through a group in the directory, until a date, with a reminder a week before; the reader written into the record of who uses what, so the impact check names them; and, outside the university, a product shared through OpenSharing, read where it lives, with a key that ends and the same contract. **Finding and getting data is part of governance.**

## The story in one paragraph

Today, in the Faculty of Health, Anil Kurian plans the faculty's places. Its health informatics certificate ends with a hospital placement, arranged months ahead, so he works out how many to offer: one for each learner a unit from finishing. In September, nobody owned the table with that number, so he asked around, and a colleague sent him a copy; it counted one learner near the certificate, Planning's census report, the university's official count, said two, and nobody could say which was right. The faculty offered one place, and a learner a unit from finishing would have waited a semester; and the copy stayed in his inbox, a code for every learner, sent with nobody's yes and no end date. Now the university's products sit in one catalogue, each with its promise and its owner, and anyone can ask, saying why and until when. This morning, Anil found Planning's product in a minute and asked; Clara said yes by the afternoon, until the end of January; her table agrees with the census report, two, so the faculty offers a second place. The film then opens each step: the marketplace, on Sam's screens and laid out by domain, and a search by the idea on Noor's map; the product's promise and its contract, two of whose columns can lead back to a learner; the request, with its reason and its ninety days, and the platform's own request button, in preview, which sends people to the same place; Clara's yes, within the service's promise, and the request kept as the record; the product's readers' group in the directory, the reminder a week before, and the access ending on the date; Anil's plan in the record of who uses what, and the impact check that now names his faculty; and Mei's *Awards*, shared through OpenSharing with a partner university, with a key that ends and the same contract, and in a private exchange of the Databricks Marketplace. On the 31st of January, Anil's access ends by itself, nothing is left in an inbox, and Clara knows who reads her table, why, and until when.

- **Words on screen** are what the files, checks and pages say, shortened only with "…". No learner's name is shown, and no date before the story's own: the reminder's day and the access's last day.
