## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone new to leading people can follow it, and an experienced manager finds nothing wrong in it. The film answers the series' second question, *what do they need?* Its idea is that wellbeing is something the work itself gives or takes away. Everyone needs three things from work: a say in it, to be good at it, and to belong. What wears people down is mostly in how the work is arranged, and so is what protects them. So a manager looks at the work before the person, changes the work first, asks the whole team before signs show, and knows where a manager's part ends. The film diagnoses nobody, labels nobody, and keeps what is private private. And at the bridge, what wears out and is made new is the rope: never the people.

**Takeaway:** "Fix the work before you ask people to cope."

## The story in one paragraph

High in the Andes of Peru, at Quehue, a bridge of grass rope crosses a gorge, about fifteen metres above the Apurímac river. Four communities look after it, and hold that its life depends on their care; by their own account, people have crossed there on rope since the time of the Incas. But grass rope is out in the sun, the rain and the wind all year, and it weakens: five months after one renewal, a visitor found the bridge sagging in the middle, one handrail lower than the other. A sagging bridge still carries people, so whoever waits for a sign gets one sign, a fall. In 2020, in the pandemic, the bridge was not renewed, and in March 2021, at twenty-one months old, it fell into the river. So the communities don't wait for that: each year, on a date, about a thousand people make the whole bridge new in three days. In June 2021 they wove it again, with their own means, and people cross it on foot today. Its rope lasts a year or two; by its people's account, the bridge has lasted more than five hundred years. The way a team's work is arranged is a bridge like that. At the university, in February 2027, Helena Novak is the new data manager, and fourteen of the team's 46 open requests wait on one person, Marta Silva, its only analyst: each was called urgent, and nobody decides their order. Helena could ask Marta to cope, and the fourteen would still be there. So she looks at the work first. On Monday 8 February the two of them write Marta's last week on one page: what the work asks, and what helps. Read against the three needs, the page is all about the work and not at all about Marta. On Wednesday 10 Helena takes it, as evidence and with no blame, to the nine people who asked; in half an hour two requests are no longer needed and twelve have an order and a date, and from Thursday Marta works on three at a time. In October 1972 the bridge, left for a time, still gleamed like gold, and its keeper had to stop two visitors: he knew its rope. A manager can learn where to look in the work, can notice three signs early, and has a part that ends: Helena is not a clinician. She doesn't wait for signs: she asks all seven the same five questions in writing, shows them the answers, says what has changed already and puts a date on the rest. At Quehue the old bridge is cut loose while it still holds. What is made new there is the rope; in a team, it is the way the work is arranged, never the people.

## The reference, and practice

The series follows no single framework: its frame is four questions a leader asks before the work, and this film answers the second with the best-tested research for it (the series' [proposal](../proposal.md) has the references and their alternatives). Each is named once; the film teaches what they share: what people need from work, and what wears them down or protects them, is mostly in how the work is arranged.

| Body of knowledge | What it adds | Where the film uses it |
|---|---|---|
| Self-determination theory: Richard Ryan and Edward Deci, "Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being" (*American Psychologist*, 2000) | Three needs people have, at work as anywhere, which researchers call autonomy, competence and relatedness. Where they are supported, people are better off and do better | "Now read the page against three things everyone needs from work: a say in it, to be good at it, and to belong." "Two psychologists, Richard Ryan and Edward Deci, named these needs." |
| G. Slemp, J. Field, R. Ryan, V. Forner, A. Van den Broeck and K. | Job demands and resources: Evangelia Demerouti, Arnold Bakker, Friedhelm Nachreiner and Wilmar Schaufeli (*Journal of Applied Psychology*, 2001) | Two sides of any job: what it asks of people, which wears them out, and what it gives them to meet it, whose lack makes them pull away | The page's two sides, and "Researchers call the two sides job demands and job resources." |

| How work is designed: Stephen Humphrey, Jennifer Nahrgang and Frederick Morgeson (*Journal of Applied Psychology*, 2007), with Frederick Herzberg's two kinds of factor, from the author's papers | Across 259 studies, the design of the work shapes how people feel about it. | Practice (the author's advice of 10 November 2026, "the team wellbeing is the top priority", and his own practice, from his papers) | Ask the whole team in writing, show them the answers, and ask again. **Where the film adopts, extends or differs.** It adopts the three needs in plain words (a say, to be good at it, to belong), the two sides of a job as two sides of one page, and the World Health Organization's three signs as it words them. It extends them from the author's practice into three things a manager can do in a week: write one person's week on one page with them, take it to the people who ask, and put the same questions to everyone in writing. It differs from wellbeing as perks, or as asking people to be tougher: the work is changed first, and the film says why a faster laptop isn't the same thing. It differs from a manager acting as a counsellor: the film says once where a manager's part ends. And it leaves out two practices from the author's papers on purpose: extra hours when a team falls behind, and opening a negotiation above one's goal.

**What it leaves to other films.** Sam's nights and the standing meetings are named in the written answers and fixed later (*The team comes first*, *Less, on purpose*). One list and one front door for requests are the data management module's. A person's hard time is *When someone falls behind*'s. Why the communities let the bridge go around 1970, and why they went back, is *People before roles*'s; the three days of building and what the story didn't achieve are *How, more than who*'s.

## The look

*Ink line*, the look the author chose for *Leading data people* on 17 November 2026, with the people kit (`films/people-first/shared`): whole people in the places where they work and meet, in flat colour with one even dark line. The film was first drawn on 12 November 2026 in the module's *Sunlit plaster*, and made again on 18 November 2026; the narration, the timings and the captions stay word for word. Nothing has film grain.

- **The opening is Quehue,** drawn in depth as *People before roles* draws it and copied here under the prefix `wwq_` (`src/ww_gorge.js`): the gorge laid out in metres and seen through cameras placed in it; hills under snow peaks, the gorge's rock walls and the river far below, a stone base on each bank, in sun or under a grey sky; the rope bridge, drawn live so it can be new, wear, sag, fall and be woven again; and the communities' people, whole, each at their own depth on the rims, standing, carrying their coils of cord or kneeling to twist it. ## Sound

**The palette, its own** (reserved by this film's thread on 12 November 2026 in the module's `palettes.md`, row 16, after every score on main and on the open branches was searched: no film plays a portative organ, and none of this module plays any organ): **a portative organ**, the small organ that sits on the player's knee, one hand on its keys and the other on its bellows. So it plays one line, a note at a time, and every phrase ends where the bellows must fill again: the pause is part of the instrument. Open wooden pipes, mostly the fundamental, a little breath, and a soft chiff as each pipe speaks; when the wind runs low a pipe is quieter, thinner and a few cents flat. Its partner is its own **two longest pipes, stopped**, so each sounds an octave lower, round and hollow: B and G sharp, touched one at a time under a turn. One instrument, the hand that plays and the hand that gives it wind, as the film's page has what the work asks and what helps. Made in `source/tools/score.py`. It is kept apart from the site's pipe and reed organs and from *Modern data basics*' serinette and organette: one line, never a chord held under the voice, a small wooden room, and the wind's own shape.

- **The mark: nine notes, on one bellows or on three.** F♯ B C♯ D♯ E D♯ C♯ A♯ B. Played with no rests, each note thinner as the wind runs out, until the last doesn't sound: the year the bridge was not renewed, and a week of nine things picked up and none finished. Played three notes to a bellows, whole, with its rests written in: the bridge made new in three days, three requests at a time, and the close. What runs out is the wind, the way the playing is arranged; the pipes are sound throughout.
- **Keys, all tuned pure** (just intonation from B): B major; G sharp minor for the problem, on the stopped G sharp. The organ plays one line, and two notes sound together only when a stopped pipe holds under it, on pure intervals. Nothing Andean for the bridge: no panpipe, quena or ocarina, and the pipes are kept from reading as one (little breath, a key's clean start and stop, even steps, no slide between notes, no vibrato).
- **After the opening,** music only at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen, or in a pause without words: three thin falling notes for plan better, push back and a day off, and the stopped G sharp for "still"; the breathless mark for the nine picked up; a full note for each need, and three quick ones for the ticks; two notes where both sides want the same; the mark in three bellows for three at a time; a thin high note for "gleaming", and two failing ones after "dying"; three falling notes for the three signs, and three rising for asks, listens, changes; five rising notes for the five questions; a quiet note and then a full one for the quiet answer; the first bellows of the mark as the new bridge is tied, the five notes again for June, and the whole mark, over the stopped B, as the end card comes. ## Rigour to check before recording

Checked on 12 November 2026; the sources are listed below. For the bridge, where a news page differs from the Ministry's study, the study stands.

- **The place.** Q'eswachaka, in the district of Quehue, province of Canas, Cusco: a hanging bridge of grass rope over a gorge of the Apurímac. The builders meet in the middle "a una altura de aproximadamente quince metros sobre el lecho del río" (the Ministry's study, page 183), so the film says "about fifteen metres above" the river; two writers say 60 feet. The film doesn't give its length (28 metres, page 175).
- **Who looks after it.** Four communities, Huinchiri, Chaupibanda, Choccayhua and Ccollana Quehue (UNESCO; the study). "Hold that its life depends on their care": "la vida del puente depende de la acción humana, así como la seguridad de la vida en las comunidades del distrito depende también de que el puente sea tratado adecuadamente" (the study, page 160).
- **Its age is their own account.** The study found nothing published about this bridge before 1970, so the film says "by their own account", twice: for people crossing there on rope since the time of the Incas, and for "more than five hundred years" (a bridge builder's own words, "over 500 years").
- **Wear.** "Las condiciones climáticas aceleraban el deterioro de cualquier material" (the study, page 32, of the Andes' rope bridges); of the fall, "debido a la acción de las intensas lluvias, el sol y el viento" (Andina, in El Peruano, 26 March 2021). The film's sun, rain and wind are those.
- **The sag.** Joshua Foer, who crossed the bridge five months after a renewal (*Slate*, 2011): "At its midpoint, the bridge sagged about 10 feet, and one of the arm rails had dropped about a foot lower than the other." The film says "a visitor", gives no measurement, and says "still carries people" of a bridge he crossed. The film never says how many people the bridge holds.
- **The missed year and the fall.** Last made new in June 2019; "el 2020 no se renovó debido a la inmovilización social obligatoria" (Andina, in El Peruano); the fall was reported on Tuesday 23 March 2021 ("Ayer (martes) nos reportaron el desplome", Cusco's culture office to AFP, 24 March), and it "cayó al río Apurímac". From June 2019 to March 2021 is twenty-one months. The film says "In March 2021", not a day, and nothing about anyone being on it, since no report says. It never says 2020 was the only year missed: the renewal also lapsed for a time around 1970.
- **Why things couldn't go on.** "People who cross a gorge on rope need it renewed before it fails, not repaired after" is the film's own wording of the problem. It rests on Foer ("an Incan bridge can't be patched up or have parts swapped out. It can only be replaced wholesale") and on the custom itself; the communities' own reasons are the bridge, which they hold to be alive and owed care, and each other, which the film says first.
- **The custom.** Made new each year, today in the second week of June; into the mid 1990s on 6 January. So the film says "each year, on a date", of today, and never "every June" or "every year since the Incas". "About a thousand people" (El Comercio, 17 June 2021; La República, 10 June 2026: "cerca de mil pobladores") and "three days" (UNESCO: "the renewal lasts only three days").
- **June 2021.** Woven again in mid June 2021 by the four communities, "con nuestros propios peculios" (the mayor of Quehue, El Peruano, 26 March 2021); La República of 14 June 2021 reports it rebuilt and open. "People cross it on foot today": residents still cross it on foot (*National Geographic*, 2018), though most people cross the gorge on the steel bridge; and La República of 10 June 2026 announces that year's renewal for 11 to 14 June, by "cerca de mil pobladores" of the four communities, after which the bridge is ready for "el tránsito peatonal durante los siguientes doce meses". The film never says it carries people every day.
- **A year or two.** "Exposed to the weather, the grass ropes of the keshwa chaca wouldn't hold up for more than a couple of years" (Foer); the bridge of June 2019 fell at twenty-one months.
- **October 1972.** As the Ministry's study quotes Loren McIntyre ("The Lost Empire of the Incas", *National Geographic* 144, 1973, pages 780 and 781, not read itself): the bridge "gleamed like Inca gold", and "Suddenly a voice cautioned: 'Don't cross! The bridge is dying!' It was our first meeting with Luis Choqueneira", "one of the chaca camayocs [keepers of the bridge]". The two visitors were McIntyre and the archaeologist Luis Barreda Murillo; the film names neither and draws nobody's likeness. "Left for a time": a visitor of 1970 wrote that the bridge had not been raised for a few years, and the study calls the lapse brief; the film gives no length. "It looked sound to them" follows the study ("el puente estaba completo y no se veía abandonado"). The keeper's people's decision to rebuild is the opening film's to tell.
- **Cut loose while it still holds.** "Cuando ya se ha establecido una vía segura de traslado entre ambas orillas, el puente del año anterior se corta y se deja caer sobre el río Apurímac" (the study, pages 178 to 185): on the second day, after someone has crossed the old bridge with a cord. So the new cables are already across when the old bridge is cut, as the film draws it.
- **What the film draws on its own.** How the bridge looked as it fell, and what was left at its bases, which no report describes; and the two visitors and the keeper at the edge. The assumptions file flags both.
- **The university's numbers.** The module's `numbers.md`, *February 2027, before Ivy*, whose rows this film confirms: Marta's fourteen of 46, each called urgent, eleven ways of asking; her week of 1 to 5 February on one page; nine people from six offices and faculties on Wednesday 10 February, two requests no longer needed, twelve with an order and a date; three in hand from Thursday 11; five questions on Friday 12, the answers on Friday 19 (two, three and seven of seven; five, four and one), the rest from Monday 15 March; six of twelve delivered by Friday 26 February, three in hand, three waiting, and no Sunday worked. In 2027 the 8th of February is a Monday and the 15th of March is a Monday. None of the fourteen is one of the three requests answered in Helena's first week in *Every area, one job*, and the film retells neither that film nor *Listen first*.
- **The references.** Ryan and Deci, 2000, with the year on screen; Slemp and others, 2024, as "hundreds of studies since, at work, in school and in sport", with "881 samples, 443,556 people" on screen, never as samples of people at work (on how well people work it has 22 samples), and "somewhat better" for an effect the study finds weak to moderate; Demerouti and others, 2001, on screen under the page. The World Health Organization (28 May 2019): burn-out "resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed", with three dimensions (exhaustion; mental distance from one's job, or negativism or cynicism about it; reduced professional efficacy), "an occupational phenomenon", "not classified as a medical condition"; the film says "a matter of work", and names the signs in plain words. Its fact sheet *Mental health at work*: "WHO recommends employers do this by implementing organizational interventions that directly target working conditions and environments". Safe Work Australia's page lists job demands, low job control and poor support among its psychosocial hazards; the film names those three and says "at work" for "psychosocial". Its model code of practice wasn't read.
- **Nothing is diagnosed.** Nobody in the film has burn-out, and nobody needs professional help: both lines are advice. The staff support service is named on screen as an example and never drawn.

No product is named, and no release status is shown or said (the author's rule of 5 November 2026).
