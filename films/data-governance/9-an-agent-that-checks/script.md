# Data governance · An agent that checks: script

*The script of the ninth film of the data governance series, *Governance you can use*, as drafted for the author's review on 21 October 2026, before anything is rendered: about 5:17, in eight chapters, in English, painted in watercolour. The narration lives in [`source/src/narration.js`](source/src/narration.js) and the pauses in [`source/src/breath.js`](source/src/breath.js); this page and those files say the same thing, and where they differ, the source wins. The title card follows chapter 1; the end card follows chapter 8. Made to read on a phone: on-screen text is at least 28 px in the frame, nothing sits under the captions, and no caption runs to three lines.*

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has waited days for someone to review their change can follow it, and an experienced data governance lead finds nothing wrong in it. The film takes binder 26, *AI and automated tools*, and its line "AI use follows the organisation's data policies", and shows both halves of it. **AI for governance:** the lines of governance's policy that can run are written as a skill, the steps an agent follows on every change, so a review that waited for one person comes at once, with a draft and the evidence for each finding, and the name of the person who decides it. **Governance for AI:** the agent is held to the same policies as everyone, with its own identity, little access, counts instead of personal data, a glossary it only reads, and no power to approve. The agent finds, drafts and flags; people decide. Where it isn't sure, it says so, and leans to protection. And an agent can only follow what's written plainly enough to check, so its steps are open for anyone to read, question and change, through the same review as everything else. **An agent checks what's written. People decide.**

## The story in one paragraph

Imagined, and labelled so: a few years from now, before going home, Anil Kurian, an analyst in the Faculty of Health, adds a column to a list: each learner's date of birth. At three in the morning, an agent reads the change against governance's written rules, and finds that nothing says the new column is personal data, so nothing would protect it. It doesn't change the change or approve it: it leaves a comment, with the line, the rule and a fix to consider. At eight, Anil agrees, and marks the column. Back to today, and Anil's first change: the list itself, the faculty's call list for its student advisers, seven learners, how far each is from finishing an award, and a name and an email to call. Three checks fail, with thirteen problems; none says what's right for this list, or who decides, so Anil asks Jun, who reviews every change nobody else owns, and waits. Yusuf and Noor write governance's rules as a skill: seven steps, and four things it never does. The agent's review of Anil's change passes two things and makes three findings: nobody answers for the list, ten columns don't say whether they identify a person, and a rule the faculty stated has no test. Where it isn't sure, it says so: one coded key has two values for seven learners, and its description says it's made from the award's code, so it may not be about a person; the skill says to draft the stricter class and ask, and Anil confirms it's a course. The faculty, Yusuf and Jun approve, each for their part; the agent decided nothing. The agent is governed too, so binder 26's line goes green. And policy 12's last line, "Staff take care to use terms correctly", is the kind no agent can check. The night a few years from now is made of what the film has shown.

## The look

*Watercolour*, the look of the series (*Off the shelf*, *The look*): a pencil line and paint on paper, only the university's own places, and colour that carries meaning: gold is governance's decisions and a person's decision, cyan the technical side, green what runs in the work, grey what can go, and red what nobody owns yet.

- **This film's places.** The campus at dusk and at night (a night wash over the painted campus, its lamps lit, the clock at three); the Faculty of Health's office in the old building, close at Anil's desk, by night, in the morning and by day; the data team's room; Yusuf's office and desk; the platform team's desk, close; and screens and papers, close. No homes: "before going home" is Anil leaving the office.
- **The agent is not a person.** It has no face and no body. It's a small four-pointed star, turning slowly, at the top of a dashed cyan card, the sign the master data films gave an agent; a cyan line shows what it's reading. What it writes is on cyan cards; what a person decides is gold.
- **Counts, never values.** Names and emails on Anil's list are soft grey bars, never words. The agent's evidence is counts and tally marks.
- **The future is labelled.** The imagined night has its corner tag, and the campus is the same campus: the film doesn't guess at how the future looks.

## Sound

A new palette ([`source/tools/score.py`](source/tools/score.py), to be written when the film is made): an alto flute and a soft electric piano, with a nylon-string guitar under them, three sounds no other film of the series uses. The guitar, slow and fingerpicked, holds the chords: under the whole opening night, as the series' rule allows, then only at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen, or in a wordless pause; never a bed under the voice. The electric piano, soft and close, is the agent's: two notes rising a fourth, never resolved, when it reads or drafts, and one low note for each thing it can't do. The alto flute is a person's: a short falling phrase that comes home each time a person decides (Anil's `direct` at eight, Anil's answer, each of the three approvals, the binder's line going green). The series' mark, 1-5-4-8, on the alto flute with the electric piano above, plays under the title and the end card. The night is in A major, today in D major. No urgent pulse, and no held notes beating against each other: the film's turn is a decision, not a rush. Nothing in it imitates the music of another culture. The effects are muted keys, a soft key to send a change, the desk lamp's click, a mug set down, paper, a cork pin, a pencil, a felt-tip pen, a highlighter's stroke, a binder's rings, and a soft rubber stamp, each at the moment its thing appears, all below about 2.5 kHz. The mix is -16 LUFS.
