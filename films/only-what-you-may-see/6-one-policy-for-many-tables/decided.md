| 5 November 2026 | The film opens in North America in the 1970s, as the module's openings list chose: when a truck or a train carrying dangerous chemicals crashed, the first to arrive were firefighters and police, and their guide gave each chemical a page of its own, a few dozen in all; to find the page a crew had to know exactly what was inside, often from papers in the driver's cab, close to the wreck; thousands of chemicals had no page; and a wrong guess could put water on a chemical that gives off a poison gas when wet, with families still in the houses nearby. In 1980 the US Department of Transportation changed the book: chemicals that behave alike in a fire or a spill share one guide, written once; each chemical's four-digit number leads to its guide, so a new chemical needs only one line; and a load no one can name gets Guide 111, which assumes every danger.
