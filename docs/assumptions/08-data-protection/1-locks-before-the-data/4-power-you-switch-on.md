Film 4 of *Locks before the data*. Rodrigo is away and asked for the films to be made on assumptions and merged with a Draft label (5 November 2026). Each row is a question this thread would have asked him, the assumption it took instead, and where it shows, for his review.

| # | Question for Rodrigo | Assumption taken | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which story opens the film? | The module's proposed time lock: robbers in the 1870s held bankers' families at night to make them open the vault; James Sargent's time lock (first fitted to a bank's vault in Morrison, Illinois, 26 May 1874) kept the vault shut until its hour, and on the night of 28 to 29 May 1875 in Great Barrington, Massachusetts, a time lock put on three days before held while robbers held the Deland family. Checked against Fred Deland's own letter (quoted in *The Berkshire Edge*, 2020), *Invention & Technology* (2006) and the lock maker's history. The robbers and the family are never shown: lantern lights and windows only. | Chapters 1 and 2; back in chapters 5 and 8 |
| 2 | What do we call it on screen: "elevate", "privileged", "just in time"? | Plain words only: an "admin account" whose power is "switched on" for a task and "switched off"; "great power over data" for what the guidance calls privileged. The narration never says "privileged", "elevate" or "just in time". On screen the badges read "Admin account · Jun Park", and the alert and the record "Jun Park (admin)". | Whole film; agreed with the module's `numbers.md` |
| 3 | Which powers does the admin account hold? | Three, from the module's `numbers.md`: read the storage (where no mask or filter applies), change the admin settings, restore a table. Only the one a task needs is switched on. | Chapters 3, 5 and 6 |
| 4 | Who approves a switch-on, and how long can it last? | Another data specialist approves; no one approves their own (Sam approves Jun's, Jun approves Sam's). At most four hours; off by itself when the time is up, or sooner when the owner is done. An alert goes to the platform team each time, and everything is recorded under the admin account's own name. | Chapter 5 |
| 5 | With only two specialists, who approves when one is away? | Not answered in the film: it says nothing about that case. Options for Rodrigo: a named deputy outside the platform team (such as Noor) may approve; or the emergency accounts; or the work waits. | Not shown; noted in the script's *Rigour* |
| 6 | Is the example real-looking enough: a load stopped on a bad date? | Invented: the 02:00 load stops on row 9,317 of last night's registrar file, a date of birth of 31 February 2006 for student S-21877 (not a real student number). Jun switches on "read the storage" for one hour, Sam approves at 09:05, Jun reads at 09:11 and switches off at 09:19, after 14 minutes. | Chapter 6 |
| 7 | Should the film change the load so the next switch-on isn't needed? | Yes, in one line, as practitioners would: "could the next one be avoided?" The load now sets a row it can't read aside, in a table the catalog guards ("Rows set aside"), which the registrar's office can see. This is a change to the series' load that the tool films may or may not build. | Chapter 6 |
| 8 | Do business users and analysts get anything like it? | Never. Mei Tanaka (registrar's office, the data's owner) and Clara Jensen (Planning, an analyst) work through the catalog; when they need more, they ask the data's owner for access, not for power. | Chapter 7 |
| 9 | And agents? | Never an admin account. An agent may propose a task that needs power; a person decides, and does it with their own admin account. One line, pointing to *AI agents in data work*. | Chapter 7 |
| 10 | Should the emergency accounts appear? | One line only: "two emergency accounts, locked away for the day nothing else works", with *Who did what* showing how they're watched, as that film's thread set them up. | Chapter 7 |
| 11 | Were the platform team's everyday accounts really admins of everything, and was storage access handed out "for one task"? | Yes, as the series' story has it: before this film, Sam's and Jun's everyday accounts held all three powers, every hour, and some powers had gone to others for one task and never come back (*Network, storage and keys* finds six). The film draws two, "A contractor" and "A tester", and gives no number. | Chapter 3 |
| 12 | Say why everyday accounts are risky? | Yes, in one clause: "the same accounts that open their email, and every link in it", as CIS 5.4 and the ASD keep admin work away from email and the web. | Chapter 3 |
| 13 | How much do we say about the 1875 family? | "Hold the Deland family at home" and nothing more: the family were held all night, so the film doesn't say they came to no harm, and it names neither the bank nor Fred's post (sources differ). | Chapter 2 |
| 14 | "Sargent's lock sells in the thousands, and banks soon expect one": safe? | Yes: *Invention & Technology* (2006) says his Model 2 "sold thousands" and that the time lock became "not just accepted but expected". The film gives no other count. | Chapter 2 |
| 15 | The sound | A diaphone (the lighthouse fog signal of 1903, switched on only while there was fog) for great power switched on: one low note (G2 or D3) swells in at each switch-on, in the beat after the other specialist's yes, and falls as the power goes off; it sounds under the title, at the two switch-ons and after the last line. A square piano of the 1870s for the everyday: the Delands' house tune at night, then the chapters' turns, dry except while a power is on. Tuned pure from G, E minor for the danger; the robbers' steps the one pulse, in chapter 1 only. The thread first took a nail violin (already in *Every change through review*) and then weighed a bugle (too near the site's brass and *Show the least*'s keyed bugle). | Score; the module's `palettes.md` |
| 16 | How does the page put the problem? | "Every hour of every day", as the film says it: the lead no longer gives an estimate of how often the power is needed (the draft's "a few hours a month" was dropped on 10 November 2026). | The film's page |
| 17 | Who is drawn | Sam and Jun as the series draws them, Sam nodding for each yes; James Sargent, bearded, in a lock maker's apron, at his bench; Fred Deland, a young man in a dark suit and tie, by the vault, arms down; Mei and Clara as badges; the agent as the module's sphere. No real-looking secret, key or combination is drawn. | Whole film |
| 18 | How is "whoever takes one of those accounts" shown? | As a coral tag, "Taken", that comes down on a dotted line from above the frame into the account it takes, and stops at a lock; no attacker is drawn. | Chapters 2, 4 and 8 |
| 19 | How does the film end? | After the last line, in the quiet before the end card, Jun's power is switched on for a task, held a moment, and switched off again, with the diaphone's note. The poster is the morning's switch-on card, approved and lit. | Chapter 8; the poster |
