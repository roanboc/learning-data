# Rules that let people decide: questions and assumptions

*Leading data people* · *Leading a data team*, film 4 of 4 · merged, PR #260 · [pull request](https://github.com/roanboc/learning-data/pull/260)

[Watch it on the site](https://data.learn-all.org/leading-a-data-team/rules-to-decide/)

The fourth and last film of *Leading a data team*, the third series of *Leading data people*. It was made on 10 November 2026 while Rodrigo was away, on his instruction to work on assumptions and merge with a draft label. Each row gives the question I would have asked, the assumption I made instead, and where it shows. The module's own questions are in [`../0-module.md`](../0-module.md). The film's numbers and people are also in the module's shared numbers, so the other films say them the same way.

| # | Question | Assumption | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the opening story right, as the sources tell it? | Checked against Lansing, *Priests and Programmers* (2007 edition: its introduction, preface and chapter 6) and Lansing and Kremer, "Emergent Properties of Balinese Water Temple Networks" (*American Anthropologist*, 1993), and told in the four beats. The quotes were checked again on 10 November. The calendar on screen ("Days 1 to 10 / 10 to 20 / 20 to 30") shows what planting in turns means; it is not a real temple's schedule. | Chapter 1, and the script's *Rigour* |
| 2 | Where and when does it open? | The seed said "Bali's subak", but a scenario needs a time. It opens in "Bali, Indonesia, in the 1970s", when the push to plant fast began (Lansing: the Green Revolution had "a firm foothold in Indonesia by the early 1970s"). "For over a thousand years" says how long the subaks had shared the water, in Lansing and Kremer's own first words. | Chapter 1, the first lines |
| 3 | Who is to blame for the problem? | Nobody. The country's aim is said plainly ("The planners wanted the country fed, and the new rice gave more grain"), so the harm comes from the plan, not from a villain. | Chapter 1, "fed" line |
| 4 | Two million tons in 1977? | Lansing's figure for the brown planthopper across Indonesia in 1977, said as "about two million tons". Lansing puts that loss down to the first new rice's weakness against the insect, and Bali's later pest outbreaks to planting without the shared fallow. The film says the pests found rice next door, then gives the number, without naming the rice variety. The harm to a family is said in general words, with no number: "a season's food and income gone". | Chapter 1, "hopper" and "harm" lines, and the card |
| 5 | Can the film quote the bank's final report? | The report (an evaluation by the bank's post-evaluation office, Manila, 1988) couldn't be read on 10 November. The voice says it in plain words ("no project it had reviewed ran itself as well as Bali's"), and the sheet quotes it as Lansing quotes it. | Chapter 1, "report" line and sheet |
| 6 | Should the bank be named? | No. It is "the bank that had paid for new irrigation works". Its name (the Asian Development Bank) is in the script's sources, since it adds nothing for a newcomer. | Chapter 1, "bank" line |
| 7 | Should the model show its numbers? | No. It is drawn as 172 cells between two rivers on a printed map, with a harvest line that has no numbers. It is a toy re-run that follows the paper's rules: each subak copies the best of its four nearest neighbours, and groups planting together appear within about eight years. The paper's yields (from a little over 5 to 8.57 tons a hectare) aren't said. | Chapters 1 and 6 |
| 8 | Should the researchers be drawn? | No. Stephen Lansing and James Kremer are real people: they are named in the voice and never drawn. | Chapters 1 and 6 |
| 9 | How to draw Bali without borrowing its art? | In the module's own sunlit plaster, lit from the right like the rooms: terraces under a volcano in haze, and water moving from field to field. The water temple is drawn plainly (a stone wall with a split gate, a shrine of stacked thatched roofs, an open pavilion), with no ornament copied from Balinese art. The farmers wear plain shirts, never ceremonial dress. | Chapter 1 |
| 10 | What plays under Bali? | Nothing Balinese: no gamelan, no bronze, and no scale or instrument of the place. The film's own whirly tubes, tuned pure from D, play there as they do everywhere else. | Chapter 1, the score |
| 11 | What is the whirly tubes' partner? | A longer tube swung slowly, sounding only its lowest overtones: a soft, breathy bass. It is one instrument in two sizes, and it is written into the module's palettes. | The whole score |
| 12 | What is the film's mark? | Tubes finding notes that fit together: a second and a third try a note that doesn't belong before finding one that does, and the three hold F sharp, A and D, a chord nobody played alone. It plays at the model's calendar, under the title, when Helena stops deciding, when groups planting together emerge, and at the close. | Chapters 1, 2, 6 and 7 |
| 13 | The data story: who asked, how many, and how long did they wait? | From July to September 2027, the faculties ask for 27 room reports for the timetable change, each about a day and a half to make and about two weeks in the list. The list was busy then: *Measure the work, not the people* has the team at nine pieces of work against its limit of six on 12 August. This is recorded in the module's shared numbers. | Chapter 2 |
| 14 | Which decision does Helena give up? | Choosing whose rooms come first when two faculties ask at once: "So she decided to stop deciding: the faculties would read the data themselves." | Chapter 2 |
| 15 | How many rules on Yusuf's list, and which stay? | Eleven, each guarding against something that had gone wrong somewhere. Of each, they ask "what goes wrong here, now, without it?". Three stay, each said with its reason: room use means one thing; the product's promise (counts, never a person); and ask Ivy before publishing a new measure. The other eight go in a drawer. | Chapter 3 |
| 16 | Which definition of room use? | The glossary's, as *The draft and the decision* wrote it ("the share of a room's teaching hours in which the sensors counted at least one person"), said as "the share of hours in which someone was counted". | Chapter 3, rule one |
| 17 | Who decides what the numbers mean: Ivy or Graham? | Ivy, who has owned *Teaching space use* since 1 July (the module's shared numbers), so a new measure from it goes to her and she writes it in the glossary. In April, Graham approved room use in the glossary as its owner (*The draft and the decision*). The film doesn't say who approves lab use, so it can still be him without changing the film. | Chapters 3 to 5, and the box on the template |
| 18 | How does Helena agree the rules? | With the four timetable officers, not for them, and any of them can ask to change one. This is Ostrom's point: the rules that last are made and changed by the people who live by them. | Chapter 3 |
| 19 | What is on the template? | Ivy and Jun make it in September: booked hours beside counted ones, room use worked out the agreed way, and one box that says who decides what. The faculties decide their classes, Ivy what the numbers mean, the team the platform, and the registrar's office the timetable. For Jun it is part of his model work in the busy September of *The team comes first*, not a project of its own. | Chapter 4 |
| 20 | How are data mesh and *Team Topologies* named? | In one line: "decisions with the people who know the work, on a platform that makes the hard parts easy". The film differs from a common reading of data mesh in two ways: the faculties read one governed product, and a new measure goes to its owner and the glossary before anyone publishes it. No owner or steward reviews a pull request. | Chapter 4 |
| 21 | Who are Lucy Tran and the other officers? | Lucy Tran, Engineering's officer, is new, and she is drawn in this film's own source, not the kit. She wears her hair in a bun and a muted olive cardigan, so she doesn't read as Ivy. It is a grey-olive, well away from the module's green, like the kit's own trees, which its code calls "a quiet grey-olive that means nothing". Health's officer has long hair and a grey blazer for the same reason. The other three officers aren't named. This is recorded in the module's shared numbers. | Chapters 2, 4, 5 and 6 |
| 22 | How can Lucy see the labs, when the module's 186 rooms leave them out? | The labs are rooms the faculty books itself (the module's shared numbers), "so they had never been in the university's room reports". I assumed they have sensors and that the product's counts cover every room with a sensor, while its bookings come from the central timetable. So Lucy puts Engineering's own lab bookings beside their counts. | Chapter 5 |
| 23 | What are the labs' numbers? | Twelve teaching labs, nine of them booked and empty every Monday morning. Two first-year courses had moved their lab classes to Thursdays, and their Monday bookings rolled over every year. Engineering releases the nine mornings and puts its request for two new labs on hold, not cancelled. | Chapter 5 |
| 24 | Is lab use a new measure? | Yes, so Lucy asks Ivy before sharing it (rule three at work). Ivy writes it in the glossary the same way as room use, and the team builds it into the product for every faculty. | Chapter 5 |
| 25 | What emerges, and how fast? | Within three weeks, with no instruction, Health copies Lucy's page for its clinical skills rooms and Arts copies it for its studios: "One faculty's question had become three". Business isn't said to copy it. | Chapter 6 |
| 26 | How does it end, in numbers? | By Friday 26 November 2027, all four faculties have sent the registrar's office their plans for February, moving one peak class in six. That is the team's April finding, and the timetable change the University Council asked for: "as the University Council had asked". In October and November, the team makes no room reports by hand. | Chapter 6 |
| 27 | Which problem brings out the fourth rule? | Health's heads of school get a copy of its page saved on Monday 1 November, and two of them plan from numbers three weeks old. So "share the live page, not a copy, because copies go stale" comes out of the drawer, and the template now shows the date of its data ("Data as of Monday 22 November, 07:00"). | Chapter 6 |
