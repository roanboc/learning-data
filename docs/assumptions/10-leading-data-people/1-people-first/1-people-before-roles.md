Film 1 of 4 of *People first*, the series that opens *Leading data people*. Made on 12 November 2026, after the author confirmed the series that day, as the module's other films were: with no script review, no render and no review page. Each line: the question I would have asked, the assumption I made instead, and where it shows. The film's script, with its sources, is [`films/people-first/1-people-before-roles/script.md`](../../../../films/people-first/1-people-before-roles/script.md).

| # | Question | Assumption | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which part of the bridge's story is this film's? | The time the bridge was left after the steel bridge of 1968, and why its people went back: the keeper's words of October 1972, the return, and everyone coming since. The wear, 2020 and 2021 are *Wellbeing is in the work*'s; the three days of building, and what the story didn't achieve, are *How, more than who*'s. | Chapter 1, and the returns in chapters 5 and 6 |
| 2 | How long was the bridge left? | The sources have one report: a geographer who visited in 1970 wrote that it hadn't been raised "for a few years", and the Ministry's study calls the lapse very brief. So the voice keeps the report his ("not raised, he writes, for a few years", "he expects the skill of making it to die"), and never says nobody made the bridge in those years. | Chapter 1, the lines "left" and "skill" |
| 3 | In which year did they go back? | The study reads as January 1973, but the article that reported it wasn't read. So the voice gives no year: "When the new year came, they did." "Four communities" and "each June" are said of today only: three communities were recognised then, and the renewal was in January into the 1990s. | Chapter 1, the lines "did" and "today" |
| 4 | Who says "Only now does everyone come"? | Baltazar Puma Llascano, the eldest of the community of Huinchiri, 95 years old in 2012. He isn't one of the bridge builders, so the voice says "one elder" and the screen "An elder of Huinchiri, asked in 2012". A first version said "one builder": corrected, and the two lines voiced again. The English words are this film's translation of "recién ahora vienen todos"; the Spanish captions give his words as the study prints them. | Chapter 1, the lines "did" and "all", and the green sheet |
| 5 | Which date for the builder's words, "it would be like if we died"? | The Smithsonian's interview with Victoriano Arizapana was held in October 2014 and published on 29 May 2015. The screen dates it by when he said it: "Interview, October 2014". | Chapter 1, the builder's sheet |
| 6 | Is the people's own telling of that time in the film? | No. In 2012 the people of Quehue told of storms and hunger in the time the custom was left, and put them down to having left it. The voice leaves that telling out and doesn't judge it; it says only that they name nobody from outside who pushed them to go back. | Chapter 1; the script's *Rigour* |
| 7 | Is anyone at the gorge drawn as a real person? | No. The keeper, the elder and the builder are named as the speakers of their own words (two of them on screen), and no figure stands for any of them. The people on the bank are unnamed, dressed from the sources' photographs and words in plain colours, with no pattern invented and none copied. Nothing sacred is drawn. | Chapter 1 |
| 8 | Which names for the bridge? | The voice says *keshwa chaca*, as the keeper says it in the article of 1973 that the study quotes; the screen says *Q'eswachaka*, as the Ministry and UNESCO spell it. His sentence is cut before "just as we have done every year", which the lapse itself contradicts. | Chapter 1 |
| 9 | What does each of the seven tell Helena? | One small, true thing each, about them at work: Noor's map, and her wish that more people argued with it; Sam's mornings when the data is simply there; Jun, proudest when someone stops asking him; Yusuf, "Ask me at the start, and I can say yes more often"; Marta, who'd like to hear what became of a number; Erin, who started the same week as Marta and still has lunch with her on Thursdays; Tomás, who can tell you who reads each report. Nothing private, and nothing a later film finds out or fixes: no nights for Sam, no count of Marta's asks, nothing of Tomás's year or of how Erin works in a meeting. | Chapter 3 |
| 10 | Is Erin's tie with Marta a new fact? | Yes, this film's: they started the same week, and still have lunch on Thursdays. It fits *How, more than who*, where the two are the quietest in the room. Added to the module's shared numbers. | Chapter 3, the line "erin" |
| 11 | Which four turn to Jun? | Marta (a number), Erin (a model), Noor (her map, before anyone else) and Sam (a load that looks odd). The module's numbers say four of the other six; Yusuf and Tomás are the two who don't. | Chapter 4 |
| 12 | What does Jun answer, when Helena asks if he wants this? | His answer isn't said in words. The room she makes says it: "in March, he'll be the one beside a newcomer". The film names nobody who hasn't arrived. | Chapter 4, the line "room" |
| 13 | How is the chart drawn? | One row of seven boxes under "Data manager", in blue, with a role in each and no names: data governance lead, data architect, data analyst, analytics engineer, platform engineer, data platform, report developer. Who turns to whom is drawn in pencil, never in a colour, so no colour ranks anyone. | Chapters 1, 2, 4 and 6; the poster |
| 14 | How is the plan she doesn't make drawn? | Dashed amber lines from the list to every box, "A plan for everyone, by lunch?", which go again. The list's lines then fall into order ("Sorted that day"), and are never dealt out to the boxes. | Chapter 2 |
| 15 | Which day for each conversation? | None is given. Her week's sheet shows a dot for each: two a day from Monday to Wednesday, and one on Thursday, with nobody's name on a day. | Chapters 2, 3 and 5 |
| 16 | What exactly does she promise on Friday? | "Who does what stays as it is this month", and never that nothing will change: the later films change things in February (an order for Marta's asks on the 10th, sprints from the 15th). The sheet says "Not promised: that nothing will change" and "Said: what stays, and for how long". | Chapter 5 |
| 17 | How much of the references? | Each named once. ISO 27500: two of its seven principles, in plain words. Ubuntu: as *The team comes first* words it, named with that film and not retold. Shared leadership: Wang, Waldman and Zhang's review of 42 samples (2014), with its authors' caution, "most of all in how they work together". Safe Work Australia's hazard, poor organisational change management, is on screen only. ISO's and Safe Work Australia's own pages couldn't be read from the session: both are worded as the series' proposal checked them that day. | Chapters 2, 4 and 5; the page's links |
| 18 | "Anyone can lead from where they are": whose words? | The idea is from the author's papers; the film says it in its own words and rests it on the research on shared leadership, which was read. The book the papers name for it wasn't. | Chapter 4, the line "title" |
| 19 | Which instruments? | A cor anglais and a bassoon, as a pair, a player to each: two reeds of one family, high and low. Nearly every instrument the site lacks is taken, so the film pairs two the site has, in a pair no film has. **Risk:** each plays in other films, so if the pair sounds like one of them, the score needs a new lead. Nothing Andean: no flute or pipe, no harp, guitar or mandolin, no drum, no five-note tune. | The whole score |
| 20 | What is the film's musical mark? | *The names*: seven short figures, each the rhythm of one person's name said aloud, each starting on its own note of the scale. The bassoon says the first names of two syllables and the cor anglais those of one: the choice follows the name and says nothing about the person. The chart's boxes get taps, and people get notes. On Friday the seven first names are one phrase, in a pause without words. | Chapters 3 to 6 |
| 21 | Which Spanish words? | *Solicitudes* and *informes*, as *Every area, one job* says them of the same week; *organigrama* and *casillas* for the chart and its boxes; *la minka*; the films as the site titles them (*Todas las áreas, un trabajo*, *Primero el equipo*, *El bienestar está en el trabajo*). The series' own page says *pedidos* and *reportes*: one of the two should follow the other. | The Spanish captions and page |
| 22 | What is the poster? | The chart on the wall, every box lit, with the seven people beneath it: the film's two ways to read a team, in one picture. | The film's page and cards |
| 23 | How do the three things that waited for this film read? | The release note lists the series' videos as each one is out. The module's line begins "The people before the work", and adds "knowing your team". The goal *lead a data team* reads "The organisation first, then the people, the team, the plan and the case for it", with *People first* as the stop before *Leading a data team*. | The Topics page, and the release notes |
| 24 | Render and review page? | Not made in the session, as for the module's other films: the merge is the release, with the series' Draft label. The soundtrack's release copy publishes itself when the change reaches main. | The film's page |
