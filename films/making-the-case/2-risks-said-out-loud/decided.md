Chater's translation): in February 1911 his expedition lays food in three depots for the way back from the Pole, about 3,000 km there and back on skis; on the flat, white ice a depot can't be seen in fog, a missed depot means no food far from any help, and marks set along the route are missed once a party drifts off it; so each depot is marked across the route, nine kilometres out on each side, at the first with twenty numbered black flags that tell which side the depot lies and how far; a party off course still crosses the line and is pointed in; ninety-nine days after setting out, on 25 January 1912, all five are home, well, with food to spare. No dogs, no other expedition, nobody drawn close; what Amundsen learned from the Netsilik is left out, as no Inuit source found says what it was.
