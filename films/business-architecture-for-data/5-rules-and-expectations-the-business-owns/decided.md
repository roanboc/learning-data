| 12 November 2026 | *Rules and expectations the business owns* tells the series' story from the rules a qanat's people kept, in its four beats, from Hans E. Wulff's account of Sehdeh (1968): in the 1960s, three hamlets, Dastgerd, Parvar and Karton, took their water from one qanat, through a basin and three outlets; a qanat's flow rising and falling with the water underground, drought about one year in four (Paul Ward English), so a hamlet that took more than its share would leave the others' fields dry, and silt washing in every year, so a qanat nobody cleaned would choke and dry up, while each hamlet needed its fair share in a dry year as in a wet one, and the tunnel kept open; in the 1600s, under Shah Abbas the Great, an engineer set their shares, eight, eight and nine, built into the outlets, eight spans wide, eight and nine, so whatever came in left in the same shares, the muqannis clearing the silt each year and the mirab, paid with a free share, seeing that each farmer got their water at the proper time; and more than three hundred years on, the hamlets still taking the shares set for them, less for each in a dry year but each its share. Wulff places Sehdeh in western Iran; the film says "in Iran", leaves the engineer unnamed, as Wulff does, and calls his water bailiff the mirab, as the series does. That the outlets' widths shared any flow in the same proportions is the film's reading of shares built into the outlets. |
| 12 November 2026 | As the series' last film, it says what the story didn't achieve, and why a practice keeps maturing: after the Second World War came deep wells with motor pumps, drawing water faster than rain put it back, and in the last fifty years about half of Iran's qanats have run dry, from poor upkeep or as wells lowered the water underground (Iranian hydrologists, reported by Fred Pearce, 18 December 2025). Rules last only while people keep them, and change them when the world changes; at the utility, each expectation has an owner who changes it when the business does, and when prices change by the hour, billing will need a reading for every hour, and its check will follow. |
