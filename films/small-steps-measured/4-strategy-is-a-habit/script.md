## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone new to data work can follow it, and a data manager, an enterprise architect or a strategist finds nothing wrong in it. As the module's last film, it closes on the module's promise: the whole job, every area at once, a plan in stages, each proved with something real, and everyone at the table; and the loop the module began with, which doesn't end: discover, define, plan; deliver, measure, improve; and again. Its own idea: a strategy stays the plan everyone works to only if it's looked at again on a rhythm. Put the year's reviews in the calendar the day it's approved, in a meeting that already exists; ask the same three questions at each (what do the measures say, what do the risks say, what has changed around us); when the world moves, move the plan out loud (what moves up, what waits, and why) and keep the one page and the map with it; and end the year with what was promised beside what was delivered, what didn't work included.

**Takeaway:** "Review the plan every quarter; move it out loud when the world moves."

## The story in one paragraph

In the 1970s, gases called CFCs are in fridges, spray cans and foam all over the world. In 1974 Mario Molina and Sherwood Rowland ask where they end up: they drift up to the ozone layer, which stops most of the sun's harmful ultraviolet light, and break it down; in 1985 the British Antarctic Survey publishes Halley's readings since 1957, the layer above it each spring only about two thirds as thick as it once was. On 16 September 1987, with much still unsure, countries sign the Montreal Protocol: a modest first step (the main CFCs cut by half, by 1998) and a habit built in (from 1990, and at least every four years, look at the science again and decide whether to go further). London in 1990 and Copenhagen in 1992 go further, out loud, with the science behind them; by 2023 nearly 99 per cent of the banned gases are no longer made or used, and the layer is healing. On Tuesday 27 April 2027 the data council approves Helena Novak's data strategy, on one page, and before anyone leaves the room she puts the year's reviews in the calendar, at the council the deputy vice-chancellor already chairs each quarter as its steering group. The first review, on 27 July, changes nothing, and the minutes say so. On 14 October the University Council decides to offer short online courses for working adults from 2028; twelve days later the plan moves: an owner and a promise for the products that choice needs move up to December, the agents' written mandates wait to March, and everyone it touches hears why that week. On Monday 31 January 2028, promised sits beside delivered: 41 days from ask to first use down to 14, promises met from 58% to 86%, 340 to 910 people a month, the old warehouse retired, three data partners named, and what didn't work, said as plainly.

Considered and not named, so the film keeps one idea: Kaplan and Norton's strategy review meetings (*The Execution Premium*, 2008: operational reviews, strategy reviews and a yearly test of the strategy), which the quarterly and yearly reviews follow in spirit (the balanced scorecard is named in *Measures that matter*); OKRs' quarterly cycle; and the Deming cycle (plan, do, check, act), which the module's loop already carries.

Where the film differs: Rumelt warns against strategies that are lists of goals, and his book doesn't prescribe a calendar; the quarterly review is a practitioner's habit, put here in a meeting that already exists, so no new committee is made for it. TOGAF's Phase H keeps an architecture current against change; the film applies its three kinds of change to the whole data strategy, not only to the architecture, and keeps Noor's map moving with it. Article 6's rhythm is at least every four years, a treaty's; the film's is a quarter, a data team's.

Practice, from the series' brief (the author's "defining strategy, implementing it, measure it and iterate", 5 November 2026) and from practitioners' advice, for the author to confirm: book the reviews the day the strategy is approved, in the meeting where its sponsor already sits; ask the same three questions each time, so the reviews can be compared; a review that changes nothing still counts, and the minutes say so; when the world moves, say what moves up, what waits and why, with the risk of the wait read at every review, to everyone it touches, that week, written on the one page with the date and the reason; keep the map with it; end the year with promised beside delivered, and what didn't work said as plainly as what did, with an action and an owner. Every one of these is said in *On Monday*.

## Rigour to check before recording

Checked on 10 November 2026:

- CFCs in fridges, spray cans and foam, chemically inert and slow to break down, drifting up over years to the ozone layer; Mario Molina and F. Sherwood Rowland's article of 1974 in *Nature*, from the University of California, Irvine: [the Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1995, press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1995/press-release/). The film's "safe to handle" is the inertness the release names.
- What the layer stops: all of the sun's UV-C, most of its UV-B, little of its UV-A; UV-B raises the risk of skin cancer and cataracts and damages plants and crops: [US EPA, *Health and Environmental Effects of Ozone Layer Depletion*](https://www.epa.gov/ozone-layer-protection/health-and-environmental-effects-ozone-layer-depletion). So the film says "most of the sun's harmful ultraviolet light"; a first draft's "most of the sun's ultraviolet light" was wrong for UV-A, and was changed before recording, on screen and in the Spanish captions too.
- Farman, Gardiner and Shanklin, *Large losses of total ozone in Antarctica reveal seasonal ClOx/NOx interaction*, [*Nature* 315, 207–210, May 1985](https://www.nature.com/articles/315207a0), dated 16 May 1985 by the [British Antarctic Survey](https://www.bas.ac.uk/news/marking-40-years-since-the-discovery-of-the-ozone-hole/); Halley's station opened in 1956 for the International Geophysical Year of 1957–58, and its record in the paper starts in 1957 ([BAS leaflet, *The Ozone Hole*](https://www.bas.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/public_information_leaflet_bas_the_ozone_hole.pdf)). "By 1984, the ozone layer over Halley Research Station in Antarctica was only about two-thirds as thick as it had been in earlier decades": [UKRI, *The story behind the discovery of the ozone hole*](https://www.ukri.org/who-we-are/how-we-are-doing/research-outcomes-and-impact/nerc/the-story-behind-the-discovery-of-the-ozone-hole/).
- "Much is still unsure": in 1985 the cause of the Antarctic losses wasn't known; the paper's own abstract says "possible chemical causes must be considered", and suggests one. The film says no more than that.
- Signed on 16 September 1987, in Montreal: [the UN, *International Day for the Preservation of the Ozone Layer*](https://www.un.org/en/observances/ozone-day).
- The first step, in the 1987 text, Article 2.4: "for the period 1 July 1998 to 30 June 1999, and in each twelve-month period thereafter, its calculated level of consumption of the controlled substances in Group I of Annex A does not exceed, annually, fifty per cent of its calculated level of consumption in 1986" (Group I of Annex A: the five main CFCs); the habit, Article 6: "Beginning in 1990, and at least every four years thereafter, the Parties shall assess the control measures provided for in Article 2 on the basis of available scientific, environmental, technical and economic information", with panels of experts convened at least a year before each assessment, which is the film's "with the science behind it". Source: the 1987 text as published in the Dutch treaty series, [*Tractatenblad* 1988, no. 11](https://www.officielebekendmakingen.nl/trb-1988-11.pdf); Article 6 as it stands: [the Ozone Secretariat](https://ozone.unep.org/treaties/montreal-protocol/articles/article-6-assessment-and-review-control-measures).
- London (27–29 June 1990) and Copenhagen (23–25 November 1992): the [Protocol as adjusted and amended](https://cil.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/1987-Montreal-Protocol.pdf) names both meetings; the main CFCs to zero by 2000 in the countries not under Article 5 (the richer ones), brought forward to 1 January 1996 at Copenhagen: [Article 2A](https://ozone.unep.org/treaties/montreal-protocol/articles/article-2a-cfcs). The fund for the poorer countries (the Multilateral Fund) was agreed in London in 1990 and set up in 1991 ([US EPA, *International Actions: The Montreal Protocol*](https://www.epa.gov/ozone-layer-protection/international-actions-montreal-protocol-substances-deplete-ozone-layer)); the film says London agreed "a fund", which is true of 1990.
- "Nearly 99 per cent": [WMO, January 2023, on the 2022 assessment](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/ozone-layer-recovery-track-helping-avoid-global-warming-05degc) ("the phase out of nearly 99% of banned ozone-depleting substances"); the [Ozone Secretariat, *Ozone and you*](https://ozone.unep.org/ozone-and-you) ("99 per cent of the ozone-depleting substances that are controlled under the Montreal Protocol have been phased out"). A first draft's "the gases are gone" said too much (some remain in old equipment, and in the air for decades), and was changed before recording to "no longer made or used".
- Back to its 1980 values "around 2066 in the Antarctic, around 2045 in the Arctic, and around 2040 for the near-global average (60°N-60°S)": the [2022 Scientific Assessment of Ozone Depletion, executive summary](https://csl.noaa.gov/assessments/ozone/2022/executivesummary/). The film gives 2040 for most of the world and 2066 for Antarctica.
- "Up to 2 million cases of skin cancer will be prevented each year by 2030": UNEP's [press release of 16 January 2015](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/press-release/montreal-protocol-averts-threat-large-increases-skin-cancers), on its Environmental Effects Assessment Panel's 2014 assessment; the Ozone Secretariat says "about two million skin cancers every year" by 2030, from one global model.
- Richard Rumelt, *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* (Crown Business, 2011), chapter 5, *The Kernel of Good Strategy*: a diagnosis, a guiding policy and coherent action. The page links no source for it: the McKinsey Quarterly article it is often read from (*The perils of bad strategy*, 2011) didn't answer on 10 November 2026.
- TOGAF's Phase H, *Architecture Change Management*: simplification change ("can normally be handled via change management techniques"), incremental change ("may require partial re-architecting") and re-architecting change ("requires putting the whole architecture through the architecture development cycle again"), as in TOGAF 9 and [the TOGAF Standard's ADM](https://pubs.opengroup.org/togaf-standard/adm/chap12.html) (its pages need a sign-in; the film's page links [the TOGAF overview](https://www.opengroup.org/togaf)).
