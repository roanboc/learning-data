## The promise

Every line passes two tests: anyone who has looked for the meaning of a word at work and found three can follow it, and an experienced data governance lead finds nothing wrong in it. Most organisations don't have one business glossary. They have several, each made for a good reason by one team: siloed, disagreeing, hidden from everyone else, or bought and never opened. Several glossaries are the same as none, because nobody knows which to trust, so everyone keeps their own. The fix is to keep one, chosen because it can be trusted and every tool can read it, not because it's the biggest: each term with a definition, an owner, a status and the date it last changed. The others are retired, or made to read from it, and every copy is made from it one way, says where its words came from, and is checked. A business glossary isn't a data dictionary or a catalog. When a word changes, the change arrives as a pull request listing what it touches and who owns each part, and its owner approves it; a number in a meaning is a rule too, kept in one place. And the glossary keeps what people agreed; it doesn't make the agreement. **Many glossaries are no glossary: keep one, and sync every tool from it.**

## The story in one paragraph

In 221 BC, the king of Qin conquered the last of the warring states and became China's first emperor. The old states had their own scripts, weights and cart widths, and the empire made them one. It even decided which words its officials used: what had been the king's was now the emperor's. Far to the south, in the county of Qianling, a scribe kept those rules on a wooden board, found in a well at Liye in 2002, and the county's records use the new words almost every time. The board was a glossary, kept where the work was done, and used. Today, Mei, who owns what *award* means, has found it defined in five places (the last line of *Who decides*). Yusuf finds five glossaries: the business glossary on the platform, with the words Mei approved, which the data team's models copy from; Planning's spreadsheet; an appendix to the registrar's policy; the faculties' dashboards, each with its own description; and a glossary tool bought in 2019, with 600 terms. Between them, three different awards. Each was made for a good reason; two can only be found by the people who made them; the biggest was last opened fourteen months ago; and when *award* changed on Monday, only one of the five changed. Yusuf brings the terms' owners together, and they keep one glossary: not the biggest, but the one they can trust and every tool can read, the glossary on the platform, beside the data. The others are retired, or made to read from it: the 2019 tool's terms with an owner move across and the rest are archived; Planning's spreadsheet links to each term; the registrar's policy says "as defined in the business glossary"; the faculties' dashboards show the glossary's words, read from the platform. The data team's copy already worked this way (*Written once*): on Monday night the sync found the change and opened a pull request listing what the new words touch and who owns each part; Mei approved it, and for once saw where her words are used; Jun saw a number in the words, and made it a setting the code reads. Monday's words were argued out by Mei and the faculties in a workshop: the glossary keeps the agreement, it doesn't make it. The people who kept the other glossaries are thanked, and their best definitions move into the one that stays. Policy 12 said reports count by the glossary's definitions: now each word has one home.

## The look

*Watercolour*, the series' look since 17 October 2026 (`films/master-and-reference-data/shared/src/wash.js` and `people.js`, bundled as they are, with the series' places in `../shared/src/rooms.js`). Only the university's own places: the floor of offices behind glass, Yusuf's office, Planning's room, the stewards' meeting room, and the registrar's, the platform team's and the faculty analyst's desks close up, with their screens.

- **The past is painted the same way,** with the place and year in the corner: a road and a town gate, and a county office, in Qin China, 221 to about 220 BC; then the board, as found in 2002. Its people wear their own time: long robes crossed at the collar, the left panel over the right, and belted, in dark, undyed and earth colours, black favoured; a long cap for the official at the gate, headcloths for the others; a writing knife and a whetstone at each scribe's belt. A warm umber wash behind them. Nothing in the picture borrows a style from Chinese painting: the same pencil and wash as the rest of the series.
- **The board's writing is not imitated.** The ink of the board, and of the scribes' slips, is drawn as faint, worn strokes in columns, too small to read; the rules themselves are given in English on a card beside it. The film doesn't draw invented characters.
- **Colour carries meaning.** The three meanings of *award* keep *Who decides*'s colours: violet (the new words, with microcredentials), rose (the words before Monday), olive (degrees only). Gold is governance's decisions; green runs in the work; grey can go.
- **Fewer diagrams, more situations.** Ideas are shown as people doing things in places: holding up a printout, looking for a login, choosing at a table, stamping a binder retired, approving on a screen, arguing over sticky notes.

## Sound

A new palette for this film ([`source/tools/score.py`](source/tools/score.py)): wood, as the board found at Liye was. It follows the series' rule from the master data films: music under the opening, then only at a chapter's turn, a moment on screen or a wordless pause; no constant bed, no urgent pulse, no voices detuned against each other.

- **A consort of recorders** holds the chords, one recorder to a note, breathy and soft: E minor and its neighbours for Qin and the board, told in the series' own voice, with no Chinese instruments or scales; G major, its relative, from "a university needs the same". Chords play all through the opening only. After it, a chord comes in at a few turns, under a moment on screen (the three meanings lit, nobody knowing which to trust, every screen reading *award*, the keepers thanked) and in the wordless ending.
- **A low marimba**, with soft mallets, gives the notes of what appears, and means the same thing all through. Each meaning of *award* has its note: violet's, the one that stays, is G, the home note; rose's is A and olive's F-sharp, on either side. Each glossary sounds its note as it's named, the three sound together for "three different awards", and a glossary made to read from the one that stays steps home to G. Every tool reading the glossary is one figure, G and D rising, left to right, as each screen is reached.
- **The series' mark,** 1-5-4-8, plays on the alto recorder, with the marimba an octave under it, under the title and the end card.
- **The effects** are ox carts' wooden wheels, wooden slips, a brush of ink, paper, pins in cork, sticky notes, a marker, muffled keys, a rubber stamp and an archive box. Each comes at the moment its thing appears, timed to the same word as the picture.
- **When the music plays,** checked by [`source/tools/quiet.py`](source/tools/quiet.py): after the opening, no chapter has music for more than half its length, or for 12 seconds without a break. The most is 37% (*Five glossaries* and *One word, one home*), and the longest run is 7.2 s (*Agreed by people*). Nothing beats on a phone's speaker, and nothing pulses.
- **Levels** were set against *When strategy moves*, a film that sounds right: the opening's chords, the notes and the effects sit where that film's do. Everything is low-passed at 2.6 kHz, and the mix is -16 LUFS.

## Rigour to check before recording

Checked on 21 October 2026; the sources are listed below.

- **221 BC.** The *Shiji* (Sima Qian, chapter 6, the Basic Annals of the First Emperor of Qin), under the year 221 BC: 一法度衡石丈尺。車同軌，書同文字。 Zheng (2024) translates: "He standardised the measurements of weight, capacity, and length. Carts all had the same axle width, and writings all had the same script." The same passage says he changed the name of the people from *min* to *qianshou*, "the black-headed". "The last of his rivals" is Qi, conquered in 221 BC; the title the king took was *huangdi*, emperor. "Their own scripts, weights and cart widths" is what the unification replaced; Zheng notes that standardising had begun before 221 BC, within the old states (Chu's manuscripts were already fairly uniform), so the film says the states had their own ways, not that nothing was shared. The ruts the carts roll in are the usual reason given for one axle width; the narration doesn't claim it.
- **"Which words its officials used."** Liye 8-461, the "wooden tablet of nomenclature changes" (*gengming fang* 更名方). Zheng (2024): found in the excavation begun in 2002 at Liye, Longshan county, Hunan, the site of a Qin county town, among more than 38,000 slips and tablets, most from a disused well; 27.4 by 12.5 by 0.6 cm, pieced together from six fragments, the top right missing; two rows of entries; 54 entries identified; 35 of them change proper names, titles and appellations, 14 set which graph writes which meaning, 2 set graphic forms, and 3 avoid taboo characters. The Liye documents date from 222 to 210 BC. Staack (2023): the well, J1, had 18 layers, and the documents are thought to come from the archives of Qin's Qianling county.
