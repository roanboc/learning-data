So this plan was made without the usual questions. Material the author shared shaped the emphasis, never the content: the generalist who joins business, solutions and data; prototyping and iterating fast; the team first; and four ideas for leading a team (minimalism, kaizen, Ubuntu, and decentralisation and emergence), each taught here from its own sources. Nothing personal about the author goes on the site. Where the author has said something before that applies, the plan follows it: people's engagement first (27 October 2026), and agents that never approve, with nothing reaching production without a person and a second approver (3 November 2026).

**The module's promise.** Data work now needs new skills, and leading the people who do it is a skill too. Each film gives one practice a newcomer can use on Monday, and shows it in a team a viewer can believe: for someone starting in data, a skill to grow; for someone leading data people, something to do differently. Light on tools, heavy on practice.

**What the site already has, and what's new here.** The site's films teach the work: modelling, governance, quality, protection, agents. People are in every story, and the governance films put people's engagement first, but no film teaches the people skills themselves: seeing across specialists, working beside an agent, learning on the job, listening for the real question, explaining for a decision, saying no well, leading a team, owning an outcome, measuring work without ranking people, and helping people grow. This module does, on one question everyone at a university understands: are there enough teaching rooms?

**Where it stops.** The module *Data management* follows the same data manager and team in the same year, at the level of strategy: Helena's first ninety days, the business case, the roadmap, one list of requests, the measures page and the Friday half-hour. This module tells the same year from the people's side, day to day, and names those films by title once they're on main. The craft of data quality is the module *Data quality*'s.

## The insights, and where each comes from

| Insight | Where it comes from | Film |
|---|---|---|
| Go deep in one thing, and learn enough of the others to see the whole route of a decision: someone has to see across the specialists | T-shaped skills; SFIA; *Range* | 1 |
| A draft is cheap; judgement isn't. When an agent drafts, framing the question, checking the answer against what you know, deciding and answering for it are the skills that grow | Advanced chess, and Kasparov on the 2005 freestyle tournament: "Weak human + machine + better process was superior to a strong computer alone"; the agent films | 2 |
| Skills grow fastest on real work, in small steps, beside someone who knows; teaching what you learn makes it last; learning needs protected time | Lave and Wenger, *Situated Learning* (1991); Ericsson on deliberate practice (1993); pairing | 3 |
| A request names someone's guess at a solution; listening finds the decision behind it; agree the question in writing | Schein; Rogers and Farson | 4 |
| Lead with the decision and the answer; show the part of the data the decision turns on, not only the average; say what you don't know | Minto; Knaflic; Tufte; Gilbert Daniels' measurements of pilots (1952) | 5 |
| Saying no to a date isn't saying no to the person: find the interest behind the position, show the trade-off, offer what you can do well, and when | Fisher and Ury; Ury | 6 |
| Simpler doesn't just happen: stop what nobody uses, together, openly and reversibly, and give the time back to what matters | Lean's waste; *kanso*; GOV.UK's "Do less" | 7 |
| Improvement is everyone's job, on their own work, in small checked steps: measure, simplify, standardise, then automate | Imai; Shewhart and Deming | 8 |
| A team's relationships make each member: nobody alone on what matters, nobody the only one who knows, a mistake said out loud thanked, not blamed | Ubuntu; Edmondson | 9 |
| A few clear shared rules, good tools and trust let decisions be made where the knowledge is, and order emerges | Ostrom; Lansing; data mesh; *Team Topologies* | 10 |
| Ownership is answering for an outcome, not only doing a task: know who uses your work, notice first, say so first; never blame | Data mesh's product owner; Dekker | 11 |
| Measure the work together to fix the system; a measure made a target for one person stops measuring, and people hide mistakes; pair each measure with one that balances it | Goodhart and Strathern; Deming; *Accelerate*; SPACE | 12 |
| People grow through goals they help set, regular feedback about the work and its effect (never about who they are), and a next step with more to own | Kluger and DeNisi; situation-behaviour-impact; *Radical Candor*; lesson study | 13 |

## Practical advice the films carry

The author wasn't asked for advice from practice: the module was planned on assumptions overnight. This is what practitioners do, from the sources above and from the earlier films' teams, for the author to correct:

- Keep depth in one area; learn the words of the others; draw the whole route of one real question on one page; spend time with each specialist; ask "who uses this, for which decision?" (1)
- Give an agent the decision, the words and what counts; check its draft against something you know is true; the decision, and the responsibility, stay with a person. (2)
- Pair with someone who knows, an hour a day at the start; protect learning time; teach what you learned within a week. (3)
- Ask what the request is for: what will you decide with it, by when, who else decides, what would change your mind; say back what you heard; agree the question in writing. (4)
- One page: the decision first, then the answer, then the evidence; the peak, not only the average; what you don't know; the same numbers for everyone. (5)
- Never a bare no: ask what the date is for, put the trade-off where everyone can see it, offer what you can do well and when, let the people who decide choose, and keep your word. (6)
- List what the team keeps running and who uses it; stop what nobody uses, openly and reversibly; say where the time goes. (7)
- Change one small thing, check it, keep or drop it; the leader asks what's in the way, gives the time and shows the small wins. (8)
- Pair, rotate and write things down, so nobody is alone on what matters or the only one who knows; thank the person who says "I made a mistake"; protect leave. (9)
- A few rules, each with its reason; a template and good tools; let people decide where the knowledge is, and check the rules still serve them. (10)
- Every product has a named owner with the authority to act; the owner says first when something is wrong, and fixes it or raises it; never blame. (11)
- Read the measures together, as a team, to find what to fix in the system; never rank people by them; pair each with one that balances it. (12)
- Weekly one-to-ones about the person, never a status report; feedback about the work and its effect, both ways; goals the person helps set; credit to the team. (13)

## The thread, the world and the people

One question across one year, at the same fictional university as every earlier film, a year ahead: February to December 2027, as in the module *Data management*. Each film says so once, where the university first appears, with the scene tag *The university, 2027 · imagined*.

**The question.** On Thursday 11 February 2027, Graham Ellis, the new Director of Facilities, asks the data team for "a dashboard of room use, by Easter". In half an hour on Tuesday 16 February, Helena and Noor find the decision behind it: whether to ask the University Council for a new teaching building of 40 rooms and 2,400 seats, about A$140 million, proposed because "the rooms are always full". The team answers from Semester 2 2026's thirteen teaching weeks: the bookings, from the timetable the registrar's office keeps, and the head counts per room per hour, from the rooms' sensors (counts, never people). The 186 centrally timetabled rooms are booked 38% of the week, but 93% from 10:00 to 14:00, Tuesday to Thursday, and in one booked hour in five nobody came. Everyone was right about what they saw: academics saw full rooms at the peak, Facilities saw empty corridors on Monday mornings. Graham's one-page paper (1 June) leads with the decision and shows the peak, not the average; on Thursday 24 June 2027 the University Council defers the building for two years and asks for the timetable to change instead.

**The team's year.** Ivy Reyes joins on 1 March and learns the whole route of the question; the team's agent drafts, and a person decides; the team stops what nobody uses, improves its own work one small change at a time, shares the night calls so nobody is alone, and agrees a few rules that let the faculties plan their own rooms. From 1 July Ivy owns her first data product, *Teaching space use*; the team reads its measures together; and her year ends, on 14 December 2027, with a next goal she chose. ## The look

**Sunlit plaster**, an assumption for the author to review, shared with the module *Data management*: the data team's rooms in daylight. A warm limewash wall, lit from a tall window on the right of the frame, whose sun falls on the wall as a slanted patch of panes with the soft shadows of leaves outside; warm timber below; timber and graphite things with soft shadows falling down and to the left. Words are on one-page sheets of thick paper pinned to the wall, each with a band of colour only where the colour means something, and every word at least 28 px as seen. The light tells the time: morning, afternoon, late, grey, or evening with a lamp. For the story of the rooms, the university's teaching building is cut open like an architect's section, each room a small stage behind the cut walls, lit when it's in use, so a viewer sees at a glance which rooms are booked, which are used and which stand empty; the camera can glide into any room.

Colour means the same in every film of both modules: **blue** design and plans; **green** done, or value; **amber** risk, cost, or waiting; **orange** data; **violet** only an agent (the agent films' ceramic sphere with its violet band, never a person). People and their clothes wear none of them. People are the agent films' people, unchanged in shape: an adult's proportions, one three-quarter head, arms down, a hand only on the thing the story is about. The room lights them: the side towards the window a little warm, the side away from it in the room's shade, and their soft shadow on the wall behind.

Why: a module needs one design language of its own, and this one is a room where people work things out, calm and spare, with the light doing the work. It is unlike the agent films' cool marble and oak, *Data quality*'s granite bench under a lamp, *Modern data basics*' navy map, *Data protection*'s night glass and the governance films' watercolour. The kit is in [`shared/`](shared/), with a README that says how a film uses it.

## Rules from the plan

- **Draft, and the merge is the release.** Each series' `series.json` says `"draft": true`; a film merges into main once its checks pass, and that is its release. Nobody runs the release workflow for these films.
- **No release status** in a film, its captions, its page or Pause and think; a film keeps the status it read, with its date, in its script's sources only.
- **Nothing personal about the author** on the site: no employer, role history, personality type or hobbies. The four ideas are taught from their own sources, never as the author's philosophy.
- **Light on tools.** A tool appears only where the story's work happens in it (a pull request, a catalog entry with its owner, a dashboard, the one list of requests), drawn at the level of what people do, with its official logo, unaltered, where a film names it.
- **Agents propose, people approve.** An agent drafts; nothing reaches production without a person, and every change is approved by someone other than its author.
- **Blameless, and fair to everyone.** People on every side of a problem, each right about what they saw. Measures are the team's, never a target for one person; nobody is ranked.
- **The kit as it is.** Every film bundles `films/the-new-skills/shared/` unchanged; a film's own pictures go in its own source, with a prefix of its own; a change every film needs goes in a pull request of its own.
- **The fictional university**, a year ahead and tagged as imagined; Spanish as captions and pages only.
