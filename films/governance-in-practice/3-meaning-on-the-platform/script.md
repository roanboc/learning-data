# Governance you can use · Meaning on the platform: script

*The script of *Meaning on the platform*, film 3 of 6 of *Governance you can use*, the third series of data governance, in nine chapters, in English, painted in watercolour. Drafted on 28 October 2026 and made the same day, when the author asked for the films of the three series to be made and merged one after another. It follows the plan the author approved on 23 October 2026 (film 16 of that plan: the tools films open on a scene of the university's own, on a line from the film they expand), and the rule of 28 October 2026 for every opening (scenario, problem, solution and benefits, each said outright; *Opening story* quotes them). Every file on screen is the example project's own, or one of the worked examples kept beside it (*What the example project has*): the glossary's export on Monday night, the sync's report on it, and the metric view, on top of *Governed in dbt*'s Tuesday change. The platform's pages are drawn from Databricks' documentation as it stood on 28 October 2026, and each feature's status is said where it isn't generally available. As the author asked for these films, it isn't rendered here: the release workflow renders it.*

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never kept a glossary can follow it, and a data steward or an analytics engineer who keeps one finds nothing wrong in it. *One word, one home* chose one glossary of five, and made every other place a copy that says where its words came from. This film shows it running on the platform: Mei's term *award* as a page, in beta, published from Noor's map, with its owner and the tables that hold it; the data team's copy beside the models, which names its source, and the descriptions each build sends on to the catalog; the nightly sync, and the pull request it proposed on Monday night, with everything the new words touch; the 6 credit points kept once, as a setting; awards conferred counted once, as a metric view the project builds, which both reports read; Genie One and the data team's agent, reading the same meaning; and three layers of meaning, each with one home. **One meaning, read by people, reports and agents.**

## The story in one paragraph

On Monday, in the registrar's office, the meaning of *award* changes: microcredentials, the certificates of short courses, count too, from 6 credit points. Mei Tanaka owns it. When Yusuf asked what slowed her down, she said: I approve the words, then never see them used. Until now, award was kept in five glossaries, with three meanings between them, so Planning's census report counted 4,812 awards and the draft submission to the government 4,867, and the university could have sent the government a number it couldn't explain. Now award has one page on the platform, every copy is checked against it each night, the 6 credit points are kept once, as a setting, and awards conferred is counted once, for every report and every agent. That night, the check finds Monday's change; on Tuesday morning, Mei sees everything her words touch; on submission day, both reports say 4,867. The film then opens each piece: award's page, with its words, its owner, its domain, the tables that hold it and where its words come from, published from Noor's map, where a change is made; Clara reading it beside the data, and Genie One citing it; the data team's copy, which names its source and the day it was synced, and each build sending the descriptions on to the catalog, one way; the nightly check, and the pull request it proposed, with the old words and the new and what they touch; Mei's approval and data governance's on Tuesday morning; the setting `min_award_credit_points: 6`, which Jun's column `counts_as_award` reads; the ledger of awards conferred, the metric view the project builds, and the two reports that read it; Genie One answering 4,867 from the metric and citing award's page, its own guess struck through, and the data team's agent giving the same answer; and three layers of meaning on Noor's map, each with one home. It ends on submission day: both reports say 4,867, counted by the same metric, with the same words.

## The look

*Watercolour*, the series' look, as in *Governed in dbt* and *Governed in Unity Catalog*, with this series' files and screens.

- **The university's own places,** from the series' shared set: the registrar's office, wide and close, Mei's desk and her screen (`gv_paintRegistrar`, `gv_paintRegistrarClose`, `gv_paintRegistrarDesk`, `gv_paintScreenDesk`); Yusuf's board and desk (`gv_paintBoardClose`, `gv_paintDesk`); Planning's room for Clara (`gv_paintPlanning`); the floor of offices (`gv_paintFloor`); Noor's map (`gv_paintMapClose`); the data team's board; and this series' big screen on a desk, on the data team's, the registrar's office's or Planning's wall, and the data team's desk from above (`gu_paintScreenBig`, `gu_paintFilesDesk`). Three are this film's, painted once in its own `src/plan.js`: the data team's room at night, nobody in it, the moon in the window and Jun's two screens lit, and Jun's screen and the big screen at night.
- **The three meanings of *award* keep their colours,** as in every film: violet for the glossary's words, rose for the old words, olive for degrees only (`GV_MEAN`, `GV_MEANINK`). The new words, wherever they're copied, are violet; a copy that's behind is rose until the sync brings it up. A copy in step with its home has a violet thread to it.
- **Tools look like their pages, drawn for the film,** as this series' shared pieces draw them: award's page ("Databricks · Unity Catalog · Pages · beta"), a table's and the metric view's pages in the catalog, the pull request and its body ("GitHub · pull request"), the SQL editor, Genie One and a Genie agent. Each carries a small bar with the product's name, in no product's colours. The only logos are on the board's cards, as in every film of the series.
- **Files look like files,** as in *Governed in dbt*: a sheet of paper-white, monospaced, few lines and large (at least 28 px after zoom), copied from the project, each line with its number, and lines folded where the film doesn't need them.
- **The setting is a dial and the count a ledger,** each with its rule written at the top, so a newcomer sees a number kept once and a count defined once before the files show them.
- **Words on screen** are what the files, pages and report say, shortened only with "…". The report's line with the day it ran isn't shown: the series sets no date for that Monday.
