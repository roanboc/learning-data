## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never run an access review can follow it, and a data protection officer and a platform engineer who runs Unity Catalog and Microsoft Entra ID each find nothing wrong in it. The series built the study's protections one by one: an agreement, a class for every column, the least shown, grants by design, tags that travel, one policy for many tables, copies that keep the rules. This last film adds no lock. It asks the question an owner, an auditor or a learner can ask at any time: can you prove it held? Each line of the agreement gets a proof, read from a record the platform or the directory keeps, not from anyone's memory: who could read (the grants, and the directory's access review), whether every personal column has its class and every class its policy (a check every morning), where the protected columns went (lineage), who read (the audit log), and that access ended on its date. Two gaps are found and closed, and their harm measured: none. *Protection you can't prove is protection you hope for.*

## The story in one paragraph

From the 1970s, heat spoiled polio vaccine on its way to clinics and left no sign, so health workers threw away vials they couldn't vouch for, or a child was given vaccine that no longer worked. The vaccine vial monitor, a square printed inside a circle on each vial's label, darkens with heat and never turns light again: it doesn't keep the vaccine cold, it shows whether the cold held, at the moment of use. Three months after Mei signed Anil's agreement, Tuesday 12 January 2027 is the study's first review, and Yusuf asks of each line: can we prove it held? The grants go only to the study's group, made from the contract. The directory's access review asks Mei about each member: Elena Lindgren, a research assistant, moved to another project in December, still works at the university, and nothing took her out; Mei removes her, and the directory does when the review closes. A check every morning found a new column, `mobile`, on the exam support table, which classification called a phone number and no owner had classed; Mei classed it `direct` and the policy for direct masked it, with no new rule. Lineage shows nothing was made from it; the audit log shows only the night load read the table in those 31 hours, and that the study's tables were read only by Anil, and by Elena until before she moved. On 30 June 2027 the directory takes the members out and the grants end; on 1 July Yusuf checks it. Proof finds the harm, and shows where there was none.

### 5 · Where it went, and who read it · 1:14

**Narration.** Lineage is the platform's record of what each query read and what it made, column by column. The mobile column went nowhere: no query made anything from it. And the study's protected columns: the student number reaches it only as the learner code, the date of birth only as the year. Names, emails and exam support never reach it. Then, who read? The audit log keeps who read each table, and when. In those thirty-one hours, only the night load read the support table. And over the three months, the study's tables were read by Anil, and by Elena until 11 December. No one else. After an earthquake in Yogyakarta, in Indonesia, the power was out for days. ## Sound

*A round lyre*, with *a tam-tam* (claimed in the module's `palettes.md` on 5 November 2026; neither was in any score or palettes list on main or on a branch). Both are made in `source/tools/score.py`; `music.py` has neither.

- **The lead:** a round lyre, the early medieval lyre of northern Europe (six gut strings over a hollow wooden body, as found at Sutton Hoo and Trossingen), plucked softly with the fingers: a short, warm ring, no vibrato. Its six strings are tuned D3, G3, B3, D4, E4, G4, in just intonation from G, so every two of them sound pure together and nothing beats. It's played strum and block, as many of its players play it today: one hand strums every string while the other stops all but the chosen ones, so only the allowed notes sound, and a stopped string gives only a muted touch. The strum is the proof that the block held: where the film's data is kept from someone, a string is stopped (Elena's place, as the review closes; the masked mobile column; the names, emails and exam support that never reach the study), and the end card's chord is struck with its one string stopped.
- **The mark:** two strings, the outer one (E4) the reference, as the monitor's circle is; the inner one plucked a step higher each time and never lower (G3, B3, D4), until it meets the outer one (E4 with E4: the square as dark as the circle, and the vial thrown away). In the data story the inner note always stays below the outer one: it held.
- **The partner:** a tam-tam, struck softly once with a large felt beater: a slow, dark bloom with no clang, kept under 2.5 kHz. Three strokes in the film: the heat that builds unseen in the opening, the turn to the review, and access ending on its date.
- **Key:** G major for the clinic and for what held; E minor for the child given vaccine that no longer worked.
- **Music:** under the opening story; after it only at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen (Elena's place as the review closes, the masked column, the vials of Yogyakarta, the series' eight films) or in a pause without words (the title card, the end card); never a bed under the voice; no figure played twice.
- **Effects,** one at a time, each tied to something on screen, soft and below about 2.5 kHz: a card's soft landing as a page or card comes in; a muffled tap as a line is lit or a row comes in; three soft knocks as the vials go into the bin; a click for each of Mei's decisions, for the alert sent and for the class that seats; a soft knock as each line of Yusuf's check is ticked; a sheet filed in the register; a far, low rumble for the earthquake.
- **No urgent pulse:** nothing in this film is a breach or a rush (`film.json` `urgent: []`).
