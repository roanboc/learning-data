# More than a platform

**Video script, first draft, 12 November 2026.** For the author to read before anything is drawn. It follows [the proposal](proposal.md), which the author confirmed that day, with two corrections said below.

About 3 minutes 20 seconds, with 405 words of narration (about 125 words a minute), in the voice the films have used so far. English narration; Spanish captions, page and narration follow once the English words are settled. Every name, place and number is fictional.

It comes after *The Inner Life of Data*, on a page of its own. It has no story from the past, no labs, no scenarios and no Pause and think (the author, 12 November 2026).

## What it corrects in the proposal

- **Three systems, not four.** The proposal had the student system, the learning platform, HR and payroll, and finance each counting *enrolled* its own way. HR and payroll doesn't count enrolments, so a practitioner wouldn't recognise it. The script uses the three that do.
- **No 312%.** In *The Inner Life of Data* the class reads 312% full because of a wrong sketch, not because offices define a word differently. Giving that number a second cause would contradict that film. The script uses two plain counts for one class.

## The numbers

One class, Data Science 101 on Tuesdays, with 180 seats. Three systems count *enrolled*:

| System | What it counts | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Student system | Everyone who signed up | 176 |
| Learning platform | Everyone who can open the course | 171 |
| Finance system | Everyone who has paid | 149 |

Each count is right by its own meaning; nobody made a mistake. The decision that waits on it: whether to open a second class. At 176 of 180 the class is full; at 149 it isn't.

## Script

### Scene 0 · It worked · 0:00–0:22

**Narration.** One tap became a decision. A new class opened, and 140 more students got a seat. The platform did its job. So why, a week later, do two offices bring two numbers for the same class to the same meeting?

**Picture.** The last picture of *The Inner Life of Data*: the phone, Enrolment confirmed, and the card with 140 more seats. The card slides aside. A meeting table, seen from above, with two sheets of paper on it: 176 and 149.

**On screen.** New class added · 140 more seats · 176 · 149

### Scene 1 · One word · 0:22–1:10

**Narration.** Start with one word: enrolled. In one office, with one system, it means one thing. Nobody has to ask. Then the university grows. The student system counts everyone who signed up. The learning platform counts everyone who can open the course. Finance counts everyone who has paid. Three systems, three meanings, and each one is right. The platform moves all three, without a fault. Now add more teams. Add an AI agent that answers from the same data. Whatever the word means now is repeated everywhere, at once, and with confidence. The platform was never the problem. What changes the answer is how the organisation works, what was defined, and who decided.

**Picture.** The word *enrolled*, alone, in white. One system card lights behind it. Then three, each in its source colour from *The Inner Life of Data*, and the word splits into three copies that drift apart, each with its count. Lanes of light carry all three into the lakehouse, unchanged. More reader cards appear around the gold layer, then an agent's card, and the three numbers multiply across them. On the last line, three plain words settle under the picture, one at a time.

**On screen.** enrolled · signed up: 176 · can open the course: 171 · has paid: 149 · the organisation · the definition · who decides

### Scene 2 · The route · 1:10–2:24

**Narration.** So where do you start? Not with the platform. Here is the route. Enterprise architecture: how does this organisation work? Data governance: who decides? Those two are the foundations. Then you build. Data modelling: what does each word mean? Modern data basics: how does data move, run and get served today? Analytics engineering: how is it built, and tested? Data quality: is it right, and in time? Two side routes leave after the foundations. One is about sharing and protecting. Master and reference data: is it the same student everywhere? Data protection and privacy: who may see it, and for how long? The other is about agents: what may an agent do with it? They all meet again at the end. Data management: how does it all fit in one plan? And last, leading data people: who leads the people who do the work?

**Picture.** The learning route draws itself, in the site's own shape and colours: the blue line first, station by station, each station's name appearing as it is said, with its question under it for as long as it is spoken. The amber loop rises for the two stations of sharing and protecting, and the violet loop drops for the agents. The loops join the line again before the last two stations. When *Data governance* is named, the two sheets from the meeting reappear beside it for a moment, and one definition replaces them.

**On screen.** Foundations · Build and monitor · Share and protect · Agents · Lead · the eleven stations' names · each station's question, one at a time

### Scene 3 · One map among many · 2:24–2:38

**Narration.** People have mapped this ground before. The best known map is DAMA's wheel, with governance at its centre. Any good map will do. This one is a route, because you have to start somewhere.

**Picture.** A wheel, drawn in the film's own hand: a centre and ten parts around it, with no words but *Governance* in the middle. It opens out and lays itself along the route.

**On screen.** DAMA-DMBOK · Governance

### Scene 4 · What you can do here · 2:38–3:10

Pick a goal, and the route lights up your stops. Or search for a word, and find the film that says it. What you have done stays with you, in your own browser.

None is a picture of the screen. On the last line, a ring fills round one station.

## Rigour

| Scene | What it claims | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | A platform that works can still feed a meeting two numbers | Each number is a correct count of something different; *From words to data* opens on four offices and four numbers |
| 1 | Three systems count *enrolled* differently, and none is wrong | Signed up, has access and has paid are three real states of one student; the counts fall as each state asks for more |
| 1 | An agent repeats the meaning it is given | *Agents at work on data*: an agent answers from the definitions and context it reads |
| 2 | The order of the stations | `modules/route.json`, as the Topics page draws it; *Leading data people* is last (the author) |
| 2 | Each station's question | Each topic's own line on the Topics page, put as a question |
| 3 | DAMA's wheel has governance at its centre and ten areas around it | DAMA-DMBOK, second edition, as *Every area, one job* cites it |
| 4 | What a visitor can do | The site as it is: the films, Pause and think, labs, scenarios, goals, search, and progress kept in the browser |
| 5 | Platforms have matured | The author's view, said as a view; no product or date is named |

Rules it keeps: no product is named in the voice; no feature's status is said; the portal's tools are shapes, not pictures of the screen; no count of topics or films is said, so the film needn't change when the site grows.
