| 1 | Which story opens the film? The module's setup proposed the oath of Hippocrates. | Bletchley Park, 1939 to 1945: everyone signed the Official Secrets Act before being told what the work was, each learned only their own part, and what was read went to a few named commanders, read in front of an officer who took it back and destroyed it. Almost 9,000 people by 1945, three in four women; the secret held for almost 30 years, until 1974. Chosen because *For this purpose only* (master data) already opens on Charaka's oath, a physician's promise of silence, so Hippocrates would repeat the idea. It's the module's third story set in England (after the City of London, 1990, and the Habeas Corpus Act, 1679) and its second of the 1940s (after Washington, 1943). Nothing violent on screen; Enigma, Ultra and Turing aren't named. | Chapter 1; chapters 3, 4 and 6 come back to it; openings.md |
| 2 | Does the agreement list the data by class? Agreed with that film's and *Show the least*'s threads; the words are in numbers.md. | Chapter 4; the end card's question; numbers.md |
| 3 | What is the agreement's "contract"? | The agreement's terms written again where the platform reads them (its columns, its group, its end date); the platform team makes the grant from it, never by hand, and every grant names its agreement. | 4 | What does each person's confidentiality agreement say, and how is it kept? | Six lines in the signer's voice: "I keep what I see to myself", "I use it only for the work it's given for", "I keep no copy where it isn't allowed", "I answer for the data I'm given", "I report anything that goes wrong, at once", "It holds after I leave". Everyone who works with the university's data signs it before any access (staff, contractors, researchers, the data team) and renews it every year; Yusuf Demir, in data governance, keeps them; a person stays in a group that gives access only while theirs is signed and current, and a lapse pauses their groups until it's renewed. *Who is it, really?* shows the same card. | Chapter 3; numbers.md |

ICO's data sharing code, NIST's access agreements and ISO 27001's confidentiality agreements are in the script's sources, not on screen. | Chapter 4 |
