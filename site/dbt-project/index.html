A goal, and its tree

Planning's goal comes first: more learners finish the award they started. Below it hang the questions that show whether it's met, each with a measure, in a tree. This is Goal–Question–Metric. Clara Jensen asked one question herself; the data team keeps the tree with her and suggests first the ones nobody has asked.

SQ1Suggested to Clara Jensen, no answer yet

Of the learners near a graduate certificate at census, how many finish within the year?

Why now
Planning puts advising where learners are near an award at census. This shows whether it worked, faculty by faculty.
The decision it informs
Where to put advising next census
Size, when suggested
Small: the date each award is conferred, beside what's already counted

SQ2Suggested to Clara Jensen, for Planning and the faculties, no answer yet

How many learners near an award can't get a unit they need next term, by faculty?

Why now
It warns before census, while there's still time to put a unit on. Counts only, no names, as BR3 asks of AQ1 in Census planning 2026.
The decision it informs
Which units to run next term
Size, when suggested
Medium: learners' study plans and the units on offer, neither read yet

SQ3Suggested to Clara Jensen, no answer yet

Of last year's new bachelor learners who didn't finish, how many came back this year, by faculty?

Why now
It's the sector's own retention rate, which the Department of Education publishes for every university, so the definition exists.
The decision it informs
Where to put first-year support
Size, when suggested
Small: enrolments, year on year

If Clara says yes in a ticket, the question takes the next AQ number and follows the steps after this one.

What the question needs

The architecture finds the question's domain, Student, and the data objects it needs: Learner, and the credit each learner holds towards an award. It records the question as driver DRV1, in the same words.

Passes on Each data object maps to a glossary entity, in data governance.

10 · Enterprise architectureStep 5 of 12

Where it's born

A learner is recorded first in the student system, the system of record, and copied in the learning platform and the short-courses system. The architecture keeps which entities each application holds.

Passes on The three systems are the dbt project's sources.

20 · Data governanceStep 6 of 12

What the words mean

Each data object maps to a glossary entity that data governance manages, owned by the area that answers for the data. Learner means anyone who has learned with the university, in any of its systems. The registrar's office owns the student domain, so Mei Tanaka, of that office, owns the term.

Passes on The data project copies the definition into its conceptual model, with its source. The glossary sync fails on any drift.

20 · Data governanceStep 7 of 12

The core entity, under contract

The glossary entity maps to a core entity, core_learner, which every team shares. Data governance's information architect writes its enterprise contract first, in the Open Data Contract Standard: columns, keys, each column's class and known limits.

Passes on The data team builds core_learner to it. A check fails if the two differ, and a change that breaks a reader needs a new version.

30 · Data projectStep 8 of 12

Built up to the core

The data team builds what it takes to reach the contract, in dbt's layers. How it starts depends on what's already there.

Nothing built yet. From the sources: each system's tables staged, one learner matched across systems, and core_learner built to its contract.

Models already exist. No core entity defined yet: the team checks first what the existing models do, then adds core_learner on top of their staging and intermediate models, and doesn't start again from the sources.

Passes on Each model carries its owner, its glossary term and each column's class, so the platform's catalog shows them too.

What dbt alone can’t tell you

Planning’s answer is 12. Ask it eight questions. A dbt project alone answers one well: how it’s built. Add the business context, one piece at a time, and see what each piece answers.

1 of 8 answered

  • How is it built, and is it tested?

    Answered by the project itself

    Its SQL, its lineage back to each source and its tests are all in the project.

    30 · dbt

  • Who asked for it, and why?

    dbt alone

    A model's description, if someone wrote one.

    With the context

    Planning, for the census and for its goal: more learners finish the award they started. Analytics recorded the question as AQ1, and the initiative Census planning 2026 carries it in ticket DATA-142.

    00 · Analytics’ records

  • What counts as an award?

    dbt alone

    A column description, written model by model, maybe differently in each.

    With the context

    One meaning, from the business glossary: a qualification for a set number of credit points, such as a graduate certificate. Mei Tanaka owns it.

    20 · Glossary

  • May a dashboard show learners' student IDs?

    dbt alone

    Nothing in the project says.

    With the context

    No. The learner asset is confidential, and a student ID is direct personal data. On Databricks it's masked for anyone not allowed to read it.

    20 · Information assets

  • If the shared learner table changes, what keeps my numbers safe?

    dbt alone

    The model's own dbt contract, if it has one. It can change in the same pull request as the code.

    With the context

    An enterprise contract in ODCS, written first by data governance. A check fails if the model differs, and a breaking change needs a new version.

    20 · Contract, ODCS

  • When is it ready, and who answers for it?

    dbt alone

    When the job finishes. An owner tag, if anyone added one.

    With the context

    Ready by 7 a.m. on census day, built every night. Clara Jensen, of Planning, owns the product, with a channel for issues.

    20 · Product, ODPS

  • Is it counted the same way in every tool?

    dbt alone

    Each tool keeps its own copy of the formula.

    With the context

    Yes. It's defined once, in Apache Ossie, and built from there into dbt's semantic layer. A check fails if dbt's version differs.

    20 · Metric, Apache Ossie

  • May an AI agent change it on its own?

    dbt alone

    Whatever its credentials allow.

    With the context

    No. An agent works alone only under a signed mandate, in test, in its own folders. No mandate covers Planning's models, and in production agents only read, with personal and sensitive data masked.

    20 · Mandates

Data contract

Open Data Contract Standard 3.1.0

Written by
Data governance's information architect, first, and only for canonical tables. The data team builds the model to it.
Holds
Columns, keys, each column's class, known limits and a version.
Lands in dbt
The core model's enforced dbt contract. A script fails if the two differ.

What its lines say

  1. Line 3The standard and its version: the Open Data Contract Standard 3.1.0.
  2. Line 5The contract's id. A data product names it to say exactly what it reads.
  3. Line 7Its version. A change that breaks a reader becomes a new version, beside this one.
  4. Line 10The product it belongs to: learner records, owned by the registrar's office.
  5. Lines 47 to 52The key: the learner's hash key, the same in every model, on every engine.
  6. Lines 56 to 60Each column's class. The business key is direct personal data, so on Databricks it's masked for anyone not allowed to read it.

Run it yourself

On your laptop

Python 3.11 or later, no account. dbt loads the sample data, builds every model and runs every test, on DuckDB.

git clone https://github.com/roanboc/learning-data-project
cd learning-data-project/30-data-project
pip install -r requirements.txt
dbt build --profiles-dir .

On Databricks

The same code, with dbt Cloud or dbt Core. Load the sample files into Unity Catalog first; the README has the steps.

pip install -r requirements-databricks.txt
dbt build --target databricks --profiles-dir .

How to run it

Where things live

The project has a repository of its own: a folder where each question starts, then three folders that are usually three teams' own, the enterprise architecture, data governance and the data project. Below, the main folders, and what each holds. Each link opens it on GitHub.

Every folder, and what it holds
  • 00-initiatives/Where a question starts: Analytics' record of every question it's asked, each initiative's business requirements, as its tickets hold them, and each goal's tree, where the data team finds the questions to suggest first.
  • 10-enterprise-architecture/The university's enterprise architecture: every area and data domain, and below them only what the questions need: their data objects and the systems of record that feed them.
  • models/What's built, by domain: staging, the core tables every team shares, and the marts each team reads.
  • sources/What comes in from the three systems: tables, keys, freshness and known limits.
  • seeds/Reference data the business owns, and published numbers to reconcile against.
  • tests/Business rules that must hold, reconciliations, and rules about the project itself.
  • macros/Conventions written once: keys, point in time, Planning's rule, and Databricks tags and masks.
  • analyses/The evidence: profiling the sources, a design choice, and checking the result.
  • exposures/Who uses each output: dashboards, apps, forecasts and agents.
  • 20-data-governance/Data governance's own folder, beside the project: the glossary, owners, the architecture map, information assets, data products, contracts, metrics and agents' mandates.
  • docs/How the work is done: the process, conventions, decisions and the quality scorecard.
  • AGENTS.md skills/What an AI agent may and may not do here, and the skills it uses.
  • examples/Pull requests, nights and Databricks set-ups that the films tell as stories.
  • databricks.yml resources/The jobs, deployed as one bundle to test and to production.
