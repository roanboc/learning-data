| 1 | Which opening story? | Norman Borlaug and his Mexican colleagues' shuttle breeding of wheat, as the module's openings proposed (1945 to 1956), carried on to 1963 for the film's return at the end. | 4 | Which four wheats came in 1948? | The memoir's: Kentana 48, Yaqui 48, Nazas 48 and Chapingo 48, "resistant to stem rust". | 5 | When did Mexico start selling wheat abroad? | 1963, as the memoir says ("By 1963, Mexico was an exporter of wheat") and *New World Encyclopedia* has it; Mexconnect says 1964, with about 500,000 tonnes a year. The film says "by 1963 Mexico was selling wheat abroad" | Chapter 7 |
| 6 | Are the Mexican agronomists named? | No. The narration says "a small team: young Mexican agronomists, and a plant scientist from the United States, Norman Borlaug", and the team is drawn together, never named one by one. | 7 | How high and how far are the two fields? | Toluca is at 2,090 m in the memoir and 2,640 m in FAO's chapter, so the film says "more than two thousand metres up"; Ciudad Obregón to Toluca is about 1,390 km in a straight line, so "over a thousand kilometres away". The summer field is "the highlands near Mexico City", as Minnesota says, not Toluca by name | Chapter 1 (the map) |
| 8 | How much faster was it? | "It halved the time to a new wheat", as the Rockefeller Archive Center says ("cut development time in half"); the memoir says "at least double the progress". No number of years is given, since the sources give none | Chapter 1 |

The film says "they helped buy land for a new research station, and taxed their own wheat to add more", which leaves room for the others who paid too | Chapter 6 |
| 11 | How is Mexico drawn without borrowing its culture? | Nothing stands for Mexico: no flag, eagle, sombrero or serape, and no Mexican instrument, rhythm or tune in the score.
